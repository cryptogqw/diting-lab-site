Why searchers and builders like candle auctions (and why it matters for the builder market post-Glamsterdam)

In briefWhy searchers and builders favor candle auctions and what it means for the post-Glamsterdam builder market

SummaryThis Ethereum Research forum post models auctions with a random end time and few participants, showing that open-bid "candle" auctions can yield higher bidder profits than sealed-bid auctions. The author argues this outcome arises as an equilibrium of the one-shot game, without explicit coordination or collusion. The post links this to frequent bid updating and discusses implications for ePBS and sealed bidding.

Delving Bitcoin Nov 2, 2026 #

Machine-checked soundness of BIP340 batch verification with 128-bit derived randomizers

In briefMachine-checked proof that BIP340 batch verification stays sound with 128-bit derived randomizers

SummaryThe author used Lean 4 with Mathlib and VCVio to machine-check the soundness of BIP340 batch verification when its randomizers are shrunk to 128 bits and derived deterministically. The proof covers independent uniform randomizers, randomizers hashed from the whole batch, and incremental derivation. In the random-oracle model the forgery bound is (q+1)/2^128, but deterministic derivation allows offline grinding, where about 2^64 hashes get an invalid batch accepted with probability around 2^-64.

It's time to scale the trust: EIP-8288 on OP Stack

In briefScaling Trust: EIP-8288 on OP Stack for Post-Quantum Signatures

SummaryAn Ethereum Research forum discussion proposes EIP-8288 to move L2 transaction signatures and proofs out of transaction data and post one aggregate proof covering many blocks to L1. The author estimates that with leanSPHINCS signatures, transactions compress to 100 to 129 bytes, needing about 2.8 to 3.3 blobs per L1 block instead of about 79 without aggregation.

Asset-level spending limits and recovery: should rate limiting live in the asset rather than the account?

In briefAsset-level spending limits and recovery: should rate limiting live in the asset rather than the account?

SummaryThis Ethereum Research forum thread proposes enforcing spending limits and key-compromise recovery at the token contract level for ERC-20s, and potentially at the protocol level for native ETH, rather than only in account code. The author argues that account-level limits can be bypassed through existing token allowances or by re-delegating under EIP-7702. The proposal sketches rolling-window limits, a 14-day delay on security-weakening changes, an irreversible compromised state, and a pre-registered recovery address, and invites feedback.

Snowball to Snowstorm

In brief"Snowball to Snowstorm": A Forum Post Sharing a Paper on Mempool Soft-Agreement Protocols

SummaryA community member on Delving Bitcoin shares a paper describing a protocol that reaches soft agreement on mempool transactions before a block is found.
