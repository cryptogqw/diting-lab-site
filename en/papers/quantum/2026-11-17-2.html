Geometry of squeezed states. The phase-space metric, relative vacua and cosmology

In briefGeometry of squeezed states: phase-space metric, relative vacua and cosmology

Rethinking Entropic Uncertainty: How measuring without a trace leads to longer keys

In briefRethinking entropic uncertainty: how unrecorded measurements yield longer keys

SummaryThe paper proposes a new perspective for analyzing quantum key distribution security by treating Alice and Bob as one joint system and adding a third register that records virtual measurements. These virtual measurements leave no trace in the real protocol branch but condition counterfactual phase error measurements. The authors show this yields tight key rates directly from the entropic uncertainty relation for protocols using any number of mutually unbiased bases. They also indicate the approach can improve many existing security proofs.

WhyThe authors introduce a virtual-measurement register as a third system, letting entropic uncertainty relations directly incorporate arbitrarily many MUBs with tight key rates.

Reinforcement Learning designs Quantum Repeater Protocols on Networks beyond the Chain Scenario

In briefReinforcement learning designs quantum repeater protocols for networks beyond simple chains

SummaryThe paper argues that entanglement routing, a common shortcut for large quantum networks that runs chain protocols on disjoint paths, produces suboptimal results. Inspired by work on linear chains, the authors apply reinforcement learning to non-linear networks and optimize over a larger space of non-routing protocols. The learned protocols outperform a routed extension of the swap-ASAP protocol, including in parameter regimes where swap-ASAP is considered state of the art. The authors conclude that routing should be replaced by non-routing methods.

Andr\'es Agust\'i Casado, \'Alvaro Troyano Olivas, Javier Faba Garc\'ia et al.

Sharp L_p-Regularity for Quantum Markov Semigroups

In briefSharp L_p regularity constants for quantum Markov semigroups

SummaryThe paper determines sharp universal constants for positive-input L_p regularity of finite-dimensional quantum Markov semigroups that have a faithful invariant state. For 1<p<∞ the constant is p·sin(π/p)/(2(p−1)) under KMS symmetry and p/(2(p−1))·tan(π/2·min{1/p,1−1/p}) without a symmetry assumption. At p=1 the endpoint values are π/2 and π/4 respectively.

Lijun Wang

In briefMeasuring microwave power at millikelvin temperatures using direct and dispersive qubit coupling

SummaryThis paper uses superconducting qubits as quantitative sensors to measure microwave power at millikelvin temperatures. Sequential strategy for depolarizing strength estimation

In briefA sequential strategy to improve estimation of depolarizing noise strength

SummaryThis paper studies whether repeatedly applying the same depolarizing channel to a quantum probe, in sequence, can improve the precision of estimating the channel's strength p. It uses qudit and two-qubit probes and analyzes the quantum Fisher information and its regularized version. The authors find that as p approaches zero, the quantum Fisher information grows linearly with the number of repetitions, while in the weak-noise regime it peaks at a finite number of repetitions.

Catalytic compression of classical communication in exact entanglement transformations

In briefCatalysts reduce classical communication needed for exact entanglement transformations

SummaryThis paper studies whether a catalyst, returned unchanged and uncorrelated with the output, can reduce the classical communication required for exact transformations between bipartite pure entangled states. It shows the message alphabet size can be reduced by a factor that grows without bound as the Schmidt rank increases. In the asymptotic many-copy limit, however, the leading communication rate is the same with or without a catalyst. The optimal catalytic cost is characterized using Rényi entropies.

Quantum adiabatic and diabatic optimization: A Hilbert space engineering strategy with Rydberg arrays

In briefQuantum adiabatic and diabatic optimization using Hilbert space engineering with Rydberg atom arrays

SummaryThis paper studies how shaping the Hilbert-space geometry of neutral-atom arrays can control quantum annealing for combinatorial optimization. The authors analyze a family of blockaded-chain models, where boundary terms encode a maximum independent set problem and the bulk induces an auxiliary problem on independent sets of cliques. When these problems are consistent, low-energy modes lie mainly in the target subspace, which motivates a diabatic annealing strategy using target-compatible excited states. The strategy is tested on the diamond-chain graph family.

Experimentally relevant witness for non-Markovianity in discrete time crystals

In briefAn experimentally usable witness for non-Markovianity in discrete time crystals

SummaryThe paper studies discrete time crystals (DTCs) coupled to a reservoir with a finite correlation time. Using a post-Markovian master equation, the authors show that the Floquet spectrum splits into the ordinary Markovian spectrum and a uniformly contracted replica of the unitary Floquet spectrum. Once the reservoir correlation time exceeds the DTC's Markovian lifetime, the signal decay rate no longer depends on system parameters, so the decay reveals the correlation time. The estimator is checked in two structurally different models.

TCBP-JOBS: GPU-Accelerated Quantum Error Correction Decoding and Noise Learning with Near-Optimal Accuracy

In briefTCBP-JOBS: GPU-accelerated quantum error correction decoding and noise learning with near-optimal accuracy

SummaryThis paper introduces two methods for decoding quantum error correction: trajectory-calibrated belief propagation (TCBP), which learns calibrated fault probabilities from belief propagation trajectories, and jittered OSD beam search (JOBS), which uses these probabilities to guide parallel GPU searches. On surface, color and bivariate-bicycle code circuits, it reaches logical error rates comparable to the Tesseract decoder at physical error rates of 0.1% to 0.4%, and up to 4.5 times lower on the largest codes. The paper also reports serial speedups over Tesseract, while noting that Tesseract is more accurate at lower physical error rates.

SCOPE: A Physical Execution Latency-Guided Quantum Circuit Compilation Framework

In briefSCOPE: A Quantum Circuit Compiler Guided by Physical Execution Latency

SummaryThe paper presents SCOPE, a quantum circuit compilation framework that uses an operator Schmidt time surrogate to estimate the minimum implementation time of fused gates and guides genetic search with it. Quantum-Assisted D-FACTS Scheduling for Moving Target Defense under Switching Limits

In briefQuantum-assisted scheduling of grid devices for moving target defense under switching limits

SummaryThis paper proposes a method for scheduling distributed flexible AC transmission system (D-FACTS) devices used in moving target defense, sharing candidates across periods and evaluating schedule completion to allocate limited physical checks. A quantum approximate optimization algorithm (QAOA) generates activation candidates, while classical scheduling enforces operating and switching constraints. On a modified IEEE 118-bus system, combining these techniques recovers the final-budget reference schedules better than period-by-period ranking in the primary operating window. Quantum and classical candidates yield identical recovery curves under this checking approach.

In briefA boson-based way to compute electronic structure on quantum Krylov subspace methods

SummaryThis paper proposes a boson representation of electronic systems, using electron-hole pair bosons as basic degrees of freedom, to reduce the quantum circuit depth of quantum subspace expansion (QSE). The authors benchmark it against fermionic QSE and CCSD on equidistant and dimerized periodic hydrogen chains (H2)n. In briefWhat kinds of quantum advantage exist and how to assess them.

SummaryThe paper proposes a multi-label taxonomy for classifying quantum advantage claims by structural, interference, and coherent mechanisms, recording separately whether each is necessary and how strong the classical comparison evidence is.
