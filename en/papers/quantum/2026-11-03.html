Quantum identity authentication protocol via perturbed probability amplitudes

In briefA quantum identity authentication protocol that encodes random sequences into perturbed probability amplitudes of entangled states.

SummaryThis paper proposes a quantum identity authentication protocol based on perturbed probability amplitudes, aimed at establishing mutual trust between communicating parties in the post-quantum era. The protocol encodes a random sequence onto the probability amplitudes of quantum entangled states and generates n-bit random authentication information from n-bit pre-shared information. The authors state that it resists impersonation, CNOT, and entanglement measurement attacks, and can be extended to a bi-directional version.

Xiangqun Fu, Zheng Han, Hongwei Li et al.

Entanglement Allocation for Quantum Integrated Sensing and Communication

In briefEntanglement Resource Allocation for Quantum Integrated Sensing and Communication

SummaryThis paper studies how a limited pool of entanglement can support both communication and distributed sensing in future quantum networks. It proposes a network-level quantum integrated sensing and communication (Q-ISAC) framework in which a quantum base station dynamically allocates Bell pairs and partitions sensing resources among multiple GHZ probes. The authors model effective quantum Fisher information and solve the allocation problem with a hierarchical constrained proximal policy optimization (HC-PPO) method. Numerical results show that GHZ probing needs about 27 to 30 percent fewer sensing qubits than separable probing under the same sensing requirements.

Shiva Kazemi Taskooh, Mehdi Monemi, Ekram Hossain et al.

Anti-distinguishability of quantum channels

In briefAnti-distinguishability of quantum channels: a study of perfectly excluding candidate channels with a single query

SummaryThis paper studies single-query perfect anti-distinguishability, or exclusion, tasks for finite ensembles of quantum channels, mainly using Choi operators and process POVMs. It distinguishes weak exclusion, where each outcome excludes a candidate with certainty, from strong exclusion, where every candidate must also be excluded with nonzero probability. The authors derive conditions based on the support of the Choi operators and apply them to measure-and-prepare, state-space contraction, Pauli, and replacer channels.

Ardra Ajitha Vijayan, Nidhin Sudarsanan Ragini

In briefGaussian quantum integrated sensing and communication: a quantum scheme that communicates over an optical link while locating a defect, and studies how to split shared entanglement between the two tasks

SummaryThis paper studies a quantum integrated sensing and communication problem over an optical link, where a low-power transmitter communicates with a receiver while locating a defect that produces a backscattered signal.
