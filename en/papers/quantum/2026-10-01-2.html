Gibbs Sampling in the Shattered Phase by Decoded Quantum Interferometry

In briefQuantum decoding sampler for hard spin-glass Gibbs distributions

SummaryThis paper applies Decoded Quantum Interferometry (DQI) to sample from Gibbs measures of classical Ising spin Hamiltonians, reducing the task to a quantum decoding problem. For Ising k-spin glasses on random hypergraphs, shattering and disorder chaos create a topological barrier that blocks many stable algorithms, including Glauber dynamics, near the dynamical phase transition. The authors prove that both a classical Prange-based algorithm and DQI with a quantum decoder can break this barrier and sample at lower temperatures for large k.

In briefQuantum de Finetti theorems for states and channels under any distance measure

SummaryThe paper proves standard and Renner's exponential quantum de Finetti theorems in the stronger form of operator inequalities, which yield bounds in every Schatten norm and for every quantum Rényi divergence satisfying data processing. The Single-Copy Quantum Bandit Is Classical: An Exact Spectral Collapse

In briefThe Single-Copy Quantum Bandit Is Classical: An Exact Spectral Collapse

SummaryThis paper studies multi-armed bandits in which each arm supplies an unknown quantum state and mean rewards are defined by a known effect F. The authors show that spectral measurement of F followed by classical KL-UCB is asymptotically instance-optimal among single-copy policies, and adaptive or randomized measurement cannot improve the leading logarithmic regret coefficient. For consistent policies with quantum memory, a converse is derived using the Umegaki half-space divergence, and the two regret floors coincide only when the arm commutes with F. Whether the Umegaki floor is attainable is posed as an open problem.

In briefA classical sublinear-time SDP solver inspired by the quantum OR lemma

SummaryThis paper presents the first classical sublinear-time solver for sparse semidefinite programs in the bounded-radius regime, without low-rank or Frobenius norm assumptions. It combines randomized Lanczos filtering with a sampling-based estimator to estimate many Gibbs-state expectation values at once, mirroring the sample-reuse mechanism of the quantum OR lemma.
