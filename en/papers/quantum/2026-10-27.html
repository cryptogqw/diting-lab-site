Prepare-and-measure quantum key distribution from a bound on information leakage

In briefQuantum key distribution secured by a bound on information leakage

SummaryThis paper examines whether, in prepare-and-measure quantum key distribution, a single bound on information leakage from the sender's source can replace a detailed model of the source. The authors show that this bound, quantified by the probability of guessing the input from everything the source emits, suffices to prove security against collective attacks, with no assumptions on the form, dimension, energy or purity of the emitted states. They note that the standard B92 and BB84 protocols cannot certify a key under this assumption, whereas closely related protocols can reach one secret bit per round in the ideal limit. For noisy implementations, key rates are computed with semidefinite programming relaxations, and the best protocols tolerate visibilities down to 0.84, corresponding to an 8% quantum bit error rate, versus the 11% threshold of device-dependent BB84.

WhyProves security from a single leakage bound without device modeling, reaching near-ideal key rates; validation rests mainly on theory and SDP computations.
