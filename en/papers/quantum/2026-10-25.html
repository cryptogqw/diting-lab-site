A Reduction Bound for Entanglement Detection by Positive Free Maps

In briefA bound showing free positive maps cannot beat the reduction criterion

SummaryThe paper studies positive maps built from free probability, extending work by Collins, Hayden, and Nechita on entanglement detection. It proves that positive free maps tied to compactly supported freely infinitely divisible distributions are no stronger than the reduction criterion in every ancillary dimension. Consequently, these maps cannot detect positive partial transpose entanglement.

Chin-Yen Lee

On the uniqueness of translation-invariant ground states of the S=1 Heisenberg and J--D antiferromagnetic chains

In briefBoundary-condition independence of infinite-chain ground states in the S=1 J-D antiferromagnetic chain

SummaryThis paper studies infinite-chain ground states of the S=1 J-D antiferromagnetic chain for sufficiently small |D|, comparing periodic chains with an even number of sites and open chains with an odd number of sites under the same magnetic field at both ends. The authors prove that both settings have infinite-chain limits along the full sequence and that the two limits coincide. The common limit is a symmetric, locally unique gapped ground state whose local expectation values depend continuously on D.

Hal Tasaki

Minimal ancilla for boosting type-I photonic fusion

In briefA minimal ancilla design for boosted type-I photonic fusion

SummaryFusion gates can create large entangled photonic states for linear-optical quantum computing, but without extra photonic resources their success probability is limited to 1/2. This paper proposes a boosted type-I fusion gate that uses only two independent ancillary single photons and no entangled ancillary input. It reaches a direct success probability of 5/8, matching a previous four-single-photon scheme while using half as many ancillary photons. The circuit is mostly passive, with one active element that applies a phase shift or swaps two modes based on intermediate photodetection results.

A. A. Melkozerov, S. S. Straupe, M. Yu. Saygin

Resonant Multiphoton Conversion Mediated by a Shared Vibration in Cavity QED

In briefResonant multiphoton conversion via a shared vibration in cavity QED

SummaryThe paper shows that a vibration shared by two emitter groups can create multiphoton resonance and constructive interference among conversion pathways. For opposite displacements with integer magnitude ratio r≥2, the displacements cancel, linking the displacement ratio to the conversion order. Independent vibrations can be tuned to resonance yet cancel conversion, showing energy resonance alone is insufficient. Relative displacement direction is presented as a means to control conversion and its noise response.

Feasibility of Distributed Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computing: From Quantum Links to Algorithms

In briefFeasibility of Distributed Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computing: Linking Quantum Processors to Run Algorithms

SummaryThis paper analyzes the resources needed for distributed fault-tolerant quantum computing, where superconducting processors are connected by microwave or optical links. Under forward-looking microwave-link assumptions of a 1 MHz Bell-pair generation rate and 90% fidelity, one communication channel per node can substantially reduce the physical qubits needed per node, but execution time rises about tenfold. Reducing that time overhead requires considerably more communication resources, such as higher Bell-pair fidelity and extensive multiplexing.

Two exact quantum queries use at most six variables

In briefTwo exact quantum queries depend on at most six variables

SummaryThis paper gives a direct analytic proof that any total Boolean function computable exactly with at most two quantum queries depends on at most six input variables, and this bound is sharp. Earlier work relied on a numerical classification of Boolean functions on at most four variables, whereas this is, to the authors' knowledge, the first unconditional proof. The key technical step is a support-and-branch decomposition that handles interference, since query-path vectors need not be orthogonal.

Yaqiao Li

Resource-Optimal Screening for Two-Tier Quantum System Characterization

In briefResource-optimal two-tier screening for characterizing quantum systems

SummaryQuantum processors need frequent benchmarking to track hardware drift, but standard methods such as randomized benchmarking consume substantial resources. The paper proposes a two-tier strategy in which a lightweight probe runs regularly and triggers full characterization only when a statistically significant change is detected, with the probe designed to maximize detection sensitivity under resource constraints. The framework is applied to gate-quality verification and compiler benchmarking, and experiments on Rigetti Ankaa-3, IQM Garnet, and IQM Emerald match the theoretical predictions for optimal circuit sizes.

In briefHidden Fisher Information in Many-Body Quantum Sensors: Readout Complexity Bottleneck

SummaryThe paper introduces a moment Fisher hierarchy that measures how much quantum Fisher information low-degree collective spin moments can access, and defines a readout-complexity scale k90. In a nonlinear collective-spin sensor for systems up to N=100, first moments access only about 10^-2 of the available information, and the required degree grows to roughly 28. This identifies a finite-size readout-complexity bottleneck in many-body quantum metrology.

Banach-Cesaro positive operator-valued measure

In briefBanach-Cesàro positive operator-valued measure: a mathematical construction of covariant measures on the real line via averaging, with an integration theory and Fourier transform for them.

SummaryThe paper constructs bounded, finitely additive, covariant positive operator-valued measures on the real line by applying generalised Banach-Cesàro asymptotic averaging to locally finite covariant POVMs. Unlike the strictly shift-invariant case, the averaged operator values may fail to commute while the total mass is normalised. The authors develop an integration theory for complex-valued functions and define an operator-valued Fourier transform on the resulting integration spaces, analysing the algebraic constraints covariance places on exponential multipliers.

Efficient Block Encodings of Vibrational Hamiltonians

In briefEfficient block encodings for simulating molecular vibrations on quantum computers

SummaryThis paper develops more efficient ways to block-encode the vibrational Hamiltonian of molecules, a step needed for quantum simulation of molecular vibrations. Induced Bell-CHSH observables in the Fewster-Verch measurement scheme

In briefBell-CHSH observables induced by probes in the Fewster-Verch framework

SummaryThis paper gives an explicit operator-level construction, within the Fewster-Verch measurement framework, of observables that test the Bell-CHSH inequality in relativistic quantum field theory. Using local couplings to Majorana probes, it obtains dichotomic Bell observables for a free Majorana field in 1+1 dimensions. Under explicit attainability hypotheses, these induced observables reach the Tsirelson bound in a state prepared from the vacuum. The construction also extends to coupled scalar fields.

Task-Directed Spectral Design for Local Quantum Tomography

In briefTask-Directed Spectral Design for Local Quantum Tomography: Choosing Probe Directions

SummaryThis paper studies local pure-state quantum tomography when a parametric model, a credible center, and a differentiable downstream task are known before measurement. It uses generalized eigendirections defined by the pullback Fubini–Study metric and the task metric to build a fixed library of geodesic fidelity probes, and proves optimality results under stated conditions. In six-qubit numerical experiments, the proposed design yields lower mean task error than the best post hoc adaptive methods while using far fewer settings and no feedback.

Guy Hay, Nir Sharon
