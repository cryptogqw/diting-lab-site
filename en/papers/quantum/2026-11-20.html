High-temperature Gibbs states are uniformly Markovian and trivial

In briefProof that high-temperature Gibbs states are uniformly Markovian and trivial

SummaryThe paper proves that above a constant temperature threshold, Gibbs states of local noncommuting qudit Hamiltonians show exponential decay of mutual and conditional mutual information. This establishes a uniform Markov property for these states. The authors build a classical representation using projective 2-designs, which yields a convergent cluster expansion with positive weights despite noncommutativity. They also note that high-temperature Gibbs states are separable and belong to the same mixed-state phase as the maximally mixed state.

Local velocimetry for atomic superfluids: from Riemann rarefaction waves to quantized vortices

In briefMeasuring local velocity in atomic superfluids, from Riemann waves to quantized vortices

SummaryThis paper presents a method for measuring local velocity in two-dimensional atomic superfluids. It uses matter-wave interferometry based on Raman-Bragg beam splitters to map the local gradient of the superfluid's wavefunction phase onto shifts of interference fringes, reconstructing the 2D velocity field. The technique is applied to two canonical problems: directly observing the self-similar evolution of velocity in 1D Riemann rarefaction flows, and mapping the full 2D velocity field around quantized vortices, demonstrating quantized velocity circulation.

Yansheng Zhang, Yi Jiang, Feiyang Wang et al.

Query-Optimal Quantum Minimum Finding from Numerical Estimators

In briefQuantum search for the minimum using numerical estimators with optimal query counts

SummaryThe paper proposes a quantum minimum-finding algorithm for objectives accessed through numerical estimators, avoiding the logarithmic overhead of amplifying estimate confidence before searching. At constant confidence, it finds a 2ε-optimal label using O(√N) base-estimator queries. The authors also give a result for preparations assigning known probability mass p to low objective values, with O(p^{-1/2}) queries. Applications include selecting near-optimal bounded expectations and guided ground-energy estimation.

WhyThe authors propose quantum minimum finding avoiding logarithmic confidence amplification, claiming O(√N) queries and worst-case tight bounds for the additive error.

Fast, in-place, and ancilla-free quantum algorithms for integer multiplication and inversion

In briefFast, in-place quantum algorithms for integer multiplication and inversion that need no ancilla qubits

SummaryThis paper presents quantum algorithms that perform integer multiplication, modular multiplication modulo a power of two, and modular inversion in place, overwriting the input with the output. These operations use O(n^{1+ε}) gates for any ε>0, with no ancilla qubits in most cases and only logarithmically many for one variant. The authors state these are the first quantum algorithms to achieve both subquadratic gate counts and sublinear ancilla counts for each of these in-place operations.

WhyThe authors give the first in-place quantum multiplication and inversion with subquadratic gates and sublinear ancillas, unified under one theorem, mainly affecting quantum arithmetic theory.

In briefNon-Binary Detector Error Models: A Noise Description Method for Fault-Tolerant Quantum Error Correction
