Causal confusion in quantum systems: distinguishing direct cause and common cause

In briefCausal confusion in quantum systems: telling direct cause from common cause apart

SummaryThe paper compares two measurement setups: a direct-cause scenario, where one quantum state is measured twice in series with a quantum channel in between, and a common-cause scenario, where a shared bipartite state is measured in parallel. It identifies input states for which Pauli observable joint expectation values match in both setups, a situation called causal confusion. The authors prove that, for arbitrary finite-dimensional systems, a channel exhibits causal confusion for some input state if and only if it is a positive-partial-transpose (PPT) channel.
