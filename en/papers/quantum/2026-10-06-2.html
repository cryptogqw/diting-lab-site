Discovering New Problems for Decoded Quantum Interferometry

In briefNew Problems for Decoded Quantum Interferometry Screened by Soundness Rules

SummaryDecoded quantum interferometry (DQI) solves optimization problems using classical error correction, but it is unclear which problems could show a quantum advantage. The paper proposes soundness rules that screen candidate problems by code parameters, decoding guarantees and classical baselines. It proves that for counting satisfied constraints under worst-case Hamming decoding, DQI's guarantee exceeds Prange completion by at most (√2−1)/2, a cap that optimal polynomial intersection asymptotically attains. An AI-agent search guided by these rules proposed two candidate applications, and on the larger instances tested, the authors report DQI's expected quality exceeded their classical solvers' results within fixed budgets.

Rhik Mazumder, Pradeep Niroula

Quantum minimum description of density matrices

In briefMinimal quantum description length for density matrices with known spectrum

SummaryThe paper studies a variant of Schumacher compression that asks for the minimal memory needed to compress many copies of a density matrix with known spectrum and unknown eigenbasis, without preserving its purification. For fixed dimension and distinct positive eigenvalues, the cost is determined through its additive constant. Achievability generalizes Werner's cloning map to GL(d,C) irreducible representations with finite trace-distance bounds, and the converse uses a quantitative Koashi–Imoto incompressibility result under a spectral-gap assumption. A Lean certificate for the proofs is provided.

Patrick Hayden, Alexander Maloney, Jinzhao Wang et al.

Streaming Regev's Quantum Factoring Algorithm

In briefStreaming Regev's Quantum Factoring Algorithm: A method to make Regev's quantum factoring algorithm use fewer qubits.

SummaryA major practical problem with Regev's factoring algorithm is its large space usage, and it was previously thought to be incompatible with qubit recycling, which streams control qubits in phase estimation. The paper builds on Ragavan and Vaikuntanathan's Fibonacci accumulator construction, using superposition masking to avoid uncomputation and phase estimation with Fibonacci powers of a unitary. Random Order in Quantum Streaming: Replenishment and Robust Lower Bounds

In briefRandom-order quantum streaming: replenishment and robust lower bounds

SummaryThis paper studies how random input order changes the value of quantum memory in streaming, showing that later classical input can make a consumed quantum state useful again, a effect called replenishment. The authors build a Hidden Matching-based problem that a one-pass quantum algorithm solves in polylogarithmic space under uniformly random order, while classical random-order and quantum settings with all updates first need polynomial space.
