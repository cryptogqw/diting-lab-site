Coherent errors, syndrome extraction and the Robustness of Magic in stabiliser codes

In briefCoherent errors, syndrome extraction, and magic robustness in stabiliser codes

SummaryThis paper studies how coherent errors change the magic of states encoded in stabiliser codes, measured by the Robustness of Magic. The authors show that a unitary error adds one copy of the error's Choi state to the encoded magic, and that syndrome extraction removes this copy, restoring the logical robustness. They also give magic lifetimes for stored single-qubit magic states under Pauli noise, reporting that choosing the plane of the stored state extends these lifetimes about 1.5x on rotated surface codes under low code-capacity depolarising noise.

Enrico Bozzetto, Jonte R. Hance

Harnessing Non-Markovian Memory for Quantum-Battery Capacity

In briefUsing memory effects to boost quantum-battery capacity

SummaryThis paper studies whether the memory in non-Markovian dynamics can be actively used to increase quantum-battery capacity, taking a thermodynamic view. Within the process-tensor framework, it compares memoryless and memory-enhanced strategies and defines their gap as a memory-assisted capacity advantage. The authors prove this advantage vanishes for Markovian process tensors and is bounded by a trace-distance measure of non-Markovianity. A continuous-time example with random telegraph dephasing shows that memory built up during evolution can improve capacity retention.

Jun-Li Jiang, Xin-Zhu Liu, Xiu-Yong Ding et al.
