Using topology to reveal all mutually unbiased bases in dimension six

In briefUsing topology to classify all mutually unbiased bases in six dimensions

SummaryThe paper studies mutually unbiased bases (MUBs), which are complementary quantum measurements in which certainty in one basis gives no information about the others. Using the topology of the space of complex Hadamard matrices, which describe transitions between MUBs, the authors classify all sets of MUBs in dimension six. They show that every mutually unbiased triple belongs to a two-parameter family or is a single isolated triple built from the Fourier matrix, which implies that no four MUBs exist.

WhyThe authors use topological connectedness to classify all mutually unbiased triples in dimension six and report that no fourth basis exists.

Arithmetic Depth, Minimal Gate Alphabets, and Exact Program-State Resources for Single-Qubit Dyadic Programs

In briefExact Resource Depth for Single-Qubit Dyadic Quantum Programs

SummaryThis paper studies a single-qubit dyadic fragment of quantum programs, where the deepest exact resource layer can be determined exactly rather than bounded. The authors prove a descent theorem for a projective Clifford-cyclotomic tower and combine it with an arithmetic-closure invariant for adaptive stabilizer processing. They show that three depth measures coincide: the minimal real cyclotomic field, the shallowest gate alphabet, and the shallowest program-state layer. The result is packaged as an exact static resource effect with an outlined certificate-producing compiler.

Tony Newton, Dan-Adrian German

Optimal Holding Times for Entanglement Swapping

In briefOptimal Holding Times for Entanglement Swapping: tuning how long quantum entanglement is stored to maximize swap throughput

SummaryThis paper studies how long entanglement should be held in memory-based quantum repeaters to maximize entanglement swapping throughput under strict, probabilistic, and mean fidelity constraints. It considers two heterogeneous quantum links that differ in decoherence rates, memory capacities, attempt rates, success probabilities, and classical communication latencies. The authors develop models to estimate expected throughput and the conditional wait-time distributions for swapping, then optimize the maximum holding times of both links to meet each fidelity requirement. The proposed model and approach are validated with a discrete-event quantum network simulator.

In briefGFlowQEC: generative networks that learn to sample quantum error-correcting codes

Entanglement Entropy in Non Relativistic Collisions

In briefQuantum entanglement between two particles colliding at non-relativistic speeds

SummaryThis paper numerically studies how entanglement entropy builds up between two non-relativistic particles that collide. The particles follow the ordinary Schrödinger equation with local, finite-range potentials, starting from the product of two independent wave packets. It examines several scenarios, including tunneling and mirror effects at low energy in front of a barrier, higher energies, and attraction instead of repulsion. Although the wave functions and density matrices differ significantly across these situations, the time trends and values of entanglement entropy look strikingly alike.

A Quantum Lower Bound for Triangle Finding

In briefA quantum lower bound for finding triangles in graphs

SummaryThis paper studies triangle finding, a basic graph problem where the input is an N-vertex graph given by its adjacency matrix. The best known quantum query upper bound was O(N^{5/4}), while the best lower bound was the basic Ω(N) from unstructured search. The authors prove that Ω(N^{13/12}) quantum queries are necessary, the first superlinear quantum query lower bound for this problem. The same bound also holds for triangle detection and for detecting any fixed connected non-bipartite graph.

WhyThe authors report the first superlinear quantum query lower bound Ω(N^{13/12}) for triangle finding, using compressed oracles and transfer methods.

Noise-regularized training of quantum circuit Born machines under a finite shot budget

In briefTraining quantum circuit Born machines with noise as regularization under limited measurements

SummaryThis paper studies how hardware noise affects the generalization of quantum circuit Born machines (QCBMs), which learn probability distributions from data. The authors show that the generalization gap, when trained with maximum mean discrepancy, is bounded by the complexity of the distributions the noisy model can reach. Residual noise acts as a regularizer, and its optimal strength can be intermediate. They propose GENIE, a framework that selects the noise level during training from held-out data within a fixed execution budget, with provably bounded regret. Simulations on synthetic and census data show GENIE stays close to the best of four noise-control strategies.

Hoang M. Ngo, Quan Nguyen, Jung Taek Seo et al.
