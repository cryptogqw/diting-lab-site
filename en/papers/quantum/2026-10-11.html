Separability criteria and lower bounds of entanglement via realignment

In briefSeparability criteria and lower bounds of entanglement via realignment

SummaryThis paper builds a family of enhanced separability criteria for bipartite and multipartite quantum systems, using realignment of density matrices and vectorization of reduced density matrices. The criteria include earlier realignment-based criteria as special cases and detect more entanglement. The authors also derive tighter lower bounds for concurrence and convex-roof extended negativity, and criteria for detecting genuine tripartite entanglement.

Yuhang Xie, Yanjun Chu, Shao-Ming Fei

Non-Stationary Quantum Systems Associated with Stationary Scattering in One Dimension

In briefNon-stationary quantum systems linked to one-dimensional stationary scattering

SummaryThis paper shows that stationary scattering in one dimension can be reformulated as the evolution of an effective non-stationary, non-unitary two-level quantum system, from which reflection and transmission amplitudes are extracted. The system's states map to points on a Bloch sphere, and quantum measurements can yield information about the reflection amplitudes. Reflectionless configurations correspond to cyclic evolution of one or both poles, while spectral singularities and coherent perfect absorption correspond to dynamical spin flips between the poles.

Ali Mostafazadeh, Alireza Shariati Joni

Proposal for a multi-ion optical clock with hundreds of ions

In briefProposal for a Multi-Ion Optical Clock Using Hundreds of Trapped Ions

SummaryThe paper proposes a circular surface-trap design for an optical clock using hundreds of trapped ions, addressing frequency inhomogeneity in large ion crystals. Using a ring crystal of 400 40Ca+ ions as a case study, the authors report that the circular geometry suppresses quadrupole shift by about two orders of magnitude compared with a linear trap. Combined with stray-field compensation and dynamical decoupling, they state the architecture could reach fractional frequency uncertainty at the 10^-18 level.

Pavel Kamenskikh, Vasiliy Smirnov, Ilya Gerasin et al.

In briefQuantum Covering Lemma: Limits of Exact Anonymous Network Counting

SummaryThe paper asks whether quantum communication can determine the size of an anonymous network when no size bound is known. Quantum interference breaks the classical covering-lemma argument, yet the authors show exact counting remains impossible. They prove a quantum covering lemma preserving local possible outcomes across cyclic covering families. For uniform synchronous protocols, this rules out zero-error counting, guaranteed finite size bounds, and probability-one terminating exact leader election on anonymous rings and similar families.

Riemannian Formulation of Sample-Based Quantum Krylov Solvers for Elliptic Problems: Energy-Norm Guarantees and Duct-Flow Applications

In briefQuantum Krylov sampling solver for elliptic problems with energy-norm error guarantees

SummaryThis paper studies a sample-based quantum linear solver that measures quantum Krylov states and reconstructs the solution of positive-definite systems from elliptic PDEs using a Galerkin solve on the sampled set. It derives energy-norm error bounds, a sample complexity result, and a computable residual certificate. The method is applied to pulsatile flow in elliptic, lens-shaped, and curved ducts, with simulations of up to 12 qubits and measurements on a six-qubit trapped-ion processor. A classical residual-greedy selection is reported to outperform quantum sampling in all cases studied.

Doyeol "David" Ahn (1, 2), Byeongyong Park (1) ((1) Center for Quantum Information Processing et al.

Breakdown of thermalization in quantum spin systems with long-range interactions

In briefThermalization breakdown in quantum spin systems with long-range interactions

SummaryThis paper studies quantum spin systems with long-range ferromagnetic coupling in one spin component and a field coupling in another, finding that they generally fail to thermalize to a paramagnetic state at finite, negative energy per spin. The effect is not tied to integrability and arises from anomalous thermodynamics in the paramagnetic phase, breaking eigenstate typicality and the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis. The non-thermal states reached at long times show long-range multipartite entanglement.

Filippo Caleca, Fabio Mezzacapo, Tommaso Roscilde

Quantum circuit representation and incomplete Bell measurements in Compton scattering

In briefQuantum circuit model of Compton scattering and incomplete Bell measurements

SummaryThe paper treats massless tree-level Compton scattering as an effective two-qubit operation on electron and photon helicities. In the Bell basis the scattering map splits into two parity sectors and can be represented by a simple quantum circuit containing one nonunitary filter. After Kraus normalization, this filter acts as an unsharp incomplete Bell measurement that distinguishes the two parity sectors but not the relative Bell label within each. The authors show the incomplete measurement alone does not exceed the classical teleportation threshold, while a subsequent complete Bell-basis readout yields unit conditional teleportation fidelity.

Wei Xie, Yu-Chen Guo, Shuang Li et al.

Criticality Concentrates Extensive Sensing Information into a Local Readout

In briefCriticality concentrates large-scale sensing information into a single local readout

SummaryThis paper studies sensing information in an all-to-all dissipative feedback network near criticality. The authors show that a single local binary trajectory has a Fisher-information rate that grows linearly with N, like the full record, and that it keeps more than half the full information across the whole channel-parameter range. Photonic realization of an electromagnetic cavity field with uniform mirror motion

In briefOptical realization of a cavity field with uniformly moving mirrors

SummaryThis paper mathematically studies the effective Hamiltonian of a non-stationary electromagnetic cavity field with uniform linear mirror motion, introduced by C. K. Law. The authors reinterpret bosonic coupling terms as generators of the symplectic algebra Sp(4,R), which can be implemented with linear optical elements, allowing strong effective interactions. They derive analytical expressions for the average photon number in each mode and show generation of two-mode squeezed states. Statistical properties such as intermode correlations, the Mandel parameter, and linear entropy are analyzed, and the Wigner function visualizes the system's behavior in phase space.

Primordial Graviton Entanglement in Quasicrystalline Inflation

In briefEntanglement of primordial gravitons during quasicrystalline inflation

SummaryThis paper studies how gravitons become entangled during quasicrystalline inflation. Both parity-even and parity-odd interactions produce graviton pairs with nonzero total momentum, though the parity-odd contribution vanishes for opposite-helicity pairs with identical momenta. The transition to radiation domination adds conventional two-mode squeezing. The authors show that homogeneous, helicity-preserving evolution is unitary within each helicity sector, preserving entanglement between the full right- and left-helicity sectors, but it can remove entanglement from the reduced state of a co-propagating pair.

Jiro Soda

Monogamy of quantum steering in exciton--cavity optomechanical system

In briefMonogamy of quantum steering in an exciton-cavity optomechanical system

SummaryThis paper studies how quantum steering is shared among exciton, photon, and mechanical modes in a hybrid exciton-cavity optomechanical system. Using the Gaussian-state formalism, the authors verify CKW-type monogamy inequalities for Gaussian steering and show that the bipartite steering distribution can be tuned by system parameters. They also find that higher temperature generally weakens Gaussian steering, while in the dynamical regime steerability can increase with evolution time over suitable windows.

Generative learning of time series with holographic quantum circuit sampling

In briefQuantum circuit method for generating time series with fixed qubits

SummaryThis paper introduces holographic quantum circuit sampling (HQCS), which uses mid-circuit measurements, qubit reuse, and independently parameterized temporal blocks to generate time-series trajectories with a fixed number of qubits. The authors prove inverse-polynomial bounds on loss variance for Haar-random HQCS under a normalized exponential kernel, contrasting with exponential concentration in global Haar-random generators. Entanglement detection in dynamical quantum systems

In briefDetecting entanglement in dynamical quantum systems using evolving stabilizers

SummaryThis paper addresses entanglement detection in large quantum systems whose Hamiltonians lack symmetries, making many-body dynamics hard to track. The authors follow the evolution of generalized stabilizers of the state and use them to build linear entanglement witnesses. Numerical experiments on Heisenberg-type Hamiltonians with up to twelve qubits show that the number of measurement settings stays modest as system size grows, and detection remains effective over longer evolution times than naive approaches.
