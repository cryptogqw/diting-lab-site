Process matrices on generalised theories

In briefProcess matrices for generalised physical theories, extending indefinite causal order beyond quantum theory.

SummaryThe paper extends the process-matrix framework for indefinite causal orders to a broad class of generalised physical theories. It re-axiomatises spacetime contexts through naturality over classical probes, capturing non-contextual assignment with compatibility for convexity and coarse-graining. It characterises which theories admit a process-matrix representation of abstract spacetime contexts, and identifies when supermap and process-matrix perspectives coincide in classical, quantum, and Boxworld-like theories.

A universal gauge structure in the photon content of light-matter systems

In briefA universal gauge structure in the photon content of light-matter systems

SummaryAfter truncating the matter space of single-mode light-matter models, gauge changes leave the spectrum unchanged but alter the ground-state photon number, whose meaning has been disputed. The paper shows that when the gauge transformation is a field displacement coupled to a matter operator, this photon number is an exact quadratic polynomial in the gauge parameter. Its minimum over all gauges is gauge-invariant and, for the quantum Rabi model, is set by the ground-state energy alone.

Arka Dutta, Marcus Kollar

Structural Bottlenecks in QAOA Mixer Design: Transport and Causal Reach

In briefStructural limits of QAOA mixer design: transport and causal reach

SummaryThe paper examines mixer design in the Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm (QAOA), identifying two structural bottlenecks for shallow circuits: probability transport across configuration space and finite-depth causal reach. It derives mixer-dependent first-order objective bounds and a non-perturbative spreading condition. For MaxCut, it adds problem-graph-aligned two-local interactions to the transverse-field mixer, and reports that X+XX and X+YY improve approximation ratios over the standard X mixer at equal depth.

Electric field control of anisotropic spin traffic between spin-filtering and magnon-scattering in a layered antiferromagnet

In briefElectric field control of spin transport anisotropy in a layered antiferromagnet

SummaryThis paper studies a dual-gate field-effect transistor built on CrSBr, a layered A-type antiferromagnetic semiconductor. Micromagnetic Lagrangian and Hamiltonian Normal-Mode Formulation of Cavity Magnon-Photon Interactions

In briefMicromagnetic Lagrangian and Hamiltonian formulation of magnon-photon coupling in cavities

SummaryThis paper studies how magnons and photons interact inside a resonant microwave cavity, starting from linearized Maxwell and Landau-Lifshitz equations. The authors introduce an electric vector potential and an interaction Lagrangian, expand fields in normal modes, and derive a Hamiltonian using the Faddeev-Jackiw approach. A generalized Bogoliubov transformation then diagonalizes the coupled system into polariton variables. As an example, a perpendicularly magnetized YIG disk in a rectangular cavity shows hybridization of the cavity mode with several magnetostatic modes and frequency shifts near conducting walls.

Claudio Serpico, Marco Lembo, Giovanni Miano

Fault-Tolerant Heisenberg-Limited Quantum Metrology with Bosonic Probes

In briefFault-tolerant Heisenberg-limited quantum sensing using bosonic probes

SummaryThis paper proposes a fault-tolerant protocol that uses bosonic probes to estimate displacements in the presence of dephasing noise. The authors construct an optimal error-correcting code under a mean photon-number constraint, with a repetition-encoded qubit register and repeated boson-parity measurements. Under circuit-level bit-flip noise, a distance-d code suppresses logical failures to order p^((d+1)/2). The Fisher information scales as NT² over interrogation times of order γ_A^(-(d+1)/2), compared with γ_A^(-1) for the non-fault-tolerant approach.

Characterizing Reaction Types through Linear Free-Energy Relationships in Cavities

In briefClassifying cavity-modified reaction types via linear free-energy relationships

SummaryThis paper extends nonempirical linear free-energy relationships to reactions inside optical cavities and finds that they fall into four reaction types set by simple response-crossing rules. Reactions may follow conventional Bell-Evans-Polanyi (BEP) or counterintuitive Opposite-BEP behavior, or switch between them as cavity frequency changes. The framework is demonstrated in the Shin-Metiu model and four molecular reactions, and the complexity is reduced to competition between permanent-dipole and polarizability responses.

Jillian G. Hoffstadt, Davis M. Welakuh, Norah M. Hoffmann

Quantum Computing through Computational Imaging: Representations, Measurement, and Inference

In briefQuantum computing via computational imaging: representation, measurement, and inference

SummaryThis paper, aimed at computational-imaging and engineering readers, examines how the representation, measurement, and decoding of a quantum state determine what information can be returned. It distinguishes amplitude encodings of input values from encodings of complete candidate labelings, and studies unknown-phase inference under complex-field interference. It links singular-value decomposition of reshaped joint amplitudes to pure-state entanglement. In a four-pixel segmentation example, one QAOA layer lowers mean energy relative to uniform sampling yet makes a nonoptimal labeling more probable than the optimum.

George Kourousias, Sergio Carrato, Roberto Pugliese et al.

Rapid mixing for Gibbs samplers for quantum spin systems with all-to-all interactions

In briefFast mixing of Gibbs samplers for quantum spin systems with all-to-all coupling

SummaryThis paper studies how quickly Gibbs states of two-local quantum Hamiltonians on arbitrary interaction graphs, including fully connected systems, can be prepared using Lindblad dynamics satisfying KMS detailed balance. The authors adapt the classical Dobrushin condition to the quantum setting and use the order-1 quantum Wasserstein distance to measure how local perturbations spread. In a high-temperature regime set by the maximum total interaction strength per site, they derive contraction estimates that yield mixing times logarithmic in system size and inverse error tolerance.

Quantum communication: a discrimination approach

In briefQuantum communication via a state discrimination method

SummaryThis paper proposes a state discrimination approach to unassisted quantum communication over a noisy quantum channel. Building on the operational duality between entanglement recovery and state discrimination, the authors construct entanglement-generation codes with explicit decoders based on Petz recovery maps. The error bounds hold for any number of channel uses and are expressed in terms of Petz-Rényi coherent information. For product sources, the error decays exponentially at rates below the coherent information.

In briefTransport in spinful non-Hermitian gauge lattices: spin-dependent skin effects and transmission in one-dimensional lattices with gauge fields

SummaryThis paper extends a generalized transfer-matrix method to one-dimensional non-Hermitian tight-binding lattices with spin and matrix-valued Abelian and non-Abelian gauge couplings. Three-dimensional QCA that are not stably circuits

In briefThree-dimensional quantum cellular automata that cannot be stabilized into circuits

SummaryThe paper proves that for every odd prime p, the three-dimensional chiral Z_p Clifford quantum cellular automata of Haah and of Sun, Yang, Wang, Tantivasadakarn and Chen are not finite-depth quantum circuits, even after tensoring with arbitrary ancillary degrees of freedom and composing with translations. Stabilizing fluctuating Cooper pairs with a terahertz resonator

In briefStabilizing fluctuating Cooper pairs using a terahertz resonator

SummaryThis paper develops an equilibrium theory of a mesoscopic, disordered two-dimensional superconductor coupled to a subwavelength terahertz resonator, focusing on how superconducting phase fluctuations interact with the resonator's electromagnetic fluctuations. Modeling the resonator as a lumped-element circuit, the authors find that coupling raises the superconducting transition temperature Tc rather than suppressing it. For a 1 µm × 1 µm, 8 nm-thick disordered film, they estimate a relative Tc enhancement of about 1%, roughly ten times the 0.1% obtained when phase fluctuations are neglected.

Frieder Lindel, Jonathan B. Curtis, J\'er\^ome Faist et al.
