Hardware-Aware Realization of Deterministic Quantum-Walk Search on Complete Bipartite Graphs

In briefHardware-compatible deterministic quantum-walk search on complete bipartite graphs, run on a gate-based processor

SummaryThis paper reports the first experimental run of deterministic discrete-time quantum-walk search on complete bipartite graphs, using a digital gate-based processor with high-fidelity operations and dynamical-phase compensation. The search circuits were compiled to the native gate set and square-lattice topology, reaching 97.6% success for N=4 and 70.8% for N=8, and 66% for exact instances up to N=16. The authors also analyze resource scaling, finding circuit depth and size grow as √N, and estimate that graphs near N∼10^5 would need per-gate infidelity around 10^-7.

Tao Rong, Meng Li, Xin Tong et al.

Polarization waves in open-barrier quantum-dot cellular automata

In briefPolarization waves in open-barrier quantum-dot cellular automata

SummaryQuantum-dot cellular automata are usually run as bistable digital logic. This paper develops a closed-form theory of small-signal polarization waves for an open-barrier regime, where tunnelling within cells is comparable to or stronger than electrostatic coupling between cells, so cells respond linearly instead of latching. Linearizing the coherence-vector equations gives a lattice dispersion with a low-frequency cutoff. Exact solutions confirm the mode as a sharp quasiparticle, and a low-temperature estimate of thermal dephasing sets an operating temperature ceiling.

Konrad Walus

Efficient AOD-Transport Compilation for Neutral-Atom Arrays

In briefEfficient atom-transport compiler for neutral-atom quantum computers

SummaryThis paper presents NEAT, a compiler that optimizes how atoms are moved with acousto-optic deflectors (AODs) in neutral-atom quantum computers. It places qubits using a single cost that combines transport time, atom-transfer loss, and crosstalk, and routes conflicting moves concurrently through intermediate waypoints. On standard benchmarks and circuits of up to 1,000 qubits, NEAT outperforms state-of-the-art compilers in fidelity, circuit duration, and compilation time.

WhyThe compiler merges transport time, transfer loss and crosstalk into one cost and uses waypoints for conflicting moves. The authors report 1.9x shorter circuits and 6.6x faster compilation on benchmar

Pulse-timing errors in exchange-only spin qubits: how one control imperfection propagates to VQE, QAOA and fault-tolerance primitives

In briefTiming errors in exchange-only spin qubit pulses and their effect on quantum algorithms

SummaryThis paper studies how an error in the pulse area of exchange-only spin qubits, where every gate is built from Heisenberg exchange pulses, propagates to algorithm results. The authors compile VQE for H2 and LiH, QAOA MaxCut, Grover search, phase estimation and a distance-3 repetition code onto up to six encoded qubits, then perturb each pulse with a systematic fractional error or jitter. In briefWhether magnetic phases of transition-metal oxides are metallic or insulating, in the Slater-versus-Mott debate

SummaryThis paper revisits the historical Slater-Mott debate over whether transition-metal oxides are insulating or metallic in their magnetically ordered and disordered phases. The authors use a parameter-free density functional theory (DFT) approach, modeling local magnetic motifs as building blocks of the antiferromagnetic and paramagnetic phases. Efficient numerical measurement design and certified risk bounds for multiparameter quantum estimation

In briefNumerical measurement design and certified risk bounds for multiparameter quantum estimation

SummaryThis paper presents Q-StARC, a numerical framework that optimizes measurements for multiparameter quantum estimation and provides certified lower bounds on the weighted variance of locally unbiased estimators. Measurements are searched on a complex Stiefel manifold using projected Adam updates, QR retraction, and Riemannian conjugate-gradient refinement. Independent risk bounds come from a matrix sum-of-squares hierarchy, which, when optimized jointly, starts at the weighted Nagaoka-Hayashi bound and converges to the optimal locally unbiased risk. Numerical results certify that the Nagaoka-Hayashi bound is unattainable for several finite-copy qubit models and two eight-parameter qutrit models.

WhyThe authors combine measurement optimization with a matrix SOS hierarchy converging to optimal locally unbiased risk, verified on qubit and qutrit models.

No complete set of mutually unbiased bases in C⁶ without Fourier vanishing

In briefProof that C^6 has no complete set of mutually unbiased bases without Fourier vanishing

SummaryThis paper concerns how many mutually unbiased bases (MUBs) can exist in six-dimensional complex space C^6. An earlier exact proof relied on the Matolcsi, Ruzsa and Weiner Fourier-vanishing conjecture, which the authors show is unnecessary. Using only orthonormality, unbiasedness, the second-moment identity of a complete family and a trivial bound on moments, they derive a contradiction from positive definite integer matrices. A standard-library program verifies the certificate in exact arithmetic.

Exact environment checkpointing for memory-limited DMRG and TDVP

In briefExact checkpointing of environment tensors to reduce memory in DMRG and TDVP

SummaryThis paper presents a checkpointing method for finite matrix-product-state sweep algorithms such as DMRG and TDVP, which store one environment tensor per site. Every k-th environment (with k about the square root of N) is kept, and the intermediate ones are rebuilt during the sweep. The method reduces environment memory by 4.6 to 10 times on cylinders of 96 to 384 sites, and reproduces energies and states bit for bit when contractions are deterministic. Implementations are provided for ITensorMPS.jl, TeNPy, MPSKit.jl and quimb.

WhyThe authors adapt checkpointing to DMRG and TDVP environment storage, reproducing results bit for bit, and report memory reductions and overheads on 144-site cylinders with implementations provided.

In briefCritical state of chiral Majorana charge Kondo effect

SummaryThis paper proposes a many-body critical point, the chiral Majorana charge Kondo (CMCK) criticality, as a more discriminating way to detect chiral Majorana edge modes than transport signatures that conventional mechanisms can also produce. In the two-terminal geometry, CMCK and conventional charge Kondo share the same critical conductance, but their leading conductance corrections scale as T^{1/3} and T, and their residual entropies differ. In briefHow bright-dark state interplay controls vibronic decoherence in molecular aggregates

SummaryThis study presents a spectroscopy-based framework linking optically bright vibronic eigenstates in excitonic molecular aggregates to their later decoherence dynamics. A Universal Algebraic Construction of Hilbert-Space Fragmentation

In briefA general algebraic method for creating Hilbert-space fragmentation in quantum lattice systems

SummaryThis paper presents a method for producing Hilbert-space fragmentation from arbitrary quantum lattice Hamiltonians on finite-dimensional local spaces. The Hamiltonian is first written algebraically using supersymmetry and parasupersymmetry generators, and enlarging their representations defines site-wise and bond-wise lifting procedures. Site-wise lifting introduces immobile zero modes that create exponentially many invariant sectors, and nearest-neighbor chains show restricted propagation in correlation functions. The method also yields several fragmented models, including lifted permutation and Ising spin chains, a lifted toric code, and XXC spin-chain families.
