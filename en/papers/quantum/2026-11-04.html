Quantum Error Correction in Nearly Critical Toric Codes

In briefQuantum error correction in near-critical toric codes

SummaryThis paper studies error correction for topological codes pushed toward a quantum phase transition, focusing on quantum information stored in the transverse-field toric code ground states under bit-flip and phase-flip decoherence. The authors show that stabilizer measurements act almost unitarily on undecohered ground states, and that the optimal post-measurement decoder can be implemented by Monte Carlo simulation of a constrained lattice gauge theory. Numerical results show a finite threshold close to the critical point, and for phase-flip noise the threshold is proven to be independent of the transverse field throughout the topological phase.

Zack Weinstein, Samuel J. Garratt

Quantum Pointwise Dimension (QUPID): An algorithm for identifying classical chaos in quantum dynamics

In briefQuantum Pointwise Dimension (QUPID): a method for spotting classical chaos in quantum systems

SummaryThe pointwise dimension measures the effective dimensionality of classical trajectories in phase space, with regular motion confined to a torus and chaotic motion filling an energy surface. This paper implements the measure in the quantum regime using Husimi representations of wavepackets, averaging them over long times to get a stationary phase-space density. The authors report excellent agreement with classical results for both autonomous and non-autonomous systems. They also explore obtaining the pointwise dimension from a time correlation function.

Jonathan Berkheim, David J. Tannor

Breaking the quantum barriers for Graph Collision

In briefBreaking quantum query barriers for graph collision

SummaryThe paper studies the graph collision problem, which asks whether a known graph on n vertices has an edge with both endpoints marked, accessed through queries to the marking input bits. The authors give a bounded-error quantum algorithm using Õ(n^{92/139}) queries, refuting a conjectured Ω(n^{2/3}) lower bound, and refine it to Õ(n^{11/17}) queries. They also prove an Ω(√(n log n)) quantum query lower bound, which exceeds the O(√n) certificate complexity barrier of the positive adversary method.

Reading Why: Born-Measured Attention with Exact Word Evidence on Light and Qubits

In brief"Reading Why": An interpretable language model with Born-measured attention and exact word evidence, compiled to optics and qubits

SummaryThe paper builds a transformer in which every step is a Born measurement, with attention computed as |⟨q|k⟩|^4 normalised per row and a measured product replacing the feed-forward nonlinearity. On e-SNLI natural language inference it matches the test accuracy of a softmax transformer of the same size, and deleting the word its evidence ranks first changes the answer more often than for the softmax model. Simple proof of conditions requiring maximal entanglement for local state verification and discrimination

In briefA simple proof of when maximal entanglement is needed for local state verification and discrimination

SummaryThis paper studies when entangled resources suffice for verifying and discriminating quantum states using local (separable) measurements. Using only linear algebra, the authors give self-contained proofs of two known results: the Schmidt rank of the resource must be at least that of the state being verified, and if the ranks are equal, the resource must be maximally entangled. They then show that, among pure entangled resources of local dimension at most d, a maximally entangled state of C^d⊗C^d is necessary and sufficient for perfect local discrimination of a generic orthogonal basis, however weakly entangled the resource is.

Somshubhro Bandyopadhyay

Universal dynamics of quantum phase transitions with fractional long-range interactions

In briefUniversal dynamics of quantum phase transitions with fractional long-range interactions

SummaryThis paper studies the full statistics of defects produced in a quantum phase transition within a nonintegrable long-range spin chain, the fractional Ising model. The authors use time-dependent variational-principle simulations based on matrix-product states and operators. The defect number cumulants follow power laws in driving time, with exponents that vary continuously with the fractional order. At low fractional order, defect decay is slower than the Kibble-Zurek prediction, while at intermediate order the mechanism holds.

Effective Hamiltonians and non-Hermitian physics

In briefEffective Hamiltonians and non-Hermitian physics: compressing discarded-sector information into one boundary parameter

SummaryEvery realistic quantum system couples to an environment, and including it restores a closed Hermitian description, but the Hilbert space becomes intractably large. The paper uses the Feshbach–Löwdin projection to eliminate unwanted degrees of freedom exactly, yielding an effective Hamiltonian on the relevant subspace alone. This effective Hamiltonian generically becomes non-Hermitian when the eliminated subspace contains an accessible continuum and outgoing boundary conditions are imposed. Using a particle in a box with a delta barrier at its center, the authors reduce the discarded half-well to a single boundary parameter β, rederive the result via the Green's function and Dyson equation, and relate β to a self-energy term.

Ugo Lair, David Gu\'ery-Odelin

DynaMite: Dynamic Measurement Scheduling To Accelerate Fault-Tolerant Quantum Programs

In briefDynaMite: A method that speeds up fault-tolerant quantum programs by adjusting measurement timing

SummaryFault-tolerant quantum computers spend most of their logical cycle time on qubit measurements, which take 43-86% of it. DynaMite is a lightweight real-time policy that selects the fastest measurement latency for each upcoming error-correction round, based on the remaining error budget and rounds left. Evaluations show it reduces logical cycle time by 1.69x on average (up to 2.29x) and lowers logical error rates to 0.46x of baseline on average (down to 0.13x).

Dongwhee Kim, Suhas Vittal, Poulami Das

Quantum Query Lower Bounds from Classical Moment Matching

In briefQuantum query lower bounds via classical moment matching

SummaryThe paper develops a quantum version of the classical moment matching method, which is used to prove lower bounds in property testing. It presents two incomparable quantum moment matching theorems, with lower bounds of Ω(N^{1/2-1/(2r)}) and Ω(N^{1/2-1/(2(2^r-1))}), the latter holding for smaller parameter ranges. The proofs combine a new continuous hybrid argument with the polynomial method. The theorems are applied to obtain optimal lower bounds for k-distinctness, k-star-freeness, planarity and outerplanarity testing.

Data informed Ansatz Selection for Variational Quantum Circuits

In briefData-Driven Ansatz Selection for Variational Quantum Circuits

SummaryThis paper gives three rules for choosing a variational quantum circuit's structure and depth from the data and gate set alone, before any training. QECert: Formal Foundations for Quantum Error-Correction Theory in Lean

In briefQECert: Lean Formal Foundations for Quantum Error-Correction Theory

SummaryQECert is a Lean library that provides reusable formal foundations for quantum error-correction theory. It connects error-correction criteria, stabilizer and CSS codes, and algebraic and homological constructions in one framework. Asymptotically good quantum LDPC codes serve as the main case study, with formalized constructions and asymptotic parameter theorems for hypergraph, balanced, and lifted products and quantum Tanner codes. Reopening the one clean qubit case of a semi-grand challenge

In briefRevisiting the one clean qubit case of a semi-grand challenge

SummaryThis paper addresses Scott Aaronson's question of whether a quantum computer that always remains in a separable mixed state can be simulated classically in polynomial time. Earlier work claimed an affirmative answer for the one clean qubit model. The authors present a counterexample in which a pure clean qubit controls three gates acting on a maximally mixed three-qubit register, keeping all four qubits fully separable while producing a final unitary that does not match the form required by the earlier simulation result. They also give a continuous-time implementation and identify the gap in the earlier proof.

Christopher W. Mansell

Recoverability of Gravitational Entanglement under Decoherence

In briefWhether gravity-generated entanglement can be recovered when decoherence erases it

SummaryThe paper asks whether gravitationally generated entanglement that decoherence has made very small can still be recovered through strictly local quantum error correction. It analyzes Newtonian gravity with localization noise, including environmental noise and collapse-model noise from GRW, CSL, and Diósi–Penrose. For GRW and CSL noise, the authors show that local error correction can make the ratio of effective gravitational interaction strength to residual decoherence rate arbitrarily large. The Diósi–Penrose model does not show this enhancement under the bipartite protocol considered.

On Revolutionizing Power Systems: Suitability of Quantum Computing-Ready Pipelines for Stochastic Unit Commitment Optimization

In briefAssessing Quantum Computing-Ready Pipelines for Stochastic Unit Commitment in Power Systems

SummaryThis paper proposes a quantum computing-ready optimization pipeline for deterministic and stochastic unit commitment, combining classical, quantum-inspired, quantum annealing, and hybrid quantum-classical solvers on test systems including the IEEE 118-bus system. It introduces a QUBO reformulation and uses a divide-and-conquer strategy with Quantum Computing Monte Carlo simulation to handle 27,730 uncertainty scenarios. Results show the hybrid quantum method matches the classical baseline and cuts stochastic UC runtime from 40.76 to 14.79 hours, while pure quantum annealing is limited to small systems.
