Sequential Capacity of Quantum Processes with Finite Memory

In briefSequential Capacity of Quantum Processes with Finite Memory

SummaryThe paper studies how complex a quantum device's responses can become over longer runs when its internal memory is fixed. It defines sequential response capacity as the number of adaptive testing stages that can keep separating possible processes by a set probability gap. For fixed system and memory sizes, the capacity grows on the order of K log K in the number of time steps per run, with a tight matching bound. The authors achieve this with time-dependent phase rotations on a single qubit, whereas classical processes measuring in a fixed basis have only linear capacity.

Yibin Wang

Fermionic Gaussianity can be tested with mode-independent sample-complexity

In briefTesting whether a quantum state is fermionic Gaussian, with sample complexity independent of mode count

SummaryThe paper studies how to decide whether an unknown pure n-mode quantum state is fermionic Gaussian or at least ε-far in trace distance from all Gaussian states. The authors introduce a new technique for upper bounding the acceptance probability of states far from a family, which bounds the sample complexity of such decision problems. They show that Θ(ε⁻²) copies suffice, which is optimal, and that the well-known Bell sampling procedure is optimal even against arbitrary collective and adaptive measurements. The same mode-independent sample complexity is also shown for deciding membership in the family of Slater determinant states.

(A Variant of) Clifford Circuit Synthesis is NP-Complete

In briefA Variant of Clifford Circuit Synthesis Is NP-Complete

SummaryThe paper studies optimal Clifford circuit synthesis, which seeks the shortest-depth quantum circuit for a given operation using a fixed set of elementary gates. The authors show that a variant of this problem is NP-hard, by reducing 3-edge colorability of 3-regular graphs to a synthesis problem using only CZ gates. They further argue that adding Hadamard, phase, and CNOT gates does not reduce the optimal depth. Simulation and equivalence checking are in P, while deciding whether a Clifford unitary can be implemented within a given depth is NP-complete.

Geometry of Knill-Laflamme Coefficients for Pauli Error Detection

In briefGeometry of coefficients used to detect quantum errors exactly

SummaryThis paper studies the geometry of Knill-Laflamme coefficients, which characterize exact quantum error detection, and how operator representation, code dimension, and detection constraints shape the set of attainable coefficient vectors. The main theorem states that when each occupied block has multiplicity at least the code dimension times the rank of the active state, the coefficient range is the full active expectation body, which is convex and connected with a closed interval as its signature spectrum. The framework covers commuting Pauli families and subsystem stabilizer codes, and examples show how ranges can shrink, collapse, or become disconnected.

In briefMetastability of the Mean-Field Interchange Model for Local Dimension ≥3

SummaryThis paper studies how quantum systems thermalize at low temperatures. The authors show that the Davies dynamics of a mean-field interchange model with local dimension d≥3 and single-site generalized Pauli couplings has two distinguished inverse temperatures, with a static first-order phase transition between them. Between these temperatures, the spectral gap of the Davies generator vanishes exponentially in system size n, indicating metastability. Outside this interval, the gap is bounded below by an inverse polynomial in n, allowing polynomial-time thermalization.

In briefUniversal coding trades away reliability in classical-quantum channels

SummaryThis paper studies channel coding for classical-quantum channels, where universal codes work without full channel knowledge. For classical channels, universal codes can achieve both capacity and optimal high-rate reliability, but the authors show this fails in the quantum case because the unknown unitary rotation of the output prevents it. In briefProving optimal query complexity for fractional quantum evolution, confirming QSVT is asymptotically optimal

SummaryThis paper studies how many oracle queries are needed to implement a fractional power U^t of an unknown unitary U=e^{iH} with 0<t<1.
