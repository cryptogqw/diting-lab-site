On-demand frequency-entangled microwave biphotons from a transmon-resonator dimer

In briefOn-demand frequency-entangled microwave biphoton source using a transmon-resonator dimer

SummaryThis paper proposes a superconducting circuit QED source that emits frequency-entangled microwave photon pairs on demand, with each photon sent into a separate waveguide for individual control. The design uses two transmon-resonator subsystems coupled by a cross-Kerr interaction, realized with Josephson-junction couplers. By varying coupling strengths, the source produces biphotons with different joint spectral intensities and Schmidt spectra. Optimized parameters yield a state with fidelity about 0.956 to a two-dimensional maximally entangled state, and about 0.949 when restricted to separated frequency bins.

E. V. Stolyarov, O. V. Kliushnichenko

In briefInteraction effects that boost photocurrent briefly but reduce it over time in quantum photocells

SummaryThe paper studies how interactions between qubit-qutrit donors and an acceptor in a quantum photocell control the photocurrent. Stronger Heisenberg exchange coupling and anisotropy raise the transient photocurrent but lower the steady-state value. The Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya interaction favors steady-state output while reducing transient enhancement. The qutrit's local purity follows the photocurrent trends in both regimes.

In briefCurvature of the Ground-State Manifold and Phase Transitions in the Kitaev Honeycomb Model

SummaryThis paper studies phase transitions in the Kitaev honeycomb model, an exactly solvable spin model whose ground state is a quantum spin liquid. Unveiling the Action of Randomized Compiling on Quantum States: A Geometric Perspective

In briefHow Randomized Compiling Reshapes Quantum States: A Geometric View

SummaryThis paper uses the generalized Bloch vector representation to study how randomized compiling affects the purity and orientation of quantum states across multi-layer circuits. For independent, isotropically distributed weak coherent errors, the averaged RC channel is close to depolarizing, shrinking the state nearly isotropically while greatly reducing orientational deviation at a small cost in purity. When dephasing is present, it dominates the remaining deviation. The findings are validated on a superconducting quantum processor using GHZ state preparation and phase estimation in a topological braiding simulation.

Kai Tang, Chao Zhan, Yao Song et al.

In briefA single-photon quantum sensor using single-atom cavity QED

Scaling of phase rigidity without eigenstate coalescence

In briefHow phase rigidity scales with system size without eigenstate merging

SummaryThe paper studies how non-Hermitian skin effects determine the way phase rigidity changes with system size, without any eigenstate coalescence. Exact analytical results are derived for three non-Hermitian chain models and checked numerically. Phase rigidity decays exponentially under the ordinary skin effect, approaches a nonzero constant as N^-2 under the scale-free skin effect, and can decay as N^-1/2 to zero under the algebraic skin effect. These results show that eigenstate nonorthogonality can be controlled without exceptional points.

WhyThe authors show skin effects give distinct phase-rigidity scaling without eigenstate coalescence, reporting exact results for three chain models with numerical checks.

In briefImpossibility Theorems for Transversal and Automorphism Clifford Gates in Prime-Dimensional Qudit Codes

SummaryThis paper studies logical Clifford gates, a basic building block of fault-tolerant quantum computation, in stabilizer codes on qudits of prime dimension p that encode k of at least 2 logical qudits. The authors prove that Clifford gadgets which are transversal with respect to a fixed partition into blocks of fewer than k qudits cannot realize the full logical Clifford group, and that this block size is tight. In briefGeneralized second law derived from fluctuation theorems in a quantum reference frame

SummaryThis paper proves the generalized second law (GSL) for black-hole exteriors undergoing unitary driving and relaxation on a fixed semifinite algebra. Instead of Wall's approach, which assumes semiclassical geometry and restricts to algebras of successive horizon cuts, it evolves the state on a fixed dressed exterior algebra and uses a gravitational charge as a quantum clock.
