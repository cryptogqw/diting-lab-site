Validation of the five-electron pO qubit in Si using full configuration interaction

In briefChecking the five-electron pO silicon qubit with full configuration interaction

SummaryThis paper examines a recently proposed charge qubit, pO, made of five electrons in a gate-defined silicon quantum dot. The authors use full configuration interaction calculations to test how Coulomb interactions affect whether this qubit is viable. They find that the interacting states occupy orbital shells beyond the simple shell-filling picture. For valley splittings above about 50 μeV, the qubit still has zero dipole moment and a nonzero quadrupole tensor.

Zero mode, Zero influence: Spatial-derivative coupling in particle detectors

In briefZero-mode-free detector coupling via spatial derivatives

SummaryParticle detector models in cavity QED often omit zero modes or assume their state, which can compromise causality or make predictions ambiguous. This paper shows that spatial-derivative coupling removes both problems. For free massless scalar fields in static periodic or Neumann cavities, cavity-frame spatial derivatives annihilate the zero-mode operator while preserving microcausality. Detector dynamics and entanglement harvesting then depend only on the reduced detector-oscillator state, nonperturbatively.

Eduardo Mart\'{i}n-Mart\'{i}nez, Ireneo James Membrere, Adam Teixid\'{o}-Bonfill

Phase-space dynamics and multicomponent wave-packet structures in the Dunkl anharmonic oscillator

In briefPhase-space dynamics and multicomponent wave packets in the Dunkl anharmonic oscillator

SummaryThis paper studies phase-space dynamics and multicomponent wave-packet structures in the Dunkl anharmonic oscillator, which models a Kerr medium. From the time-evolved state, the authors derive analytical expressions for the Dunkl-Husimi Q-function and a normalized angular Husimi profile. The Dunkl parameter changes coherent-state excitation weights and parity-dependent phase accumulation, producing angular anisotropy and asymmetric phase-space separation. Triple descent in quantum linear regression

In briefTriple descent in quantum linear regression

SummaryThis paper studies prediction error in quantum linear regression models, such as quantum extreme learning machines and quantum fidelity kernels, when features are estimated from finitely many measurement shots. As the number of measurement outcomes grows, the error shows a triple-descent profile with two peaks of different origins. A classical peak arises when the measurement first becomes informationally complete and can be suppressed by more shots at inference. A quantum peak appears when outcomes match training states and cannot be removed by more shots. Using random-matrix theory, the authors derive both peak shapes analytically, including ridge regularization.
