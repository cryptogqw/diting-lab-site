Secret-shared teleportation of a two-qudit gate with a deferred recipient

In briefSecret-shared teleportation of a two-qudit gate to a later-chosen recipient

SummaryThe paper shows how the ability to apply a two-qudit entangling gate can be secret-shared among several candidate nodes in advance. A provider encrypts half of a Bell pair into one share per node, and after a later key release only the selected node can complete an exact non-local CSUM^k gate with the user's data. The authors state that any coalition smaller than the number of candidates, or the key register alone, holds no information.

Time-optimal quantum dynamics under fixed-norm generators

In briefFastest quantum evolution under fixed-norm generators

SummaryThis paper studies quantum systems whose evolution is driven by linear generators with a fixed unitarily invariant norm. For this class, it derives a universal time-optimal generator that converts one given state into another, yielding a time-optimal non-Hermitian Hamiltonian for pure-state conversion. The general result is then used to obtain quantum speed limits for mixed-state evolutions governed by Lindbladians and non-Hermitian Hamiltonians.

WhyThe authors derive a universal time-optimal generator and quantum speed limits under fixed unitarily invariant norms, using GL(n,C) geodesic geometry for arbitrary dimension and mixed states.

Weighted hypergraph states as a resource for quantum metrology

In briefWeighted hypergraph quantum states as resources for quantum sensing

SummaryThis paper studies weighted hypergraph states, a family of multi-qubit quantum states, and identifies subclasses defined by their graph topology that perform better in quantum metrology. Using quantum Fisher information and a new Dicke-basis representation, the authors show these subclasses outperform separable states and standard weighted graph states over broad ranges of edge weights. Analytical formulas for any number of qubits are provided, with a detailed analysis for four qubits. The authors also find that adding higher-order hyperedges can keep a quantum advantage under higher local dephasing noise.

B. J. Alexander, M. S. Tame

In briefWhich gaps survive in the unitary almost Mathieu operator, and which close

SummaryThe paper determines exactly which gap labels correspond to open spectral gaps for the unitary almost Mathieu operator at every irrational frequency and all coupling constants in (0,1]. Functional Tensor Trains for High-Order Greeks and Exposure Tails of Multi-Asset Structured Products

In briefFunctional tensor train methods for high-order Greeks and exposure tails of multi-asset structured products

SummaryThis paper proposes solving the backward pricing recursion for worst-of autocallable notes using a piecewise-polynomial functional tensor train (hp-FTT), so that one solve yields all Greeks at any spot and the potential future exposure quantile without Monte Carlo sampling noise. Comparing against strong Monte Carlo estimators on worst-of autocallables observed monthly on three to five assets, the authors find Monte Carlo cheaper for first-order Greeks at 1% accuracy, while hp-FTT is cheaper for third-order Greeks at that accuracy and for all checked Greeks at 0.1%. Topologically Protected Edge States in a Single Flux Qubit

In briefTopologically protected edge states in a single superconducting flux qubit

SummaryThis paper shows that topological edge states can be realized in a simple superconducting circuit built from one inductively shunted flux qubit. The circuit's low-energy dynamics map analytically onto an extended Su-Schrieffer-Heeger (SSH) chain, and numerical simulations of the full circuit confirm this mapping. Near a chiral point in parameter space, the edge states are numerically found to be topologically protected against chiral-symmetry-preserving perturbations such as charge noise. The authors conclude that protection effects previously associated with multi-qubit systems can also occur in a single qubit.

Pablo Perez-Fernandez, Manuel Pino

In briefComparing quantum and classical kernel methods for classification under controlled protocols

SummaryThis paper compares quantum kernel methods with classical kernels for classification under a protocol-controlled setup. Logical Bell Inequalities for Marginal Wigner Negativity

In briefLogical Bell inequalities for marginal Wigner negativity

SummaryThis paper constructs logical Bell inequalities whose violations are determined by the Wigner function of a reduced quantum state. For odd-prime qudits, each phase-space point of a nonempty proper subsystem defines an inequality that is violated when its Gross Wigner value is negative. The normalized violation gives a lower bound on the contextual fraction in the complete Weyl measurement scenario, valid for any joint state with that marginal. A family of product states that saturates this bound is also identified.

No generalised contextuality from the vacuum

In briefNo generalised contextuality arises from the vacuum

SummaryThis paper shows that, under certain conditions, generalised contextuality in the sense introduced by Spekkens cannot be created from the vacuum of a quantum field. Initially noncontextual qudit states do not become contextual merely by interacting with a quantum field. The authors verify this in two scenarios: a perturbative approach expanding the time evolution operator in powers of the coupling constant, and the limit of zero energy gap in the spectrum of the free detector Hamiltonian.

GENIE: learned quantum circuits as embedding impurity solvers, and why compactness, not correlation strength, decides when they work

In briefGENIE: Learned quantum circuits as embedding solvers and when they work

SummaryThe paper introduces GENIE, an impurity solver for density matrix embedding theory in which a generative transformer learns the sampling circuit, trained on the quantum-selected configuration interaction energy. On N2, hydrogen chains, water and a hydrogen ring, it compares GENIE at equal determinant budget with classical selected CI methods and with sample-based quantum diagonalization. GENIE does not beat the best classical selection, and its failures are governed by wavefunction compactness relative to the budget rather than correlation strength. A cheap coupled-cluster-based estimate of compactness works as a router that predicts when GENIE should be used.

Locality, Probability and the Relevance of History

In briefLocality, Probability and the Relevance of History: A theory paper on how quantum nonlocality relates to relativity

SummaryThe paper proposes a generalization of relativity's causal constraint to probabilistic theories: the probability of an event should be functionally determined by the probabilities of event occurrences in its immediate causal past. The author argues this differs from Bell's criterion of local causality and implies it only under an extra assumption that history is irrelevant. Consequently, Bell-inequality-violating quantum phenomena need not conflict with relativity, locality, statistical independence, or realism; they instead show that history is relevant.

Sebastian Horvat

Lifting the mixed to the pure: quantum phases of matter from canonical purification

In briefFrom Mixed to Pure: Understanding Quantum Phases via Canonical Purification

SummaryThis paper studies quantum phases of matter. Pure-state phases are defined through finite-depth local unitary connections, a notion recently extended to mixed states linked by locally reversible channels. The authors show that such a channel between mixed states can be lifted to a local isometry between their canonical purifications, so mixed states in the same phase have canonical purifications in the same pure-state phase. They give an explicit construction of the lifting isometry and illustrate the results with toric code under bit-flip noise and Chern insulator under fermion-number noise.

WhyThe authors show locally reversible channels lift to local isometries between canonical purifications, with explicit Petz-based construction checked on toric code and Chern insulator examples.

Xiaoyang Huang, Shengqi Sang, Chong Wang et al.

Channel dichotomies

In briefChannel dichotomies: interconverting channels under non-signalling superchannels

SummaryThis paper studies how quantum channels can be converted into one another using non-signalling superchannels, with the asymptotic conversion rate given by the ratio of channel capacities.
