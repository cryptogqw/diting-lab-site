In briefDoes conservation hold for every individual quantum measurement outcome?

SummaryThe paper examines conservation laws in quantum mechanics, which are usually understood only statistically and only under unitary evolution, while individual measurement outcomes break even that weak form. Quantum Charge as a Particle/Antiparticle Label

In briefQuantum charge as a particle/antiparticle label via the Weyl-Heisenberg group

SummaryThis paper argues that particles and antiparticles are linked to the Weyl-Heisenberg group, using a central element that defines quantum charges of +1/ħ and −1/ħ for their wave functions. The authors treat these charges as intrinsic properties of the wave functions, similar to spin and electric charge. They attribute negative probabilities and energies, and the Klein paradox in Klein-Gordon and Dirac theories, to a violation of U(1)_v invariance. Restoring this invariance is said to eliminate these paradoxes at the first-quantized relativistic level.
