Stablecoin Runs as Safe-Asset Stress Signals: Episode-Level Evidence for Central Bank Surveillance

In briefStablecoin Runs as Stress Signals for Safe Assets: Episode-Level Evidence for Central Bank Monitoring

SummaryThis paper examines whether stablecoin runs contain information about U.S. Treasury yields, using daily data on USDT, USDC and BUSD from 2021 to 2025 across five stress episodes. Flows are measured by changes in token units rather than market capitalisation. During three panic-driven runs, a one-billion-dollar five-day redemption was associated with an 8.5 basis point fall in the three-month bill yield within ten trading days, while two regulatory episodes showed no such signal.

Ahmad Qadi, Hui Gong, Francesca Medda

Signed but Unbound: The Authority Gap Between Agent Commerce Mandates and Crypto Wallet Signatures

In briefSigned but Unbound: The Gap Between What AI Payment Mandates Specify and What Crypto Wallets Enforce

SummaryThe paper examines the gap between the payment terms an AI agent's commerce protocol specifies, such as merchant, order, amount, and facilitator, and the fields a token contract actually enforces through the wallet signature. The authors define nine constraint classes and derive them for 12 authorization configurations, including Coinbase's deployed Commerce Payments Protocol, with all 31,800 trials on local copies of deployed contract code matching the derivation. They introduce MandateLock, a minimal implementation that rejects all 10,800 modified mandates on deployed token code while accepting signatures from two independent signers.

Correctable Enrollment and One-Transaction Seed Proofs for Ethereum's Post-Quantum Migration

In briefCorrectable Enrollment and One-Transaction Seed Proofs for Ethereum's Post-Quantum Transition

SummaryThis paper proposes a migration design for Ethereum to post-quantum cryptography. Owners can cheaply enroll post-quantum keys using their existing signatures before ECDSA is scheduled for retirement, and can correct the enrollment with a zero-knowledge proof of their BIP-39 seed until a common deadline. Unenrolled accounts stay frozen and can be recovered by seed proof without a deadline. The authors prove account guarantees, model-check the rules, implement the design in go-ethereum and a Lean consensus client, and report a proof of about 190 KB that verifies in 38 ms.

WhyThe authors combine correctable enrollment with deadline-free seed proofs for Ethereum's post-quantum migration. They report a go-ethereum implementation and a 190 KB, 38 ms verifying STARK.

Xin Jin, Maximillian Liao, Handong Chen et al.

Release-Bound Withdrawal Authorization for Bitcoin Custody

In briefRelease-Bound Withdrawal Authorization (RBWA) for Bitcoin Custody

SummaryThe paper addresses the risk that a compromised cryptocurrency withdrawal backend could request signatures on unauthorized payments. It proposes Release-Bound Withdrawal Authorization (RBWA), which orders permits against pause in a replicated journal and restores exposure when closing evidence disappears from Bitcoin's active chain. In matched synthetic schedules, active probes reduced permits through compromised leases from 20 to 9 out of 33, with one false fence.

Jian Cui (University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign), Rui Zhu (Yale University), Haixu Tang (Indiana University Bloomington)

DispersedBFT: Bottleneck-Free Primary-Backup Consensus using Dispersed Replication

In briefDispersedBFT: A Byzantine-Fault-Tolerant Consensus Protocol That Removes the Primary Bottleneck

SummaryThis paper proposes DispersedBFT, a high-performance consensus protocol similar to PBFT. It uses information dispersal to spread the work of replicating requests from the primary to the backup nodes, removing the primary bottleneck. Several variants are presented, using message authentication codes and Merkle proofs for low CPU cost, or digital signatures for low communication cost. The authors report a throughput of 3.4 million transactions per second with 43 replicas, up to 6.2 times faster than PBFT and 2.6 times faster than HotStuff.

Nancy Kansal, Celine Sana, Jelle Hellings

ChainIT Transaction Truth: Stablecoins, Web3 Wallets, and Multi-Rail Commerce. A Zero-Trust Control Plane for Verified Identity, Organizational Authority, Runtime Compliance, Secure Wallet Execution, and Immutable Settlement Evidence

In briefChainIT Transaction Truth: A Zero-Trust Control Architecture for Stablecoin and Web3 Wallet Payments

SummaryThis paper proposes the ChainIT Transaction Truth Architecture, a control system for commercial transactions using stablecoins and Web3 wallets. Before wallet execution, it adds verified identity, organizational authority, transaction-level compliance checks, exact-digest approval, atomic capacity reservation, and a single-use execution credential. After the transaction, it records settlement, reconciliation, and lifecycle evidence. The authors argue that controlling a key is not identity, and that network confirmation is not complete commercial settlement.

Eric Tacl, Matthew Koepp, Tim Hines et al.
