Proving the Wrong Program: Miscompilations as a Threat to zkVM Guarantees

In briefProving the Wrong Program: Compiler Bugs as a Threat to zkVM Guarantees

SummaryA zero-knowledge virtual machine (zkVM) lets an untrusted prover convince a verifier that a program, usually Rust compiled to RISC-V, ran correctly. This paper presents the first empirical study of miscompilation as a threat to the SP1 and Jolt zkVMs, assembling 88 known rustc and LLVM bugs with Rust reproducers. Of these, 48 cause SP1 guests and 46 cause Jolt guests to commit wrong outputs. Existing guest programs are rarely affected by accident, but an attacker can deliberately trigger these bugs by contributing crafted code.

WhyThe authors report the first empirical study of miscompilations threatening zkVM guarantees, reproducing 88 known bugs on SP1 and Jolt and showing LLM-crafted triggering patches.

In briefRegionFence: adaptive concurrency control that speeds up parallel transaction processing on blockchains.

SummarySmart contract execution limits blockchain throughput, and most parallel engines use one concurrency policy per block, forcing whole transactions to re-execute or wait.
