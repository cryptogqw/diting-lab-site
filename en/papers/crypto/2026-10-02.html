Mixture-of-Experts for Cryptocurrency Order Execution: Training Stability, Tail Risk, and Failure Modes

In briefMixture-of-experts models for crypto order execution: stability, tail risk, and failure modes

SummaryThis paper compares vanilla Double Deep Q-Learning, mixture-of-experts variants, and parameter-matched dense networks for cryptocurrency order execution, using 5-minute BTC/USDT limit order book data from Binance. No learned configuration significantly beats vanilla DDQL on mean implementation shortfall, and all underperform TWAP and immediate liquidation. The authors find that annealed exploration alone eliminates policy collapse, suggesting expert partitioning masks a training-specification problem rather than providing an intrinsic benefit.

Alexander Ardaiz, Varun Budati, and Ali Habibnia

Asymptotic Analysis of Trading Fees in CFMM

In briefAsymptotic Analysis of Trading Fees in CFMM: Studies how much trading fees liquidity providers earn from arbitrage when fee rates approach zero.

SummaryThis paper examines trading fees in automated market makers (AMMs), the dominant mechanism in decentralized finance. It derives a closed-form formula for the fees liquidity providers can receive from arbitrage trading as the fee rate approaches zero. The authors find that, when prices are continuous, arbitrage-generated fees can fully offset LVR loss. The analysis extends to price processes with jumps, concluding that jumps, along with market risk, are the only causes of LP losses.

Peiyang Jin, Clouds, Jing Qian

From TS-SUF-2 to TS-SUF-4: Practical Security Enhancements for FROST2 Threshold Signatures

In briefUpgrading FROST2 Threshold Signatures from TS-SUF-2 to TS-SUF-4 Security

SummaryThis paper studies FROST2, a practical threshold Schnorr signature scheme used to secure digital assets. Prior analysis showed FROST2 reaches only TS-SUF-2 security under static corruption and centralized key generation, due to TS-UF-3 attacks. The authors propose FROST2+, which adds pre-processing token verification and achieves TS-SUF-4 security under the same assumptions, also when initialized with PedPoP. Benchmarks with ZCash's FROST library report performance comparable to FROST2 and about 64-79% faster than FROST with precomputation.

Will Wang, Syh-Yuan Tan, Ryan Chow et al.

Rateless Nested Lattice Codes for Secure Cooperative V2X Broadcast over Fading and Erasure Channels

In briefRateless lattice codes for secure V2X broadcast under fading and erasures

SummaryThe paper proposes a vehicular communication framework combining rateless Raptor lattice codes, nested-lattice physical-layer security with spread spectrum, and blockchain-based trust management for cooperative relaying. Under block-Rayleigh fading with random erasures, rateless Raptor reception achieves 0.3% outage with about 2.3 packets on average, outperforming LDLC and LDPC under identical channels. An eavesdropper with 8 dB combined SNR fails in 62% of sessions.

SoK: Stablecoins in the Quantum Era

In briefStablecoins in the Quantum Era: A Systematization of Knowledge

SummaryThis paper is a systematization of knowledge on post-quantum stablecoins. It builds a stablecoin-specific threat model, maps cryptographic dependencies to monetary control points, and classifies migration choices by who can authorize change, where it must occur, and whether it is hybrid, post-quantum native, or a classical-component encapsulation. The authors highlight an authority-liability gap, where the party able to migrate often differs from those who bear losses. The paper also reviews interoperability proposals and identifies open problems.

DuraShare: BIP39-native secret sharing with full manual fallback

In briefDuraShare: A BIP39-compatible secret sharing scheme with full manual recovery

SummaryDuraShare splits a BIP39 word sequence used by Bitcoin wallets among n Sharekeepers, so any k of them can reconstruct it, while k-1 colluding holders learn nothing. It combines Shamir secret sharing over F_2053, row and column checksums that detect transcription and physical errors, and an affine inner-product MAC that detects tampering. The design ensures recovery is possible using only paper, a calculator, and instructions.

Jeroen van de Graaf, Renato Schiavinato Lopez

RANDAO Manipulation in the Presence of MEV

In briefRANDAO Manipulation When MEV Is Present: how Ethereum's randomness can be gamed for profit

SummaryEthereum's RANDAO randomness beacon can be manipulated, and earlier work calculated the share of blocks a strategic proposer can propose. This paper introduces a generalized reward model covering forms such as consensus reward rollover, multi-block MEV, and CEX-DEX arbitrage, and gives a method for computing an ε-optimal strategy for any such reward scheme. The authors find that partial reward rollover or super-linear scaling with consecutive blocks amplifies the incentive to manipulate. They also study tail-slot slashing, modeled as a reward function, and show that honest equilibria can be recovered.

Kaya Alpturer, Nicholas Hope, S. Matthew Weinberg

A Hybrid Approach to Malware Detection: Integrating Few-Shot Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning with Autoencoders

In briefA hybrid malware detector pairing autoencoders with few-shot meta-learning

SummaryThis paper proposes a hybrid deep learning framework for detecting malware, especially ransomware, when few labeled samples are available. An autoencoder compresses inputs into compact latent features, and a Model-Agnostic Meta-Learning (MAML) classifier adapts quickly to new threats from limited data. Experiments on the Ransomware Dataset 2024 across one- to fifty-shot settings report consistently high accuracy, F1 score, and Matthews Correlation Coefficient, including under extreme data scarcity.

From Network Intrusion Detection to Blockchain-Backed Endpoint Detection and Response: Mapping the Landscape of Decentralized Detection-and-Response Architectures

In briefFrom Network Intrusion Detection to Blockchain-Backed Endpoint Detection: A Survey of Decentralized Response Architectures

SummaryThis Systematization of Knowledge paper reviews research from 2019 to 2026 on blockchain-assisted intrusion detection and prevention for IoT and industrial IoT networks. It proposes a three-axis taxonomy covering detection-system class, blockchain's functional role, and response-automation maturity. The authors find that a true per-endpoint, blockchain-anchored response loop is nearly nonexistent. They also discuss cross-cutting challenges such as consensus latency, post-quantum vulnerability, and smart-contract attack surfaces, and propose a research agenda centered on hybrid on-chain and off-chain orchestration.
