AuditFL: A Contract-Backed Clearing Layer for Open Federated Learning

In briefAuditFL: A Contract-Backed Clearing Layer for Auditable Open Federated Learning

SummaryThis paper presents AuditFL, a clearing layer for federated learning in open settings that uses smart contracts to make the coordinator's decisions auditable. Training and aggregation run off chain, with model artifacts stored in an off-chain store and only content identifiers and economic state recorded on chain. On full MNIST with non-IID splits, 12 rounds and up to 200 clients, AuditFL preserves the learning trajectory of plain federated learning. Stress tests with dropouts, invalid updates and unavailable artifacts reduce participation but still finalize rounds with small accuracy changes.

Alexandros Balla, Spyros Lalis

Resource Locks and Fast Unlock for Shared Balances in Certificate-Based Payment Systems

In briefResource Locks and Fast Unlock for Shared Balances in Certificate-Based Payment Systems

SummaryThis paper studies locking and unlocking in certificate-based payment systems such as FastPay when balances are shared among several debiting accounts. The authors prove that no unlock rule is safe unless certificate formation is visible to validators. They propose a hybrid protocol that keeps the client-driven path for personal accounts, while shared resources are certified by a two-phase ECHO and READY broadcast. When concurrent debits conflict, validators freeze the affected resource version and resolve it with one agreement instance. The protocol is proven to satisfy consistency, no overdraft, validity, finality and liveness, and composes with the PDCC reconfiguration protocol.

Lingkang Shangguan

Who Swept My ETH? Uncovering EIP-7702-Based Ether Redirection Attacks on Ethereum

In briefWho Swept My ETH? Uncovering EIP-7702-Based Ether Redirection Attacks on Ethereum

SummaryThis paper examines a new attack enabled by EIP-7702, which lets externally owned accounts temporarily delegate execution to arbitrary contract code. In the described attack, a victim is delegated to a malicious sweeper contract that redirects incoming ETH to an attacker-controlled address. The authors present the first formal analysis of this attack model and introduce SweeperHunter, a transaction-analysis framework for detecting it. Analyzing ten months of Ethereum mainnet data, they identify millions of malicious delegations, tens of thousands of attack transactions, and 508 sweeper contracts, and propose mitigation strategies.

WhyThe authors give the first formal analysis of EIP-7702 ether redirection attacks and report a detection framework that found over 20,000 attack transactions on Ethereum mainnet.

Ziwei Li, Tianwei Chen, Jiajing Wu et al.

An Incentive-Aligned Trust-Centric Coordination Layer for Heterogeneous Blockchain Interoperability

In briefA coordination layer aligning incentives and trust for blockchain interoperability

SummaryThis paper proposes a coordination architecture that handles cross-domain trust and privacy separately from underlying blockchain protocols, without requiring protocol changes. It combines cryptographic unlinkability with incentive-compatible relay selection, using stake-weighted participation and accumulated reputation. The authors formalize an adversarial model and analyze robustness against Sybil attacks, collusion, and stake concentration. Simulations examine trade-offs among quorum thresholds, reputation memory, and anonymity parameters.

Saeed Moradi, Sara Rouhani
