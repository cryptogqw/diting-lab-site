V2V Communications Using Blockchain-Enabled 6G Technology and Federated Learning

In briefVehicle-to-vehicle communication using blockchain, federated learning and 6G

SummaryThis paper proposes a vehicle-to-vehicle (V2V) communication approach combining blockchain, federated learning, and latency and resource allocation optimization. It is evaluated using packet delivery ratio, model accuracy, and latency, and is reported to outperform existing techniques. The system adds security through consensus optimization and k-anonymity for data privacy. The authors claim higher reliability, lower latency, and better resource utilization than traditional 5G.

Tahir H. Ahmed, Jun Jiat Tiang, Azwan Mahmud et al.

Cleve's Bound Is Tight: Optimally Fair Coin Flipping for Every Fixed Number of Parties

In briefCleve's Bound Is Tight: Optimal Fair Coin Flipping for Fixed Party Counts

SummaryThis paper studies fair multi-party coin flipping, where colluding parties can bias the result by aborting when there is no honest majority. The authors present an r-round protocol for n parties whose bias against n-1 corrupted parties is O(n^3 2^n / r), under assumptions of constant-round oblivious transfer and passively secure computation. This shows that Cleve's Ω(1/r) lower bound is tight for every fixed number of parties.

LLMs as Validators for Cross-Chain Bridges: Evaluating Source-Level and Transaction-Level Attack Detection on Solana

In briefLLMs as Validators for Cross-Chain Bridges: Testing Attack Detection on Solana

SummaryThe paper builds a lock-and-mint cross-chain bridge in Rust on a local Solana validator, with four exploitable variants, two reproducing flaws from the Cashio and Wormhole incidents and two based on vulnerability classes in Solana security audits. Five open-weight language models in six configurations were tested on source code with and without comments, and their 16,800 responses were graded by two blinded LLM graders. Without comments, models correctly diagnosed 24% of vulnerable source inputs while often flagging the secure bridge; original comments raised source diagnosis to 60%.

Yasir Rahman, Ishraq Samiha, Shahnewaz Karim Sakib et al.

Incentive Alignment for Electricity Data Assets in China: A Blockchain-Enabled Contribution-Credit Framework

In briefIncentive Alignment for Electricity Data Assets in China: A Blockchain-Based Contribution-Credit Framework

SummaryThis paper proposes a blockchain-based contribution-credit framework that encourages truthful reporting of operational contributions when supporting evidence arrives late. It links authenticated evidence to contribution recognition through dynamic credit updating, escrow, report-linked penalties, and automated incentive execution. The Chinese electricity market is the primary setting, with two representative coordination scenarios and adaptations for UK and US market structures. Simulation, security analysis, and blockchain implementation experiments assess its behavioural effects and feasibility.

Jiazheng Zhang, Shouwei Li, Yutian Deng et al.

RESILAGENT: A Three-Pillar Architecture for Cyber-Physical Resilience in Electrical Power and Energy Systems

In briefRESILAGENT: A Three-Pillar Architecture for Protecting Power Systems Against Cyber-Physical Threats

SummaryThis paper proposes RESILAGENT, a conceptual architecture for electrical power and energy systems facing combined cyber intrusions, physical sabotage, and climate-driven stress. It is built on three pillars (sensing and data intelligence, resilience analytics and reporting, and incident response and recovery), coordinated by a cloud-based orchestrator and a Detect-Simulate-Decide-Sign loop. The framework integrates agentic AI, multimodal large language models, real-time digital twins, and a permissioned blockchain, and is evaluated on two operator scenarios and an IEEE-bus micro-benchmark.

Nikolaos Kekatos, Georgios Koutidis, Konstantinos Antonakopoulos et al.
