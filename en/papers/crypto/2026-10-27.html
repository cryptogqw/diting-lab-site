UniPCH: A Unified Framework for Cross-Chain Payments via Payment Channel Hubs

In briefUniPCH: A Unified Framework for Cross-Chain Payments via Payment Channel Hubs

SummaryThis paper presents UniPCH, a two-layer framework for private cross-chain payments across heterogeneous blockchains. The lower UniPC layer defines common channel state transitions and security requirements, with realizations for UTXO and smart-contract account ledgers. The upper layer introduces selective re-signing blind conditional signatures (S-RBCS) to coordinate conditional authorization across hubs, enabling a four-phase payment protocol. The authors report security properties and a Go prototype evaluation of running time and bandwidth.

Yuanming Shao, Yuming Feng, Weizhe Zhang et al.

An Empirical Study of Undisclosed Charges in Hook-Based Decentralized Exchanges

In briefUndisclosed Fees in Hook-Based Decentralized Exchanges

SummaryThis paper presents a large-scale empirical study of Hook contracts on Uniswap v4 and PancakeSwap Infinity across Ethereum and BNB Smart Chain. The authors analyze 7,873 Hook deployments and 50,011 pools using bytecode analysis to identify settlement charges not itemized in standard fees. They find that 24.30% of Hooks can impose such deductions, which occur in 59.23% of relevant swaps and generate substantial revenue. The results suggest a gap between displayed fees and actual trading costs, motivating explicit disclosure.

Kai Ma, Tianle Sun, Shun Zhou et al.

Tight Characterizations of AMM Mechanism Design under Weak Efficiency

In briefTight limits on fair AMM mechanism design under weak efficiency

SummaryThis paper studies miner extractable value (MEV) in automated market makers (AMMs), where block builders can profit by controlling transaction order. It proves that no deterministic, anonymous batch AMM can be both incentive compatible and weakly locally efficient under plain consensus without fair sequencing, even with censorship resilience and unlimited block space. The impossibility relies on Sybil identities, over-reporting budgets, and misreporting values. Removing any one of these strategic capabilities makes both properties achievable under censorship resilience.

WhyThe authors prove deterministic anonymous batch AMMs cannot satisfy both IC and wLE under plain consensus; removing any one of three strategic capabilities restores feasibility.

Sybil-Proofness of Generalized Adaptive Clinching and Implications for Blockchain Mechanism Design

In briefSybil-proof adaptive clinching auctions and their use in blockchain market design

SummaryThis paper examines whether the adaptive clinching auction for budget-constrained bidders stays robust when a bidder splits its budget across fake identities. The authors propose a generalized adaptive clinching framework and prove that bidders gain nothing by splitting into any number of identities, as long as their total reported budget does not exceed their enforceable true budget. They extend the mechanism to a two-sided automated market maker for decentralized finance and show, through impossibility results, that these guarantees are essentially the best possible.

FIDES-Live: Evaluating Behavioral Concordance in Sequential Trading Agents

In briefFIDES-Live: Checking Whether Trading AI Agents Behave Consistently

SummaryThis paper presents FIDES-Live, an extension of the FIDES framework for auditing sequences of trading agent actions, orders, execution receipts, and reports. It assesses decision validity, adherence to numerical commitments, reconciliation with execution and ledger records, and report accuracy grounded in receipts. The authors retrospectively audited ten inference routes over 7,299 cryptocurrency account-rounds and a 310-round equity pilot. The paper states that its findings do not establish long-run trading skill, faithfulness of explanations, or causal benefits of audit feedback.

Arther Tian, Alex Ding, Simon Wu et al.

Fuji: The Shape, Composition, and Spread of Demand Bursts on Robinhood Chain

In briefFuji: How Demand Bursts Take Shape, What They Consist Of, and How They Spread on Robinhood Chain

SummaryThis paper measures sudden surges in demand on a blockchain marketplace, using Robinhood Chain, a layer-2 network, over its first three active months and more than 50 million Uniswap swaps. During bursts, traders touch about as many pools as in quiet periods, mostly different ones, and trade more intensively, with median peaks 2.5 times normal load. The rise and fall of demand are described by two exponential functions, and the authors compare two explanations for bursts, finding externally triggered cascades fit the data better than self-excitation.

Pranay Anchuri (Offchain Labs)

Weekend Crypto Volatility and Monday Market Risk

In briefWeekend Crypto Volatility and Monday Risk: Whether weekend crypto price swings predict Monday variance in traditional markets.

SummaryThis paper studies whether volatility in cryptocurrencies such as Bitcoin, Ether and XRP, which trade through the weekend, predicts Monday variance in the S&P 500, gold and a US Treasury ETF. The authors add a common weekend crypto factor to heterogeneous autoregressive models using daily data from 2017 to 2026. Predictive content is significant after 2024 and reduces out-of-sample losses, though statistical evidence varies across markets.

Pedram Ebadollahyvahed, Nneka Umeorah
