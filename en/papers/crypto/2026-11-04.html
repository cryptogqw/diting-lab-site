Weaver: Unlocking Blockchain Transaction Parallelism through State-Decoupled Symbolic Execution

In briefWeaver: a method that speeds up blockchain transactions by running them in parallel without waiting for state data.

SummaryThis paper presents Weaver, a transaction processing approach that lets execution continue before concrete state values are fetched. It represents states as symbolic references, predicts branch directions through hierarchical symbolic pattern matching, and schedules individual expressions as soon as their dependencies are resolved. Evaluated on real Ethereum blocks and synthetic workloads from four dApps, it achieves 41% to 63% higher throughput than state-of-the-art parallel engines and up to 7.5 times that of serial execution.

WhyThe authors decouple state access via symbolic references and expression-granular scheduling, reporting throughput gains on real Ethereum blocks and four dApp workloads.

Ru Cheng, Maofan Yin, Liangrong Chen et al.

GRIDSHIELD: Verifiable Grid Feasibility and Manipulation Resistance in Privacy-Preserving Peer-to-Peer Energy Trading

In briefGRIDSHIELD: Verifiable Grid-Feasible, Privacy-Preserving Peer-to-Peer Energy Trading

SummaryGRIDSHIELD is a blockchain-based peer-to-peer energy trading system that keeps bids sealed while proving that cleared trades respect grid constraints. It clears trades with an order-independent batch program, so a compromised operator cannot profit by reordering them, and uses an aggregated congestion-feasibility proof to verify feasibility without trusted setup. The paper also introduces a fairness metric that assigns curtailment according to each party's responsibility for congestion, and evaluates the system on a Hyperledger Fabric network across four feeders.

Abdullahil Kafi, Md Zahidul Islam, Alvi Ataur Khalil

Blockchain-Anchored Proactive Refresh for Dynamic Threshold Services

In briefBlockchain-anchored proactive share refresh for dynamic threshold services

SummaryThis paper addresses long-lived threshold services that must admit new participants, refresh shares against mobile adversaries, and let offline participants recover missed updates. It shows that tracking exposure only for committed epochs is insufficient, and it instead budgets security per fine-grained state, including aborted refresh attempts, using an append-only release ledger. Encrypted update records are stored in blockchain blob storage, and an on-chain bisection dispute protocol identifies faulty records. An end-to-end Rust implementation on an EVM chain benchmarks lifecycles with up to 1,280 participants, reporting sub-second group recovery and practical gas costs.

Seunghyun Cho, Jaeyeon Lee, Myeongjun Kim et al.

Anduin: A sybil-resistant agentic marketplace reputation system

In briefAnduin: A sybil-resistant reputation system for autonomous agent marketplaces

SummaryThis paper examines whether existing onchain payment histories can produce provider ratings that resist Sybil attacks, where one actor creates many fake identities. The authors model 66 million agentic transactions worth $6.1M across 16 blockchains over 157 days as a bipartite graph linking buyers and providers. They propose three Sybil-resistant rating algorithms, including a max-flow rating and two relaxed variants. Against simulated adversaries, manipulating ratings costs 20% of the affected buyers' total spend and is about 23 times harder than the standard collaborative filtering similarity rule.

Reza Ramji, Preston Vander Vos, Ziming Zeng et al.

Shielded Bitcoin: Private Transfers on the Bitcoin L1

In briefShielded Bitcoin: a protocol for private transfers directly on Bitcoin's base layer

SummaryThis paper presents Shielded Bitcoin, a protocol for private Bitcoin transfers on the base layer that requires no changes to Bitcoin consensus. Value is held as encrypted notes, spends are marked with public nullifiers, and validity is proven with zero-knowledge proofs. All protocol data is published on Bitcoin, and shielded state is rebuilt off-chain by replaying accepted transfers in Bitcoin order.

Clara Shikhelman, Mikhail Komarov, Aleksei Moskvin
