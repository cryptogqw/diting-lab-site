SoK: Privacy-Preserving Order Matching

In briefA systematic review of privacy-preserving order matching across 22 protocol families

SummaryThis paper systematizes privacy-preserving order matching, covering 22 protocol families across financial markets and blockchain-based exchanges. It divides the order lifecycle into four phases (order representation and submission, discovery and collection, matching, and settlement) and characterizes each by protected and exposed information, access roles, privacy mechanisms, and trust assumptions. The authors find that privacy is not a single protocol-level property, since protecting some attributes or phases may still leak information elsewhere in the lifecycle. They also identify recurring trade-offs among privacy, functionality, trust, and verifiability.

Prerna Arote, Adrian Saiz, Lucianna Kiffer

On-Chain Archaeology of Bitcoin Oracles: Evidence of Use under Limited Observability

In briefOn-chain archaeology of Bitcoin oracles: tracing their use despite incomplete data

SummaryThis study traces the use of oracles on Bitcoin, services that supply external data or act as arbiters for contracts, from early days through July 2026. It combines a full census of 1,149 Counterparty bets, analysis of the Bitcoin chain through block 958,628, and searches of a public-key index. Early contracts remain on-chain, but many event descriptions are gone, and public registries can misrepresent actual oracle use.

Giulio Caldarelli

The Economic Security of Exponential EIP-1559

In briefEconomic Security of Exponential EIP-1559: Setting Blockchain Gas Fee Parameters with Revenue Guarantees

SummaryThis paper studies how to choose parameters for EIP-1559, a gas pricing mechanism used by blockchains. The goal is to guarantee a lower bound on total collected fees whenever usage over a time window exceeds a set threshold, economically discouraging usage above a desired level. The authors analyze the revenue-minimizing gas-usage distribution for the pure exponential version used by Ethereum and for a variant used by Robinhood Chain and Arbitrum, then show how to set economically secure parameters and discuss the tradeoffs.

WhyProposes economic-security parameter selection for EIP-1559, characterizing revenue-minimizing usage distributions with provable approximation guarantees for a variant.

Ben Berger, Edward W. Felten, Robin Fritsch
