Proving the Wrong Program: Miscompilations as a Threat to zkVM Guarantees

In briefProving the Wrong Program: Compiler Bugs as a Threat to zkVM Guarantees

SummaryA zero-knowledge virtual machine (zkVM) lets an untrusted prover convince a verifier that a program, usually Rust compiled to RISC-V, ran correctly. This paper presents the first empirical study of miscompilation as a threat to the SP1 and Jolt zkVMs, assembling 88 known rustc and LLVM bugs with Rust reproducers. Of these, 48 cause SP1 guests and 46 cause Jolt guests to commit wrong outputs. Existing guest programs are rarely affected by accident, but an attacker can deliberately trigger these bugs by contributing crafted code.

WhyThe authors report the first empirical study of miscompilations threatening zkVM guarantees, reproducing 88 known bugs on SP1 and Jolt and showing LLM-crafted triggering patches.

Hui Xuan Tan, Stefanos Chaliasos, Alastair F. Donaldson

How market volatility responds to a preceding price trend: a lag-resolved measurement in cryptocurrencies and equities

In briefHow market volatility reacts to past price trends, measured by time lag in crypto and stocks

SummaryThis paper measures, at minute resolution, how market volatility responds to a preceding price trend as a function of lag, and examines the asymmetry of that response. The sample covers trades in three large cryptocurrencies on one exchange and three large US stocks plus an S&P 500 ETF from 2020 to 2026. The asymmetry is split under time reversal into T-even and T-odd parts, and the authors report a detectable T-odd part for the cryptocurrencies but none for the equities at a 20-minute lag.

Gulsah Bicki, Taylan Akdogan

Revisiting Elliptic++ for Bitcoin Forensics: Feature Reconstruction and Leakage-Resistant Evaluation

In briefRechecking Elliptic++, a Bitcoin Fraud Dataset, Without Future Data Leaks

SummaryElliptic++ is a widely used benchmark dataset for Bitcoin forensic analysis, but its split does not respect time order, letting future information leak into training. The authors recover the exact definitions of its 55 address-level features by linking to public ledger data, then recompute them using a time-based split by each address's first appearance. With leakage removed, the random forest's F1 score on the illicit class drops from 0.846 to 0.291, and it no longer outperforms a simple logistic regression baseline.

WhyThe authors identify temporal leakage in Elliptic++, reconstruct feature definitions via public ledger linkage, and report the illicit-class F1 for random forest dropping from 0.846 to 0.291.

Runlin Liang, Simon Trimborn, Noud van Giersbergen

Attacking P2P Networks: Liveness Attacks on the GossipSub and DOG Gossip Protocols

In briefAttacks That Stop Messages Reaching Nodes in P2P Gossip Networks

SummaryThis paper studies how two peer-to-peer gossip protocols, GossipSub (used by Ethereum) and DOG (designed for CometBFT), hold up against attackers who only aim to keep honest nodes from receiving fresh messages. For GossipSub, the authors show that a few geographically distributed nodes with a latency advantage can capture the peer-scoring metric for first message deliveries and separate a constant fraction of nodes from the honest mesh. For DOG, they show a single physical node can disable all forwarding routes, leaving only direct publisher-to-neighbor delivery, and they verify this attack in a simulated network.

WhyThe authors report liveness attacks exploiting GossipSub's First Message Deliveries scoring and DOG's route filtering, verified in simulation, with cost and mitigation analysis.

Jiaheng Li, Anton Paramonov, Yann Vonlanthen
