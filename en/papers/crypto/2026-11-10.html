Deceleration without identification: testing a power-law trend in Bitcoin prices under persistent noise

In briefTesting whether Bitcoin's long-run price growth follows a power-law trend

SummaryThe paper examines whether Bitcoin's price since its first block follows a power-law trend in time. Using fifteen years of weekly prices, the authors apply a pre-declared test built on low-frequency averages, calibrated over a specified family of persistent deviations. Constant drift is rejected, while a log-age coefficient of 5.7, close to the published exponent, is not rejected. The authors note the rejection does not hold if persistence beyond a random walk is allowed, and a Gompertz curve with a ceiling fits about as well, so reversion to trend cannot be decided.

Carlos Baquero, Raquel Menezes

Optimal Trading Speed on Blockchain

In briefStudy of how faster blockchain block speeds affect arbitrage and liquidity provision

SummaryThis paper examines how block speed on blockchain-based exchanges affects arbitrageurs and liquidity providers. Faster blocks weaken priority-fee competition among arbitrageurs and shift rents from the block producer to them. Evidence from Base's Flashblocks upgrade, which increased block speed tenfold, confirms the predictions: priority-fee bids fall sharply and arbitrageurs' net profit rises. Pool depth falls and effective spreads widen.

Ruizhe Jia, Shihao Yu

DetTreeChain: Parallel Block Production on an N-ary Block Tree with Bounded Lane Divergence

In briefDetTreeChain: A Ledger That Produces Blocks in Parallel on a Tree with Bounded Lane Divergence

SummaryThis paper presents DetTreeChain, a blockchain ledger in which up to N blocks are produced in parallel and arranged in an N-ary tree without later merging or reordering. Transactions are routed to lanes by a hash of the locking scripts they spend, so blocks in different lanes do not conflict. In a Rust prototype and in simulation, the multi-lane design wasted less work than a single chain at the same block rate.

Mahesh Karri, Nikhileswara Rao Sulake

Early Rug Pull Detection on Solana

In briefEarly detection of rug pulls on Solana, a blockchain, using transaction-graph models.

SummaryThis paper studies how to flag rug pulls, scams where token creators drain liquidity, on newly launched Solana tokens in their first minutes. The authors analyze 2,700 tokens from SolRPDS using one-minute market data and token transfer graphs. They compare graph neural networks with non-graph models on identical features across observation windows of 12.5, 35.5, and 93 minutes.

Splitting for Stability: a Modified Reward Scheme for Transaction-Fee-Only Regimes

In briefSplitting Rewards to Stabilize Mining When Only Fees Remain

SummaryAs Bitcoin's block subsidy declines, miner revenue will depend more on transaction fees, which can encourage undercutting and fork-based mining. The paper proposes a reward-splitting scheme in which the winning block producer receives an α portion of the block reward and the rest goes to other miners. Analysis and simulations indicate parameter ranges where honest mining beats fork-based deviations, and the authors report that the scheme does not appear to strengthen selfish mining incentives.

Eric Brigham, Baigang Chen, Nicholas Hopper

Useful Work Can Increase Energy Use: Demand Elasticity and Proof of Useful Work

In briefUseful Work May Raise Energy Use: Demand Elasticity and Proof of Useful Work

SummaryBitcoin's Proof-of-Work consumes large amounts of electricity, so Proof-of-Useful-Work (PoUW) was proposed to let mining perform useful computations instead. This paper shows that switching to PoUW may increase total electricity consumption, echoing Jevons' paradox. PoUW acts as a subsidy that lowers the effective price of useful work, and if demand is elastic, total consumption rises. The result is proved in a simple model with electricity-only costs.

Isaiah Hull, Or Sattath

BioBind: Binding Biometric Deduplication to Zero-Knowledge Authentication

In briefBioBind: Linking Biometric Deduplication to Zero-Knowledge Authentication

Adaptively Secure (Threshold) Batched IBE from Static Assumptions

In briefAdaptively Secure Batched Identity-Based Encryption from Static Assumptions

SummaryThis paper presents the first adaptively secure batched identity-based encryption scheme based on static assumptions. Its ciphertexts, digests, and batch keys each consist of a constant number of group elements, independent of batch size, and it supports exponential identity and epoch spaces. The authors also give a threshold version and translate the scheme to prime-order bilinear groups under the static MDDH assumption.

WhyThe authors report the first adaptively secure batched IBE from static assumptions, avoiding q-type assumptions and random oracles, with a threshold variant.

Sourav Das, Pratish Datta, Aditi Partap et al.
