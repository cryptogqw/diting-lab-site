SoK: Multiple Concurrent Proposer Blockchains

In briefOverview of blockchains where several proposers add transactions at once

SummaryThis survey paper reviews multiple concurrent proposer (MCP) protocols, in which several parties contribute transactions to the same ledger update. It describes a common architecture covering transaction receipt through state finality, and identifies four direct MCP families. The paper compares these with constrained assembly, where several parties restrict a block built by one party, across 39 configurations. It concludes that additional proposers alone do not guarantee transaction inclusion.

Benjamin Marsh, Alejandro Ranchal-Pedrosa, Max Resnick

Sybil-Proofness of Generalized Adaptive Clinching and Implications for Blockchain Mechanism Design

In briefSybil-Proof Generalized Adaptive Clinching for Blockchain Market Design

SummaryThis paper introduces a generalized adaptive clinching framework and proves it is Sybil-robust incentive compatible: a bidder gains nothing by splitting its budget across any finite number of identities, provided the aggregate reported budget does not exceed its enforceable true budget. Motivated by blockchain decentralized finance, the authors design a new two-asset automated market maker mechanism that supports two-sided markets and state-dependent supply functions. They show it satisfies Sybil-robust incentive compatibility and a relaxed Pareto optimality, and argue these guarantees are essentially the best possible given known and new impossibility results.

WhyThe authors prove Sybil-robust incentive compatibility for generalized adaptive clinching and design a two-sided AMM mechanism, with theoretical results compared against impossibility bounds.

Chengkai Zhang, Yuhao Li, Elaine Shi et al.

Sparse Autoencoders as Implicit Diversification Regularisers for Cross-Sectional Cryptocurrency Returns

In briefSparse autoencoders as a diversification tool for crypto returns

SummaryThis paper uses sparse autoencoders (SAEs) to describe daily returns of many cryptocurrencies with only a few active features. In a time-ordered evaluation against common benchmarks, SAE portfolios show consistently lower volatility, linked to lower correlations among selected assets. However, the SAE does not outperform a simple contrarian strategy on risk-adjusted returns, and its factor identities are unstable across training runs.

Weijia Han, Lisha Qu

DICE: Decentralized Incentive-driven Federated Learning for Efficient & Scalable Customization of Pretrained Models in Web3

In briefDICE: A decentralized, incentive-driven federated learning method for efficiently customizing pretrained models in Web3.

SummaryThe paper presents DICE, a blockchain-based federated learning algorithm for customizing large pretrained models among Web3 participants. It uses dynamic progressive learning and model slice verification so that participants unable to deploy the full model can still take part. It also introduces a tree-structured committee consensus protocol and an efficiency-driven incentive scheme to support more data holders. The authors report that theoretical analysis and experiments on vision and language models demonstrate its efficiency, effectiveness, and security.

Hong Lin, Zhongle Xie, Lidan Shou et al.
