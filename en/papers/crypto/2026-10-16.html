Graphlet-based Decentralized Finance Transaction Method Classification

In briefClassifying DeFi transaction methods using network graphlet patterns

SummaryThis paper examines whether network topology can identify the transaction methods that describe Decentralized Finance (DeFi) operations. The authors extract directed graphlet features to classify the 30 most frequent methods. They report that graphlet features complement event log counts and improve classification performance. Using SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations), they derive model explanations that reveal method-specific graphlet structural patterns.

On the (In)stability of Adaptive Fee Mechanisms in Multi-Chain Systems

In briefPaper on whether adaptive transaction fees stay stable across multiple blockchains

SummaryThis paper studies whether adaptive base fee mechanisms like Ethereum's EIP-1559 remain stable when several blockchains are linked by cross-chain transfers. The authors model multi-chain fee markets as a nonlinear dynamical system with delayed feedback and identify delay-amplified over-correction as the root cause of instability. Experiments show that cross-chain coupling shrinks the safe range of update rates and that instability spreads to peer chains. The authors also propose a lightweight protocol-level mitigation that removes the over-correction.

Yifan Zhou, Jiang Xiao, Xuechao Wang et al.

QuarkShard: Decomposing Cross-Shard Requests into Single-Input Transactions

In briefQuarkShard: Splitting Cross-Shard Requests into Single-Shard Transactions

SummaryThis paper proposes QuarkShard, a sharding protocol for blockchains that splits cross-shard user requests into transactions that each need consensus within only one shard. A request decomposition algorithm is said to preserve fund or location atomicity, and the design is said to improve resistance to MEV and flooding attacks. In tests across four AWS regions, the authors report a peak throughput of 1024k requests per second with latency under 4 seconds, and up to 210.7% and 314.8% of the maximum throughput of Kronos and 2PC.

Changyu Duan (1), Zhixuan Fang (1) ((1) Tsinghua University)

Aerie: Efficient Aggregation of Falcon Signatures

In briefAerie: A proof system that aggregates post-quantum Falcon signatures into one short proof

SummaryThis paper presents Aerie, a proof system that aggregates Falcon-512 and Falcon-1024 signatures, the post-quantum signatures selected by NIST, into a single short proof. Aerie keeps each signature's short random salt in the aggregate, so the verifier computes hashes directly and the proof covers only algebraic and norm checks. On an Apple M4 Max laptop without GPU acceleration, it aggregates 32,768 Falcon-512 signatures in 0.65 seconds, and verification takes 54 milliseconds on one core. The authors prove knowledge soundness in the random-oracle model.

Agentic AI in Automated Markets

In briefAgentic AI in Automated Markets: How Different Trading Behaviors Affect Market Stress and Volatility

SummaryThis paper studies automated markets run by formulaic market makers, where three reinforcement-learning trading agents are modeled: a rational arbitrageur, a manipulator, and a retail herd-follower. A pilot on hourly cryptocurrency data (AAVE, ETC, SUI) compares how each behavior affects stress episodes, risk-adjusted performance, and volatility. The authors conclude that regulation should target specific behaviors rather than apply uniform limits on volatility or turnover.

TCDA: Tunable Coded Distributed Arrays for Data Availability Sampling

In briefTCDA: Tunable Coded Distributed Arrays for Blockchain Data Availability Sampling

SummaryThis paper proposes TCDA, a coded network layer for data availability sampling in blockchains that remains robust against an unbounded number of Byzantine nodes. A tunable parameter sets how much of its assigned data each node keeps, trading off dissemination and storage costs against retrieval cost. At Ethereum's parameters for a 32 MiB block, the authors report that the fully replicated RDA misses the attestation deadline, while TCDA meets it from 868 to 40,000 good nodes, using 1.7 to 32 times less storage per node and 2.5 to 7.7 round-trips per sample.

Duc A. Tran, Dung Truong, and Duy Le

Capital Costs Protect the Network: Mining Gap Dynamics in Proof-of-Work Blockchains Under a Stochastic Mempool

In briefCapital Costs Protect the Network: Idle Mining Periods in Proof-of-Work Blockchains

SummaryAs block subsidies decline, Proof-of-Work blockchains increasingly depend on transaction fees to keep miners active. The paper models the backlog of high-fee transactions as a Lindley-type recursion and uses its stationary distribution to characterize the mining gap, the idle time miners face before enough profitable transactions accumulate. Under Bitcoin's difficulty adjustment, the expected gap grows with network hashpower and can fill an entire round as hashpower grows without bound. A free-entry condition yields a unique long-run equilibrium hashpower, and capital cost caps that equilibrium so the gap never occupies the whole round.

Hasan Sel\c{c}uk K{\i}l{\i}\c{c}, Nail Akar
