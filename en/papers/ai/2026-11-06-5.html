A Theoretical Analysis of a Bilevel Optimization Formulation for Combined Local-Global Optical Flow Models

In briefTheoretical analysis of a bilevel optimization method for tuning combined local-global optical flow models

SummaryThis paper studies a bilevel optimization approach for learning the regularization parameter of combined local-global (CLG) optical flow models. The lower level computes flow from a Lucas-Kanade data term and a quadratic or total variation (TV) regularizer, while the upper level chooses the parameter minimizing squared distance to ground-truth flow. For the quadratic model, the authors give conditions for existence, positivity, and strict local optimality of optimal parameters. For TV regularization, an optimal parameter always exists, and a sufficient positivity condition is provided that accounts for coupling between flow components via local structure tensors. Synthetic examples illustrate the conditions, including cases where they fail.

El Mostafa Kalmoun

Unifying Central Flow, Rod Flow, and the Edge Coupling

In briefUnifying central flow, rod flow, and edge coupling: a common basis for continuous-time models of gradient descent at the edge of stability

SummaryAt the edge of stability, gradient descent iterates oscillate along sharp Hessian directions, which standard gradient flow cannot capture. The paper introduces the edge coupling, a functional on consecutive iterates whose critical points are gradient descent's fixed points and period-two orbits, and derives central flow and rod flow from it. The two flows coarse-grain over the fastest and intermediate time scales respectively, and the framework extends to momentum methods such as heavy ball and Adam. Experiments on neural networks show the flows track discrete iterates across all training phases.

Eric Regis, Sinho Chewi
