MPIG: Multi-angle Polarization Image Generation from a Single Polarization Observation

In briefMPIG: Generating Multi-Angle Polarization Images from One Polarization View

SummaryThis paper proposes MPIG, a Swin Transformer-based framework that predicts the missing 45°, 90°, and 135° polarization images from a single 0° observation. The authors also introduce HighPol, a real-world dataset of 5,474 groups of high-resolution four-angle polarization images from indoor and outdoor scenes. The reported experiments show improved image and polarization accuracy, and downstream glass and semantic-segmentation tests suggest the generated images keep task-relevant information.

Jue Wang, Ruilin Huang, Liquan Shen et al.

Where Groups Split: A Probe Prior for Nonzero Within-Group Variance in RL Post-Training

In briefPaper overview: a probe-based prior for finding prompts whose sampled groups split in RL post-training

SummaryThis paper addresses a waste in group relative policy optimization: when all sampled rewards in a group agree, within-group variance and advantage are zero, so the rollouts teach nothing. The authors propose Aperture, which trains a small subset with the same recipe, scores prompts on the base and probe models, and uses the two pass rates to estimate the chance a training-size group is all wrong or splits, as a static sampling weight. On mathematical and logical reasoning with 1.7B and 4B models, the prior improves greedy accuracy over uniform GRPO and increases the fraction of groups with nonzero advantage.

Jonah Ellison, Mateo Ruiz, Owen Pell et al.

Where and Why Search-Augmented LLMs Fail When Reasoning over Rapidly Evolving Knowledge

In briefWhy search-augmented LLMs fail when reasoning over fast-changing knowledge

SummaryThe paper introduces TREK, a suite with TREKTRACE, a framework that attributes failures to specific retrieval or reasoning stages, plus two benchmarks: TREKBENCH-WILD for real-world questions and TREKBENCH-CTRL with controlled distractors. The authors find the main bottleneck is post-retrieval reasoning, as frontier models are often misled by conflicting evidence even after retrieving the needed information. They also show that benchmarks dominated by outdated distractors substantially underestimate task difficulty.

Tianyi Hu, Bhavyajeet Singh, Niket Tandon et al.
