Self-training for cross-centre coronary vessel segmentation in X-ray angiography using pre-contrast frame subtraction

In briefSelf-training with contrast-frame subtraction for coronary X-ray vessel segmentation across hospitals

SummaryThis paper addresses the drop in performance of coronary X-ray angiography vessel segmentation models on data from centres not seen during training. The authors generate pseudo-labels from nnU-Net predictions and a contrast map obtained by subtracting a pre-contrast frame, then self-train without extra manual annotation. Self-training improved results on external datasets, and the size of the gain depended largely on the sensitivity of the model that produced the initial pseudo-labels.

Danial Moafi, Aida Moafi, Mostafa Aryafar et al.

MAServeBench: Benchmarking Multi-Agent LLM Serving Systems with Hierarchical Collaboration Workloads

In briefMAServeBench: Benchmark for Serving Multi-Agent LLM Systems with Hierarchical Collaboration Workloads

SummaryThis paper presents MAServeBench, a benchmark for evaluating serving systems that run multi-agent large language model applications. It treats how agents collaborate and exchange context as a configurable part of the workload, so the same tasks can produce different serving demands. The benchmark records realized operations and dependencies for reproducible comparisons and links them to hardware behavior, showing why higher serving efficiency does not always mean tasks finish sooner.

Shengxuan Qiu, Haochen Huang, Shuzhang Zhong et al.

DolphinRouting: Cluster-Level Binary Routing for Disaggregated LLM Inference Serving

In briefDolphinRouting: Cluster-Level Routing for Split LLM Serving

SummaryThis paper presents DolphinRouting, a method for routing requests in large language model serving systems that separate prefill and decode phases. Requests are grouped into clusters by prompt length, cache-adjusted input lengths, and output-length information, and each cluster learns a binary choice between local and remote prefill from end-to-end SLO feedback. Evaluated on a real disaggregated cluster with public benchmarks, a mixed workload, and real data, the k-means variant reduces P90 TTFT by 5.7 to 7.9% and P90 TPOT by 2.6 to 10.1% compared with Dynamo.

Yishuo Yuan, Shaoyu Yang, Chunrong Fang

Leveraging Recourse and Size Bounds for OnlineKnapsack with Removable Items

In briefOnline knapsack with removable items using recourse and size bounds

SummaryThis paper studies the online unweighted knapsack problem, where items arrive one by one and must be accepted or rejected immediately to maximize the total size of accepted items. It allows previously accepted items to be removed and considers two extensions: knowing an upper bound on item sizes and being able to reinsert previously rejected items. The authors establish close upper and lower bounds on the competitive ratio under these combined assumptions. They also introduce adaptive online algorithms that do not require knowing the size upper bound yet achieve comparable performance guarantees.

Dimitris Fotakis, Laurent Gourv\`es, Aris Pagourtzis et al.

Only Accepted Tokens Count: An Energy Accounting of Speculative Decoding Under Latency Targets

In briefOnly Accepted Tokens Count: Energy Accounting of Speculative Decoding Under Latency Targets

SummaryThis paper examines the energy cost of speculative decoding, in which a drafter proposes several future tokens that a target model verifies in one forward pass. The energy saving depends on how many drafted tokens are accepted, which varies by model, drafting scheme, and text. Measured on H100 and H200 GPUs with SGLang at batch sizes up to 64 across 5 corpora, the saving shrinks as batch size grows and can become a loss at large batches. Speculation only adds energy savings when acceptance is high.

Bole Ma, Ayesha Afzal, Jan Eitzinger et al.

LoRe-MIM: Lost-or-Replaced Masked Image Modeling for Self-Supervised Representation Learning in Whole-Body PET/CT

In briefLoRe-MIM: A self-supervised method for learning features from whole-body PET/CT scans using masked image reconstruction

SummaryThis paper addresses the shortage of expert annotations for whole-body PET/CT deep learning by assembling a multi-tracer, multi-institutional dataset of 6,531 scans. It proposes LoRe-MIM, a self-supervised framework that learns multimodal representations by reconstructing masked images, where masked regions are either zeroed out (Lost) or replaced with the corresponding complementary imaging modality (Replaced). The learned representations are evaluated on tasks including multimodal reconstruction, cancer versus non-cancer patch classification, whole-body lesion segmentation, label-efficient training, and robustness assessment. The authors report that LoRe-MIM consistently outperforms corresponding state-of-the-art baselines across these tasks.

Amartya Bhattacharya, Bashirul Azam Biswas, Biratal Raj Wagle et al.

Neighborhood Elites: Quality-Diversity Search for Uncertainty Quantification in Question Answering

In briefNeighborhood Elites: Quality-Diversity Search for Detecting Wrong Answers in QA

SummaryThis paper addresses the difficulty of detecting wrong answers from large language models in question answering. It proposes neighborhood elites (NE), a method that uses quality-diversity search to build answer-preserving input rewrites spread across two axes: the kind of edit and how explicitly the context supports the answer. Across five extractive-QA datasets and five instruction-tuned LLMs, NE improves failure detection over other neighborhood constructors in at least 24 of 25 pairs, and also outperforms semantic entropy computed over decoder samples.

In briefDistribution-calibrated, budget-neutral token credit for multimodal RL with verifiable rewards

SummaryThe paper proposes TACE, a two-stage estimator for advantage computation in GRPO. The first stage maps rewards to a standard normal via a one-dimensional optimal-transport map to align reward distributions across tasks and cap outliers. The second stage rank-calibrates token-level evidence weights within each trajectory and renormalizes them to mean one, conserving total credit and sign. On a multimodal reasoning suite, TACE achieves the best results on most benchmarks but not in every setting.

Judged in Company: In-Context Batching Makes LLM Verdicts Depend on Other Items

In briefJudged in Company: Batching Items in One Prompt Makes LLM Verdicts Depend on Neighbours

SummaryThis paper examines whether asking a language model to grade many independent items in one prompt, a common cost-saving practice, changes the verdicts. Across three judgement tasks and five open-weight models, placing the same 2,000 items into different random batches changed 11-22% of decisions on harm and grading. Most of this variation comes from the order and position of neighbouring items, and majority voting over permutations does not remove it. The authors recommend grading ambiguous items one per prompt and reporting agreement with single-item grading when batching.

WhyThe authors show batched grading makes verdicts depend on co-batched items, measuring flip rates across three tasks and nine models and decomposing them.

Peng Xie, Amr Alanwar

ScienceJudge: Pattern Perturbation Augmentation for Sentence-Level Scientific Claim Verification

In briefScienceJudge: Pattern-Based Data Augmentation for Checking Sentence-Level Scientific Claims

SummaryThis paper addresses checking whether each sentence in an AI-generated scientific paragraph is supported by a given multimodal evidence bundle. The authors propose Pattern Perturbation Augmentation (PPA), which models reporting errors as faithfulness-breaking transformations to create hard negative examples. Their ScienceJudge system combines vision-language and LLM predictors, raising the local development-set average score from 0.8124 to 0.8692. In briefMining translation pairs from linguistic archives for low-resource languages

SummaryThis paper proposes extracting parallel translation examples from linguistic archival materials such as grammars and dictionaries, which cover languages with little online presence. The authors build a pipeline combining an OCR model and a vision language model so that extracted text stays aligned with visual layout information. SummaryThis paper proposes EpiAgent, a framework that gives LLM-based agents a learnable layer for maintaining beliefs about others' private information.
