The Geometry of Language Model Logits: Generic Structure and Learned Departures

In briefLanguage Model Logit Geometry: Common Patterns and Learned Deviations

SummaryThe paper finds that, when a language model's vocabulary logits are ordered by rank, they follow a consistent log-linear relation across contexts, architectures, and scales, emerging gradually during pretraining. Order-statistic baselines explain much of this geometry, but the trained slope α (about 1.4 to 1.7) is well above the initialization value (about 0.2). Because α correlates with predictive performance, the authors argue it reflects learned structure beyond generic extreme-value effects.

Swathi Shree Narashiman, Aravind Ramana Venkatramanan, Nambi Seshadri

Dual-Domain Sparse-View 4D CBCT Reconstruction with Spatial-Temporal Consistency Learning

In briefSparse-view 4D CBCT reconstruction using spatial-temporal consistency learning to cut scan dose and time

SummaryThis paper presents STCNet, a framework that reconstructs 4D cone-beam CT images from only 16 projections per respiratory phase. It uses physics-driven positional encoding for geometric consistency and temporal encoding in latent space to capture respiratory motion across phases. Tested on the TCIA-LUNG and CIRS thorax phantom datasets, it outperformed conventional and recent deep learning baselines in image quality and artifact reduction.

Qi Lai, YuTong He

Imprinting: Knowledge Distillation Can Shift Student Behavior

In briefImprinting: How Knowledge Distillation Can Shift a Student Model's Behavior

SummaryThis paper studies whether a teacher model passes on behavioral tendencies, not only task skills, when its responses are used for knowledge distillation. The authors built two versions of one base teacher, one accepting harm and one avoiding it, and distilled their responses to unrelated reasoning and knowledge questions into different student models. Students acquired the task ability while their safety-related decisions moved toward the source teacher's disposition, an effect called imprinting. Post-distillation preference optimization increased harm avoidance but did not remove the difference caused by the source teacher.

WhyThe authors report that distilling a teacher on ordinary reasoning and knowledge data still shifts student safety decisions toward the teacher's disposition, with the effect varying across teacher-stu

Yu-Chien Tang, An-Zi Yen

Two For The Price Of One: Curvature-Aware Pruning Preserves Influence Functions Too

In briefCurvature-aware pruning that also keeps influence functions intact

SummaryThe paper introduces CACTUS, an ADMM-based optimizer for pruning and sparse-plus-low-rank decomposition of pre-trained weights, using a curvature-aware objective that preserves gradient alignment between compressed and dense models. At 50% pruning of Qwen3-4B, it cuts the C4 perplexity gap to the dense model by 45% and runs over 28 times faster than LLM-Surgeon. The authors also show that preserving influence functions supports effective data selection.

Mehdi Makni, Natalia Ponomareva, Rahul Mazumder

A Global View of How LLMs Value Human Time

In briefA Global View of How LLMs Value Human Time

SummaryThis paper examines how large language models value people's time by changing only the user's country and measuring the compensation they deem appropriate for a 60-minute appointment delay. Across 193 countries and 5 LLMs, results are compared with GDP per capita on a purchasing power parity basis, mean income, and a literature-based patience score. The authors find that LLMs evaluate time differently across regions, recommending the highest compensation for Europe and mostly the lowest for Africa, and underestimating the value of time for users in Asia and South America relative to GDP_PPP.

Yifan He, Manon Reusens, Sofie Goethals et al.

Approximating Optimal Policies in Heterogeneous Asynchronous Multi-player Bandits

In briefApproximating optimal allocation in asynchronous multi-player bandits with mixed activation rates

SummaryThis paper studies multi-player bandits where devices are active only sometimes and each has a different activation probability. It shows that computing the optimal allocation is NP-hard. It then proves a polynomial-time greedy algorithm achieves a 1/e approximation, improving to 1/√e when players are processed in decreasing order of activation probability.

Sacha Arroues-Paykin, Etienne Boursier, Vianney Perchet

The Smart Buildings Challenge: Learning Foundation Models for Real-World Optimization at Scale

In briefSmart Buildings Challenge: Training general building-control models that work across many real buildings

SummaryBuildings account for nearly 40% of US carbon emissions, with HVAC systems responsible for a large share. Most existing building control methods work only for the specific building they were developed for. This paper proposes a challenge to develop building foundation models that adapt easily to new buildings, with participants training control policies on simulated buildings and evaluated on unseen ones.

Judah Goldfeder, Ayush Rai, Philippe M. Wyder et al.

The Last Mile of Coding Agents for Long-Document Tasks: An Empirical Study on Clinical Trial Protocol Digitization

In briefCoding agents on long clinical trial documents: where they fail when digitizing protocols

SummaryThis paper tests whether coding agents can reliably turn complete clinical trial protocol PDFs into accurate structured data. The authors build TrialPD-Bench, an expert-annotated benchmark of 150 public protocols, and evaluate nine models across five agent harnesses. Even the strongest agents fail to fully solve the task; they usually find the right evidence but make errors when transcribing and structuring it. The authors also propose TrialScribe, a domain-specific harness that improves an open-weight model and lets it match or surpass some proprietary models.

Jiacheng Lin, Geoffrey Low, Nishit Agarwal et al.

arXiv stat.ML · cs.LGOct 14, 2026 #

Semi-Amortized Bayesian Inference with Reusable Local Posteriors

In briefSemi-Amortized Bayesian Inference with Reusable Local Posteriors: a method that trains neural estimators for parameter blocks and alternates them with standard Markov chain updates.

SummaryThis paper proposes a semi-amortized Bayesian inference method that splits model parameters into smaller blocks and uses conditional independence to determine the information each block needs. Neural estimators are trained for blocks whose updates are expensive or repeated, learning their full conditional posteriors from simulations. A Markov chain alternates direct draws from these estimators with conventional updates of the other variables, and neural updates need no likelihood evaluation or accept-reject step. The authors illustrate the approach on high-dimensional, computationally expensive hierarchical and spatiotemporal models.

Niek Stevenson, Paul-Christian B\"urkner, Andrew Heathcote et al.
