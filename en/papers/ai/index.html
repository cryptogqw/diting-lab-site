What Hallucinations Have We Measured? Semantic Illusion, Confabulation, and Expressed Uncertainty

In briefMeasuring Hallucinations: Semantic Illusion, Confabulation, and Expressed Uncertainty

SummaryThis paper argues for a strict definition of hallucinations in large language models as confident incorrectness, separate from expressed uncertainty. It proposes a re-elicitation test that splits errors into semantic illusions, where correct knowledge is accessible, and confabulations, where it is not. Re-evaluating 14 recent hallucination methods, the authors find that detectors flag expressed uncertainty more often than hallucinations, and that mitigation works far better for semantic illusions than for confabulations.
