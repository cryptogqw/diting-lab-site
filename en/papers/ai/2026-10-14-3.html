From Global to Tail Convergence: Dynamics of $\alpha$-Stable Score-Based Generative Models

In briefFrom Global to Tail Convergence: Stable-Process Generative Models

SummaryThis paper studies score-based generative models driven by α-stable Lévy processes and gives a non-asymptotic convergence analysis for compactly supported and power-law targets. In p-Wasserstein distance, it quantifies the effects of early stopping, initialization, score approximation, and time discretization, and shows that the initialization error decays exponentially. Compared with Brownian dynamics, global Wasserstein convergence alone does not separate the two models, but their tails differ sharply: stable dynamics yields power-law tails with index α at any positive generation time, while Brownian dynamics stays light-tailed. For a target with tail index ν, asymptotically correct extreme-quantile scaling is possible precisely when α equals ν, offering a principled explanation for stable models' ability to capture extreme events.

In briefLikelihood Is Not Authorship: Measuring Human Contribution in AI-Assisted Writing

SummaryThe paper shows that a common method of estimating human contribution to AI-assisted text, based on how well a person's prompt predicts the final text, can be easily manipulated. The authors introduce SPECIFICITY PADDING, where asking a model to copy random IDs from the prompt makes a title-only request appear more human, and show that changing only which IDs the evaluator sees shifts scores substantially for the same text. They report this effect across news and scientific abstracts and LLMs ranging from 3.8B to 753B parameters. The authors conclude that likelihood-based scores cannot reliably establish human contribution without evidence of what the person added and how the text was produced.
