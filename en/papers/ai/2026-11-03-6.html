Metadata-Conditioned Audio Transformers for Adaptive Respiratory Sound Classification

In briefMetadata-conditioned audio transformers for adaptive respiratory sound classification

SummaryThis paper addresses how recording device, auscultation site, and patient characteristics introduce variability in respiratory sound classification that is unrelated to pathology. It proposes metadata-conditioning mechanisms for the Audio Spectrogram Transformer, progressing from gated residual fusion to FiLM, and introduces two variants, TAFiLM and SoftFiLM. On the ICBHI benchmark, all conditioning strategies improve results, with SoftFiLM reaching 64.11% on the 4-class task (+3.94% over baseline) and 72.40% on the 2-class task.

George Kontogiannis, Pantelis Tzamalis, Sotiris Nikoletseas

The SIMPLE Law for Energy Cost of LLM Inference

In briefSIMPLE Law: A Four-Parameter Model for Predicting LLM Inference Energy

SummaryThe paper derives closed-form expressions for the computation and memory-access costs of LLM inference from architectural hyperparameters and proposes SIMPLE, a four-parameter energy model that separates prefill from decode. Tested on 13 dense LLMs from six families on NVIDIA H100 GPUs, it reaches R² of 0.969 and 11.97% MAPE without per-model calibration. The paper also analyzes how input length, grouped-query attention, reasoning token budgets, and agentic serving with prefix caching affect energy use.

WhyThe authors derive closed-form energy models from architecture hyperparameters, proposing a four-parameter law reporting R2 0.969 and 11.97% MAPE across 13 LLMs, relevant to inference energy planning.

Hiari Pizzini Cavagna, Andrea Proia, Daniele Cesarini et al.

From FAIR to AI-FAIR Metadata-First Infrastructure for AI-Enabled Science

In briefFrom FAIR to AI-FAIR: Metadata-First Infrastructure for AI Science

SummaryThis paper proposes AI-FAIR metadata criteria, an extension of the original FAIR data principles, placing AI-enabled science within a bidirectional Question-Data-Model triangle. It also introduces an agentic evaluation workflow that rates existing datasets against both AI-FAIR and traditional FAIR criteria and gives actionable feedback where critical metadata is missing or incomplete. Multi-layer Indexer: Overlapping Selection and Attention in DeepSeek Sparse Attention

In briefMulti-layer Indexer: a method to speed up sparse attention in long-context language models by reusing token selections across layers.

SummaryThe paper addresses the decoding bottleneck in DeepSeek Sparse Attention (DSA), where a lightning indexer scanning the full cache dominates time at long context. Because adjacent layers select nearly the same tokens, the proposed Multi-layer Indexer lets a layer reuse the previous indexer layer's selection, so each indexer runs concurrently with attention, with no retraining or new weights. Combined with IndexCache, it decodes faster at equal retention on DeepSeek-V3.2 and GLM-5, while its LongBench, RULER and LongBench v2 accuracy stays within a few percentage points of IndexCache.

Serdarcan Dilbaz, Arjun Krishna, Kiran Kamble et al.

Steering with Multilingual Lexicons for Sentiment Control in Low-Resource Languages

In briefUsing multilingual sentiment lexicons to steer sentiment in low-resource languages

SummaryThis paper examines whether multilingual sentiment lexicons can replace the sentence-level contrastive data normally needed for activation steering in languages where such data is scarce. Across three model families, aggregated multilingual lexicons outperform English-derived steering vectors for mid- and low-resource languages, while monolingual lexicons work better for languages the model specialized in during pre-training. The authors also give a mechanistic analysis of how representational density, pre-training priors, and vector magnitude affect cross-lingual steering.

Lucinda Laurent, Muhammad Dehan Al Kautsar, Alfan Farizki Wicaksono et al.

In briefLLMs rely on specialist tools to generalize to unseen MS/MS structure elucidation

An Axiomatic Framework for Reinforcement Learning in Open Multi-Agent Systems

In briefAn axiomatic framework for reinforcement learning in systems where only some agents can be controlled

SummaryThis paper studies reinforcement learning in open multi-agent systems, where only some agents are controllable, such as in smart grids. It derives value function learning from the Shapley axioms (Efficiency, Symmetry, and Linearity) within the n-agent ad hoc teamwork (NAHT) setting, linking it to cooperative game theory and the Shapley value for Dec-POMDPs. Two generic algorithmic templates, Shapley Machines and Banzhaf Machines, are introduced and can be combined with standard reinforcement learning methods. The authors report improved learning efficiency and generalisation to unseen agent types on NAHT benchmark tasks.

Jianhong Wang, Yang Li, Samuel Kaski et al.

SQLoom: Set-Contrastive Learning of Syntactic, Semantic, and Logical Dependencies for Text-to-SQL

In briefSQLoom: Set-Contrastive Learning for Text-to-SQL Syntax, Semantics, and Logic

SummaryThis paper presents SQLoom, a constraint-aware fine-tuning method for large language models that converts natural language questions into SQL queries. It supervises outputs at three levels (syntactic validity, semantic alignment with the question, and logical consistency across clauses) using a set-contrastive objective that favors correct output sets over minimally different incorrect ones. On Spider, the approach raises Qwen3-1.7B's execution accuracy on the development set from 62.9% zero-shot to 88.7%, and reaches 73.4% on BIRD development.

Hao Fu, Degang Sun, Wan Wei et al.

Inferring Transferable Rewards via Active Inverse Reinforcement Learning

In briefLearning rewards that transfer across environments by actively choosing which dynamics to study

SummaryInverse reinforcement learning seeks to recover expert rewards that remain useful when the environment's dynamics change. The paper proposes a two-phase method that first cycles through available dynamics to get a reliable reward estimate, then actively selects dynamics to reduce uncertainty about the expert reward. The authors provide sample complexity guarantees on transfer that hold for any dynamics, with a rate independent of the number of available dynamics. Experiments show the method substantially reduces the number of expert demonstrations needed to learn a transferable reward.

Victor Villin, Till Freihaut, Andreas Schlaginhaufen et al.

Mesh-Agnostic Refinement Models for Latent Space Encoding of 3D Human Body Shapes

In briefMesh-agnostic refinement of 3D human body shape latent encodings

SummaryThis paper proposes a framework for refining reconstructions from parametric human body models such as SMPL. It uses the geometric difference between an initial decoded surface and a target mesh, without needing vertex correspondences. The framework has three methods: Latent-VaRefine corrects the latent code using varifold-distance gradients, Vertex-VaRefine predicts vertex displacements in a canonical pose with a DiffusionNet backbone, and ElaOpt optimizes surface deformation under elastic regularization and normal-direction constraints.

A Human-Verified AI Workflow for Annotating Environmental Sound Data

In briefA human-verified AI workflow for labeling environmental sound recordings

SummaryThis paper describes a human-AI workflow used to annotate the Kenya Forest Sounds Dataset (KFSD), a working collection of 7,531 recordings across 21 categories. The fine-tuned Audio Spectrogram Transformer reached 89.36% accuracy for binary detection of person talking, below the predefined 95% threshold for autonomous labeling, so its predictions were used as suggestions and checked by a human. DeepWaveRL: Self-Supervised Full Waveform Inversion via Reinforcement Learning

In briefDeepWaveRL: Reinforcement Learning for Seismic Subsurface Velocity Estimation

SummaryThis paper proposes DeepWaveRL, a framework that applies reinforcement learning to Full Waveform Inversion (FWI), a geophysics method for estimating subsurface velocity from seismic measurements. The authors recast FWI as policy learning, where a policy network predicts velocity maps as actions and a forward operator is used only for simulation. Learning relies on a single global scalar reward based on data misfit, so no gradients from the forward operator are needed. Experiments show competitive performance against supervised and self-supervised baselines.

Peng Jin, Yinan Feng, Yinpeng Chen et al.

The Cost of Mismatch: Noise Amplification in Zeroth-Order Reinforcement Learning

In briefThe Cost of Mismatch: Noise Amplification in Zeroth-Order RL

SummaryThis paper studies whether zeroth-order (ZO) optimization can reliably train large language models with reinforcement learning from verifiable rewards (RLVR). The authors show that gradient estimation reliability depends on whether the two finite-difference evaluations use matched data: mismatched on-policy RL amplifies noise as the perturbation scale shrinks, while the matched-data BOLT method cancels this noise. Building on this, they propose ZO-BOLT, which consistently outperforms the pretrained baseline across three model variants and two mathematical reasoning benchmarks while keeping memory use close to inference levels.

Lianmin Chen, Junbin Qiu, Chenxing Wei et al.

Poisson Super Learning For Survival And Competing Risks On A Discretized Time Scale

In briefPoisson Super Learning for Survival and Competing Risks on Discretized Time

SummaryThis paper introduces the Poisson Super Learner, a scalable ensemble method for survival and competing risks prediction with right-censored data. It uses the link between piecewise-constant hazard models and the Poisson likelihood, training candidate hazard learners under a common Poisson loss and combining them with a Poisson meta learner. The ensemble estimates survival probabilities and cause-specific absolute risks, and is illustrated by predicting cardiovascular disease risk in type 1 diabetes patients using Steno Diabetes Center data from 2005 to 2022, supported by simulations. The methods are implemented in the R package poissonsuperlearner.

Gabriele Pittarello, Thomas Gerds, Pernille Falberg R{\o}nn et al.

VagueBench: Benchmarking the Robustness of Embodied Agents on Ambient Context Illusions

In briefVagueBench: Testing Embodied Agents' Reliance on Surrounding Context

SummaryThis paper identifies the Ambient Context Illusion, where embodied agents fail when non-essential contextual details are removed from instructions while the task meaning stays the same. The authors built VagueBench, a benchmark of 1,303 instruction pairs in the AI2-THOR simulator, organized into a taxonomy of four categories and nine subtypes. Testing six state-of-the-art agents, they found task success rates dropped by a relative 19.65% and empty plan rates rose by a relative 54.10%. The authors also report the same effect in real-world robotic systems.

Yang Qu, Qiang Fu, Zonghao Ying et al.
