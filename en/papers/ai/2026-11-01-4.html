QVR3D: Query-Guided Visual Reasoning for Training-Free 3D Grounding

In briefQVR3D: Query-guided visual reasoning for locating objects in 3D scenes without training

SummaryThis paper introduces QVR3D, a framework that locates objects in 3D scenes from natural language descriptions without any task-specific training. It operates on RGB-D observations using off-the-shelf foundation models, selecting complementary query-relevant views via Query-Guided Maximum Coverage and estimating grounding probability by combining instance confidence, attribute consistency, and relationship consistency. The authors report state-of-the-art zero-shot results on ScanRefer and Nr3D, and show the view selection also improves frame efficiency on several 3D vision-language tasks.

Jung-Hao Kao, Jing-En Huang, Jia-Wei Liao et al.

Robust Safety Assurance for Robotic Swarms Using Decentralized Collision Verification

In briefDecentralized collision checking for drone swarms, tested outdoors

SummaryThis paper describes deploying a decentralized safety verification algorithm on a swarm of small drones to prevent collisions in real time outdoors. The authors modify and analyze a reachability-based collision avoidance method, testing it over 140 outdoor flights including six flights with eight UAVs. Each agent takes about 500 to 520 milliseconds on average to verify its safety for the next 4 seconds, with reachable set broadcasting accounting for most of that time.

Grant Phillips, Hoang-Dung Tran, Justin M. Bradley

CuPH: Parallel Cubical Persistent Homology via Level-Synchronous Component Tracking

In briefCuPH: A parallel GPU method for computing persistent homology of images

SummaryThis paper presents CuPH, a GPU-based parallel algorithm for computing cubical persistent homology, a method that describes the shape and connectivity of image data at multiple scales. It processes pixels directly, grouping them by intensity levels and tracking connected components with a lock-free union-find method. The authors prove that CuPH matches standard cubical persistent homology on integer inputs and give a stability bound for floating-point inputs. The method is reported to run 18.6 to 99.6 times faster than the existing GPU baseline.

Guangyu Meng, Pengfei Gu, Erin Wolf Chambers et al.

In briefCaching Multi-Turn Chatbot Replies to Cut Language Model Serving Costs

SummaryThis paper proposes a semantic caching method for large language models that reuses stored responses in multi-turn conversations. Synergetic Autonomous Intelligence: Three Axioms for Recursive Semi-Closed Learning

In briefSynergetic Autonomous Intelligence: Three Axioms for Learning Systems

SummaryThis paper introduces Synergetic Autonomous Intelligence (SAI), a theory and design program for learning systems that form, preserve, revise, and compose relations while continuing to learn. It is built on three axioms: consequential distinctions must not be orphaned, identity is set by future-faithful continuation under a declared contract, and unresolved defects are repaired by the least lawful local completion at the lowest admitted grade. The authors establish relative consistency and conditional non-redundancy of these axioms under explicit finite or affine contracts, and define the GRC-Close generative and repair schema.

Dongxu Li

On Sparse Graph Limits for Neural Combinatorial Optimization

In briefSparse Graph Limits for Neural Combinatorial Optimization

SummaryThis paper studies which graph information must be preserved so that graph neural networks trained on small graphs can transfer normalized combinatorial optimization values to larger graphs. It establishes size-transfer guarantees using finite collections of energy features, with separate results for affine objectives and general Lipschitz objectives. The theory relies on Shapley-Folkman bounds and a fixed-weight message-passing network on bounded-treewidth graphs. Experiments with exact solvers and trained models test the resulting guarantees.

Thien Le, Melanie Weber

Label Smoothing in Disguise? Gated Knowledge Distillation for Low-Resource Bangla Generation

In briefLabel Smoothing in Disguise? Gated Knowledge Distillation for Low-Resource Bangla Generation

SummaryThis paper tests two gated knowledge distillation methods for Bangla text generation: EWAD, which weights tokens by teacher confidence, and CHAD, which weights examples by gradient alignment with validation data. Across six Bangla-centric tasks, three student models, and two teacher families, neither gate significantly beats plain distillation in 45 comparisons, and distillation fails to beat cross-entropy fine-tuning on gold outputs. The authors conclude that the main benefit of distillation is regularisation, which teacher-free label smoothing largely reproduces.

Dipto Sumit, Zobeida Zaman Chowdhury, Syeda Fabiayee Jahan et al.

SummaryThis paper examines how a language model's confidence changes over five rounds of self-training, using Qwen3-1.7B with three seeds per method. Adaptive Land Units from Earth Embeddings

In briefLand Units Built Adaptively from Earth Satellite Embeddings

SummaryThe paper clusters AlphaEarth Earth embeddings with parallel Bayesian hierarchical clustering to form adaptive land units that are large where land is uniform and small where it is heterogeneous, without using labels. Applied to Denmark, it reduces 402 million pixels to 1.27 million units, about 320 times fewer. Land-use models trained on these units stay within a few accuracy points of pixel-based models at a small fraction of the cost, and enable whole-country estimates in under a minute.

Albert Alonso, Jamal Jokar Arsanjani, Andres R. Masegosa

Infinite Worlds from One Example: What Local Generators Must Share

In briefInfinite Worlds from One Example: Sharing Randomness in Local Generators

SummaryThe paper studies local generators that create unbounded game worlds on demand, with every small window required to come from a single example, as in Wave Function Collapse. It shows that a generator applying one position-independent rule to independent randomness at each cell cannot be both exactly valid and faithful to the example's statistics when constraints vary across the example. It gives a finitely checkable condition for exact validity using seed-based row types and position modulo a period, and tests it on 52 public Wave Function Collapse examples and 27 from other implementations.

WhyThe authors show local generators need shared randomness, prove independent randomness cannot be both exactly valid and faithful, and give a matching 1/r lower bound.

Amin Najafgholizadeh, Mohsen Afsharchi

In briefEntropy-regularized reinforcement learning for a regime-switching consumption-investment problem

SummaryThis paper develops an entropy-regularized reinforcement learning framework for continuous-time consumption and investment decisions in a regime-switching market with general utility functions. It derives closed-form optimal exploratory strategies, showing the investment strategy is multivariate Gaussian and the consumption strategy is a Gibbs measure, and that classical deterministic decisions correspond to the mode of these distributions. Self-Normalized Inference from Riemannian Stochastic Gradient Paths

In briefSelf-normalized inference for constrained parameters using Riemannian stochastic gradient paths

SummaryThe paper studies building confidence regions for parameters estimated by Riemannian stochastic gradient paths on constrained manifolds. It represents retained iterates in a common random tangent space, so an unknown covariance factor cancels in a Brownian bridge pivot. Under regularity conditions, a joint expansion of the averaged estimator and integrated path controls nonlinear coordinate errors. Numerical studies report coverage competitive with plug-in Wald inference, without estimating the Hessian or residual density.

Xiaotian Chang, Qirui Hu, Yi Liu

Robust Pixel-wise Digital Image Correlation under Large Motions and Illumination Variations via a Global-to-Local Hybrid Neural Field

In briefRobust pixel-wise digital image correlation using a global-to-local hybrid neural field under large motion and lighting changes

SummaryThis paper presents a neural framework for digital image correlation (DIC), a method for measuring full-field displacement without contact. A first stage estimates the dominant rigid-body motion through rotation search, phase correlation, and masked refinement to provide a reliable starting point. A second stage models the remaining displacement with a hybrid Kolmogorov-Arnold network and multilayer perceptron, optimized with an illumination-robust structural loss. Simulated and real experiments report stable displacement recovery under noise, non-uniform deformation, large rotation, and illumination variation.
