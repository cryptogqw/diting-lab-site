WeightBench: Evaluating Language Models as Weight-Space Programmers

In briefWeightBench: A Benchmark for Language Models as Weight-Space Programmers

SummaryThis paper introduces WeightBench, a benchmark for testing how well large language models can directly work with the parameters of other neural networks, a practice called Weight-Space Programming. It covers 42 tasks across five areas: reading, fixing, and writing weights, recognizing impossible edits, and compressing networks without data. Results show that frontier models can read small networks and write working control policies, but struggle to patch language models, compress without data, and distinguish impossible edits from hard ones. Code, the task registry, the sandbox harness, and a live leaderboard are publicly released.

WhyThe authors introduce WeightBench, a new benchmark for weight-space programming with 42 tasks across five tracks, and report results for several frontier models.

Restoring Diversity in Distilled Image and Video Generators by Probing Activations

In briefRestoring sample diversity in distilled image and video generators by probing internal activations

SummaryDistilling diffusion models into few-step student models cuts sampling latency but causes severe diversity collapse, where samples from different seeds barely vary. The authors find that students keep total activation variance similar to teachers yet concentrate it into far fewer directions, a collapse localized to an intermediate block at the initial denoising step. They propose ReDiverse, with ReDiverse-FT, which fine-tunes a lightweight adapter to regress student activations onto the teacher's, and ReDiverse-TF, a training-free method that steers activations along precomputed teacher principal directions. Experiments on image and video distillation benchmarks show it restores diversity while preserving generation quality.

Imitation Bootstrapped Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning

In briefImitation-bootstrapped multi-agent reinforcement learning (IBMARL)

SummaryThis paper proposes IBMARL, a framework that extends imitation-bootstrapped reinforcement learning to cooperative multi-agent teams. Each agent receives action proposals from both an imitation policy and an RL policy, and an ensemble of centralized critics selects between them, with a gated penalty pulling the RL policy toward imitation where the imitation action is valued higher. The imitation policies are trained with round-robin behavior cloning, and after training each agent runs its RL policy alone. On four cooperative benchmarks, the authors report it surpasses its imitation policies 2.6 to 4.2 times faster than demonstration-based baselines on two tasks and exceeds the demonstrator on two of four.

Tony H. Zhang, Connor Mattson, Daniel S. Brown

In briefA mathematical theory of why finite information stays useful for infinitely many tasks

SummaryThis paper develops a mathematical theory of how a finite information representation can preserve decision value in an infinite environment. Its central concept, future-task information, is the minimum information retained to keep decision value relative to available evidence.
