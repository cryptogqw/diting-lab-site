LEAP: Learned Block-wise Evidence Retrieval for Long Audio-Video Perception

In briefLEAP: Learned Block-wise Evidence Retrieval for Long Audio-Video Question Answering

SummaryThis paper addresses hour-long audio-visual question answering, where context limits make encoding whole recordings impractical. LEAP splits a recording into fixed-duration blocks, scores candidate windows in each block with a lightweight localization pass, and answers from the top-ranked windows in one bounded pass. The authors report 4.5–16.8% gains over the Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B baseline across several AVQA benchmarks, and transfer to MiniCPM-o 4.5 with gains of 3.1–13.0% over its published results.

Juyi Lin, Zhiqiang Lao, Jiali Cui et al.

Reliability-Aware Checkpoint Selection for Domain Generalization

In briefReliability-Aware Checkpoint Selection for Domain Generalization: Reselecting Models Using Source Reliability Within an Accuracy Tolerance

SummaryThe paper argues that choosing checkpoints in domain generalization by source-validation accuracy alone may not yield reliable probabilities on unseen target domains. It proposes accuracy-constrained reliability selection (AC), which keeps checkpoints near the best source accuracy and ranks them by source-side NLL and class-wise calibration error, without using target data or extra training. Across 360 runs on OfficeHome and TerraIncognita, the reference rule reduced target calibration error and NLL relative to source-accuracy selection, with a small rise in mean target accuracy.

Jinshi Liu, Jiahao Li, Pan Liu et al.

ConflictGuide: AutoResearch Improves When Competing Behaviors Are Made Visible

In briefConflictGuide: Making Competing Model Behaviors Visible Improves AutoResearch

SummaryThis paper argues that LLM-based AutoResearch systems, which edit model code based only on a single task score, ignore trade-offs between competing model behaviors. The authors propose ConflictGuide, which identifies competing behaviors and designs probes to measure them, then uses a two-stage evolution that explores with task feedback and later steers edits toward conflict alleviation. Across five model families, it reduces task and conflict-related errors by up to 28% and 14% relative to scalar-only AutoResearch.

Binqian Xu, Qiran Zou, Xiangbo Shu et al.

TauIndianBankBench: Procedure-Aware, Action-Oriented Evaluation of Tool-Using Conversational Agents

In briefTauIndianBankBench: A Benchmark for Procedure-Aware Evaluation of Banking Chatbots

SummaryThis paper introduces TauIndianBankBench, a benchmark of 1,000 Indian retail-banking tasks in which an assistant helps a simulated customer. In addition to checking the final records, it verifies that the assistant performed every required procedural step and made no unrequested changes. When only final records are checked, 6.5% to 12.0% of sessions per model are judged successful even though the assistant skipped a required step or made an unrequested change. The authors argue that scoring both procedure and outcome gives a more accurate picture of language model performance in Indian retail banking.

Krishanu Adhikary, Prashant Devadiga

RoPE at the End of Its Rope? Theory, Diagnosis, and Mitigation of Long-Context Failures

In briefIs RoPE Reaching Its Limit? Theory, Diagnosis, and Fixes for Long-Context Failures

SummaryThis paper studies why language models using RoPE (rotary position embeddings) fail on long contexts, arguing that RoPE trades off stable token preferences against distinguishing nearby positions. The authors extend prior theory to allow unequal query-key scales across frequencies and introduce RoPE Profiler, a toolkit that reuses cached activations to produce semantic and positional diagnostic scores. Across 49 long-context task settings, reasoning tasks mainly suffered semantic reversal while retrieval tasks mainly suffered positional insensitivity. Targeted high-frequency rescaling, without additional training, improved accuracy by up to 20 points on Qwen3-8B and 25 points on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct.

Yuyang Wu, Yufeng Du, Hao Peng

AdaGEPA: Adaptive Feedback Allocation for Reflective Prompt Optimization

In briefAdaGEPA: Adaptive feedback allocation for improving AI prompts through reflection

SummaryThis paper introduces AdaGEPA, a method that adaptively chooses which examples to use as feedback when revising prompts for language models, based on the prompt's performance and task structure. In each feedback minibatch, it replaces at most one example to target an identified weakness. Across six downstream benchmarks under matched rollout budgets, AdaGEPA achieved higher mean validation scores than non-adaptive feedback selection. In an initial Schema-Guided Dialogue study, half-budget prompts outperformed full-budget baseline prompts on joint goal accuracy.

Junyang Chen, Zecheng Wang, Jingbang Chen

Super-Resolving Unseen Hyperspectral Sensors at Any Scale via Spatial Operators

In briefHyperspectral image super-resolution that generalizes to unseen sensors at any scale

SummaryThis paper proposes OmniHSR, a model for hyperspectral super-resolution that aims to handle new sensors and arbitrary upsampling scales with one network. Instead of predicting spectral values directly, it predicts band-shared spatial operators that are composed into a continuous field and applied to all bands. Trained only on ARAD with 0.538M parameters, it outperforms directly transferred baselines on six unseen datasets without target-domain data, and runs inference up to 36 times faster.

Ji-Xuan He, Guohang Zhuang, Bo Junge et al.

CoVisco: Codec-Native Vision Encoder with Native Token Compression for Unified Image-Video Understanding

In briefCoVisco: A Codec-Native Vision Encoder for Unified Image-Video Understanding

SummaryThis paper presents CoVisco, a vision encoder that works directly with codec-format inputs and compresses visual tokens natively during encoding, for unified image and video understanding. Long videos are split into temporal segments, each with learnable abstract tokens, and segmented attention with abstract-communication layers preserves video-level context. In the four-segment, 64-frame setting, abstract-only inference uses 400 visual tokens and reaches video understanding performance close to, and on some benchmarks above, OneVision-Encoder.

Yulong Liu, Xiaotian Han, Junyuan Shang et al.

MCD: Causal Distillation of Multimodal In-Context Learning in Large Vision-Language Models

In briefMCD: Causal Distillation of Multimodal In-Context Learning in Vision-Language Models

SummaryLarge vision-language models learn well from examples in context, but this ability weakens as models get smaller. The paper proposes Multimodal Causal Distillation (MCD), which uses structure-preserving token interventions to find causal evidence and transfers how a teacher responds when that evidence is kept or removed. Across three model families and seven benchmarks, MCD improves student performance by 7.23 points on average and beats vanilla distillation by 4.68 points.

Yanshu Li, Jiaqian Li, Canran Xiao et al.

Scores That Hold, Benchmarks That Leak: Measuring Dataset Contamination in Public Brain-Tumor MRI Classification

In briefStable Scores, Leaking Benchmarks: Measuring Contamination in Public Brain-Tumor MRI Datasets

SummaryThis paper examines whether public brain-tumor MRI classification datasets keep test data independent of training data. It proposes a three-layer contamination framework (duplicate, patient, and source-label leakage) and audits the three most-used corpora using a chest-radiograph control. The authors find severe leakage, including near-duplicate images across splits and patient overlap, and show that removing leaked test images leaves accuracy essentially unchanged. They conclude that stable accuracy alone does not show a model recognizes tumors, and they release contaminated-file lists and deduplicated splits.

NavHarness: Adaptive Goals for Agentic Vision-Language Navigation

In briefNavHarness: Adaptive Goals for Agentic Vision-Language Navigation

SummaryThis paper presents NavHarness, an agentic framework for vision-language navigation, where robots move according to instructions and observations. It uses four agents: a Goal Agent that sets adaptive goals, a Verify Agent that checks goal completion, a Memory Agent that compresses multimodal history, and a Visuomotor Agent that executes navigation actions. The authors evaluate it on R2R-CE and RxR-CE, and in real-world tests over eight challenging routes, each run three times, it reports 83.3% success and 1.51 m navigation error.

Haoxiang Shi, Zaijing Li, Muhe Ding et al.

TRACE: Trajectory Selection for Parallel Scaling of Search Agents

In briefTRACE: Choosing the Best Search Trajectory in Parallel Search Agents

SummaryThe paper introduces TRACE, a lightweight learned selector that ranks completed search-agent trajectories using the evidence behind their answers, instead of relying on majority voting. It links rollouts through shared content or document identity and propagates evidence across them, without extra search or generative aggregation. The authors report gains over voting on six WebQA policies and six long-horizon dataset-backbone combinations, and at least 10x higher throughput than compared aggregation methods.

WhyFrames parallel search consolidation as trajectory selection with cross-rollout evidence propagation, validated across multiple benchmarks against voting and generative aggregators.

DoGBench: Can Agents Meet Expert Standards for User-Facing Documentation?

In briefDoGBench: Can AI Agents Write Software Docs to Expert Standards?

SummaryThis paper introduces DoGBench, which the authors describe as the first benchmark for generating and maintaining real user-facing software documentation. It contains 292 items from open source projects such as Helm, PostHog, and Mautic, where agents must decide whether docs need updating and either produce an acceptable patch or abstain. The best-scoring of seven agents reached 47.3 out of 100 on a 117-item held-out split. An audit of failures highlights gaps such as describing interfaces without checking how readers use them and leaving other affected pages stale.

WhyA first benchmark for user-facing documentation generation, with 292 real open-source items and maintainer-validated rubrics, giving solid evidence and useful analysis.

Frances Liu, Manny Silva, Paige Calvert et al.

Understanding Parents' Complex Views of AI for Children's Pretend Play

In briefParents' Mixed Views on AI in Children's Pretend Play

SummaryThis study interviewed 10 U.S. parents of children aged 4-15 about AI supporting children's pretend play. Parents saw potential benefits, such as making role-based play accessible and helping them join family play, but also worried about lost imagination and creativity, emotional attachment to AI, and reduced human interaction. Transferable Graph Metanetworks

In briefTransferable Graph Metanetworks: Generalizing Across Neural Network Widths

SummaryA weight space network takes another neural network's weights as input and predicts its properties. The paper proposes Transferable Graph Metanetworks, modified to be invariant to different widths representing the same function and continuous so similar functions get similar predictions. These changes markedly improve size generalization on all tasks studied. Performance is strongest on networks trained with maximal-update parameterization, remaining robust up to 42 times the training width.

Do Better Goal Representations Improve Goal-Conditioned Reinforcement Learning?

In briefDo Better Goal Representations Improve Goal-Conditioned RL?

SummaryThe paper studies how much the quality of goal representations affects offline goal-conditioned reinforcement learning. The authors build an exact temporal-distance goal representation in deterministic mazes and deliberately degrade its geometry while keeping the learner fixed. On OGBench navigation tasks, large changes in goal quality barely affect performance, whereas the same interventions on the current state more than double success, identifying the state pathway as the main bottleneck.

Syed Nazmus Sakib, Abdul Monaf Chowdhury, Nafiul Haque et al.

Learning Where to Look: Anatomical Grounding and Guided Attention for Cardiac MRI Vision-Language Models

In briefLearning Where to Look: Guided Attention for Cardiac MRI Vision-Language Models

SummaryThis paper studies fine-grained visual question answering on cardiac MRI through anatomical grounding and guided attention. The authors built 128,915 anatomical-grounding and 42,799 clinical QA pairs across several CMR sequences. They propose Cardiac Anatomy-Routed Attention (CARA), which uses predicted anatomical priors to guide decoder attention, and combine it with grounding pretraining to form CARA-VL. Experiments report strengths in clinical assessment and regional localisation, with promising generalization to an external cohort.

Bangwei Guo, Xiao Chen, Boris Mailhe et al.
