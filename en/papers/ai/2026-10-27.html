Center-of-Mass Interpretation of Evolution Strategies

In briefEvolution strategies as moving toward a reward-weighted center of mass

SummaryThe paper offers a geometric view of evolution strategies (ES) updates, treating perturbed models as particles whose rewards act as masses. It shows that the mean-subtracted ES update exactly equals a displacement toward the reward-weighted center of mass, scaled by a factor depending on mean reward, for any finite population. In large populations or under antithetic sampling, the centroid lies on the model itself.

On the stability of learning

In briefOn the stability of learning: unstable when gradient signals arrive only intermittently

SummaryThis paper studies whether learning stays stable when gradient information is noisy and only intermittently available. The authors show that even if average learning dynamics converge to the correct optimum, learning variance can grow to infinity asymptotically. Using analytically tractable settings such as single-layer and multi-layer linear networks, they derive explicit conditions for stable learning that differ conceptually from existing stochastic gradient descent conditions. They conclude that unbiased credit assignment alone is insufficient, and sufficiently frequent and informative feedback is also needed.
