Large language models are vulnerable to incidental information in clinical documentation and reasoning

In briefLarge language models are vulnerable to stray details in clinical notes and reasoning

SummaryThis paper tests how sensitive large language models are to information from a patient visit that is not clinically relevant, in both note-writing and clinical reasoning. Across 576 patient-clinician dialogues, frontier models inserted small talk into 35% of notes, and in 3.7% of those notes they misattributed it or used it clinically. In 57 mock consultations, background speech from another encounter leaked into 48.2% of transcripts, contaminating 5.3% of downstream notes from four open-weight models. The authors propose a dual encoding hypothesis and recommend testing resistance to incidental information before clinical use.

SummaryThis paper studies power sampling, a training-free way to improve LLM reasoning by sampling from a sharpened version of a base model's output distribution. SummaryMachine learning can speed up molecular design, but when target molecules are very rare, existing algorithms perform little better than random guessing.
