Program Distillation: Transferring LLM Capability from System 2 to System 0

In briefProgram Distillation: Moving LLM Ability from System 2 to System 0

SummaryThis paper proposes Program Distillation, a framework in which an LLM supplies task knowledge and draft strategies during learning, and a student program executes the resulting policy at deployment. The authors introduce PDBench, a benchmark of 27 control tasks, and PDMetaHarness, a model-harness-independent system for monitoring learning. The strongest model reaches a normalized mean score of 0.728, above PPO at 0.656, while running on CPUs with lower inference cost.

Pujun Zheng, Wanying Ren, Guoxiu He

Predicting, Fast and Slow: SSD Already Knows Which Guess Is Wrong

In briefPredicting Slow SSD Reads: The Drive Already Knows Which Guesses Are Wrong

SummaryThe paper reports that Heimdall, a learned SSD controller, labels 52% of reads as slow when only 30% are actually slow. Nine uncertainty estimators perform no better than chance at finding these wrong guesses. The authors show that the drive's own recent read history identifies them better: an untrained 4,096-entry table reaches a median AUROC of 0.68 across 57 held-out traces, and also predicts slow reads better than Heimdall.

Philipp Bogdan

Text Speeding Itself Up: Recency and Restraint in Session-Local Drafting

In briefSpeeding Up Text by Copying Recent Matches Within a Session

SummaryThis paper studies speculative decoding with session-local drafters that predict upcoming tokens from the prompt and generated text, without training. It compares a recency-weighted, backing-off, confidence-gated session memory with two prompt lookup decoding variants, one copying the earliest match and one the most recent. On general, medical, and legal text, copying the most recent match provides the speedup, while the memory's weighting and backoff add no measurable benefit. On medical and legal text, drafters that abstain on weak evidence save energy per token.

Antal van den Bosch

Efficient and Accurate Causal Validation of Training Data Attribution

In briefMaking Training Data Attribution Checks Cheaper and Accurate

SummaryChecking whether training data attribution methods are causally valid normally requires retraining models on many subsets of the training data, which is expensive. This paper reduces the computation needed to estimate the Linear Datamodeling Score (LDS) by lowering the number of sampled subsets, retraining seeds per subset, and models in the attribution ensemble, while removing the bias each factor introduces. Experiments on ResNet-9/CIFAR-10, BERT/QNLI and GPT-2/WikiText-2 show the same estimation accuracy with fewer retrainings.

Simon Vary, Alireza Mousavi-Hosseini, Roger Grosse

CLEW: Log-Anchored Backward Tracing for Root-Cause Code Localization

In briefCLEW: Tracing Backward from Logs to Locate Root-Cause Code

SummaryThis paper introduces CLEW, an agentic method for locating root-cause code in industrial software failures. It combines rule-based anchoring of log events to code with LLM-guided backward tracing from symptom functions, returning ranked candidate functions, an affected module, and an explanation linked to log events. The authors built the LogLoc benchmark of 192 real issue and fault-injection cases, where CLEW achieved the highest reported Acc@1 and MRR on LogLoc-Issues and about 73% lower wall-clock time than LocAgent.

Wenji Bai, Muhammad Waseem, Jaakko Peltonen et al.

Architectures and KV Replay for Depth-Recurrent Language Models

In briefMemory-efficient KV caching for depth-recurrent language models

SummaryThe paper studies how to reduce the key-value cache memory that grows with recurrence depth in depth-recurrent language models during autoregressive decoding. It compares looped hybrid architectures with periodic attention anchors, a recurrent-workspace design in a Parcae-style setup, and a looped Transformer trained with recurrent KV replay, where only selected iterations write KV states. The findings are that looped hybrids need sufficiently frequent attention anchors and their performance depends strongly on the recurrent mixer. Recurrent KV replay lets several iterations share the same KV states while staying close to a full looped Transformer with separate caches per iteration.

Lucia Licakova, Niklas Schmidinger, Lukas Hauzenberger et al.
