Patch Attention Wisely: Understanding Patch Length in Attention-Based Forecasting

In briefChoosing patch length for attention-based forecasting, with a theory explaining why it matters

SummaryThis paper studies how to choose patch length in attention-based time series forecasters, where it has mostly been selected by trial and error. For linear state-space models, the authors derive an exact factorization of the forecasting coefficient matrix that separates forecast dynamics from filtering dynamics. Combined with estimation bounds, they propose a patch-selection criterion that balances representation and estimation errors under temporal dependence, and they state conditions under which balanced patching is justified.

Junyu Chen, Likai Chen, Weining Wang

Improving Context-Shift Robustness of Convolutional Networks via Context-Regularized Cross-Entropy

In briefMaking Convolutional Networks Less Reliant on Background Context Using a New Training Loss

SummaryThis paper proposes a training framework to make convolutional neural networks more robust when objects appear in unfamiliar backgrounds at test time. The authors first introduce ContrastiveCAMs, a feature attribution method that gives more faithful attribution maps than existing approaches. They then build on it with Context-Regularized Cross-Entropy (CR-CE), a loss that suppresses the influence of contextual regions. The method is evaluated on Waterbirds, Spawrious, ImageNet/ImageNet-BG and Hard-ImageNet, with consistent gains in context-shift robustness reported.

Jinen Setpal, Chaoyue Liu

La-JEPA: Compact Robot Policies with Latent World Modeling

In briefLa-JEPA: Compact Robot Control Policies Using Latent World Modeling

SummaryThe paper introduces La-JEPA, a compact world-action model whose policy transformer is trained from scratch in the feature space of a pretrained V-JEPA 2.1 encoder. It predicts future features alongside actions during training and generates only actions at inference. Among policies without embodied pretraining, it reports state-of-the-art results on RoboTwin 2.0 and LIBERO-Plus with far fewer parameters and faster inference than Fast-WAM.

Mateo Cl\'emente, George Z. Xue, Amin Abyaneh et al.

Interpretable Multimodal MRI-Clinical Fusion for Functional Outcome Prediction After Acute Ischemic Stroke

In briefInterpretable MRI-clinical data fusion for predicting stroke recovery after acute ischemic stroke

SummaryThis study examines whether day-1 multimodal MRI adds prognostic value for three-month functional outcome in acute ischemic stroke. Sixty-six patients' ADC, DWI, and FLAIR scans and clinical data were analyzed using pretrained ResNet features fused with clinical variables and evaluated with cross-validated linear support vector machines. FLAIR-clinical fusion performed best, followed by ADC-clinical fusion, suggesting compact imaging representations provide complementary prognostic information beyond clinical variables.

Sina Raeisadigh, Henning M\"uller, Abderrahmane Hedjoudje

A Generative Foundation Model for Multimodal Tokamak Tasks

In briefA reusable generative foundation model for multimodal tokamak tasks

SummaryTokamak experiments record waveforms, profiles, fields, and scalars used to reconstruct plasma, forecast its evolution, and estimate disruption risk. The authors extend the TokaMark benchmark to 20 MAST tasks in six groups and introduce GenToka, a generative model that jointly generates schema-selected targets using a shared conditional diffusion Transformer with signal-specific codecs. On MAST measurements, GenToka lowers macro-average normalized RMSE relative to TokaMind by 4.7% on Groups 1-4 and 17.7% on Group 5, and raises Group 6 macro-average window-level F1 by 8.4%.

Yingshuai Ji, Haodong Feng, Guohong Deng et al.

Detecting Weather Fronts from Satellite Observations: What Does Reanalysis Add?

In briefUsing satellite data to detect weather fronts: what reanalysis data adds

SummaryThis paper studies machine learning methods that detect weather fronts, the boundaries between air masses with different thermodynamic properties, pixel by pixel from geostationary infrared satellite observations. The authors compare a satellite-only model with one that also uses 28 ERA5 reanalysis fields, tested over the North Atlantic and Europe. The satellite-only model reaches a 150 km tolerance CSI of 0.402, rising to 0.575 with ERA5. Low-level winds are the hardest to predict from satellite data, and removing winds or moisture causes the largest drop in detection performance.

Ioannis Dravilas, Miruna Stoicescu, Roope Tervo et al.

MCUFlowNet: Hardware-Aware Neural Architecture Search for Optical Flow on Microcontrollers

In briefMCUFlowNet: Hardware-Aware Architecture Search for Optical Flow on Microcontrollers

SummaryThis paper designs dense optical flow networks that run on microcontrollers with neural accelerators, aiming to help small drones avoid obstacles. The authors propose PACE-NAS, a search framework that fixes the backbone and peak SRAM first, then searches operators and depths to trade accuracy against latency. The fastest resulting model, MCUFlowNet-S, runs 1.76x faster than a retrained EdgeFlowNet with 1.6% higher EPE, and reaches the goal without collision in 51.5% of simulated forest flights versus 41.8%.

Enmin Lin, Jona Beysens

Detecting Silent Faults at the Environment-Trainer Boundary in Reinforcement Learning

In briefCatching silent faults where reinforcement learning environments meet the trainer

SummaryThe paper studies silent faults that occur when a robot reinforcement learning environment runs in a separate process, machine, or language from the trainer. The authors injected faults into the data crossing this boundary on Pendulum, HalfCheetah, Hopper and a 7-joint arm (Pusher). Learning-curve thresholds and comparisons with fault-free seeds caught very few of them. A step ledger kept on both sides of the boundary and compared step by step caught and located the faults.

Zuo Gou, Chenhao Lu

Fault-Guided Cross-Layer Prediction for Unsupervised Anomaly Detection

In briefCross-Layer Prediction Method for Unsupervised Industrial Defect Detection

SummaryThis paper proposes a single-class anomaly detector for industrial images that is trained only on normal images from one product category. It treats the layers of a frozen vision transformer as a sequence of transitions, learns token-wise hidden-state prediction between blocks, and uses adjacent-layer cosine similarity to identify fault lines that set source and target roles. Describe Your Facial Expressions by Linking Image Encoders and Large Language Models

In briefLinking image encoders with large language models to describe facial expressions in words

SummaryThis paper introduces a new task of describing facial expressions in natural language, capturing nuances beyond emotion categories or facial action units (AUs). The authors propose a three-stage training framework that trains a vision-to-language model using synthesized image-text pairs and BLIP-2 pre-training techniques. Captions are synthesized with GPT-3.5 from existing emotion or AU annotations to overcome the lack of training data. Experiments show the method generates captions describing facial actions, emotions, and their relationship, and that the approach also improves visual features for AU detection and emotion classification.

How Far Can Untrained Neural Networks Go in Anomaly Detection? From Tables to Pixels

In briefHow Far Can Untrained Neural Networks Go in Anomaly Detection? From Tables to Pixels

SummaryThis paper proposes PRISM, which combines fixed random nonlinear features with Mahalanobis scoring against normal references for tabular anomaly detection and visual localization. On 47 ADBench tabular datasets, PRISM-T achieves the highest mean AUROC (86.4%). On MVTec AD and VisA across 27 visual categories, PRISM-V reaches mean image-level and pixel-level AUROCs of 90.4% and 96.4%. Both models work without neural weight optimization or pretrained representations.

Hyojun Yi, Geunyoung Kim, Hogeon Seo et al.

Who Can Evaluate AI Independently? A Framework for Analysing Evaluation Infrastructure

In briefWho Can Evaluate AI Independently? A Framework for Analysing Evaluation Infrastructure

SummaryThis paper proposes PAAI (Power, Access, Adequacy, Independence), a framework for examining who controls the infrastructure behind AI evaluations, on what terms it is available, and how much autonomy it allows. The authors apply it to twelve African AI evaluation projects from 2022 to 2025 and to the roles of member institutes in three joint testing exercises of the international AI Safety Institutes network. They conclude that participating in an evaluation does not by itself confer evaluative authority, and that credible evaluation requires control over priorities, methods, publication and continuity.

Sumaya Nur Adan, Joanna Wiaterek, Nourhan Abdelrhim et al.

Beyond K/V Caches: Cross-Model State Handoff in Hybrid Linear-Attention LLMs

In briefBeyond K/V Caches: Transferring Model State Between Hybrid Linear-Attention LLMs

SummaryThis paper addresses the cost of switching between language models during long conversations, where the new model must re-process the accumulated context. Hybrid linear-attention models keep K/V caches along with recurrent states and convolution histories, which interact and complicate transfer between models. The authors propose Hybrid State Transfer (HST), which handles all three state types, uses structured recurrent maps, and calibrates adapters against the frozen target model's continuation outputs. On five benchmarks, transfers from Qwen3.5 4B and 9B to 27B retain 89.88% and 94.11% of task performance on average, and handoff speedups on synthetic 16K inputs reach about 21.66x and 21.91x.

Jiacheng Cai, Tao Zhang, Siqi Liang et al.
