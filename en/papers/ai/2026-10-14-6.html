Backpropagating Verifier Rewards Through Continuous Flow Language Models

In briefBackpropagating verifier rewards through continuous flow language models, a reinforcement learning method for models that generate continuous token embeddings.

SummaryThe paper proposes a reinforcement learning post-training framework for continuous flow language models, which denoise token embeddings over several steps and decode to discrete tokens only at the end. A critic estimates the value of intermediate embeddings, bypassing the non-differentiable decoding step so that low-variance pathwise gradients can be backpropagated. Across four reasoning tasks, the method reportedly improves the model and outperforms baselines of the same type.

Theoretical and Practical Advances in Hierarchical Bayesian Deep Learning Methodology

In briefImproved Hierarchical Bayesian Deep Learning for Uncertainty Estimates

SummaryThis paper introduces MLPMMH, a multilevel sampling method that jointly infers neural network parameters and prior hyperparameters to quantify uncertainty. The approach combines pseudo-marginal inference for hyperparameters with coupled sequential Monte Carlo across a hierarchy of network-width truncations. The authors prove a mean-square error and computational cost rate under standard multilevel coupling assumptions and verify it on a Bayesian regression problem. They also propose S-MLPMMH, a practical approximation using a PCA-based hierarchy, which shows competitive results on MNIST.

What Information Buys in Active Inference

In briefWhat Information Buys in Active Inference: When Calibrated Information Gain Beats Confidence Widths

SummaryThis paper compares two exploration methods, Active Inference (AIF) and Bayes-UCBVI, on tabular MDPs. It finds that AIF's information gain decays too quickly, causing greedy agents to miss optimal actions, and that square-root calibration yields regret of order √T for an exact abstraction. With fixed rewards, calibrated AIF offers no better guarantee than Bayes-UCBVI, but when an adversary chooses rewards from the agent's data, calibrated information gain acts as a reward-agnostic ℓ1 confidence set and is preferable.
