BRAT: Bounded Residual-Aligned Training for Stable Multi-Pass LLM Post-Training

In briefBRAT: A stable training method for reusing rollouts across multiple gradient passes in LLM post-training

SummaryThis paper studies the instability that arises when reinforcement learning for LLM post-training reuses sampled rollouts for several gradient passes. The authors propose Bounded Residual-Aligned Training (BRAT), which gives each token a gradient weight that decreases linearly with its probability ratio and vanishes at a trust-region boundary, and normalizes group-centered rewards by their maximum magnitude. With four reuse passes, the authors report that BRAT avoids training collapse, limits policy drift, and achieves higher average accuracy with lower seed variance on most benchmarks.

Xunlan Zhou, Yuzhe Zhao, Xuhong Huang et al.

In briefOnline imitation learning to stabilize Vlasov–Poisson plasmas

SummaryThis paper studies how to control instability in Vlasov–Poisson plasma systems, a core challenge for nuclear fusion control. Because plasma states are only partially observable in practice, the authors train imitation learning algorithms on a fully observable expert controller while operating only with macroscopic measurements. They show theoretically that online imitation learning avoids the exponential error compounding of offline behavior cloning, where even small errors amplify instability over time, and can instead polynomially control rollout stability. SummaryThis paper introduces TRUST, a framework that lets AI agents improve by refining their own harnesses without retraining the underlying language models. Proximal Preference Optimization: A Unified Framework for Stable and Reward-Free Alignment

In briefProximal Preference Optimization: A stable, reward-model-free method for aligning AI with human preferences

SummaryThis paper proposes a framework that combines the implicit reward approach of Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) with the trust-region stability of Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO). It introduces P²O, which adds a clipped importance ratio to the DPO margin and needs no reward model, and PKTO, which builds on P²O using KTO's prospect-theoretic utility margins, an adaptive baseline and asymmetric weighting. The authors provide theoretical analysis covering KL trust-region bounds, a policy-improvement lower bound and convergence guarantees. Experiments on several LLMs and three preference benchmarks report alignment quality matching or exceeding DPO and KTO, with better stability under repeated dataset reuse.

Qisai Liu, Zhanhong Jiang, Zahid Hasan et al.

Same Measurements, Different Velocity Risk: Complementary Rolling-Shutter Readouts under Clock Uncertainty

In briefSame measurements, different velocity risk: complementary camera readouts under timing uncertainty

SummaryThe paper studies how two cameras with rolling shutters estimate velocity when exposure timing is uncertain. Repeated readouts have a worst-case velocity risk that shrinks only on the order of the noise level, while complementary readouts achieve minimax risk on the order of the noise variance. Mounting configurations where both designs are regular are identified, and a risk floor caused by metric uncertainty is quantified.

Universal Approximation by Equivariant Message-Passing Graph Neural Networks with a Bilinear Readout

In briefUniversal approximation proof for a graph neural network interatomic potential

SummaryThis paper proves that the bilinear-readout equivariant message-passing network (BREMP), a reference architecture for equivariant graph neural network interatomic potentials, can universally approximate atomic energies. It constructs a one-layer feature that determines the local atomic environment up to permutations of same-type atoms, and shows the readout approximates any continuous invariant function of these features. The authors also state that DPA4, an architecture in production use, inherits this result by approximating the BREMP layers constructed in the proof.

Pingbing Ming, Han Wang
