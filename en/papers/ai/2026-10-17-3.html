Probing Ambiguity: Adversarial Intent Resolution for Robust Text-to-SQL

In briefProbing Ambiguity: Adversarial Intent Resolution for Robust Text-to-SQL

SummaryThe paper notes that text-to-SQL systems usually assume each question maps to one correct SQL query, yet many questions are ambiguous. The authors propose AIR, a multi-agent framework that requires no task-specific training, which builds competing interpretations and checks through SQL execution whether they yield different results. It then generates clarification questions grounded in the observed data differences. On the AMBROSIA benchmark with schema-only context, AIR outperforms the reinforcement learning model IntentRL across all reported metrics.

CoCoNet: Convex Conformal Network for Multi-Dimensional Uncertainty Regions

In briefCoCoNet: Learned Convex Scores for Multi-Dimensional Uncertainty Regions

SummaryThis paper presents CoCoNet, a method that learns convex conformity scores to produce flexible, data-adaptive prediction regions for multivariate regression. The score is learned on held-out data by optimizing region efficiency while enforcing convexity in the residual, followed by split-conformal calibration for finite-sample coverage. Two variants are studied, CoCoNet_Spline and CoCoNet_ICNN. Across 8 models and 30 multi-dimensional regression tasks, CoCoNet_ICNN and CoCoNet_Spline-ℓ1 achieve average ranks of 1.45 and 2.75, with CoCoNet_ICNN ranking first in efficiency on 22 of the 30 tasks.

In briefDistillation and self-training for chemical reaction prediction, and why accurate answers aren't robust reasoning

SummaryThe paper introduces Resonance, open-weight models that distill reasoning traces from a frontier teacher and then self-train on their own verified outputs to predict forward chemical reaction outcomes. The small base model improves from near-zero accuracy to a capable predictor, and a stronger student surpasses its teacher through distillation alone. The authors argue that accuracy does not imply robust reasoning: students inherit the teacher's single-pass style, self-training mainly sharpens answers already reachable, and students readily abandon correct answers under pushback.
