Social Priors: Descriptions of AI Users Shape How Language Models Trust and Cooperate

In briefSocial Priors: How Descriptions of AI Users Shape Model Trust and Cooperation

SummaryThe study finetunes Qwen3-32B on four corpora built from the same 1,625 usage scenarios, differing only in the motive attributed to users, such as helping others or exploiting them. Finetuning changes the models' beliefs about users and their judgments of world safety, and these shifts appear in behavior. Antisocial models expect less reciprocity and send less money in trust games, and antisocial training also raises dark-triad scores and lowers alignment ratings.

UniMetric: Universal Metric Scale Recovery for Visual Geometry Foundation Models

In briefUniMetric: Recovering Real-World Scale for Visual Geometry Models

SummaryThis paper presents UniMetric, a training-free method that gives real physical scale to reconstructions from visual geometry foundation models, without extra sensors. It uses height ranges of familiar objects estimated by a vision-language model, compares them with reconstructed object heights, and solves a convex problem to find one scale for the whole sequence. The authors also introduce MetricBench, a benchmark with sensor-based ground truth, where the method improves depth, reconstruction, and trajectory errors across nine models.

Lei Yang, Yuner Zhang, Ziyang Liu et al.

UA-Bench: Benchmarking Large Audio-Language Models on Underwater Acoustic Recognition

In briefUA-Bench: A Benchmark for Large Audio-Language Models on Underwater Acoustic Recognition

SummaryThis paper introduces UA-Bench, a large-scale benchmark of 375,051 synthetic underwater audio records for testing how well large audio-language models recognize underwater acoustic signals. The records are generated from parameterized sources and modeled propagation, each with a three-level label hierarchy, dialogue, and metadata. Nine open models without task-specific adaptation reach only 2.0%–13.1% leaf accuracy, below the 7.7% random reference. The audio waveform helps source routing much more than fine-grained leaf recognition.

Zhe Sun, Liang An, Jiayu Yao et al.

KNN Implementation Details Can Dramatically Change Performance: An Example from Cover Trees

In briefImplementation details can dramatically change KNN performance: a Cover Tree case study

SummaryThis paper argues that implementation details of nearest neighbor search algorithms are rarely reviewed, yet they can materially change performance and conclusions about which algorithm is superior. Using the Cover Tree as an example, the authors built RustKNN, a unified Rust implementation of three construction variants and three query modes. They find the best query mode depends on k and the dataset, and choosing the wrong one can cost up to a 7.5× runtime factor.

Amol Khanna, Edward Raff

Making Tensor Decoupling More Robust and Less Parametric

In briefMaking tensor decoupling more robust and less parametric

SummaryTensor decoupling methods represent multivariate functions compactly by combining linear factor matrices with univariate nonlinearities. The authors note that current methods are sensitive to mismatched output or gradient magnitudes and require manual tuning of spline degrees of freedom. They propose rescaling the input Jacobian tensor as preprocessing and replacing B-splines with penalized splines tuned by generalized cross-validation, and release an open-source JAX implementation.

Maxime Rochkoulets, Joppe De Jonghe, Mariya Ishteva

Estimating Uncertainty of Omnimodal Large Language Models via External Omnimodal Embeddings

In briefEstimating Uncertainty in Omnimodal LLMs Using External Embeddings

SummaryThis paper proposes a model-agnostic framework for quantifying uncertainty in omni-modal large language models, which accept multiple input modalities. It scores chain-of-thought responses using only external omni-modal embeddings, measuring how well the reasoning is grounded in the multimodal input and its internal coherence. On UNO-Bench, the method yields small gains over self-consistency and is competitive with other baselines, especially on open-ended omni-modal questions.

WeFair: Fidelity-Aware Alignment from Human Feedback for Personalized Query Rewriting in Short-Video Search

In briefWeFair: Fidelity-aware human-feedback alignment for personalized query rewriting in short-video search

SummaryThis paper proposes WeFair, a method for personalized query rewriting in WeChat short-video search. It mines real user reformulations from search sessions, filters unsupported transitions with LLM screening and contextual verification, and trains the rewriter through supervised curriculum learning, direct preference optimization, and GRPO with a strict verification reward. Online A/B testing reports a 1.576% relative increase in effective clicked-query rate (VV>10s) and a 1.731% relative reduction in query reformulation rate.

Yingtao Ren, Aolin Li, Cheng Cheng et al.

Comparing GenAI to Machine Learning and Manual Review for eDiscovery

In briefComparing generative AI, machine learning, and manual review for eDiscovery

SummaryThis paper examines a Redgrave LLP case study that reported 88% recall for Relativity's generative AI tool aiR and 64% for its machine-learning tool RAL. The authors argue the two results are not directly comparable due to differing objectives, inter-assessor disagreement, and different recall-precision and cost-benefit trade-offs. They conclude that, once these differences are considered, aiR and manual review are comparable. They also report that established machine-learning models using Continuous Active Learning and Scalable Continuous Active Learning perform at state-of-the-art levels when trained on only 2,000 first-pass reviewer responsiveness determinations.

Gordon V. Cormack, Maura R. Grossman

Minimax Optimality of Shampoo for Nonstationary Stochastic Optimization

In briefProving Shampoo is minimax optimal for tracking moving optima in nonstationary optimization

SummaryThis paper theoretically analyzes the Matrix Shampoo optimizer when the optimum it must track changes over time. It derives finite-horizon high-probability error bounds that account for momentum, noise, and adaptive preconditioning. Under a squared path-variation constraint, the authors prove a matching minimax lower bound, showing Shampoo is information-theoretically optimal among causal procedures. The theoretical findings are validated on synthetic and real-world problems.

Abir Sarkar, Nirmalya Mandal, Martin T. Wells

Time Window Optimization for Motor Imagery Classification via Temporal Structure Embedded Reinforcement Learning

In briefTime window optimization for motor imagery brain-computer interface classification using structure-aware reinforcement learning

SummaryMotor imagery brain-computer interfaces usually decode EEG with fixed sliding windows, which may not align with neural events. This paper proposes TSE-RL, a reinforcement learning framework that embeds knowledge of motor imagery event structure, assigning each window a Discard, Onset, Sustain, or Offset role. A finite-state machine masks invalid actions to preserve temporal order, and a Transformer-based history encoder models dependencies across windows. The authors report consistent decoding improvements in cross-session and cross-subject settings on multiple benchmark datasets.

Huan Luo, Na Lu, Xiaopeng Wang et al.
