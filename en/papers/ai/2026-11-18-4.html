Measuring the Conservatism of Latent Safety Filters for World Models

In briefMeasuring how conservative latent safety filters are for world models

SummaryA latent safety filter uses a world model's latent representation to decide whether a planned action is safe, with conformal calibration setting its threshold so unsafe actions are accepted only with small probability. The paper defines an "overlap" measure of the conservatism a latent forces on the filter, grouping similar latents and actions into cells and computing the share of safe transitions lying in cells that contain unsafe ones. It proves the overlap is the minimum share of safe transitions such a filter must refuse to accept no unsafe transition in the data. In a visual navigation task, the separate-encoder design had the lowest overlap, refused the fewest safe actions, and let the planner reach the most goals among three compared designs.
