LatentTriad: Pre-Action Risk Representations for Malicious Agent Skill Detection

In briefLatentTriad: Pre-action risk representations for detecting malicious agent skills

SummaryLarge language model agents are increasingly extended with reusable skills, but untrusted skill content can trigger operations beyond the user's task. The authors find that hidden states formed during prefill contain readable information about whether a task and its operations are consistent. They introduce LatentTriad, which combines contextual boundary features, group-adjusted operation directions, and departures from benign state relationships. Detection runs on a single shared prefill without generating output tokens, invoking tools, or executing skill code.

Zhe Yu, Yi Liu, Ying Zhang et al.

Sparse Anchors for Visual Multi-Session Registration in Changing Environments

In briefSparse Anchors for Aligning Repeated Robot Recordings in Changing Environments

SummaryThe paper addresses aligning a new robot recording of an environment to a previously built reference map, even when the scene has changed. The method gathers absolute pose estimates via place recognition, feature matching and geometric checks, keeps the consistent ones as anchors, and combines them with within-session relative poses in a robust pose-graph optimization. On 15 cross-session experiments from the HILTI26 construction-site dataset, it reaches 94.4% recall within 1 m and 100.0% within 5 m, outperforming independent image localization and direct registration of reconstructed geometry.

Yunus Talha Erzurumlu, Niklas Trekel, Saurabh Gupta et al.

Manifold Embedding of Deep Image Features for Image Matching via Neural Adjoint Maps

In briefImage matching method using deep feature manifold embedding and neural adjoint maps

SummaryThis paper addresses finding semantic correspondences between different object instances of similar categories in real-world images. It adapts functional maps, used for 3D shape matching, by deriving a discrete Laplace-Beltrami operator from a manifold embedding of deep image features via Galerkin's method, giving image grids a meaningful geometric structure. A neural adjoint map is added to represent non-linear mappings. The authors report state-of-the-art results among zero-shot image correspondence methods on multiple dense correspondence benchmarks.

Nafie El Amrani, Moritz Heep, Florian Bernard

CRONOS: Benchmarking Non-Episodic Reinforcement Learning for Robotic Manipulation

In briefCRONOS: A benchmark for reinforcement learning on robot manipulation without episode resets

SummaryThis paper introduces CRONOS, a simulation benchmark for fine-tuning vision-language-action (VLA) robot models with reinforcement learning when scenes are not reset between episodes. The authors find that naively fine-tuning pre-trained policies fails in this setting because of shifts in object configurations and end-effector poses. They report that targeted reset allocation and addressing biases in pre-trained models can mitigate these problems. Non-episodic training is also found to improve long-horizon manipulation and generalization to unseen object configurations and task sequences.

Po-Yi Wu, Djengo Cyun-Jyun Fang, Dian Cheng et al.

In briefRollout-free world model for scoring self-driving trajectories

VIGOR: Visual Gain Ordering for Hallucination Mitigation in Multimodal Discrete Diffusion Language Models

In briefVIGOR: Visual Gain Ordering to Reduce Hallucination in Diffusion-Based Multimodal Models

SummaryMultimodal discrete diffusion language models generate answers by iteratively predicting masked tokens, but they can still hallucinate content that fits language patterns yet lacks support in the image. The paper proposes VIGOR, a training-free, plug-and-play inference method that reorders which masked positions to fill next, based on how much each prediction depends on visual evidence. It compares token confidence with the original image against a pass where the visual input is ablated. The authors report that VIGOR reduces hallucination and improves multimodal reasoning on LLaDA-V and Lumina-DiMOO.

Junzhe Chen, Tian Qin, Eugenie Shi et al.

Effects of Parton Pair Correlations on Direct-Photon Yields and Elliptic Flow in Heavy-Ion Collisions

In briefHow correlations between emitting partons affect direct-photon yields and elliptic flow in heavy-ion collisions

SummaryThis paper studies how correlations between partons that emit photons affect the yield and elliptic flow of direct photons in heavy-ion collisions. The authors show that the local 2→2 photon rate depends on the joint distribution of incoming partons, so a correlation can change photon emission without altering single-particle distributions. Folded over the evolving medium in Au+Au and Pb+Pb collisions, the correlation raises the photon yield toward fitted PHENIX and ALICE data and increases v2 mainly at intermediate transverse momentum.

Xingjian Lu, Shuzhe Shi

Positional Encoding is All You Need for Scalable Equivariance Constraint Relaxation

In briefPositional encoding to relax equivariance constraints during training, more efficiently and scalably

SummaryThe paper addresses optimization difficulties in strictly equivariant neural networks, which can struggle when data only approximately follow a symmetry. Prior work relaxed equivariance during training with non-equivariant dense layers, but at significant computational cost. The authors propose injecting a positional encoding with learnable scale to break the symmetry, gradually annealing it to zero so the original equivariant model is recovered by the end of training. They report significant gains for translational and rotational equivariant models with only marginal compute and memory overhead.

Hagay Michaeli, Haggai Maron, Daniel Soudry

ALBI: LLM-Guided Inductive Logic Programming via Iterative Generate-Test-Constrain Refineme

In briefALBI: LLM-guided inductive logic programming through iterative generate-test-constrain refinement

SummaryThe paper introduces ALBI, a framework that replaces the ASP-based hypothesis generator in Learning from Failures with a large language model while keeping SWI-Prolog as a formal verifier. It runs an iterative loop in which the LLM proposes candidate hypotheses, Prolog tests them, and diagnostic feedback guides refinement. Whose Effect Is It? How Objectives Inflate Learned Subspace Interventions

In briefWhose Effect Is It? How Objectives Inflate Learned Subspace Interventions

SummaryThe paper examines activation patching, a method that copies part of a language model's internal state between inputs to locate concepts by measuring output change. The authors prove that, to first order, the effects of orthogonal parts sum to the whole state's effect, so a learned part exceeds the whole only when the remaining components push the output back. Objectives that reward larger output effects select such parts, and changing the objective to target the network's own output removes most of the excess.

Toygar Tanyel, Serkan Ayvaz

DRAP: Dynamics-Risk Adaptive Plasticity for Continual Learning in Physical AI

In briefDRAP: A Replay-Free Method for Continual Learning in Physical AI

SummaryThis paper studies continual learning for physical AI, where small per-step policy errors can accumulate along a trajectory and cause closed-loop task failure. It proposes DRAP, a replay-free method that estimates each adaptable tensor's historical importance from action gradients weighted by local trajectory-amplification risk, and uses this to reduce plasticity in components that supported earlier behavior. Applied to a fixed subnetwork of OpenVLA and tested on the LIBERO Spatial, Object, and Goal suites, DRAP is reported to retain previously learned behaviors substantially better than standard AdamW fine-tuning and EWC while still acquiring new suites well.

Oswaldo Ludwig

Finite Spectral Depth Controls Gaussian Entrance Rank and Avoidance of the First Gradient-Flow Saddle

In briefHow finite spectral depth affects early rank selection and bypassing the first gradient-flow saddle in matrix factorization training

SummaryThis paper studies training dynamics of matrix factorization, a simple model of neural network training, under small initialization. It analyzes continuous-time dynamics that applies a finite number of Newton-Schulz iterations to the normalized matrix gradient (Muon). The authors show that the number of iterations can change the rank selected early in training and let trajectories bypass the first nonzero stationary point that gradient flow approaches. Under an additional residual-inertia condition, they prove bypass occurs for all sufficiently large finite depths, give an explicit sufficient threshold, and construct an example where the threshold is four.

Yanlin Long
