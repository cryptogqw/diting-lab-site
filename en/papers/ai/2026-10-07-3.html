Optimal Regret for Online Market Making with Limit Order Book

In briefOptimal regret bounds for online market making with limit order book feedback

SummaryThe paper studies online market making, where a market maker posts bid and ask prices before seeing the market price and a trader's private valuation, which is revealed only when no transaction occurs. It improves a prior expected regret bound of O~(T^{2/3}) to a high-probability bound of O~(√T). The authors first achieve this under full feedback using two coupled grids with Hedge, then extend it to limit order book feedback. They also show that when both prices and valuations are adversarial, sublinear regret is impossible even with full feedback.

The Silhouette Operator: Identifiability of Low-Rank Measures from One-Dimensional Projections

In briefSilhouette Operator: Recovering Low-Rank Measures from 1D Projections

SummaryThis paper develops a recovery framework for two-dimensional low-rank signed measures, which are finite sums of product measures with one-dimensional factors, using linear "silhouette operators" that map a measure to one-dimensional projected marginals. The authors show that 2k suitably chosen projected marginals identify every compactly supported signed measure of rank at most k, that this number is optimal, and that projection directions cannot be chosen arbitrarily. They also give conditions under which pairwise marginals identify higher-dimensional models and introduce silhouette mixture estimation (SME), which matches projected marginals to empirical ones in Wasserstein distance. Combined with one-dimensional density estimators, SME is reported to perform well against several baselines at moderate dimension and sample size.

In briefShared or structured inputs can make reasoning AI agents' collective random choices biased.

SummaryThe paper tests six reasoning models for threshold and divisibility rules in identifier-based random choices. The authors argue that selection rates alone miss these vulnerabilities, so input-dependent bias, correlation and predictability should be central targets in agent evaluation.

On-Policy Distillation Teaches New Skills but Not New Knowledge

In briefOn-policy distillation teaches models new reasoning skills, not new facts

SummaryThis paper examines whether on-policy distillation (OPD) lets language models acquire new factual knowledge or multi-step reasoning skills. Using a controlled synthetic framework, the authors separate these two capabilities across four models and also test factual QA and competition mathematics. Reverse-KL OPD transfers compositional reasoning skill to unseen structures but transfers little factual knowledge, while switching to forward KL restores factual transfer. The authors conclude that OPD does not expand a model's parametric knowledge but improves how it organizes and composes existing knowledge.
