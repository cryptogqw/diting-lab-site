PRISM-Net: Dynamic Routing Network with VLM-Guided Disentangled Attribute Distillation for Explainable Fine-Grained Product Retrieval

In briefPRISM-Net: A Routing Network That Separates Line and Color Features for Explainable Product Image Search

SummaryThe paper presents PRISM-Net, a multi-expert network for fine-grained e-commerce product retrieval that splits visual features into line (structure) and color expert pathways. A meta-router adaptively fuses these with base features, and the experts are supervised by zero-shot text prompts from SigLIP, avoiding manual attribute labels. Across 5-fold cross-validation on 275,454 images, product mAP@10 on SwinV2 rises from 80.98% to 83.90%.

Chung-Ming Lo, Che-Wei Chang

Compositional Self-Improvement: Promises and Pitfalls

In briefCompositional Self-Improvement: Promises and Pitfalls

SummaryThis paper examines whether models can use compositional structure to create training labels for harder problems by combining their predictions on simpler subproblems. Controlled experiments identify three failure modes: unreliable subproblem predictions, errors introduced by composition, and limited supervision coverage. In more realistic coding and reasoning tasks, composed labels are more accurate than direct self-generated labels and improve performance even on longer compositions than seen in training, though the same failure modes remain visible.

Changho Shin, Daiwei Chen, Brenden Lake et al.

From Lookups to Lessons: Turning Retrieved Knowledge into Parametric Memory

In briefFrom Lookups to Lessons: Turning Retrieved Knowledge into Parametric Memory

SummaryThis paper introduces ResMem, which has a language model read retrieved text offline to produce teacher targets, then trains a parametric memory to learn an additive logit residual that aligns a same-backbone student with the teacher. The method avoids storing large token representations, unlike kNN-datastore memory approaches. On a Wikipedia subset it cuts the storage retained for teacher construction by 94.7% compared with the evaluated kNN pipeline. Across five question answering datasets, mean token F1 rises from 32.83% for the frozen LLM to 34.48%, and it breaks 21% fewer correct answers than a kNN-trained memory.

Changjiang Han, Bowei He, Ye Yuan et al.

SpBench: An Agent-Native Framework for Cross-Engine Lakehouse Benchmarking

In briefSpBench: An Agent-Native Framework for Benchmarking Lakehouse Engines Across Platforms

SummarySpBench is a framework for benchmarking lakehouse data engines across platforms, and it is already in production use for Snowpark and Snowpark Connect for Apache Spark. It runs standardized suites such as TPCx-BB and TPC-DI alongside custom benchmarks drawn from production workloads, detecting performance regressions on a continuous basis. The paper introduces a typed, code-generated benchmark substrate, a structured telemetry contract that lets LLM agents query results, and an agent-native harness of three composable skills. The authors report that this architecture enabled over 400 benchmark queries and that regressions it caught led to production improvements.

Lyndon Shi, Nic Crouch, Yi Hu et al.

arXiv cs.LG · cs.AI · cs.NAOct 31, 2026 #

Learning to evolve by matching velocities

In briefLearning dynamics by matching velocities: a simulation-free training method for nonlinear systems

SummaryThis paper studies how to learn the continuous-time evolution of nonlinear dynamical systems. The authors propose Velocity Matching (VM), a training objective that avoids simulating trajectories and backpropagating through numerical solvers. They develop stability estimates that control trajectory errors through velocity errors. In numerical experiments on nonlinear ODEs and semi-discretized time-dependent PDEs, VM is reported to be more accurate than trajectory-based objectives.

WhyThe authors propose simulation-free Velocity Matching, with stability and universal approximation theory linking it to A-stable Runge-Kutta schemes; reported gains on ODEs and semi-discretized PDEs.

Nicola Farenga, Andrea Manzoni

arXiv cs.AI · cs.CLOct 31, 2026 #

Quantiles, Not Points: Streaming Quantile Matching for Ordinal-Aware LLM-as-a-Judge Calibration

In briefQuantile-based calibration of LLM judge scores against human ratings

SummaryThe paper addresses the distributional shift between LLM-as-a-judge scores and human ratings, which post-hoc calibration on a small labeled anchor set aims to correct. The authors treat LLM scores as ordinal ratings drawn from a distribution and propose streaming quantile matching (SQM), which computes the optimal-transport map between LLM and human score distributions. Their evaluation reports that SQM respects the ordinality of the human anchor set and achieves the best distributional fidelity and top overall rank across models and data. The paper also notes that post-hoc calibration narrows the raw error gap between smaller and larger judge models.

Ilija Subasic, Andrew Rabinovich, Zhao Chen
