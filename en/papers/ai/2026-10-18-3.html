First-Order LoRA Training with Phone NPUs

In briefTraining LoRA Models on Phone NPUs

SummaryThis paper presents a system that fine-tunes large language models on a phone's neural processing unit (NPU), so user data never has to leave the device. The NPU runs the three frozen-weight computations, while the training graph, adapters, and optimizer stay on the CPU. The authors evaluate it on two Qualcomm Snapdragon platforms using four Qwen and Llama models.

SL-Ground: Sentence-Level Grounding for Fine-Grained Open-Vocabulary Object Detection

In briefSL-Ground: Sentence-Level Grounding for Fine-Grained Open-Vocabulary Object Detection

SummaryThis paper argues that token-level scoring in grounding-based open-vocabulary detectors becomes a bottleneck for fine-grained categories. The authors propose SL-Ground, which encodes each category description as a complete sentence-level representation and scores object queries against it directly. On a multi-granularity Stanford Cars benchmark, AP at the finest car-model level rises from 14.5 to 70.6 under matched training candidate sampling, and SL-Ground achieves the best average AP among compared methods on FG-OVD.

Yang-Syu Li, Cheng-Huan Yang, Chia-Yung Jui et al.

Tilted Mondrian: Triage and Mitigation of Fine-grained Disparities in Conformal Prediction

In briefTilted Mondrian: Reducing Hidden Unfairness in Finer Groups of Conformal Predictions

SummaryConformal prediction methods that calibrate separately for each group guarantee coverage only at a coarse level, so large disparities can persist within finer subgroups. The paper proposes propensity standardization to assess how group composition affects these disparities, and introduces Tilted Mondrian, which reduces coverage and prediction set size gaps within finer strata without retraining the model. The method keeps groupwise coverage guarantees and comes with a method-agnostic bound on how much finer-stratum coverage disparity can be reduced.

Ziang Gao, Pengqi Liu, Archer Yi Yang et al.

SwitchSplat: Learning Discrete Object States from Casually Captured Scenes

In briefSwitchSplat: learning discrete object on/off states from casually captured scenes

Summary3D Gaussian Splatting enables fast 3D reconstruction, but existing methods assume a static scene, which fails when users interact with objects in casual captures. SwitchSplat uses a base-plus-slot decomposition, with a shared static base and one slot per interactable object that holds its on/off state. The model learns each state's geometry directly from video without manual annotation. On a new casual-capture dataset, it outperforms static and dynamic baselines in novel-view synthesis and can render state combinations not seen during capture.

Yizheng Xie, Yalda Foroutan, George Shramko et al.

Directionally Factorized Illumination with Spherical Harmonic Rotation

In briefDaFISH: Lighting Factorization for Camera-Mounted Lights in View Synthesis

SummaryMany capture setups, such as camera flashes and endoscopes, use a light source fixed to the camera, so illumination changes across views. DaFISH splits each point's emitted color into surface albedo and a spherical harmonic illumination factor, and can be added to NeRF-style pipelines. It evaluates lighting analytically for new camera poses without retraining. Averaged over held-out frames of five scenes, it reports the best mean PSNR, SSIM, and LPIPS among the compared methods.

Medical Fine-Tuning Reshapes, Not Reduces, Patient-Facing AI Risk: A Rubric-Based, LLM-Judge Evaluation Framework

In briefMedical fine-tuning changes the type of risk in patient-facing AI rather than lowering it

SummaryThis paper proposes a risk-sensitive evaluation framework that scores medical question-answer responses for six categories of risk-bearing language, such as treatment directives and dosage expressions, with severity judged by an ensemble of three LLM judges. The authors tested three general and three medically adapted 7-9B models on 200 real patient questions from the K-QA benchmark. They found that risk-bearing language is statistically independent of factual hallucination rate, and that medical fine-tuning reshapes risk profiles rather than reducing aggregate risk.

In briefDo Myoelectric Controllers Still Need User Calibration? A Closed-Loop Comparison

SummaryThis study compares six calibration-free controllers, trained across many users and applied without any test-participant calibration data, against three calibrated controllers in a closed-loop target-acquisition task with sixteen participants. Offline accuracy did not predict closed-loop performance, so the accuracy gained through calibration should not be taken as evidence of a closed-loop benefit.

Who Gets Unmasked? Sampler-Induced Bias in Diffusion Language Models

In briefWhich Gender Appears First? Sampler-Induced Bias in Diffusion Language Models

SummaryDiffusion language models generate text by gradually unmasking a canvas, and their samplers decide both which token to write and when to commit it. The paper shows that common commit rules, including LLaDA's default, favor majority options by committing high-probability positions first, amplifying occupational gender stereotypes and collapsing opinion distributions. The authors propose Fork-First Sampling, which decides key forks such as a protagonist's gender before other text is written, at the cost of one extra forward pass.

Cooperation Without Shared Rewards via Reciprocal Q-Augmentation

In briefCooperation without shared rewards using Reciprocal Q-Augmentation (RQA), a learning method for AI agents

SummaryThe paper introduces Reciprocal Q-Augmentation (RQA), which uses a latent-variable opponent model to augment an agent's Q-function with estimates of how its actions affect other agents. RQA lets agents learn both cooperative and defecting policies using only their own Q-function. Experiments on four benchmarks, including the Iterated Prisoner's Dilemma and CleanUp-Lite, report that RQA agents cooperate faster and reach higher cooperation than fully cooperative methods with shared rewards, and can induce cooperation in standard RL agents.

Francesco Morri, Adel Abusitta, Quentin Cappart et al.

Information Theory of Random Composition Trees

In briefInformation theory of random trees modeling text's hierarchical structure

SummaryThis paper studies random composition trees, a statistical model that generates hierarchical semantic structures by recursively splitting texts into coherent chunks. The authors show that the entropy of these trees grows linearly with root size and derive an analytical expression for the entropy rate as a function of the branching factor. They also prove that large trees exhibit typicality, meaning they lie in a typical set with high probability. The model thus shares linear entropy growth and typicality with language modeled as a stationary, ergodic process.

Doron Sivan, Weishun Zhong, Tankut Can et al.
