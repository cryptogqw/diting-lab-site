RuleForge: Inferring and Instantiating Latent Visual Rules as Executable Programs

In briefRuleForge: Learning hidden visual rules and turning them into executable programs

SummaryThe paper introduces Bongard-Forge100k, a controlled benchmark of 108,797 problems, and RuleForge, a framework that jointly learns to understand visual rules and generate executable programs conditioned on those rules. Training combines rule-understanding, joint understanding and scene-program supervision, and execution-grounded reinforcement learning. With UniLIP as the backbone, RuleForge raises multiple-choice accuracy by 20.93 points on familiar rules and 28.42 points on held-out rule compositions, and increases scene-level rule satisfaction on familiar rules from 2.91% to 70.99%.

Weifeng Xu, Yaxiong Wang, Zhedong Zheng

Adversarial Self-Distillation

In briefAdversarial Self-Distillation: Keeping Language Models' Refusals Under Attack

SummaryThis paper addresses the problem that safety-aligned language models can still be bypassed by adversarial perturbations, and that standard adversarial training on template refusals does not reliably teach refusal. It proposes Adversarial Self-Distillation (ASD), in which the model given a clean prompt acts as teacher and the same model under attack acts as student, distilling safe behavior at the prefixes the student visits. According to the paper, ASD achieves lower attack success than state-of-the-art defenses with little loss in multiple-choice accuracy, and can recover a refusal even after a compliant opening.

Mohammad Sadegh Akhondzadeh, Soroush H. Zargarbashi, Aleksandar Bojchevski

From Grind to Grasp

In briefFrom Grind to Grasp: How Work, Knowledge and Self-Improvement Relate in Intelligent Systems

SummaryThe paper introduces a formalism that compares achievements under limits on both work and information, showing how learning and persistence can make deep achievements shallow by reusing earlier work. It defines "grasp" as performance across different work budgets at a fixed information allowance, and uses this to give a criterion for recursive self-improvement. The paper also discusses collectives and organisation, and cautions against letting an emergent agent society take authority over social coordination.

Peter Sunehag

The Quine Test: How Much of a Model's Self Survives Its Own Description?

In briefQuine Test: How Much of a Model's Self Survives Its Own Description?

SummaryThe paper asks whether a language model's self-description can serve as a portable specification of its preference profile, testing whether the text can regenerate its behavior in other models. Ten models wrote self-descriptions, which were placed in the prompts of other models and fresh instances of the author, and their choices on a 110-item preference battery were compared to the author's. Native self-descriptions produced no reliable reconstruction gain, while explicitly listing the author's answers reproduced its profile with high fidelity.

WhyThe authors propose a quine test of self-descriptions as portable preference specifications, reporting experiments on 110 items across ten models.

Bibin Babu, Ivan P. Yamshchikov, Aleksei Shpilman

inkGround: Benchmarking Fine-grained Visual Grounding Capabilities of Vision-Language Models for Cursive Handwriting in the Wild

In briefinkGround: A benchmark testing how well vision-language models trace handwritten words in photos

SummaryThis paper introduces inkGround, a benchmark for recovering pen stroke trajectories from full-page, camera-captured handwriting. It contains 537 page images with about 18.7k words and 163k strokes, with reference trajectories generated by a pretrained offline-to-online model, filtered by OCR agreement, and checked by human raters. The task asks models to locate a target word in the image and output its ordered stroke points, which is evaluated on inkGround and on the IAM-OnDB dataset across four open-source and three closed-source vision-language models. Closed-source models, especially Gemini-3.1-Pro, produce legible trajectories close to the reference, and this ranking also holds on IAM-OnDB.

Sidra Hanif

Learning Scientific Trajectories to Predict Future Biomedical Discoveries

In briefLearning scientific trajectories to predict future biomedical discoveries

SummaryThis paper introduces ForeSCITE, a framework that groups published biomedical conclusions into themes and projects them along directions separating earlier from more recent findings. A language model then decodes these projections into natural-language hypotheses. In retrospective time-sliced tests across 407 clusters from about three million conclusions, it outperformed five baselines on semantic coverage, entity-pair novelty, and post-cutoff re-observation. Experts identified 37 CAR-T hypotheses as both novel and biologically plausible.

In briefCalibCause: A Quality-Gated, Interpretable and Calibrated Deep Learning Framework for Diabetic Retinopathy Grading

SummaryThis paper presents CalibCause, a deep learning framework for grading diabetic retinopathy that combines image-quality conditioning, per-expert visual explanations, knowledge distillation, and calibrated confidence with deferral. The framework was evaluated on 51,310 fundus images from EyePACS, DDR, and APTOS 2019, with IDRiD and DeepDRiD used for external testing. They conclude that reliable deployment still requires local validation and adaptation.

Latent Decision Flow Rewards for Clinical Guideline Reasoning

In briefTraining clinical reasoning models with guideline-based decision flow rewards

SummaryThis paper introduces GUIDER, a method for training clinical reasoning models using decision flows derived from clinical practice guidelines. It jointly optimizes a decision flow controller and a reasoning policy, computing process rewards over several generated flows and gating them on answer correctness to address path multiplicity and the path-answer gap. On the MedGUIDE benchmark, GUIDER with Qwen3-14B reports 82.5 weighted accuracy and 73.5 guideline adherence, and it also outperforms CRPO, MRPO, and FaithMed on out-of-domain benchmarks.

Gaurav Bhatt, Marinka Zitnik

In briefSatOps-Decision: Fast Decision-Making Method for Onboard Satellite Communication Operations

In briefGeometry Matters in Packed 3D Attention

SummaryThe paper argues that packing a 3D volume into a token sequence removes the geometric meaning of sequence order, so position mechanisms indexed by sequence position can learn memory layout rather than the object's structure. It proposes PACK3D, a local-global attention operator that keeps local neighborhoods exact, compresses distant context into contiguous 3D block summaries, and applies axial RoPE on true grid coordinates. Experiments on a controlled synthetic task and on Allen WTC-11 hiPSC microscopy data show gains over baselines, and a fused Triton implementation is reported for scalable training.

Deogen: Automatic Benchmark Generation for Reasoning over Policies

In briefDeogen: automatic benchmark generation for testing rule-based reasoning

SummaryThis paper presents DeoGen, a scalable and parametrizable tool that automatically generates benchmarks for deontic reasoning, which is applying rules to facts to reach a verdict. It autoformalizes policy rules into first-order logic and builds decision trees whose paths become benchmark entries. Frontier models gave correct verdicts 89-92% of the time on entries with enough information, but only 22-40% correct on entries where they should abstain, even when instructed to do so.

Sam Kouteili, William Liang, Keeran Murray et al.

On the Generalization Gap of Streaming Federated Learning with Markovian Data

In briefGeneralization gap of federated learning on Markovian streaming data

SummaryThis paper studies the expected generalization gap of a shared model trained by streaming federated learning when client data follow Markov processes. It uses information-theoretic and rate-distortion techniques to derive bounds under cross-entropy loss, specialized to linear and self-attention next-token prediction. The bounds reveal a communication–generalization tradeoff, where frequent aggregation may weaken generalization. Experiments use GPT-style language-model pretraining on TinyStories and real-world time-series forecasting.

Masoud Kavian, Milad Sefidgaran, Youssef Attia El Hili et al.

Quantum-Inspired Pairwise Ranking Aggregation via Active LLM Feedback

In briefRanking merger that learns from selective LLM pairwise judgments for document reranking

SummaryThis paper introduces LLM-IRA, an extension of a quantum-inspired interactive ranking aggregation framework to document reranking. It uses a large language model only for pairwise judgments on high-disagreement document pairs to learn fusion weights, and the final ranking comes from fast pointwise linear score fusion. Experiments on BEIR and TREC Deep Learning show it matches or numerically exceeds strong static fusion baselines on 5 of 6 benchmarks, with statistical significance on 4 of them.

ExplainCC: Explaining Correlated Clusters by Node Labels

In briefExplainCC: Explaining Correlated Clusters Using Node Labels

SummaryThis paper studies Correlation Clustering, where objects are grouped by pairwise similarity, and requires the clusters to be explainable by node labels. The authors restrict attention to clusters that a label tree, a decision tree splitting objects by labels, can describe. They propose a top-down greedy algorithm that finds locally optimal splits, using maintained counters for efficiency. Experiments show it processes graphs with over a million edges in minutes, recovers ground truth on synthetic data, and yields interpretable clusters on real networks.

Explore Where It Matters: Targeted Exploration for Efficient Rover Adaptation

In briefTargeted Exploration for Efficient Rover Adaptation: Exploring Where Errors Matter Most

SummaryThis paper presents TERRA, an online framework that helps planetary rovers correct errors between simulation and real deformable terrain during deployment. It updates a world model with a reward-weighted prediction loss and trains a loss predictor to estimate where performance-relevant errors are likely, guiding exploration during MPC. In simulation, TERRA reaches about 90% slope-climbing success within five trials under severe out-of-distribution pretraining, and on the ERNEST rover it recovers coordinated active-suspension locomotion on a Mars-simulant slope within three to five real-world trials.

Jacob Levy, Arthur Bouton, Tristan D. Hassler et al.
