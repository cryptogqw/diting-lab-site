Functional Welfare for AI Safety

In briefFunctional Welfare: Treating Welfare-Like AI States as a Safety Variable

SummaryThis paper argues that AI welfare should be treated as a safety issue as well as an ethical one, because models show welfare-like states that causally influence their behaviour, which the authors call functional welfare. It proposes that such states be used in evaluation design, monitoring, and post-training, and offers three tests for whether a functional welfare attribution is warranted. In preliminary experiments on Gemma 3 27B, rejecting the model's answers raised frustration indicators and made rule-breaking more likely, while fine-tuning on its own narrated solutions lowered frustration. The authors stress these results are preliminary.

Rikhil Jhaveri, Jamie Johnson, Punya Syon Pandey et al.
