Denoised but Not Improved: What EEG Denoising Does to a Downstream Task

In briefDenoised But Not Improved: Effects of EEG Denoising on a Downstream Task

SummaryThis paper audits how scalp EEG denoising methods are evaluated, arguing that waveform fidelity to a clean reference is an unvalidatable objective. With a seizure-detection pipeline held fixed and only the preprocessing varied, five standard denoising methods failed to beat unprocessed signals, and most degraded performance. The authors introduce a label-only, classifier-free probe that decomposes changes into between-class distance and within-class scatter, finding that a helpful transform's gain came from reduced within-class scatter. They conclude that downstream models benefit from variance suppression on task-relevant structure, which fidelity metrics do not measure.
