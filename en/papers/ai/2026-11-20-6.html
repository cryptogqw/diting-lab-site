Scalable fault vulnerability analysis for ReRAM-based process-in-memory CNN accelerators

In briefFast analysis of fault vulnerability in memory-based CNN chips

SummaryThis paper proposes a fast, scalable analytical framework to measure how vulnerable convolutional neural networks are to permanent faults on ReRAM-based in-memory processing accelerators. It scores vulnerability at the level of individual weights, kernels, and layers without exhaustive simulation. Comparisons with fault injection results confirm its effectiveness, with a reported speedup of roughly 10^4 to 10^5 times over exhaustive fault injection.

SKILL.PNG: Learning Agent Skills in Pixel Space

In briefSKILL.PNG: Learning AI Agent Skills as Optimized Images

SummaryAI agents are usually specialized with text-based skills, which cannot be optimized continuously. This paper introduces Skill.png, which represents a skill as an image whose pixels are optimized by gradient descent through the vision encoder of a frozen vision-language model. In 9 of 16 settings across 4 models and 4 benchmarks, it outperforms text skills optimized with frontier LLMs, using 64 to 256 image tokens instead of roughly 900 to 2000 text tokens.

WhyThe authors optimize skills as image pixels via gradient descent through a frozen VLM encoder, reporting gains over textual skills in 9 of 16 settings.

BADAS-3.0: Real-Time Driving Perception and Planning from One Camera

In briefBADAS-3.0: A Single-Camera Model for Real-Time Driving Perception and Planning

SummaryThis paper presents BADAS-3.0, a 1-billion-parameter video encoder with four small heads that uses about two seconds of footage from one forward camera. In under 50 ms it predicts current speed, the path just driven, a collision-risk score, and six candidate trajectories over 6.4 seconds. On five trajectory benchmarks it reports 44% lower minADE₆ than the 34-billion-parameter Alpamayo 2 Super, which uses six cameras. The authors also add two open-loop safety benchmarks, where BADAS-3.0 achieves a higher pass rate than every published planner tested.

Roni Goldshmidt, Hamish Scott, Lorenzo Niccolini

Who Moved My Z Boson? Revealing Resonance Features Inside a Neural Network

In briefWho Moved My Z Boson? Finding Resonance Features Inside a Neural Network

SummaryThis paper examines ShellFlow, a generative collider physics model trained on real LHC data, to find internal features representing resonances such as the Z, J/ψ, and Υ. The network was never told which muons come from a resonance and was trained only to generate final-state particle kinematics. The authors use these features to change the Z boson mass between about 80 and 105 GeV, and show that removing the feature broadens the Z peak. The rest of the invariant mass spectrum stays mostly unchanged in both cases.

Kevin Alberto Urqu\'ia Calder\'on, Midori Kato, Inar Timiryasov et al.

dLoop: Looped Diffusion Language Models via Recurrent Latent Refinement

In briefdLoop: Looped diffusion language models with recurrent latent refinement

SummaryThe paper presents dLoop, which restructures a pretrained diffusion language model into a Prelude-Loop-Coda architecture and repeatedly applies a parameter-shared intermediate block to the same partially masked state. The extra loops add latent computation before tokens are committed, letting information across uncommitted positions integrate more fully and making predictions more reliable. Experiments on GSM8K, MATH, MBPP, and HumanEval show dLoop achieves the highest or tied-highest accuracy on every benchmark.

In briefSTAGE: A method that expands spatio-temporal graphs to discover cause-and-effect links across tweets with weak supervision

SummaryThis paper addresses the difficulty of finding cause-and-effect relations across separate posts in disaster-related social media. It proposes STAGE, which starts from local causal seeds, retrieves related event occurrences, and uses a causal scorer to build weakly labeled cross-tweet pairs. Experiments on California Wildfire and Hurricane Harvey show that cross-tweet supervision improves performance, and transfer between disasters is asymmetric.

In briefPrism: A Minimalist Design for Hierarchical Text Detection

In briefEvidence-first ranking to resist cover and title manipulation in cold-start video recommendation

SummaryShort-video platforms rank new uploads using content features such as covers, titles and frames before any user has watched them. The paper introduces REAP, an attack using simulated platform re-encoding that raises a cold video's rank percentile by 32.0 points among unrelated audiences. It then proposes Evidence-First Ranking (EFR), which scores cold videos mainly from their body and admits packaging only through a bounded residual. Time Series Structure Learning with Local Causal Parent Discovery

In briefLearning causal structure in time series by finding local parents

SummaryThis paper introduces Iterative Temporal Parent Discovery (ITPD), a constraint-based method for learning causal structure in multivariate time series with causal nonstationarity. It works locally-to-globally, sequentially finding the parent set of each variable to build the full graph. With an exact oracle, ITPD ran the fewest tests and used the smallest average conditioning sets compared with baselines. On graphs with 2000 nodes, it ran 2.3×, 9.1×, and 40.9× faster than iterative IAMB, DF-PC, and PCMCI, respectively.
