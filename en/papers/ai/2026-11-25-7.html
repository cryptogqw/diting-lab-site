Measuring Layer-wise Intrinsic Dimensionality of FFNs in LLMs via PCA

In briefMeasuring how much capacity each layer of LLM feed-forward networks needs using PCA

SummaryThe paper applies principal component analysis to the inputs of feed-forward network layers in large language models to estimate each layer's intrinsic dimensionality. Across 58 open-weight models, middle layers tend to show higher intrinsic dimensionality than edge layers. Using this signal to guide width pruning in a distilled student model outperforms uniform-width allocation under the same parameter budget.

Daehee Kim

NABLA: A Noise-Adaptive Blockwise Left-Right Preconditioning Algorithm for Stochastic Optimization

In briefNABLA: A noise-adaptive method for stochastic optimization using block-wise left-right preconditioning

SummaryThis paper proposes NABLA, an algorithm for mini-batch stochastic nonconvex optimization. It combines an exponential moving average of stochastic gradients with block-diagonal row and column preconditioners built from matrix-valued second-moment statistics. The second-moment averaging coefficient is adjusted by a closed-loop feedback rule based on cumulative gradient energy. Under standard assumptions, it establishes a stationarity guarantee of order O~(T^{-1/2}+σ^{1/2}T^{-1/4}), which reduces to O~(T^{-1/2}) when gradient noise vanishes.

Yanlin Li, Ganzhao Yuan

Beyond Fixed-Context Likelihood: Zero-Shot AI-Generated Text Detection via Context Perturbation Discrepancy

In briefZero-shot AI text detection using context perturbation discrepancy beyond fixed-context likelihood

SummaryThis paper introduces Context Perturbation Discrepancy (CPD), a zero-shot method for detecting AI-generated text using a single pretrained language model. CPD compares each token's log-probability gain under the full prefix against a short recent-token window, subtracts the expected gain, and scales the summed deviations by an analytical reference standard deviation. On unedited texts from GEN, DetectRL-X, and HART, subtracting the expectation raises AUROC by 32.78 to 35.81 points. CPD averages 94.80% AUROC and 79.60% recall at a 5% false-positive rate, outperforming seven zero-shot baselines.

WhyThe authors add a expected-gain correction with an analytical reference standard deviation, reporting 94.80% average AUROC against seven zero-shot baselines on GEN, DetectRL-X, and HART.

Yifan Tang, Jingran Li, Yihao Wang et al.

Beyond Static Semantics: Instance-Adaptive Vision-Language Alignment for Building Damage Assessment

In briefInstance-adaptive vision-language alignment for assessing damage to individual buildings

SummaryThis paper proposes LAMIA-IBDA, a vision-language framework for assessing damage to individual buildings from bi-temporal remote sensing images. It adapts shared damage descriptions to local image evidence and then links them to each building to form instance-specific prototypes for fine-grained damage classification. The authors report state-of-the-art results on xBD and BRIGHT under both optical-optical and optical-SAR settings.

WhyThe authors align text damage descriptions with building regions via instance-adaptive prototypes, reporting state-of-the-art results on xBD and BRIGHT for damage assessment.

Learning World Models through Contrastive Identification of Actions

In briefLearning World Models by Contrastively Identifying Actions

SummaryThis paper proposes Contrastive Identification of Actions (CIA), a method for training latent world models from offline visual data. It trains the model to pick out the executed action from alternative actions sampled from a history-conditioned behavior proposal. A controlled learning example shows that CIA can reward action information that squared-error action regression does not capture. Across five visual control benchmarks, CIA achieves higher planner-averaged mean success than the base world model and a regression inverse-dynamics baseline, without modifying the planner.

Hayeong Lee, Youngjun Suh, Byung-Jun Lee

RubikSQL-HB: Agentic Text-to-SQL with Knowledge in the Loop

In briefRubikSQL-HB: An agentic text-to-SQL system that consults knowledge during reasoning

SummaryThe paper presents RubikSQL-HB, which gives database agents queryable, database-specific knowledge workspaces covering business meanings, stored values and reference queries, accessible throughout SQL construction and checking. On 500 BIRD Mini-Dev questions, the full pipeline reaches 75.2% execution accuracy, compared with 71.2% for first-candidate selection and 70.4% for execution-majority voting. The authors attribute most of the gain over the selection baselines to evidence-aware adjudication with tools and revision.

Haorui Jia, Zihan Qu, Yundi Dong et al.

EviCraft: Can Minecraft Agents Find What They Cannot See?

In briefEviCraft: Can Minecraft Agents Find What They Cannot See?

SummaryThis paper examines Minecraft, a common testbed for embodied agents, where tools such as mineBlock select targets from the simulator's loaded world state without a visibility check. As a result, agents can reach buried ores they have never seen, and scores cannot show whether they actually searched. The authors formalize a requirement called evidence closure and present EviCraft, a runtime and benchmark that compares a perception-grounded admission regime with a loaded-state regime reproducing standard tool stacks.

WhyThe authors formalize evidence closure and compare perception-grounded and loaded-state regimes on Minecraft Survival, revealing tool-level state leakage.

Wenyou Yi, Lantian Zhang, Guanlin Chen et al.

SGD with Large Learning Rates: General Analysis & Improved Rates

In briefStochastic gradient descent with large learning rates and faster convergence

SummaryThis paper studies how Stochastic Gradient Descent behaves when the learning rate is large, rather than below the usual curvature-based stability threshold. For the cross-entropy loss with linear predictors and two-layer neural networks, the authors prove an accelerated O(T^{-2}) convergence rate. Their analysis gives a fine-grained description of the stochastic large-stepsize dynamics, which also yields tighter guarantees within the stable regime. The results indicate that large-stepsize acceleration persists under stochastic noise and extends beyond linear models.

WhyThe authors report an accelerated O(T^-2) rate for large-stepsize SGD with cross-entropy on linear and two-layer networks, answering an open question and improving stable-regime bounds.

Konstantinos Emmanouilidis, Ziqing Xu, Salma Tarmoun et al.
