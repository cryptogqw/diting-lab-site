VITAL: A Framework for Validating Agentic AI in Pharmaceutical Drug Development and Clinical Trial Operations

In briefVITAL: A Framework for Checking Agentic AI Used in Drug Development and Clinical Trials

SummaryThis paper presents VITAL, a framework for judging whether evidence is sufficient to validate AI agents that act autonomously in pharmaceutical clinical development. It combines context of use, controls, behavioural validation and lifecycle evidence in a risk-adjusted structure. The authors applied it to tasks such as epidemiology analysis, site selection, enrolment rescue and trial-design support. They suggest it may guide evidence requirements and lifecycle accountability for higher-risk pharmaceutical uses.

Sandeep Burugupalli, Pedro Matos-Filipe, Andrea Hita Ardiaca et al.

Rescaling OLS-Merged Adapters To Improve Personalized Image Generation

In briefRescaling merged image-model adapters to improve personalized generation

SummaryThe paper examines merging multiple adapters into one image generation model, a method used to cut deployment costs. It shows that ordinary least squares reconstruction, used by methods such as RegMean, Mix-of-Show, and Subspace Routing, shrinks task-specific signals when activation distributions of different tasks overlap. The authors derive a decomposition that quantifies this shrinkage and propose rescaling the merged update to restore signal strength. Tested on multi-subject personalization with up to 30 merged adapters, the rescaling substantially improves subject fidelity over unscaled baselines.

Leander Girrbach, Niklas Schweiger, Zeynep Akata

In briefMI-Hyper: Hypernetwork-generated weights for transferring pathology slide models across tasks

SummaryMI-Hyper is a hypernetwork pretrained on mixed pathology tasks that generates the aggregation and classification weights of multiple instance learning models for whole-slide image prediction. ResoLUT: Distilling Residual Networks into Lookup-Table Networks

In briefResoLUT: Distilling Residual Networks into Lookup-Table Networks

SummaryThis paper presents ResoLUT, which distills a trained residual network (ResNet) layer by layer into lookup-table (LUT) trees, producing logic networks suited to efficient hardware inference. It keeps the multi-bit residual stream and BatchNorm, takes connectivity from the teacher rather than learning it, and uses hard LUT decisions throughout training and inference. The authors report 95.66% on CIFAR-10 for ResoLUT-20, and 78.87% on CIFAR-100 and 53.69% on 64px ImageNet for ResoLUT-56. A further optimized ResoLUT-8 fits on an FPGA, reaching 90.04% on CIFAR-10 at 189k images per second.

Symmetries of Counterfactuals: Identification and Testability of Counterfactual Maps from Covariate Heterogeneity

In briefSymmetries of Counterfactuals: Identifying and Testing Counterfactual Maps Using Covariate Variation

SummaryCounterfactuals usually cannot be determined from data alone, since different causal models can generate the same observed distributions. The paper studies what data can reveal when covariates affect the outcome only through its latent cause. It characterises all counterfactual maps consistent with the data, which differ from the true map by a group of symmetries preserving the conditional outcome distributions given covariates, with the group shrinking as these distributions vary more across covariate values. For assumed classes such as triangular monotone maps and optimal transport maps, the authors show that consistency with the data is equivalent to a single conditional independence, giving a test of the assumed class. Both results are illustrated on synthetic data and on the ACTG 175 HIV trial.

Marc Braun, Adel Daoud, Jose M. Pe\~na

A Transformer-Derived Iterative Preconditioner

In briefA Transformer-Derived Iterative Preconditioner

SummaryThis paper shows that a softmax-attention Transformer trained to solve linear systems Xw=y implicitly improves matrix conditioning. Tracing the internal computation involving XX^T reveals an iterative preconditioning method, which the authors develop into RAPID, a standalone algorithm with no learned components. RAPID uses randomized mixing and sparse local corrections to precondition symmetric positive definite matrices, and experiments show fewer conjugate gradient iterations are needed to reach a target accuracy. Under a Haar-mixing idealization, the paper proves convergence to any fixed target condition number.

Patrick Lutz, Themistoklis Haris, Aditya Gangrade et al.

Implementing Binary Transformers with Approximate Memories with Permutation Invariance Enabled Selective (PIES) Protection

In briefRunning Binary Transformers on Approximate Memory with Permutation-Invariance Selective Protection

SummaryThis paper uses BERT as a case study to evaluate how binary Transformers hold up against memory bit errors, finding a large accuracy loss only at high bit error rates such as 5% or 10%. The authors propose PIES, a selective protection scheme that exploits Transformers' invariance to row and column permutations to group and protect critical binary weights at low overhead. Simulations show PIES recovers most performance lost at a 10% bit error rate, at much lower cost than traditional error correction. The results suggest binary Transformers can run on approximate memory with aggressive voltage scaling on low-power edge devices.
