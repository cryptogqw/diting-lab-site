FTN Signaling: Spectral Efficiency from BPSK to 16-QAM

In briefFTN Signaling: How Spectral Efficiency Varies from BPSK to 16-QAM

SummaryThis paper studies faster-than-Nyquist (FTN) signaling, which sends symbols closer together than the Nyquist limit to raise spectral efficiency at the cost of added inter-symbol interference. It analyzes achievable information rates for BPSK, QPSK, and 16-QAM using an MMSE channel-shortening detector under a power constraint. Lower-order constellations tolerate more aggressive acceleration, while 16-QAM performs best closer to the Nyquist limit, with optimal factors of about 0.65, 0.75, and 0.90 at 6 dB SNR.

In briefA Tighter Capacity Upper Bound for the Primitive Diamond Relay Channel

SummaryThis paper studies the capacity of the primitive diamond relay channel, a network with one source, two relays, and one destination. Using the Csiszár–Körner–Marton sum identity and Gallager-type identification, it derives an upper bound that improves on existing ones. Sequential Random Sampling PIR with Multiple Colluding Servers in DNA-Based Data Storage

In briefPrivate DNA data retrieval using random sampling across multiple colluding servers

SummaryThis paper studies sequential random sampling private information retrieval for DNA-based data storage, where the database is split across several equal-sized servers that may collude. It analyzes the tradeoff between download cost, measured as the expected number of queries, and privacy leakage, measured by mutual information. Lower bounds are derived, including one given by an optimization problem, and this bound is shown to be tight when each server stores two files. For servers of arbitrary size, schemes are constructed that apply a single-server scheme to a randomly chosen subset of servers.
