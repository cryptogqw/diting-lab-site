Veto Proportionality in Multiwinner Voting with Ranked Preferences

In briefProportional veto fairness in multiwinner elections with ranked ballots

SummaryThis paper studies veto proportionality in committee elections where voters submit ranked preferences. It introduces the proportional multiwinner veto core (PMVC), which extends Moulin's singlewinner veto core by letting voter groups partially veto sets of candidates. The authors analyze how the PMVC relates to existing fairness notions and design voting rules guaranteed to select committees from the PMVC.

Haris Aziz, Yuhang Guo, Patrick Lederer

arXiv cs.GTNov 14, 2026 #

Spreads Don't Price a Rival Market Maker's Information

In briefRival Market Makers' Spreads Do Not Widen When a Competitor Gets Better Information

SummaryThis paper asks whether uninformed market makers widen their bid-ask spreads when a competitor receives a more precise private signal about a persistent state driving order flow. The authors model this as a stochastic quoting game, learn strategies with independent reinforcement learning, and benchmark against an exact dynamic-programming solution for the single-agent case. Across all configurations studied, the uninformed makers' average spread does not increase with the rival's precision; it is unchanged where competition is weak and tightens where competition is strong.

Oliver James Oberle, Damien Berriaud, Roger Wattenhofer

Entropy-Based and Quadratic Conditional Innovation Metrics for Informed Dynamic Scheduling of Belief-Propagation Decoding for LDPC Codes

In briefEntropy- and quadratic-based metrics for scheduling LDPC belief-propagation decoding

SummaryThis paper proposes two improved conditional innovation (CI) metrics, E-CI and Q-CI, to guide informed dynamic scheduling in belief-propagation decoding of LDPC codes. Both metrics separate each posterior probability into a decision side and a reliability-related distance term, which is replaced by an entropy-based quantity in E-CI and approximated by its leading quadratic term in Q-CI. Numerical results indicate that the proposed metrics generally achieve lower frame error rates than the original CI metric, with advantages depending on the code and decoding procedure.

Tofar Chih-Yuan Chang, Chia-En Liu, Ying-Ren Jian et al.

Zero-shot diffusion image compression with sectioned codebooks and one channel code

In briefZero-shot diffusion image compression using sectioned codebooks and a single channel code

SummaryThis paper proposes sectioned codebooks for zero-shot diffusion image codecs such as Turbo-DDCM, which compress images without training. Each coding step is split into sections, each with its own large codebook, and the best vector is sent as a short bit field. At equal bits, the method raises PSNR by 0.18 to 0.51 dB on three datasets and saves 16 to 21% of bits at equal PSNR. Compared with a codec protected by one BCH channel code at equal total bits, it outperforms reported robust codecs on two datasets, by up to 7.74 dB at the highest bit error rate.

Dinanath Padhya

New constructions of optimal Ferrers diagram rank-metric codes for quasi-block monotone Ferrers diagrams

In briefNew optimal rank-metric codes for quasi-block monotone Ferrers diagrams

SummaryThis paper studies Ferrers diagram rank-metric codes, which are used in constructing constant dimension subspace codes for random network coding. Using the multiplicative property of the kernel filtration of a nilpotent map, the authors construct optimal codes for block monotone diagrams. By puncturing and shortening on columns, they extend these results to block strictly monotone and quasi-block strictly monotone diagrams.

Providing Accuracy Guarantees for the Fixed-Point Emulation of Matrix Multiplication

In briefAccuracy Guarantees for Fixed-Point Emulation of Matrix Multiplication

SummaryThis paper studies emulating double-precision matrix multiplication on fast low-precision hardware using fixed-point methods such as Ozaki-II, with software that chooses the mantissa bit count automatically. The authors analyze worst-case and average-case errors of fixed-point and floating-point dot products and define exponent span capacities that set the extra mantissa bits an input needs. They prove that 53 bits plus such a capacity and a small constant preserve the classical worst-case error bound for a floating-point dot product, and demonstrate this in software.

Cole Brower, Peter Caday, John Gunnels

Rank Functions of Flag Matroids with Applications to Order-Neutral Submodular Valuations

In briefRank functions of flag matroids and their use in order-neutral submodular valuations

SummaryThe paper gives a rank function axiomatization of flag matroids, defined as sequences of matroid quotients, and shows it is cryptomorphic to that definition. It links flag matroid rank functions to order-neutral submodular valuations from computational economics, showing they coincide for length-k flag matroids. The authors prove every order-neutral submodular valuation is a gross-substitute valuation, but not every one is a Rado valuation.

NVFP4-MLA: Unified FP4 KV-Cache Storage and Attention Computation for Multi-Head Latent Attention

In briefNVFP4-MLA: Unified FP4 Storage and Computation for Attention Cache in Large Models

SummaryThis paper introduces NVFP4-MLA, the first design that uses FP4 precision for both key-value cache storage and attention computation in multi-head latent attention. It combines a quantization method that adds residuals only to sensitive channels with two-dimensional quantization blocks that avoid requantization between the two attention matrix multiplications. On DeepSeek-R1 and Kimi-K3, it matches BF16 MLA average accuracy across nine benchmarks while compressing the MLA KV cache by 3.20x and 3.05x. On NVIDIA Rubin GPUs, the attention kernel runs up to 2.82x faster than FP8 MLA on DeepSeek-R1 and up to 2.71x faster on Kimi-K3.

Chao Ni, Qi Zhang, Xiao Wang et al.

In briefUsing Factory Load Valleys to Host Distributed AI Servers with Less Grid Connection

Joint Channel Estimation, Data Detection, and Phase Noise Tracking in Massive MIMO Systems

In briefA unified method for jointly estimating channels, detecting data, and tracking phase noise in massive MIMO

SummaryThis paper addresses uplink massive MIMO systems, where phase noise from imperfect oscillators degrades communication and grows worse at higher carrier frequencies. It proposes a unified framework that tracks the evolving phase noise consistently across pilot and data transmission while jointly estimating the channel and detecting data. The framework has three parts: VB-Tracking for channel estimation and phase noise tracking during pilots, VB-Detecting for joint data detection and phase tracking, and VB-Refining for periodically re-estimating the channel during data transmission. Simulations show lower channel NMSE and symbol error rate than the baselines, with the advantage widening at high SNR and under severe phase noise.

Toan-Van Nguyen, Duy H. N. Nguyen

Surrogate Symbols in Wireless Communications

In briefSurrogate Symbols in Wireless Communications: Using Phase Optimization to Turn Co-Channel Interference into Usable Signal

SummaryThe paper addresses co-channel interference (CCI) in a network of single-antenna transmitters and receivers that reuse the same channel. It proposes a discrete phase-optimization framework called "Game of Phases: Surrogate Symbol Optimization" with two algorithms, MiPIP and MaPRIS. The authors argue that MaPRIS can make the interference approximate a desired symbol even when a receiver's own desired link is zero, and report that simulations confirm these findings.

The Walsh spectrum of the Dobbertin APN function

In briefFull Walsh spectrum of the Dobbertin APN function

SummaryThis paper studies APN functions induced by monomials over finite fields, which resist differential attacks when used in block ciphers. The authors determine the complete Walsh spectrum, and thus the linearity, of the Dobbertin APN monomial, answering a problem open for over 20 years. Using known connections, the result also gives the asymptotically largest Sidon sets constructed so far in elementary abelian 2-groups and nearly optimal binary linear codes with minimum distance 5.

Stochastic Games with Distributional Entropy Objectives

In briefStochastic games where entropy of a probability distribution is the payoff

SummaryThis paper introduces two-player zero-sum stochastic games whose configurations are probability distributions over the states of a finite arena and whose stage reward is Shannon entropy. MIN tries to concentrate the distribution while MAX tries to disperse it. Because entropy is nonlinear, the paper formalises three semantics depending on whether randomness is averaged or sampled before entropy is evaluated, and analyses their determinacy, optimal strategies, and computational complexity.

Shankaranarayanan Krishna, Durgam Latha, Dion Reji et al.

Disenfranchisement in Spatial Voting Games

In briefVoting Rights Lost in Spatial Voting Games

SummaryThe paper studies a self-referential problem in which group members decide who may join the group, as with voting rights and membership rules. The authors introduce franchise elections, where voting members vote to change the electorate. They show that in a two-dimensional ideological space these elections can produce complex behavior for any social choice function. BPU-SpikeMM:Exact Matrix Multiplication via Spatio-Temporal Spike Encoding on Real Neuromorphic Hardware

In briefBPU-SpikeMM: Exact Matrix Multiplication via Spike Encoding on Neuromorphic Chips

SummaryThis paper presents BPU-SpikeMM, a framework that performs matrix multiplication on real neuromorphic hardware (BPU) using spatio-temporal spike encoding. The authors prove exact equivalence of spike-encoded matrix multiplication under the BPU execution paradigm and distinguish it from mathematical equivalence. They build an offloading pipeline supporting multiple encoding schemes and evaluate them on correctness, temporal and spatial resource cost, and event-driven metrics. Results confirm direct-readout equivalence across all evaluated encodings, with applicability depending on trade-offs among these costs.

Rongyuan Guo, Yufeng Zhang, Haotian Wang et al.
