TAO: Tool-Aware Joint Scheduling for LLM-Based Agentic Serving

In briefTAO: Tool-Aware Joint Scheduling for LLM Agent Serving

SummaryThis paper presents TAO, a scheduling framework for serving LLM-based agents that alternate between GPU model inference and external tools running on CPUs. TAO's value function combines near-term tool readiness, node-specific cache credit with time-aware decay, and token footprint, and a dynamic program jointly selects and places waiting sessions under capacity limits. Across 12 reported model-workload-concurrency settings, TAO reduces latency by up to 26.8%, mean time to first token by up to 36.3%, and increases decode throughput by up to 29.5% relative to the best evaluated baseline for each metric.

Shuhuai Li, Shengdong Ye, Dongdong Ge et al.

Deep Holes and Aperture: Sign Laws for the Empirical Correlation of Lattice-Sampled Random Wave Fields

In briefDeep Holes and Aperture: Sign Laws for Lattice-Sampled Random Waves

SummaryThis paper studies whether the empirical lag correlation of a Gaussian random wave field, sampled on a lattice within a bounded aperture, keeps a positive real part throughout the Voronoi cell. It identifies deep holes as the minimizers that govern failure and gives explicit sign-class counts for square, hexagonal, simple cubic, and body-centered cubic lattices. In briefA unified queueing model for comparing serverless autoscaling under bursty workloads

SummaryThis paper compares three serverless autoscaling strategies: scale-per-request, concurrency-level scaling, and metric-based scaling, under bursty workloads.
