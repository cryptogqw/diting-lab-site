Resource-aware Sub-network Allocation for Heterogeneous Decentralized Learning

In briefResource-aware sub-network allocation for heterogeneous decentralized learning

SummaryThis paper addresses synchronous decentralized learning, where each training round is limited by resource-constrained nodes with different computation, communication, and memory. It proposes RASA-DL, which assigns each node a maximum sub-network width based on hardware and learning-utility profiles, formulating deployment as bottleneck-latency minimization under memory and utility constraints solved by binary search. In experiments with controlled heterogeneity, the minimum-latency deployment improved accuracy over the largest globally feasible uniform deployment by 2.80 points (Moderate) and 2.43 points (Severe) at the same round latency, with larger gains under strong Non-IID data.

Zhuojun Tian, Ming Xiao, Mikael Skoglund

Why Alternation Speeds Up Counterfactual Regret Minimization?

In briefWhy alternating updates speed up counterfactual regret minimization

SummaryThis paper examines why alternating the two players' updates makes counterfactual regret minimization with RM+ faster in imperfect-information games. It tests the common intuition that seeing the opponent's fresh strategy acts like optimism, using an analytic witness family of deterministic two-player zero-sum self-play. The main result is a dichotomy theorem linking trajectory separation to recurrent exact-vertex play, with measurements nearly matching the predicted constant.

On Minimum Divergence for Distribution Reconstruction

In briefMinimum-divergence methods for recovering probability distributions from moments

SummaryThis paper studies whether maximum-entropy reconstruction, which recovers a distribution from finitely many generalized moments by minimizing KL divergence to a reference measure, can be generalized. Replacing KL with a smooth f-divergence still yields a reconstruction, but the best-approximation property fails. The authors show that, up to equivalence, KL is the only C² f-divergence with this entropy-adapted property, and illustrate the consequences numerically.

It Takes Two Moments: Rigidity and Limits of Continuous Encoders for Over-the-Air Computation

In briefTwo Moments Suffice: Limits of Continuous Encoders for Over-the-Air Computation

SummaryThis paper studies over-the-air computation, where transmitters send locally transformed observations over one use of a complex multiple-access channel so a receiver can compute functions such as empirical variance from two separately postprocessed sums. With four or more transmitters and exact noiseless recovery, the authors show that every continuous encoder must share a common affine structure preserving both sums, reducing encoder design to choosing finitely many coefficients. They define a robustness score, cast robustness maximization as an exact convex semi-infinite program, and derive closed-form optimal encoders for several function classes, including empirical variance.

It studies two generalized information measures: mutual f-information, based on f-divergence, and variational (G,f)-information, defined by minimizing transformed f-divergences. In briefCache-aided linear function computation: how caching cuts the data sent to compute linear combinations

SummaryThis paper studies distributed systems where each source stores its own data and a receiver requests a linear combination of these data.
