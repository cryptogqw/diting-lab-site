Asymptotically good codes from Siegel modular varieties

In briefAsymptotically good codes built from Siegel modular varieties

SummaryThe paper constructs asymptotically good codes by evaluating Siegel modular forms at superspecial points on congruence towers of Siegel modular varieties. For each genus g≥2 and fixed weight w≥g+1, it gives such code families in sufficiently large characteristic over a field independent of the tower level, which can be taken to be F_{p²}, along with an explicit lower bound on the asymptotic rate. It also proves that for p≥5 and 0≤w≤p+1, the kernel of evaluation in every genus consists exactly of multiples of the ordinary Hasse invariant.

Yota Maeda

Classical Tensor Codes and Quantum Tanner Codes Achieve Capacity on Erasure Channels

In briefPaper showing classical tensor codes and quantum Tanner codes reach capacity on erasure channels

SummaryThe paper shows that on the classical erasure channel, the tensor product of two capacity-achieving code families is also capacity-achieving under mild conditions. It further proves that duals of tensor products of independent uniform random binary linear codes achieve erasure capacity. Using these local codes, the authors construct binary quantum Tanner codes of rate at least 1-2ρ-ε, with bounded check weights, linearly growing minimum distance, and a linear-time erasure decoder whose error decays exponentially with blocklength.

WhyThe authors prove tensor products of capacity-achieving codes stay capacity-achieving on erasure channels, and build quantum Tanner codes with linear distance and exponentially decaying decoding error

Energy-Proportional Serverless Computing on SBC Clusters

In briefEnergy-proportional serverless computing on single-board computer clusters

SummaryServerless computing runs code only on demand, but the underlying servers still draw power while idle, so energy use does not fall to zero with the workload. The authors exploit the statelessness of serverless functions to extend scale-to-zero from function instances to physical nodes, using a tinyFaaS-based platform on single-board computer clusters that powers idle workers off and back on under load. On a physical cluster, mean power draw fell by 39.6% on a bursty trace-based workload and by 46.7% on a sparse one, with no detectable change in median latency on the bursty workload. The cost appears in tail latency: invocations arriving while all workers are off wait about a minute for a node to boot.

Yuliya Sasinskaya, Natalie Carl, Niklas Kowallik et al.

Scaling Shapley Energy Attribution to Large LLM Batches

In briefFairly splitting LLM batch energy among requests using Shapley values

SummaryLLM serving engines batch requests, and GPUs report only total energy, so providers need a fair way to assign energy to each request for billing. This paper proposes StepSplit, which splits each inference step's energy into a shared part and a request-specific part to derive a closed-form Shapley value. Unified Far-field and Near-field Codebook Design for XL-array Systems

In briefA unified codebook for far-field and near-field XL-array systems

SummaryThis paper addresses the inefficiency of conventional DFT-based Type-I codebooks and the high storage or feedback costs of existing near-field alternatives in extremely large-scale antenna array (XL-array) systems. It proposes a unified codebook for both far-field and near-field settings by expressing the near-field steering vector as a Hadamard product of an angular vector and a curvature-related vector. The legacy Type-I codebook is reused for angle quantization, with only a lightweight auxiliary curvature codebook added. Numerical results show near-optimal beamforming performance, close to the perfect-CSI upper bound, with significantly reduced feedback overhead compared with benchmark schemes.

SummaryThis paper studies how recurrent and state-space language models compress the past into a fixed-size state. Third-Order Asymptotics for Fixed-Length Lossy Compression of Discrete Memoryless Sources

In briefThird-order asymptotics for fixed-length lossy compression of discrete sources

SummaryThis paper determines the third-order asymptotics of the logarithm of the minimum codebook size for fixed-length lossy compression of discrete memoryless sources. For every interior distortion level under an excess-distortion probability constraint, it establishes a third-order correction of (1/2)log n + O(1), where n is the blocklength. The result sharpens general bounds by Kostina and Verdú (2012). The main technical contribution is the converse for positive dispersion, proved with a multivariate weighted-strip estimate.

Statistical Analysis of the Structure from Noise Phenomenon in Generalized Procrustes Problems

In briefStatistical analysis of structure appearing from pure noise in generalized Procrustes alignment

SummaryIn cryo-electron microscopy, very noisy observations are aligned and averaged using reference templates, which can yield output resembling the template even when the data contain only noise, a phenomenon called Einstein from noise. The paper studies template matching and averaging in the generalized orthogonal Procrustes problem. It shows that under pure Gaussian noise the mean of the aligned-and-averaged estimator shares the template's left and right singular-vector bases while its singular values may be distorted. The authors bound this distortion, give finite-sample operator-norm bounds, and specify sample sizes needed for the population mean to dominate random fluctuations.

Jiayang Yin, Shuyang Ling

SHADOWPREFILL: Keeping Your Next Model Warm for Per-Step Model Routing in Agentic LLM Serving

In briefSHADOWPREFILL: Pre-warming the Next Model for Per-Step Model Routing in Agentic LLM Serving

SummaryThis paper presents SHADOWPREFILL, a method for serving agentic LLM workloads where a router switches models at each step. Switching models discards cached context, forcing the new model to reprocess the session context before responding. SHADOWPREFILL uses spare capacity on instances to prefill the context on the model the router did not choose, so the prefix is already cached at the next switch. In experiments across five agentic benchmark suites with 24 model pairs, it reduces switch-call latency by 8.5-21.4% and routed token cost by 22.5-46.1%, while normal traffic keeps its SLO.

Haoran Qiu, Rohan Mahapatra, Rodrigo Fonseca et al.
