Uniform-Price Mechanism Design for Automated Market Makers

In briefUniform-price mechanism design for automated market makers

SummaryThis paper studies batch double auctions in decentralized exchanges where an automated market maker (AMM) must keep post-trade reserve products at least as large as initial reserves. It proposes a randomized uniform-price mechanism based on binary quantity buckets, which randomly selects among three clearing modes and is ex-post feasible, individually rational, and weakly quantity incentive compatible. The mechanism achieves an expected volume of Ω(1/(1+log γ)²) times the optimal unconstrained volume minus O(q̄). The authors also show that full incentive compatibility forces a worst-case approximation ratio of O(1/γ).

Zhike Peng

Bayesian Learning of Parametric Mean Field Games with Common Noise

In briefBayesian learning of mean field game parameters under common noise, recovering unknown settings from partial random observations

SummaryThe paper studies how to infer unknown parameters of Mean Field Games from partial observations of population trajectories made random by common noise. The authors introduce the Bayesian-Extended Mean Field Game (BE-MFG), whose single Nash equilibrium encodes equilibrium policies for all parameters, enabling efficient learning of the conditional density of mean-field trajectories. An experiment on public electricity market data suggests it can also be applied to real-world data.

A Generalized Half-Singleton Bound for List Decoding of Insertion/Deletion Errors

In briefUpper Limit on Code Rate for Insertion/Deletion List Decoding

SummaryThis paper derives an upper bound on the rate of linear codes that can be list decoded from insertion and deletion errors. It shows that, with a fixed list size L, a linear code can list decode from at most n − (L/(L+1))k + 1 insdel errors, and that this bound is strict when the code does not contain the all-ones vector. The authors also give a simple construction achieving the optimal rate for any list size while correcting a single insdel error. Results apply to linear codes over finite fields of size q ≥ 3.

On the Convergence of Iterated Round Robin

In briefOn the Convergence of Iterated Round Robin

SummaryRound Robin is a simple, widely used method for allocating indivisible goods that guarantees envy-freeness up to one item under additive preferences. This paper studies an iterated version where agents observe outcomes and revise their reported preferences via best-response dynamics. With full information, cycles can occur even under strong restrictions, but restricting to item-preserving swaps guarantees convergence to a pure Nash equilibrium. With partial information, a more cautious agent behaviour makes iterated Round Robin strategyproof.
