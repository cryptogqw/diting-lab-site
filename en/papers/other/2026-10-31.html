Rethinking KV Cache Eviction for Agentic LLM Serving

In briefRethinking KV Cache Eviction for Agent LLM Serving

SummaryThis paper addresses memory pressure from key-value caches in large language model serving, which requires eviction when capacity runs short. It proposes keeping shared prefixes, evicting cache from the start of the remaining history, and retaining the cached suffix, and builds a system called Penelope around this idea. Implemented in SGLang and tested on three Qwen3 model sizes with real agent traces, it reduces mean time to first token by up to 60% and job completion time by up to 37% with a GPU-only cache. With a two-tier GPU-CPU cache, the reductions reach up to 76% and 60%.

Jiakun Fan, Han Meng, Yuxuan Xia et al.

The Pareto Secretary Problem

In briefThe Pareto Secretary Problem: online selection of Pareto-optimal observations

SummaryThis paper studies an online selection problem in which observations arrive one at a time from an i.i.d. sample, and the goal is to pick one that is Pareto-maximal, meaning no other observation is at least as good in every coordinate and strictly better in one. It determines the optimal failure-probability order for fixed dimensions, covering independent coordinates and fixed positive definite Gaussian covariances. It also reports results under arbitrary dependence, where optimal success can tend to about 0.58.

Andr\'es Cristi, Yilin Kong, Jiechen Zhang

From Scalar Games to Unconstrained Network Zero-Sum Games: Transfer Principles for Gradient-Based Learning

In briefTransfer principles for gradient-based learning from simple scalar games to large network zero-sum games

SummaryThe paper studies a broad class of gradient-based learning rules in zero-sum games. It shows that if a rule converges on all scalar zero-sum games, it also converges on any network zero-sum game, under a step-size condition tied to the norm of the game operator. Finite-time rates also transfer mode by mode, so the worst-case network response equals the largest scalar rate. Thus, analyzing scalar games is enough to characterize convergence over the network class.

Gaoqi Xue, James P. Bailey

Capacity Meets the Four Subspace Problem: Distributed Linear Computation and the Cross-Ratio

In briefCapacity and the Four Subspace Problem: Distributed Linear Computation and Cross-Ratio

SummaryThe paper studies a setting where several users each observe a fixed linear function of common data, and a server must recover another linear function with zero error from one message per user. It examines the smallest case, three users observing a, b, and a+b while the server wants a+λb, whose four functions form lines with cross-ratio λ. The capacity depends on λ and the prime subfield: it equals 2 for harmonic cross-ratios, 3/2 for λ outside the prime subfield, and lies in [3p/(2p-1), 2] for other prime-subfield values.

Hua Sun

Decentralized Dynamic Stable Matching

In briefDecentralized Dynamic Stable Matching: Computing and Learting Optimal Stable Matchings Without Clocks or Direct Communication

SummaryThis paper studies how players can decentrally compute the player-optimal stable matching when players arrive and depart unannounced, without a common clock or direct communication. For known preferences, it gives a deterministic polynomial-time self-calibrating protocol with improved cost bound O(t_*+K^2 log T). For unknown preferences, it presents a decentralized learning protocol with expected cost O(t_*+K^4 Δ_min^{-2} log T), and a lower bound shows the dependence on T and Δ_min is tight.

In briefApproximate-support Donoho–Stark uncertainty principles for k-Hadamard operators

Rates and Nearly-Lossless State over a Multi-Access Channel with Causal State Information

In briefAchievable rates and nearly lossless state transmission over a multi-access channel with causal state information

SummaryThis paper studies transmitting both data and state over a memoryless state-dependent multi-access channel where encoders have causal state information. The state has a common component known to both encoders and two private components, each known to one encoder. Data must be recovered with arbitrarily small error probability, and the state sequence with arbitrarily small symbol-error rate. Assuming the capacity region without the state reconstruction constraint is nonempty, the authors derive the capacity region and show it is achievable using a Block-Markov scheme with binning and superposition coding.

Batch Universal Prediction via Conditional Additive Smoothing

In briefBatch universal prediction via conditional additive smoothing

SummaryThe paper studies batch regret, a measure of how well a predictor performs on a fresh test sequence after observing a batch of training sequences from the same unknown source. It analyzes conditional additive smoothing estimators for binary memoryless and first-order Markov sources, deriving their asymptotic batch regret. For memoryless sources, the optimal bias and resulting regret are shown to vary with the training batch size, extending Krichevsky's Poisson-based analysis. The authors also introduce a Rényi analogue of batch regret and identify the optimal predictors as conditional α-NML estimators.

Marco Bondaschi, Michael Gastpar
