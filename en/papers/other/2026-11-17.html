Similarity-Aware Client-Side Reordering for Server-Side Storage Compression in Distributed File Systems

In briefClient-side reordering of data blocks to improve server-side compression in distributed file systems

SummaryThis paper proposes a client-side framework that reorders physical block layout before data is persisted, so that similar blocks share the same compression scope. It works with existing storage-server compressors without replacing them. Implemented in the SkyFS distributed file system with Zstandard on OpenZFS, it reduces physical storage by up to 42.9% compared with logical-order writes. Sequential-read throughput and random-read IOPS remain at 94-108% and 91-99% of baseline, respectively.

Yuanhong Huang, Zicong Chen, Yili Ma et al.

Exploiting Aggregate Feedback for Decentralized Equilibrium Learning in Markov Games: A Smart Grid Application

In briefDecentralized equilibrium learning in aggregate-reward Markov games, applied to smart grids

SummaryThis paper studies Markov games in which each agent's payoff depends on its own action and an aggregate of others' actions. It proposes a stage-based V-learning algorithm that combines aggregate-based prediction with hybrid-feedback Exp3-IX, requiring no opponent information or transition kernel knowledge. The authors prove a high-probability regret bound tied to aggregate prediction errors and show the method learns approximate coarse correlated equilibria. In a microgrid with prosumers and shared storage, it yields smaller deviation gaps than three learning baselines.

Siying Huang, Yifen Mu, Ge Chen et al.

Byzantine Resilience of Robot Gathering under Round-Robin Scheduling

In briefByzantine-tolerant robot gathering under round-robin scheduling

SummaryThis paper studies deterministic gathering of n anonymous, oblivious mobile robots in the plane under round-robin scheduling with rigid movement, when f Byzantine robots are present. The goal is for all correct robots to gather at one point in finite time and remain there forever. The authors analyze ToMax, a simple algorithm in which robots move to a point of maximum multiplicity and stay still only if their position is the unique maximum. They prove ToMax achieves gathering whenever f is at most floor(sqrt(n)/2), establishing an Ω(sqrt(n)) lower bound on achievable Byzantine resilience.

Fabian Frei, Yuichi Sudo, Koichi Wada

Hardness and Inapproximability of the Covering Radius for LDPC Codes

In briefComputational hardness of the covering radius for sparse LDPC parity-check codes

SummaryThis paper studies the covering-radius decision problem for sparse binary parity-check matrices with bounded row and column weights, asking whether every syndrome can be produced by an error vector of Hamming weight at most r. It proves the problem is Π₂ᴾ-complete when the maximum row and column weights are three, and remains Π₂ᴾ-hard under a constant multiplicative gap. The authors also derive an explicit NP-hard approximation factor under the same weight constraints, starting from Vertex Cover on 3-regular graphs. They note that if either maximum weight is at most two, the covering radius is computable in polynomial time.

WhyThe authors show covering-radius decision for LDPC codes is Π2P-complete at weight three, with a constant gap and a sharp weight-threshold result, mainly impacting coding-theory complexity.

Massimo Battaglioni, Fabrizio Marinelli

CheckCo: Checkpoint-Coordinated Load Balancing for Skewed Event Streams at Scale

In briefCheckCo: a checkpoint-based method for balancing uneven event streams across workers

SummaryIn event streams, a few heavy keys can generate far more events than others, overloading some workers. CheckCo is a load-balancing algorithm that estimates per-key frequencies from the live stream without prior statistics. At each checkpoint, it merges the counts into a partitioning function that splits keys in proportion to their estimated frequency and assigns them to workers. On a production pipeline handling over 100 billion events per day, the most loaded worker received 9.8% more events than the daily average.

In briefSubsystem stabilizer (r,δ) locally recoverable codes: characterization, bounds, constructions

SummaryThis paper studies locally recoverable subsystem stabilizer codes, where each coordinate lies in a repair set of size between δ and r+δ-1 that corrects up to δ-1 erasures while preserving the logical subsystem. In briefResolving the Computational and Structural Complexity of Bertrand Networks

SummaryThis paper studies the Bertrand network game of Babaioff et al. (2013), in which sellers must post a single price across captive and shared markets. It proves that computing a constant-error approximate Nash equilibrium in general Bertrand networks is PPAD-complete, even on sparse bipartite graphs of maximum degree five. In briefFixed points and disequilibrium of negators for hyperbolic-valued probability distributions

SummaryThis paper studies the fixed-point structure of probability negators acting on hyperbolic-valued probability distributions and how disequilibrium changes along their iterates. Disequilibrium is defined as the separation between a distribution and the uniform distribution determined by its hyperbolic mass, using Euclidean, ℓ1, and Jensen–Shannon metrics. Axioms for relative entropy

In briefAxiomatic characterization of relative entropy

SummaryThe paper characterizes positive finite multiples of Araki relative entropy among non-degenerate divergences for normal states on arbitrary von Neumann algebras. The axioms are monotonicity under normal unital completely positive maps, tensor additivity, superadditivity for product second states, and separate lower semicontinuity in the predual norm. A conditional-expectation identity can replace the two tensor assumptions. The proof relies on order bounds and asymptotic estimates on tensor powers, and an appendix establishes the needed analytic estimates using modular theory and Haagerup L^p spaces. A corresponding characterization is also obtained in the C*-algebraic setting.

WhyThe authors axiomatically characterize Araki relative entropy on arbitrary von Neumann algebras and extend the result to the C* setting.
