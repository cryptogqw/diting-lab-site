Almost-Isometric Embeddings of Graphs into Riemannian Manifolds

In briefNear-Isometric Embeddings of Graphs into Curved Surfaces and Network Coding

SummaryThis paper studies embedding the shortest-path metric of a finite weighted graph into a smooth closed surface. For any small ε>0, the authors construct a smooth closed orientable surface in ℝ³ that admits a noncontractive (1+ε)-embedding of the graph's vertices. The construction is also used to transfer network communication schemes between graphs and surfaces, comparing their coding advantages.

WhyThe authors report that any finite weighted graph embeds into a smooth closed surface with distortion approaching one, transferring coding advantage bounds between graphs and surfaces.

Less Rational, More Transparent: Censorship as (Robustly) Optimal Disclosure

In briefLess Rational, More Transparent: Censorship as Optimal Disclosure

SummaryThe paper studies how platforms should disclose information to boundedly rational users who follow the logit quantal response model, where the platform may not know their rationality level. It finds that censorship-style disclosure, which pools all sufficiently favorable options under a common message and reveals the rest individually, remains optimal or robustly optimal whether rationality is known or uncertain. The authors conclude that platforms should keep badge-based disclosure but narrow its pooling coverage to be more transparent.

Yiding Feng, Chien-Ju Ho, Wei Tang et al.

In briefMotivo: Faster, More Energy-Efficient Genome Graph Read Mapping via Motif-Based Co-Design

SummaryThis paper addresses the bottleneck of mapping sequenced genome reads to genome graphs, which represent genetic diversity as alternative paths. CXL Coherence Writes Back What Surviving Hosts See, Leaving Flushes Only for Ordering

In briefCXL coherence writes modified lines back before other hosts read them, so flushes are needed only for ordering

SummaryThis paper studies data safety in multi-host systems sharing memory over CXL when one host crashes. The authors argue that when the crashed host's clients die with it, the flushes needed after a host crash are those that order lines on the memory device, not those that make completed operations durable. Their argument rests on CXL's requirement that a modified line is written back before another host accesses it. The claim is proved for single-store operations and tested on small models of three published designs, including a persistent hash index that writes fewer lines in two workloads.

Harvey Dam, Ganesh Gopalakrishnan

Exponential memory/query-complexity tradeoffs in high-accuracy feasibility problems

In briefMemory and query trade-offs in high-accuracy feasibility problems

SummaryThis paper studies the feasibility problem of finding a point inside a hidden small ball within the unit ball, given access to a separation oracle, focusing on very high accuracy. Previously known low-memory algorithms need query counts exponential in the dimension, but no nontrivial lower bound linking memory and queries existed. The authors prove that any algorithm using d^{1+δ}ln(1/ε) bits of memory requires at least (d ln(1/ε))^{Ω̃(d^{(1-δ)/8})} oracle calls.

WhyThe authors give a first non-trivial memory/query tradeoff lower bound for high-accuracy feasibility, exponential in dimension, matching known low-memory algorithms.

From Footprints to Service Regions: Modeling and Analysis of Dense LEO Satellite Networks Using an Orbit-Aware Cox Point Process

In briefFrom Footprints to Service Regions: Modeling Dense LEO Satellite Networks

SummaryThis paper studies the service region of each satellite in dense low Earth orbit networks, meaning the set of locations it actually serves. Using an orbit-aware Cox point process on a common orbital shell, the authors model each service region as the intersection of a spherical-cap footprint and a spherical Voronoi cell under nearest-satellite association. Palm calculus yields exact expressions for mean area and perimeter and a coverage-load identity, which are connected to an approximate noise-limited user rate. The model is also compared with Starlink-type Walker constellations and reproduces their mean area and perimeter over broad latitude ranges.

WhyThe authors model LEO service regions with an orbit-aware Cox process, derive exact mean area, perimeter and coverage-load expressions via Palm calculus, and compare against Walker constellations.

In briefWhen Does Splitting LLM Serving Help? A Workload-Architecture Study

Across 150 audited test conditions, four independent single-GPU replicas gave the lowest end-to-end latency for short chat and long output and were stable in every condition.
