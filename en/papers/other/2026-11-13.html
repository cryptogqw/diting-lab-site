New Results on the Subfield Metric

In briefNew Results on the Subfield Metric: Advances in Error-Correcting Code Theory

SummaryThis paper further develops the recently introduced λ-subfield metric, studying extremal codes, classical bounds and asymptotic densities. It examines Maximum λ-subfield Distance (MλD) codes that attain a Singleton-type bound, establishing structural restrictions on their parameters and extending the theory to sublinear codes. The asymptotic density of MλD codes is compared with that of MDS and MRD codes across several parameter regimes. The paper also addresses Plotkin-type bounds, Griesmer-, Elias- and Johnson-type bounds.

Ana Kosovac, Jeanette Plechinger, Nikola Spirovski et al.

Go under Chinese Rules with a Succinct Game History is Complete for Exponential Space

In briefGo under Chinese rules with succinct game history is EXPSPACE-complete

SummaryThis paper studies the computational complexity of Go under Chinese rules, which include a no-repetition (super-ko) rule. The authors introduce a succinct game history, a Boolean circuit that determines whether a board position already occurred in the game. They prove that deciding whether Black has a winning strategy under this representation is EXPSPACE-complete. The starting position in the hardness proof can be reached by legal play.

WhyThe authors introduce succinct game histories and report EXPSPACE-completeness of Go under Chinese rules with no-repetition, resolving a long-open question.

Markus Bl\"aser, Felix Czerner

In briefUpper bounds on codes that correct and detect errors together

SummaryThis paper derives upper bounds on the size of codes over q-ary Hamming spaces that jointly correct errors of weight up to c and detect errors of weight between c+1 and c+c'. Wireless Node Ranking via Fourier-Driven Maximum-Likelihood Estimation (extended version)

In briefRanking wireless nodes in a line using Fourier-based maximum-likelihood estimation

SummaryThis paper applies non-abelian Fourier theory to maximum-likelihood estimation of node order in a line topology, using signal strength measurements affected only by shadowing. For uncorrelated shadowing, the authors report up to a 60-fold speedup over standard methods. For correlated Gaussian shadowing with a singular covariance, recovery is exact and independent of shadowing variance, a result the implementation confirms for σ/α up to 7.0. In briefProof of a conjecture on maximizing entropy for cube projections

SIMTurn: Lightweight Divergent Subgroup Scheduling for Intra-Warp Synchronization

In briefSIMTurn: Lightweight Scheduling of Divergent Thread Groups to Prevent Deadlock in GPU Warps

SummaryThis paper presents SIMTurn, a lightweight mechanism combining compiler and hardware support to let divergent thread subgroups within a GPU warp make forward progress while keeping stack-based reconvergence. The compiler inserts a yield instruction at potentially blocking loops, and a bounded pool of resumable subgroups lets a waiting subgroup hand execution to a sibling that can advance. Implemented on the open-source Vortex GPGPU, SIMTurn adds 17-24% scheduler-area overhead relative to IPDOM, versus 39-91% for per-thread ITS. On kernels with cross-subgroup parallelism it tracks ITS closely and reaches up to 2.7x the speed of a software-serialized IPDOM baseline.

Nikhil Rout, Joe Samuel Rosh, Blaise Tine

Toward the Minimal Set of Information Inequalities in Regenerating Codes: An Algebraic Approach

In briefMinimal Information Inequalities for Regenerating Codes via Algebra

SummaryThis paper studies information-theoretic outer bounds for exact-repair regenerating codes, which can be written as linear programs but whose Shannon-type systems contain many implicit equalities and redundant inequalities. The authors specify a reduced system for the (n,n-1,n-1) exact-repair model using the closure structure induced by repair dependencies, and prove it is equivalent to the original system. They provide an algorithm that constructs this system for any n, counts its entropy coordinates, and bounds its number of inequalities. For every n≥2, they show the reduced system has no implicit equalities and that each distinct defining inequality is nonredundant and defines a facet.

The Tournament Equilibrium Set Is PSPACE-Complete, Even under Hereditary Uniqueness

In briefProves that checking membership in the tournament equilibrium set is PSPACE-complete

SummaryThis paper studies the tournament equilibrium set (TEQ), a voting rule proposed by Schwartz in 1990 that relies on pairwise majority comparisons and is defined recursively. It proves that deciding whether a given alternative belongs to TEQ is PSPACE-complete, closing the gap between the previously known NP-hardness and PSPACE membership. The hardness holds even when every nonempty subtournament has a unique minimal retentive set. The paper also shows how the complexity changes with the bound on the number of dominators.

WhyThe authors prove TEQ membership is PSPACE-complete, even under hereditary uniqueness, and give a fuller dominator-bound complexity classification for tournament solutions.

In briefSubmodular secretary problem: tight 1-1/e for monotone objectives, and a ratio strictly between 1/e and 1/2 in general

SummaryThe paper studies the submodular secretary problem with a cardinality constraint, where elements arrive in random order and must be accepted or rejected irrevocably to maximize a nonnegative submodular function over sets of at most k elements. For monotone objectives, the optimal competitive ratio tends to exactly 1-1/e as k grows, achieved by an algorithm using at most 4n+1 value queries. For general objectives, the optimal limiting ratio is shown to lie strictly between 1/e and 1/2, using a new technique called eligibility attenuation.
