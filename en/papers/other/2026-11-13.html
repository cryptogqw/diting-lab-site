The Tournament Equilibrium Set Is PSPACE-Complete, Even under Hereditary Uniqueness

In briefProves that checking membership in the tournament equilibrium set is PSPACE-complete

SummaryThis paper studies the tournament equilibrium set (TEQ), a voting rule proposed by Schwartz in 1990 that relies on pairwise majority comparisons and is defined recursively. It proves that deciding whether a given alternative belongs to TEQ is PSPACE-complete, closing the gap between the previously known NP-hardness and PSPACE membership. The hardness holds even when every nonempty subtournament has a unique minimal retentive set. The paper also shows how the complexity changes with the bound on the number of dominators.

WhyThe authors prove TEQ membership is PSPACE-complete, even under hereditary uniqueness, and give a fuller dominator-bound complexity classification for tournament solutions.

Ethan Dickey, Athina Terzoglou

The Submodular Secretary Problem: Tight 1-1/e for Monotone Objectives, and Strictly Between 1/e and 1/2 in General

In briefSubmodular secretary problem: tight 1-1/e for monotone objectives, and a ratio strictly between 1/e and 1/2 in general

SummaryThe paper studies the submodular secretary problem with a cardinality constraint, where elements arrive in random order and must be accepted or rejected irrevocably to maximize a nonnegative submodular function over sets of at most k elements. For monotone objectives, the optimal competitive ratio tends to exactly 1-1/e as k grows, achieved by an algorithm using at most 4n+1 value queries. For general objectives, the optimal limiting ratio is shown to lie strictly between 1/e and 1/2, using a new technique called eligibility attenuation.

WhyThe authors give a tight 1-1/e ratio for monotone objectives with one algorithm, and improve general objectives to a value strictly between 1/e and 1/2 via eligibility attenuation.

Hamed Abdi, Kiarash Banihashem, MohammadTaghi Hajiaghayi et al.

Fictitious Play When Agents Are Not Expected-Utility Maximizers: A Geometric Analysis in 2-Action Games

In briefFictitious play for agents using nonlinear risk-based evaluation in 2×2 games

SummaryThis paper studies nonlinear fictitious play, where agents evaluate each action with a possibly nonlinear functional of the opponent's payoff distribution rather than maximizing expected payoff. The authors identify a single-crossing property that preserves the classical geometry of fictitious play in 2×2 games and analyze convergence geometrically even when this property fails. They show that in a 2×3 game, convergence can fail even in continuous time when the two-action player uses conditional value-at-risk. They also give a finite-crossing condition that ensures convergence in general 2×n games.

Hao Liang, Solaleh Mohammadi, Kaiqing Zhang

Sowing the Seeds of Intractability: Kalah is PSPACE-complete

In brief"Sowing the Seeds of Intractability: Kalah is PSPACE-complete" — a proof that deciding a forced win in the sowing game Kalah is PSPACE-complete as board size grows

SummaryThis paper studies Kalah, a classic two-player sowing game in the mancala family, with the number of pits per side treated as part of the input. It shows that deciding whether a designated player can force a win from an arbitrary position is PSPACE-complete. The proof introduces an intermediate game, Line Kalah, reduces quantified Boolean formulas to it, and embeds the line positions into Kalah with both stores empty.

WhyThe authors prove that deciding forced wins in generalized Kalah is PSPACE-complete via a reduction from QBF through an intermediate Line Kalah problem, valid under both capture conventions and bonus

Ethan Dickey, Ethan Mader

PACER: GPU Excursion Management for ML Workloads

In briefPACER: Managing GPU Power Overshoots for Machine Learning Workloads

SummaryThis paper examines intra-GPU performance variability caused by brief power excursions, which occur when GPU power temporarily exceeds its rated limits. The authors are the first to characterize how large and how frequent these overshoots are, finding them especially common in machine learning workloads with short, power-hungry kernels. They design hardware and firmware mechanisms to make power management more consistent and reduce this variability. Across popular workloads, the approach improves performance by 3-12% over state-of-the-art GPU power management and comes within 3% of an oracular solution.

Rutwik Jain, Nathaniel Morris, Yiwei Jiang et al.

Learning Dynamics of the Influencer's Game in Resource Landscapes

In briefLearning dynamics of a spatial game on resource landscapes

SummaryThis paper introduces the influencer's game, a spatial game in which players occupy positions in a trait space and receive shares of resources based on their relative influence at each trait point. The authors show that a unique symmetric Nash equilibrium exists when the influence kernel is strictly log-concave, and they analyze how stability depends on kernel parameters, player count, and resource distribution shape. Beyond the equilibrium analysis, the paper identifies stability thresholds and bifurcation types, and it supports the results with real-world resource data and multi-agent reinforcement learning experiments.

WhyThe authors introduce a new spatial game, prove a unique symmetric equilibrium, derive a stability threshold and bifurcations, and test with real data and reinforcement learning.

Mark Lovett Feng Fu

Broadcast, Don't Replicate: Eliminating Weight HBM in Datacenter LLM Serving

In briefBroadcast Instead of Copying: Removing Weight HBM from Datacenter LLM Inference

SummaryThis paper proposes Optical Broadcast Memory (OBM), a hierarchical fiber architecture that replaces an earlier optical fiber loop memory design for serving large language models. A central memory server continuously broadcasts model weights, and inference nodes passively tap the data stream using receive-only co-packaged optics. Evaluated at 80,000 accelerators, the approach eliminates petabytes of HBM while matching or exceeding an HBM-based system in throughput and energy per token.

Hannah Atmer, Yuan Yao, Thiemo Voigt et al.
