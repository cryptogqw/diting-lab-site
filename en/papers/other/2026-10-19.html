Regularizing the Veto Core

In briefRegularizing the Veto Core: adding a small weight to each candidate so voting rules stay Pareto-efficient

SummaryThe paper studies voting rules that pick a candidate close to voters using only their rankings, a setting called metric distortion. It adds a small regularization term to each candidate's weight, which restores Pareto-efficiency to the SimultaneousPluralityVeto rule. The authors also analyze a rank score veto core and bound its distortion. The results address an open question posed by Kızılkaya and Kempe.

M. August Rossi

Basis Selection For Analog Coded Computing Under Differential Privacy Constraints

In briefChoosing encoding basis functions for analog coded computing under differential privacy, preserving exact recovery while balancing privacy and accuracy.

SummaryThis paper studies how to choose encoding basis functions in analog coded computing. The authors note that these functions affect not only reconstruction and numerical stability but also the statistics visible to colluding workers, and thus data privacy. They relax the full orthogonality constraints of the Lagrange basis to obtain a parameterized family of polynomial bases that still allow exact recovery, and derive a basis-dependent differential privacy characterization. Numerical optimization then selects data and noise basis functions to meet a given (ε,δ)-DP requirement. Results show that, at the same privacy level, the optimized bases substantially reduce floating-point reconstruction error compared with conventional ALCC.

Aaditya Sharma, Rimpi Borah, and J. Harshan

Waveguide Deployment Optimization for Pinching-Antenna Systems

In briefOptimizing Waveguide Layout for Pinching-Antenna Communication Systems

SummaryThis paper studies how to optimally deploy waveguides for pinching-antenna systems over a rectangular service region, rather than assuming parallel waveguides. For the two-waveguide case, it derives a closed-form sufficient condition, based on waveguide height and region aspect ratio, under which a deployment parallel to the longer side is globally optimal. For multiple waveguides, it jointly optimizes orientation and offset using an adaptive exchange algorithm. Numerical results show that nonparallel layouts offer clear gains at intermediate heights, while parallel or center-concentrated layouts are preferable in other regimes.

Sustainable and Envy-free Multi-Period Allocation

In briefFair, Sustainable Allocation of Renewable Resources Over Multiple Periods

SummaryThis paper studies how to fairly allocate renewable resources to agents over multiple periods, where resource quantities evolve over time according to renewal functions. Allocations must satisfy viability and capacity constraints and remain envy-free over the whole time horizon. The authors analyze the computational and parameterized complexity of finding such allocations across the numbers of agents, resources, and periods. They show the problem is NP-hard under unary encoding even when any one of these dimensions is fixed to a constant, with other cases depending on the dimension pair, renewal function class, and numerical encoding.

On the Adaptive Covering Complexity of Strings: Unifying Relative and Self-Referential Compression

In briefAdaptive Covering Complexity: Linking Dictionary-Based and Self-Referential String Compression

SummaryThe paper introduces adaptive covering complexity, the minimum number of contiguous blocks needed to partition a string, where each block may come from a fixed external dictionary or the already decoded prefix. It proves that dynamic self-reference can reduce the phrase count by at most an exponential factor compared with static dictionary covering. The authors establish equivalences with relative Lempel-Ziv and non-overlapping LZ77, show the longest-match greedy parse is optimal, and give an O(M + n) implementation using dual suffix automata. Dictionary selection is shown to be NP-hard.

From Formulation to Articulation: Considerations for AI-Assisted Research in Information Theory

In briefFrom Problem Setup to Explanation: AI-Assisted Research in Information Theory

SummaryThis article examines how AI contributes to information-theory research in different ways, such as suggesting proofs, taking part in mathematical dialogue, coordinating computation, or acting as part of a search framework.
