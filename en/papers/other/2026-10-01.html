Robustness in Mechanism Design

In briefRobustness in Mechanism Design: A Worst-Case Review of Auction and Exchange Mechanisms

SummaryThis article reviews robust design and analysis of auctions, using the computer science paradigm that measures robustness by the worst-case ratio, over environments, between a robust auction's performance and an optimal auction's. It covers robust analysis of the first-price auction for welfare, prior-robust auction design for revenue, and prior-robust exchange mechanism design for gains from trade. It also shows that the revelation principle can lose performance in robust design, quantified by the revelation gap.

Jason Hartline

Efficient Demodulation for Coset Codes on Lattices

In briefFast demodulation for lattice-based coset codes (quantize-walk method)

SummaryThis paper addresses the high complexity of soft demodulation for lattice constellations by proposing fast quantize-walk demodulation (FQWD) for multilevel coset codes. The method uses radial projections onto the Voronoi cells of the D4 and E8 lattices to reduce complexity from O(d2^{sd}) to O(sd2^d). Experiments show only negligible block error rate loss relative to maximum likelihood demodulation under AWGN.

Chloe Makdad, Leopold Bertholet, Mairead Cummins et al.

Optimal Universal Coding of Integers

In briefOptimal Universal Coding of Integers: Numerically Determining the Minimum Expansion Factor

SummaryThis paper studies universal coding of integers (UCI), which assigns binary codewords to positive integers so that the average codeword length stays within K times max{1,H(P)} for every nonincreasing source distribution. The authors show that the worst-case ratio for any universal code is attained by a family of one-point-plus-uniform-tail distributions. They also introduce a UCI inequality, analogous to the Kraft inequality for prefix codes, which yields an equivalent definition of the optimal minimum expansion factor C*. Numerical computation gives C* = 2.000124757036101…, with the first fifteen decimal digits certified.

Wei Yan, Yunghsiang S. Han, and Leqian Zheng

In briefGPU-Initiated Communication: A Low-Level Performance Dissection

SummaryThis paper examines how GPU threads post RDMA operations directly to the network interface card, a mechanism used by NVSHMEM, NCCL GIN, and DeepEP. The authors build minimal GPU and CPU-proxy transports and benchmark them against several libraries on NVIDIA H100, H200, B200, and GB200 systems. SummaryThis paper adds a short shaping code to LDPC-coded QPSK so that some codeword bits control how often lower- and higher-energy constellation points are sent.
