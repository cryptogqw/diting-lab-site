You All Need to Hear This: Wisdom of Crowds with Deliberating Jurors

In briefCollective Wisdom from Deliberating Jurors: Does Discussion Improve Group Accuracy?

SummaryThe paper presents a formal model of deliberation in which agents probabilistically acquire evidence and selectively disclose it to peers to induce opinion change. Using a jury theorem and simulations, the authors examine whether deliberation improves collective accuracy. They identify conditions under which wisdom of crowds emerges after a few deliberation steps. The effect is shown to be sensitive to the evidence acquisition mechanism and the diversity of the initial evidence distribution.

Rosa Bauer, Adrian Haret

DIALER: A Case for Improving Rare-Class Accuracy in Retraining-Free Edge Video Analytics

In briefDIALER: Improving Rare-Class Accuracy in Retraining-Free Edge Video Analytics

SummaryLightweight models used for edge video analytics lose accuracy as live video streams shift in distribution, and continuous retraining strains limited edge compute. The paper finds that vision foundation models offer high average accuracy without retraining and with substantial compute savings, but often misclassify critical rare classes as similar common ones. The authors present DIALER, which uses spare GPU cycles to run pre-built multi-stage corrections for common rare-to-common confusion pairs. On four real-world driving datasets, it improves rare-class accuracy by up to 14.0% without interfering with real-time multi-stream inference.

In briefHow hard is it to compute Nash equilibria in Colonel Blotto games?

SummaryThe paper studies how hard it is to compute Nash equilibria in discrete Colonel Blotto games. It shows that approximate equilibria are PPAD-hard even with only two battlefields, with PPAD-completeness for the hardness results. On the signaling dimension of the cross-polytope

In briefSignaling dimension of cross-polytopes: how many classical resources are needed to mimic them

SummaryThis paper studies the signaling dimension of cross-polytopes in the general probabilistic model of information theory. The signaling dimension is the smallest classical communication complexity needed to perfectly reproduce a system's input-output correlations. It was previously known to be 3 for three, four, and five dimensional cross-polytopes. The authors give logarithmic lower and upper bounds for higher dimensions, determining the value up to an additive gap of 2. The lower bound shows the signaling dimension grows without bound, unlike balls and hypercubes.

Tam\'as Bencze, P\'eter E. Frenkel

On the Tightness of Omura's Strong Converse Exponent and Critical Rates Above Capacity

In briefOn whether Omura's and Dueck–Körner's strong converse exponents match above capacity

SummaryThe paper studies a lower bound on the probability of correct decoding above channel capacity, Omura's sphere-packing-type exponent, and its refinement by Dueck and Körner. It notes that a claimed equality of the two exponents at all rates contains an error. The authors show that beyond a threshold rate the strong converse exponent is a line of unit slope set by the order-infinity Rényi capacity, and that the two exponents coincide up to a higher second threshold and differ strictly beyond it. Examples include modulo-additive channels, with no gap, and the binary erasure channel, where thresholds and the gap are given in closed form.

Hamdi Joudeh, Vincent Y. F. Tan
