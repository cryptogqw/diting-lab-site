Linear Network Coding for Sum All-Reduce over Ring Networks

In briefLinear network coding for summing data across ring networks

SummaryThis paper studies Sum All-Reduce, a collective operation in which every node on a directed ring obtains the componentwise sum of all nodes' vectors. The authors analyze the problem using linear network coding, which includes reduce-multicast as a special case, and give a necessary and sufficient feasibility condition based on the coding matrices. They prove a lower bound on the number of time units needed for a ring of N nodes with unit-capacity links, and show the bound is tight over every field, with explicit codes achieving it.

Yi Chen, Zhuoqi Tu, Shenghao Yang

A Matter of Stages: Energy-Latency Trade-offs in Multimodal LLM Serving

In briefStage Splitting: Energy and Latency Trade-offs in Multimodal LLM Serving

SummaryThis paper examines energy and latency trade-offs when serving multimodal large language models online, comparing fused serving with EPD disaggregation that places visual encoding, prefill, and decode on separate GPUs under a fixed three-GPU budget. Across 18 workload combinations, the authors also test single-stage and uniform frequency reduction. Disaggregation lowers GPU energy per successful request by 13.3-37.0% but raises mean TTFT, while uniform downclocking to 80% cuts energy by 11.7-18.6%. Based on these findings, the authors propose a budgeted frequency exploration strategy for selecting energy-efficient configurations under latency constraints.

Yifan Sun, Jiantong Jiang, Zhexiang Zhang et al.

The Exact Optimal Delivery Scheme for the Cyclically L-Separated Placement in Multi-Access Coded Caching

In briefExact optimal delivery for cyclically L-separated placement in multi-access coded caching

SummaryThis paper studies the multi-access coded caching system proposed by Hachem et al., where users access consecutive cache nodes in a cyclic topology. For cyclically L-separated placement, the authors derive a new converse bound and propose a determinant-based interference alignment scheme that achieves this bound exactly. They also give a general converse bound for arbitrary uncoded placements and show that the scheme's load is within a factor of two of the global bound under uncoded placement.

Kai Wan, Huimei Wei, Minquan Cheng et al.

Analysis and Design of Spatially Coupled LDPC Codes for Pulsed-Jamming-Resilient Space Telemetry

In briefDesigning Spatially Coupled LDPC Codes Resistant to Pulsed Jamming in Space Telemetry

SummaryThis paper addresses jamming threats to space telemetry downlinks by proposing an analysis and design framework for protograph-based spatially coupled LDPC codes. It derives a two-state EXIT recursion that tracks clean and jammed symbols separately, and studies non-interleaved pulsed jamming under random lifting. The codes are optimized with a constrained differential-evolution search. Under random pulse positions with jamming state information, the designed ensembles, decoded by sliding window decoding, reach a bit error rate of 10^-4 at lower signal-to-noise ratios than the benchmark codes.

Massimo Battaglioni, Mauro M. M. Costantino, Rebecca Giuliani et al.

Nearly Settling the Complexity of Connected ǎrepsilon-Envy-Free Cake Cutting

In briefNearly Settling the Complexity of Connected ε-Envy-Free Cake Cutting

SummaryThis paper studies fair cake cutting, seeking connected ε-envy-free allocations using Robertson-Webb queries as the measure of query complexity. It shows that polylogarithmic queries suffice for up to five agents, while seven or more agents require polynomial-in-1/ε queries even with additive valuations, leaving six agents unresolved. The paper also analyzes computational complexity, finding polynomial-time solvability for five agents under certain valuation models and UEOPL- and PPAD-hardness results for larger numbers of agents.

WhyThe authors settle the five-agent query complexity case, giving polylogarithmic upper bounds and polynomial lower bounds for seven or more agents, plus computational hardness results.

Network-Native DNN Architecture Synthesis for Distributed Inference

In briefDesigning neural networks that fit the physical network they run on

SummaryThis paper reverses the usual approach to distributed deep neural network inference by letting the physical network's topology define the dependencies between nodes, rather than partitioning a predefined model onto the network. Neural computation is allocated across nodes under communication, computation, storage, synchronization, and latency constraints. The authors introduce LPAS, a linear programming-based synthesis algorithm, which reports relative gaps below 0.6% to the LP upper bound and higher classification accuracy after training than the model-first baseline.

A Recursive Construction of Perfect Arrays of Every Shape 2^a₁×cdots×2^a_d over the Unit Quaternions

In briefA recursive method for building perfect quaternion arrays of any power-of-two shape

SummaryThe paper extends Blake's recursive construction of perfect sequences over the unit quaternions to arrays of any dimension. Operations applied one direction at a time produce perfect arrays of shapes 2^a × M_2 × ... × M_d. In contrast, perfect binary arrays of such shapes exist only under a restriction from a theorem of Jedwab and Kraemer, while the quaternion construction imposes no restriction on shape. The paper also shows that single arrays with a non-central twist exist in two dimensions.

Sam Blake

Top-Level Operational Equilibrium: When is the response of a distributed system predictable from its own telemetry?

In briefTop-Level Operational Equilibrium: Can a Distributed System's Response Be Predicted from Its Own Telemetry?

SummaryThe paper defines top-level operational equilibrium (TLOE), a condition under which a running distributed system's response to a control change can be predicted from its passive telemetry. TLOE requires stationarity with time-scale separation, sufficiency of a top-level aggregate for the controls, and time-reversibility of that aggregate. The authors prove that such an aggregate exists if and only if the minimal sufficient statistic of the controls is time-reversible, and they test the conditions on reference models. Validation on real telemetry is left to future work.

V\'ictor Casamayor Pujol, Nefeli-Marina Rouska, Boris Sedlak

A Combinatorial Characterization of Immunity to the Strong Braess Paradox

In briefA combinatorial test for when traffic-style games avoid the strong Braess paradox

SummaryThis paper studies the strong Braess paradox in nonatomic congestion games, where cutting resource costs or demands raises at least one group's equilibrium cost. It gives a combinatorial characterization of when a system of strategies is immune to this paradox, using series-parallel matroid conditions, and resolves an open question posed by Fujishige et al. (2017). The authors also show that whenever immunity fails, a paradox can be constructed with unit demands and affine costs, and that nonimmunity has polynomial-size certificates when strategies are listed explicitly.

WhyThe authors resolve an open question of Fujishige et al. with a matroid-based combinatorial characterization of immunity to strong Braess paradox.

Xujin Chen, Changjun Wang, Daorong Yu

In briefL-systems on the Large Witt Design

Weighted Sum-Rate Optimization of Near-Field STAR-RIS-Aided ISAC Systems Under Hardware Impairments

In briefWeighted sum-rate optimization for near-field STAR-RIS ISAC systems with hardware impairments

SummaryThis paper studies maximizing weighted sum-rate in a multi-user MIMO integrated sensing and communication system assisted by a STAR-RIS under the energy splitting protocol. The base station to STAR-RIS link uses far-field propagation, while STAR-RIS to user links use near-field spherical wavefronts, and hardware impairments such as phase perturbations and signal-dependent distortion are included. An alternating optimization and successive convex approximation approach is used to solve the active and passive beamforming subproblems. Numerical results show stable convergence and higher weighted sum-rate than direct-link transmission, with severe hardware impairments causing saturation at high transmit power.

Huy T. Nguyen, Nhu-Thinh Bui Doan, Minh-Tuan Pham et al.

Payoff-Based Certificates for Convergence to Pure Nash Equilibria under Replicator Dynamics

In briefPayoff-based conditions certifying convergence to pure Nash equilibria under replicator dynamics

SummaryThe paper studies finite bimatrix games and gives a payoff-based sufficient condition, E>0, under which an interior starting state converges to a specified strict pure Nash equilibrium under replicator dynamics. The construction combines growth-rate bounds on log probability ratios with a first integral of a two-dimensional comparison system. It also provides a finite exact test for rational inputs, closed-form box probabilities and convergence-time bounds, and extensions to multiplayer games and discrete-time exponential-weights learning.

Beyond Service Discovery: Coverage Awareness in LLM-Driven Service Composition

In briefBeyond Service Discovery: Coverage Awareness in LLM-Driven Service Composition

SummaryThis paper distinguishes reuse discovery, finding relevant published assets, from reuse coverage, whether those assets support the transformations a request needs. It evaluates PipeFinder, an LLM-driven framework, on 99 consumer requests against a fixed catalogue. Relevant data products were found reliably, but successful composition was much harder, and when required services were missing the system usually built a pipeline anyway. Explicitly adding coverage checks to planning substantially improved recognition of unsatisfiable requests.

Sepideh Masoudi, Ronja Froelian, and Stefan Tai

Functional Information, Optimal Channel Simulation and Lossy Source Coding

In briefFunctional Information and Optimal Limits for Channel Simulation and Lossy Coding

SummaryThis paper studies the minimum achievable rates of one-shot coding problems, such as channel simulation and lossy source coding, to within a constant number of bits. It introduces a new information measure, the functional information I_F(X→Y), which characterizes the minimum rate of channel simulation with common randomness to within 1.45 bits. The authors use this result to define a functional rate-distortion function and bound the one-shot operational rate-distortion function, and they extend the framework to a Rényi-type functional information.

WhyThe authors introduce functional information and characterize one-shot channel simulation rate within 1.45 bits, extending to lossy source coding and Rényi variants.

Achievable Information Rate for FTN under a Reduced Complexity BCJR Receiver

In briefAchievable information rate of faster-than-Nyquist signaling with a reduced-complexity BCJR receiver

SummaryThis paper studies the achievable information rate (AIR) of faster-than-Nyquist (FTN) signaling with finite constellations, using full-state and reduced-state BCJR receivers with bidirectional decision feedback. The detection problem is formulated in a reproducing kernel Hilbert space, and detection errors are characterized by distances between competing sequences. The authors also derive an outage criterion for reduced-state detection, where the AIR is zero. The results show that reduced-state detection can suffer outage even at high signal-to-noise ratio due to residual inter-symbol interference.

Zichao Zhang, Gokhan M. Guvensen, Melda Yuksel et al.
