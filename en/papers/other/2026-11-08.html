The Query Complexity of Envy-Free Division: Cake, Chores, and Mixed Resources

In briefQuery Complexity of Envy-Free Fair Division for Cake, Chores, and Mixed Goods

SummaryThis paper studies how many queries are needed to achieve envy-free division of cake, chores, and resources with both positive and negative values. It gives a deterministic exact protocol for arbitrary nonnegative nonatomic chore costs, and Las Vegas protocols for cake and chores with polynomial expected query counts. It also shows that exact protocols generally need Ω(n²) expected queries, and that no uniform finite expected-query bound exists for mixed cake and chores.

Alireza Farhadi, Mohammadtaghi Hajiaghayi

Joint Design of Communication and Control in Center-Fed Pinching-Antenna Systems

In briefJoint communication and control design for center-fed pinching-antenna systems

SummaryThis paper proposes an analytical and optimization framework for jointly designing communication and control in a center-fed pinching-antenna system (C-PASS). It characterizes the coupling between data throughput and closed-loop control cost and establishes an achievable communication-control performance region. A throughput maximization problem is formulated to jointly optimize digital and pinching beamforming under control-cost constraints, and solved with an alternating optimization algorithm. Numerical results show 110.2% higher frame-average throughput than conventional PASS, and up to 66.7% gain of concurrent data-control transmission over separated transmission.

Xu Gan, Yuanwei Liu, and Hyundong Shin

Linear Set Reconciliation with O(d) Additive Redundancy

In briefSet reconciliation with linear sketches needing only O(d) extra bits

SummaryThis paper studies set reconciliation, where two parties recover the symmetric difference of their sets from short F_2-linear sketches. The authors construct a randomized sketch of length L(n,d)+O(d) plus lower-order terms, with polylogarithmic update time and near-linear decoding time. The construction encodes permuted rows with randomly scaled Pinsketch syndromes, sketches the bit array column by column, and decodes using a recursive sketch called HintSketch.

WhyThe authors achieve L(n,d)+O(d) bits for linear set reconciliation via randomly scaled Pinsketch syndromes and column-wise hinted decoding, with stated polylog update and near-linear decoding time.

Tianren Liu, Rui Xu, Tong Yang et al.

A Per-Step Load-Balancing Engine for Expert-Parallel MoE Serving

In briefRelief: a per-step load-balancing engine for expert-parallel MoE serving

SummaryIn expert-parallel Mixture-of-Experts serving, each layer waits for the most loaded GPU in its group, and routing shifts at every decode step. The paper introduces Relief, a bidirectional pairwise relaxation that avoids exact linear-program solving and reaches within a fraction of a point of the per-step optimum in a few integer-halving passes. The authors measure three deployments: CUDA kernels, an FPGA over PCIe, and a fixed-function block beside the accelerator. They report that the kernel alone matches or exceeds every deployable scheme in effective utilization across group sizes.

Jingyuan Li, Maolin Wang, Hayden So

A Simple Phase-King Protocol with Predictions

In briefA simpler Phase King protocol that uses predictions about faulty processes

SummaryThis paper studies synchronous binary Byzantine agreement in a learning-augmented setting, where each process receives predictions about which processes are honest or Byzantine. In briefImproving bounds on hash and trifferent codes

SummaryThe paper improves probabilistic lower bounds on the rates of hash codes and list-recoverable codes. In briefWelfare in envy-free contracts: when agents have few interests

SummaryThe paper studies welfare maximization under envy-free contracts, where a principal assigns tasks and offers success-based payments to heterogeneous agents with hidden effort. For unit-demand agents, the problem is polynomial-time solvable when each agent is interested in at most two tasks, but NP-hard with three. When agents may receive multiple tasks, no polynomial-time constant-factor approximation exists unless P=NP.

Analysis of the Effect of Doppler on Direct-to-Cell: Real-World Performance and Proposed Improvements

In briefEffect of Doppler Shift on Direct-to-Cell Satellite Links: Measurements and Proposed Improvements

SummaryThis paper examines direct-to-cell (DtC) satellites that serve unmodified LTE phones from low Earth orbit, where orbital motion causes Doppler shifts of tens of kHz. The satellite can pre-compensate Doppler for only one reference point per beam, so phones elsewhere in the beam retain a residual offset, which earlier field studies report can reach 10 kHz. The authors corroborate this with measurements of Starlink transmissions, link poor handover performance to inadequate residual compensation, and propose a UE-side method based on the UE's location relative to the beam reference point and the known satellite location.

Yuxi Chen, Muhammad Iqbal Rochman, Kyler McCormack et al.

Optimal Sampling and Transmission Policy for Information Freshness in Gossip Networks

In briefOptimal sampling and transmission for information freshness in gossip networks

SummaryThis paper studies how a transmitter should decide when to sample a source and when to transmit updates to several receivers that also exchange information among themselves. Freshness is measured with Version Age of Information (VAoI), and the problem is formulated as an infinite-horizon average-cost Markov decision process solved by relative value iteration. The authors prove that the sampling decision has a threshold structure in the transmitter's VAoI, and that under reliable links transmission and idling follow a VAoI-difference threshold structure. Numerical results show the optimal policy consistently outperforms natural baselines.

Irtiza Hasan, Ahmed Arafa

Efficient SC-Flip Decoding for Polar Codes through Decision-Boundary Normalization

In briefEfficient SC-Flip decoding for polar codes using normalized decision thresholds

SummaryThis paper addresses the difficulty of identifying the first erroneous position in successive cancellation flip (SCF) decoding when only a few re-decoding attempts are allowed. It proposes decision-boundary normalization (DBN), which scales each decision log-likelihood ratio by the standard deviation predicted by Gaussian approximation, and a DBN-SCF decoding algorithm built on it. The method achieves better error-correction performance with limited re-decoding attempts and lower average decoding complexity than existing SCF methods at comparable performance.

In briefUpper limits on constant-composition codes via quantum channels

SummaryThis paper extends a quantum-channel optimization method of Alrabiah and Guruswami to q-ary constant-composition codes. In briefDICOMPUTE: A Framework for Cost- and Privacy-Aware Distributed AI Inference

SummaryThis paper proposes DICOMPUTE, a framework in which AI inference requests are scheduled under policy constraints across privately controlled, regional, and independently operated heterogeneous computing resources rather than only centralized facilities.
