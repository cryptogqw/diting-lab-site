Thinflow: Organizing HPC Benchmarking Campaigns as Rerunnable Directory Trees

In briefThinflow: a small tool for keeping HPC benchmark campaigns rerunnable as directory trees

SummaryThis paper presents Thinflow, a small Bash engine that organizes high-performance computing benchmarking campaigns as rerunnable directory trees of tasks and runs. Each task is a directory with code and a short shell script, and one command builds the code, runs the benchmarks, and plots the results, with per-supercomputer profiles submitting jobs to Slurm. In a case study, the authors ran one campaign with identical task files on three Slurm partitions of two supercomputers, on up to 16 nodes, producing 455 benchmark runs.

Richard Schulze, Ari Rasch, Anna-Lena Roth et al.

Held While Waiting: What LLM Agent Sessions Reserve on Serverless and Agent Runtimes

In briefWhat LLM agent sessions hold while waiting on serverless and agent runtimes

SummaryThis paper examines how LLM agents occupy computing resources while waiting for model replies. Using Amazon Bedrock AgentCore as its main example, it finds that each conversation reserves a separate isolated environment until the conversation is closed, so environments run out long before processing power does. The authors propose closing environments promptly after conversations end and sharing one environment among many conversations, noting that sharing requires platform support. Keeping conversations separate without reserving an environment for each waiting conversation is presented as an open problem.

WhyThe authors identify that LLM agent sessions hold dedicated environments while waiting on the model, and propose closing and sharing fixes, evaluated via a simulation and a benchmark.

Mekhala Hithyshini Sreenivasa

StarMPI-SVD: Distributed-Memory Parallel Star-M Tensor SVD

In briefDistributed-memory parallel Star-M tensor SVD

SummaryThis paper presents the first distributed-memory parallel implementations of two Star-M tensor decompositions, t-SVDM-I and the adaptive multirank t-SVDM-II, generalized to N-way tensors. It uses asynchronous, non-blocking communication to speed up data redistribution and key computational kernels. Strong-scaling tests report at least 70% parallel efficiency on up to 128 nodes (6144 cores), and the methods are applied to application datasets up to 768 GB.

Naman Pesricha, Grey Ballard, Srinivas Eswar et al.

Aggron: Aggressive Compression of Agentic KV Caches during Head-of-Line Stalls in the Request Queue

In briefAggron: a serving system that aggressively compresses agent KV caches during request-queue stalls

SummaryLLM agents run long, multi-turn programs whose context grows each step, and their serving is limited by KV cache capacity. This paper presents Aggron, which summarizes agentic requests at every turn and selectively compresses only parts of the KV cache, while launching these summarizations from a separate Summarization Queue during head-of-line stalls in the main queue. Summarizations are issued only when the server predicts they will finish before the stalled request executes. In evaluation at high load, Aggron reduces P99 program latency by 27–31% and raises throughput by 1.34–1.40× compared with state-of-the-art asynchronous summarization.

Rishabh Ravi, Tony Hong, Chen Wang et al.

Optimal Preemption: Guaranteeing Information Freshness in Mean Age and Beyond

In briefOptimal Preemption for Keeping Information Fresh in Mean Age and Beyond

SummaryThis paper studies Age of Information (AoI), where an update in service must normally wait even when heavy-tailed delays raise its age. The authors propose a framework in which the sender can preempt an in-service update and sample a fresh one, for general service-time distributions and explicit action costs. Under nondecreasing age costs, a finite mean delay is shown to suffice for finite average age even with infinite delay variance. For exponential service the optimal rule uses constant thresholds, while for general delays the optimal preemption rule depends on both the age and the elapsed service time.

WhyThe authors allow preempting in-service updates and reduce VaR and CVaR minimization to the same optimality equations, validated under Pareto and log-normal delays.

Aimin Li, Yi\u{g}it \.Ince, Elif Uysal

Accelerating Mixture of Block Attention via Dynamic Routing, KV-Centric Execution and Runtime Pipelining

In briefFaster Long-Context Attention via Dynamic Routing and KV-Centric GPU Execution

SummaryThis paper presents LightningMoBA, a method that speeds up Mixture of Block Attention (MoBA), which reduces long-context computation by selecting key-value blocks for each query. It replaces fixed Top-k selection with dynamic thresholding, groups work by KV block so that loaded tiles are reused, and overlaps memory transfers with Tensor Core computation on Hopper GPUs. At matched Top-16 retention across five checkpoints, end-to-end generation is 1.20 to 1.60 times faster, and mean RULER score rises from 74.42 to 75.28.

Yingqi Shi, Junru Lu, Yun Liu et al.

Cooling Noisy Population Protocols -- Vanishing-Gain Averaging and Localisation under Persistent Errors

In briefCooling noisy population protocols: averaging and localisation under persistent errors

SummaryThis paper studies how anonymous population protocols, where agents interact in random pairs, coordinate when communication and measurement errors persist. It proves that pairwise averaging with independent centred finite-variance communication errors converges with probability 1 to a common finite random value. For one-dimensional localisation, a signed update converges to the true agent locations up to one random translation, while a one-sided update fails to stabilise. The paper also shows that the signed protocol still converges when errors depend on inter-device distance.

Leszek G\k{a}sieniec, {\L}ukasz Kuszner, Paul Spirakis et al.

Spherical Tessellation of Circular Arrays for Massive MIMO: Favourable Propagation Conditions and Spectral Design

In briefSpherical arrangement of circular antenna arrays for massive MIMO wireless systems

SummaryThis paper studies a concentric circular antenna array for upper-mid-band XL-MIMO, built by tessellating a sphere so that facet orientations are not separable. Under fixed normalised chord spacing and ideal facet visibility, the authors prove that user channels become asymptotically orthogonal and matched-filter SINR approaches the single-user SNR. Simulations confirm interference decay and SINR convergence. A spectral comparison shows that facet-normal diversity avoids the full-amplitude grating ridges of the cylindrical baseline, and the residual between facets decreases as the array grows.

Geili T A El Sanousi, Tuan Anh Le, Raed A A Abd-Alhameed et al.
