Coding Length and Optimal Power Allocation in Correlated Block Fading

In briefCode length and optimal power allocation in correlated fading blocks

SummaryThis paper studies codewords spanning a fixed number of correlated Gaussian fading blocks, where the receiver knows the channel gains and the transmitter knows only their distribution. At fixed average signal-to-noise ratio, decoding error probability must decay exponentially with the number of receive antennas. The authors examine when outage probability predicts the achievable rate and identify a critical block-length-to-array-size ratio above which positive rates are possible. Under a total energy constraint per codeword, they determine the exact limiting rate over all codes for sufficiently large ratios.

Haoran Xu, Huazhong Zhao

Asynchronous Byzantine Lattice Agreement with Logarithmic Response Time and Optimal Resilience

In briefAsynchronous Byzantine lattice agreement with fast, fault-tolerant consensus on comparable values

SummaryThis paper presents an asynchronous algorithm for lattice agreement, where processes decide on comparable rather than identical values, under Byzantine failures. It tolerates up to f faulty processes among more than 3f total, requires no authentication, and completes in O(log f) time. The approach extends the classifier-tree framework of Attiya and Rachman and introduces Supported Reliable Broadcast, which combines reliable broadcast with checks that messages are consistent with the sender's prior state.

Hagit Attiya, Yao David Kolegain, Arnaud Labourel et al.

IsoMoE: Placement-Invariant Hybrid CPU-GPU MoE Inference with Lossless 12-bit Experts

In briefIsoMoE: Placement-Invariant Hybrid CPU-GPU MoE Inference with Lossless 12-bit Experts

SummaryIsoMoE is an inference system for Mixture-of-Experts models that splits experts between CPU and GPU while guaranteeing outputs do not change with expert placement. It combines a GPU expert kernel that bitwise replays the CPU kernel's arithmetic, a memory layout enabling efficient lossless 12-bit experts on the CPU using AVX-512, and a dual micro-batch pipeline overlapping CPU and GPU work. Experiments on three MoE models show bitwise identical outputs across placements and an average decoding speedup of 1.26 times, with a maximum of 1.54 times, over the fastest baseline.

Hang Gu, Teng Wang, Zhendong Zheng et al.

The Complexity of Fair and Stable Data Exchange

In briefComputational Complexity of Fair and Stable Data Exchange

SummaryThis paper studies how multiple participants can exchange data fairly and stably, so that each receives returns matching its contribution and no group can profit more by exchanging among itself. The authors show that with submodular utilities, which reflect diminishing returns from overlapping data, approximately reciprocal and core-stable exchanges can be computed efficiently under Shapley sharing. With supermodular utilities, which reflect complementarities, finding approximately reciprocal and core-stable exchanges is PPAD-hard even when contribution credits are easy to evaluate.

WhyThe authors separate computational complexity of fair, core-stable data exchange: polynomial algorithm for submodular utilities, PPAD-hardness for lattice-supermodular utilities.

Hannaneh Akrami, Bhaskar Ray Chaudhury

Balanced Boolean Functions from Sparse Niho-Type Permutation Polynomials: Few-Valued Walsh Spectra and Applications

In briefBalanced Boolean functions from sparse Niho-type permutation polynomials and their few-valued Walsh spectra

SummaryThe paper studies balanced Boolean functions defined through two-to-one mappings P(x+x²) and P(x+x⁻¹), where P is a sparse permutation polynomial of generalized Niho type. For even m it determines the full four-valued Walsh distribution of a function built from Q(x+x²), and it generalizes a permutation polynomial of Gupta and Sharma to a parameterized family over F_{q²}. For each admissible parameter, the complete Walsh distribution is shown to be independent of the parameter. The supports of these functions are also linked to binary codes, hyperplane intersection sets and Cayley graphs.

Chenxu Wang, Jun Zhang

User-Irrepressible Sequences for Multiple-Packet Reception

In briefSequences ensuring each user transmits successfully in multi-packet reception channels

SummaryThis paper studies deterministic transmission schemes for slot-synchronous multiple-packet reception channels that use periodic binary protocol sequences, with no feedback or phase synchronization. A sequence set is called γ-UI if every user is guaranteed at least one successful transmission per period, regardless of cyclic shifts. The authors propose a unified explicit construction based on the Chinese Remainder Theorem for any MPR capability γ≥2, and establish the γ-UI property through bounds on pairwise Hamming cross-correlations.

Improved (Minimum) Spanning Tree Construction in the HYBRID Model

In briefImproved Spanning Tree Construction in the HYBRID Model

SummaryThis paper studies spanning tree construction in the HYBRID model, where vertices exchange messages over input edges (local communication) and directly with vertices whose IDs they know (global communication). The authors present a randomized algorithm that, under standard capacity, builds a spanning tree and a well-formed tree in every connected component in O(log n) rounds with high probability. The approach uses a Δ-pseudo-spanning representation, with the main contribution being an O(1)-PSR constructed in O(log n) rounds. The authors also apply this to minimum spanning trees and to computing biconnected components, articulation points, and bridges.

WhyThe authors introduce a Δ-pseudo-spanning representation and build an O(1)-PSR in O(log n) rounds under standard capacity, with applications to MST and biconnected components.

Joint SLA Admission and Risk Allocation for Cloud Resource Provisioning

In briefJoint SLA Admission and Risk Allocation for Cloud Resource Provisioning

SummaryThe paper studies how a cloud controller should split allowed shortage risk between CPU and memory under a shared SLA limit when forecast-error histories are short or correlated over time. For each split, the controller uses historical forecast errors to set extra reserved capacity, checks whether the history supports that split, and picks the valid option with the lowest combined shortage and idle cost, otherwise falling back to a calibrated policy. In simulation with a 95% joint SLA target and 192 hours of history, it reduced shortage time slots by 1.04 percentage points on average versus always using fallback, at a 0.0091 increase in normalized cost.

Yihua Xu, Jize Li, Yan Lyu et al.

Incentive Compatible Mechanisms for Sequential Fair Resource Allocation with Private Demands

In briefIncentive-Compatible Mechanisms for Fair Sequential Resource Allocation with Private Demands

SummaryThis paper studies a strategic version of the online fair allocation problem, in which a planner gives a divisible resource to agents over several stages, while each agent's demand is known only to that agent. Because demand is self-reported and money cannot be transferred, agents may misreport their needs. The authors characterize the structure of incentive-compatible allocation rules and identify conditions under which the optimal mechanism has a simple cap structure. They also develop an approximation method that reduces a discretized dynamic program to a longest-path problem on a directed acyclic graph. Numerical experiments show that ignoring strategic reporting can be considerably more costly than enforcing incentive compatibility.

Ramtin Salamat, Sean R. Sinclair, Seyed M.R. Iravani

In briefWhat LLM agent sessions hold while waiting on serverless and agent runtimes

SummaryThis paper examines how LLM agents occupy computing resources while waiting for model replies. Using Amazon Bedrock AgentCore as its main example, it finds that each conversation reserves a separate isolated environment until the conversation is closed, so environments run out long before processing power does. The authors propose closing environments promptly after conversations end and sharing one environment among many conversations, noting that sharing requires platform support. Keeping conversations separate without reserving an environment for each waiting conversation is presented as an open problem.

WhyThe authors identify that LLM agent sessions hold dedicated environments while waiting on the model, and propose closing and sharing fixes, evaluated via a simulation and a benchmark.

Aggron: Aggressive Compression of Agentic KV Caches during Head-of-Line Stalls in the Request Queue

In briefAggron: a serving system that aggressively compresses agent KV caches during request-queue stalls

SummaryLLM agents run long, multi-turn programs whose context grows each step, and their serving is limited by KV cache capacity. This paper presents Aggron, which summarizes agentic requests at every turn and selectively compresses only parts of the KV cache, while launching these summarizations from a separate Summarization Queue during head-of-line stalls in the main queue. Summarizations are issued only when the server predicts they will finish before the stalled request executes. Optimal Preemption: Guaranteeing Information Freshness in Mean Age and Beyond

In briefOptimal Preemption for Keeping Information Fresh in Mean Age and Beyond

SummaryThis paper studies Age of Information (AoI), where an update in service must normally wait even when heavy-tailed delays raise its age. The authors propose a framework in which the sender can preempt an in-service update and sample a fresh one, for general service-time distributions and explicit action costs. Under nondecreasing age costs, a finite mean delay is shown to suffice for finite average age even with infinite delay variance. For exponential service the optimal rule uses constant thresholds, while for general delays the optimal preemption rule depends on both the age and the elapsed service time.

WhyThe authors allow preempting in-service updates and reduce VaR and CVaR minimization to the same optimality equations, validated under Pareto and log-normal delays.

Cooling Noisy Population Protocols -- Vanishing-Gain Averaging and Localisation under Persistent Errors

In briefCooling noisy population protocols: averaging and localisation under persistent errors

SummaryThis paper studies how anonymous population protocols, where agents interact in random pairs, coordinate when communication and measurement errors persist. It proves that pairwise averaging with independent centred finite-variance communication errors converges with probability 1 to a common finite random value. Spherical Tessellation of Circular Arrays for Massive MIMO: Favourable Propagation Conditions and Spectral Design

In briefSpherical arrangement of circular antenna arrays for massive MIMO wireless systems

SummaryThis paper studies a concentric circular antenna array for upper-mid-band XL-MIMO, built by tessellating a sphere so that facet orientations are not separable. Under fixed normalised chord spacing and ideal facet visibility, the authors prove that user channels become asymptotically orthogonal and matched-filter SINR approaches the single-user SNR. Simulations confirm interference decay and SINR convergence. A spectral comparison shows that facet-normal diversity avoids the full-amplitude grating ridges of the cylindrical baseline, and the residual between facets decreases as the array grows.

Geili T A El Sanousi, Tuan Anh Le, Raed A A Abd-Alhameed et al.
