Fair Selection of Generalized Nash Equilibria: A Comparability Perspective

In briefChoosing a Fair Equilibrium in Shared-Constraint Games: A Comparability View

SummaryGeneralized Nash equilibrium problems model self-interested agents whose costs and constraints are coupled, as in controlled allocation of a scarce shared resource. Such problems usually have many equilibria, so one must be selected, and the choice determines how the shared constraint's cost is split among agents. The paper uses comparability from social choice theory to show that the set of generalized Nash equilibria needs no inter-agent cost comparability, while the commonly used variational equilibrium implicitly assumes cardinal unit comparability. It introduces the fair generalized Nash equilibrium, which optimizes a fairness metric matched to the available comparability, and gives constructive computation methods for single-constraint and linear-quadratic games. An electric-vehicle charging game serves as the running example.

Sophie Hall, Heinrich H. Nax, Saverio Bolognani
