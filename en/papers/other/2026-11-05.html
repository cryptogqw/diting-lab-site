Capability Gain from ISA to Shared Execution on AMD MI300A and MI250X

In briefFrom ISA to Shared Execution: Capability Gain on AMD MI300A and MI250X

SummaryThis paper compares AMD's MI300A (CDNA3) with its predecessor MI250X (CDNA2) and introduces "capability gain," a metric for how much of an instruction-level speedup survives at higher levels such as libraries, applications, or shared devices. It traces the measurements from ISA microbenchmarks through hipBLASLt, hipSPARSELt, LLM inference, and mixed-precision HPL, including a device shared by up to eight concurrent streams. Results show FP8 keeps most of its speedup through the library, while unfused quantization causes per-layer slowdown in an LLM, and 2:4 sparsity keeps at most about half its speedup in hipSPARSELt.

In briefPreemption Based on Elapsed Service Time to Minimize Age of Information

SummaryThe paper studies age of information and peak age of information in a status update system where several independent sources share one server with generally distributed service times. DMuon: Scaling Matrix Optimizers in Data Parallelism with Topology-Aware Matrix Ownership and Communication

In briefDMuon: Scaling Matrix Optimizers in Data Parallelism with Topology-Aware Matrix Ownership

SummaryMatrix optimizers such as Muon update whole weight matrices, which clashes with the element- or row-wise sharding used by data-parallel training systems. DMuon is a matrix-granular optimizer runtime for PyTorch FSDP and HSDP that assigns each matrix a unique owner, which runs the Newton–Schulz iteration and update on the full matrix, with load balanced by computational cost. On up to 256 H100 GPUs across three dense LLMs and two VLA models, DMuon matches or exceeds the training throughput of FSDP2 with AdamW, reaching 1.05 times that at the median. Compared with a gather-then-replicate Muon baseline, its median throughput is 2.1 times higher.

Starrick Liu, Ruizhi Chengze, Lufang Chen et al.

In briefImproved Randomized Online Matching Beyond 0.55 Competitive Ratio

SummaryThis paper presents a randomized algorithm for edge-weighted online bipartite matching with free disposal, achieving a competitive ratio of 0.556195. This improves on the previous 0.5368 guarantee by Blanc and Charikar. The algorithm builds on online correlated selection and is analyzed with a state-dependent primal-dual method, with some selection inequalities verified by a computer-assisted proof whose verifiers are publicly available. Tight bounds for fractional and randomized truthful scheduling

In briefTight bounds for fractional and randomized truthful scheduling

SummaryThis paper studies truthful scheduling on unrelated machines and proves that every deterministic truthful fractional mechanism has worst-case approximation ratio at least (n+1)/2, matching the known upper bound. Because truthful-in-expectation mechanisms induce truthful fractional mechanisms, the same lower bound extends to all truthful-in-expectation and universally truthful mechanisms. The authors also give a universally truthful mechanism with approximation ratio n/2+o(n). Together, the results establish that the optimal leading constant for randomized truthful scheduling is 1/2.

WhyThe authors resolve a longstanding open problem, proving a (n+1)/2 lower bound for truthful fractional scheduling and matching randomized upper bounds asymptotically.

Georgios Anastasiadis, George Christodoulou, Elias Koutsoupias et al.

In briefflash-comm: A communication library that synchronizes data transfers themselves to speed up LLM decoding.

SummaryThe paper presents flash-comm, a communication library for LLM decoding that merges readiness synchronization with data movement.
