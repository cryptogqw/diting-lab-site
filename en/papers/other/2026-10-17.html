Spectra of Multivariate Vandermonde Matrices and Min-max Error Bounds for the High-dimensional Super-resolution Problem

In briefSpectra of multivariate Vandermonde matrices and error bounds for high-dimensional super-resolution

SummaryThe paper studies rectangular multivariate Vandermonde matrices whose columns correspond to irregularly spaced points on a high-dimensional torus. For points forming well-separated clusters, the column subspaces of different clusters are shown to be nearly orthogonal, so the spectral analysis reduces to each cluster. This yields sharp scaling for all singular values and min-max error bounds for recovering clustered point sources, matching known optimal one-dimensional rates.

SummaryRDMA networking offers low-latency, high-bandwidth communication in large systems, but existing observability tools either target specific libraries or report only aggregated data. In briefScalable Multi-Slot First-Price Pacing Equilibrium via Entropic Smoothing

SummaryThis paper studies pacing equilibria, which describe how budget-constrained advertisers clear an ad market when each request fills several slots. Orientation Diversity Provably Increases the Angular-Domain Rank of 3D MIMO Arrays

In briefOrientation diversity provably boosts angular-domain rank in 3D MIMO arrays.

SummaryThe paper proves that antenna element orientation is an effective spatial resource complementary to position. For collinear or coplanar arrays, angular-domain rank is capped by the number of projection-ambiguity classes of scattering directions. Orientation-diverse elements with sufficiently rich analytic patterns restore the maximal rank min(N,L) for almost every orientation draw. A closed-form correlation expression, monotone in orientation spread under isotropic scattering, is given, and Monte Carlo simulations corroborate the exact-rank and capacity behavior.

Envy-Free Randomized Apportionment

In briefRandomized seat allocation with no envy

SummaryLegislative seats are allocated by rounding population or vote shares to whole numbers, and randomized rounding can leave a larger party or state with fewer seats than a smaller one, which the authors call envy. The paper designs apportionment rules that minimize the expected number of such envious pairs and shows that rules ignoring lower quotas can have worst-case envy of Θ(n²). It also introduces a polynomial-time linear programming framework for objectives such as expected and variance of envy. Empirically, envy-free allocations exist in every historical US decennial census instance.

Revisiting the Fairness of Pure Nash Equilibria of Round-Robin

In briefRe-examining the fairness of pure Nash equilibria in Round-Robin allocation

SummaryThe paper proves that, for additive valuations, not every pure Nash equilibrium of the Round-Robin mechanism yields an allocation that is envy-free up to one good (EF1) with respect to true valuations, correcting an earlier claim by Amanatidis et al. On the positive side, every pure Nash equilibrium of any fixed recursively balanced picking sequence mechanism is 1/2-EF1 and proportional up to one good (PROP1). The authors also give a family of Round Robin equilibria showing that no strictly better guarantee than 2/3-EF1 holds for all equilibria.

On the Length of Maximum Sum-Rank Distance Codes

In briefOn the Length of Maximum Sum-Rank Distance Codes

SummaryThis paper extends the classical geometric theory of arcs in finite projective spaces to maximum sum-rank distance (MSRD) codes, studying how their block structure limits code length. In dimension three, it determines the possible intersection patterns of lines with associated linear sets and gives formulas for the codes' characters and weight distributions. It shows that a block of rank at least two reduces the maximum total length by at least q-1, that sufficiently long MSRD codes are classical, and that three-dimensional one-weight MSRD codes do not exist. An exceptional three-dimensional code over F_9 is also constructed.
