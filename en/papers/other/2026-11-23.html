Torcalia.jl: An MPI-based Runtime System for Asynchronous Task Parallelism in Julia

In briefTorcalia.jl: An MPI-based Julia runtime for asynchronous task parallelism

SummaryThis paper presents Torcalia.jl, a Julia package that orchestrates asynchronous task execution across shared and distributed memory systems, built on MPI.jl and Julia's native multithreading. It uses a dependency-aware scheduler combining work-sharing and work-stealing for adaptive load balancing, and supports arbitrarily nested tasks and application-level distributed shared memory. Evaluated on the ARIS supercomputer with four workloads, it reduced wall-clock time by up to 18.1% compared with Dagger, a Julia task-based engine.

Argiris Sofotasios, Dimitris Metaxakis, Chrysostomos Stylios et al.

In briefA variant of the Erdős–Gyárfás problem for K_9 and K_12

SummaryThe paper studies edge-colorings of complete graphs in which every copy of a given graph H contains a color used an odd number of times, with r_H(n) denoting the minimum number of colors needed. Stay or Switch? Metric Distortion with Online Candidate Arrivals

In briefStay or Switch? Metric Distortion When Candidates Arrive Online

SummaryThis paper studies metric distortion in sequential elections where candidates arrive one at a time. Each round, a new challenger faces the incumbent, and a voting rule picks one of the two based on voters' pairwise preferences. For deterministic rules, the authors show an asymptotically optimal distortion of Θ(√t) at round t. Randomized rules achieve constant distortion, with persistent randomized dictatorship reaching optimal distortion 5 against adaptive adversaries and 3 against oblivious ones.

Kaito Fujii, Yeeseok Oh, Shinsaku Sakaue

Principal Revelations of the Revelation Principle

In briefPrincipal Revelations of the Revelation Principle: A Theory Paper on Mechanism Design

SummaryThis paper proves a converse to the revelation principle, showing it rests on an optimistic assumption: if it must hold in all environments, the principal must assume agents play her favorite equilibrium. When the principal knows only that the prior lies in some set, she must also assume that prior is her favorite one. With private values, independent types, and quasilinear payoffs, unrestricted transfers let the principal approximate any mechanism's equilibrium payoff using a direct mechanism whose unique equilibrium is truthful.

WhyThe authors prove a converse to the revelation principle, showing it embeds optimism about equilibrium selection. With private values and transfers, a direct mechanism with unique truthful equilibrium

A Pipeline-Parallel System for Depth-Recurrent Models

In briefA pipeline-parallel system for models that loop through their layers

SummaryDepth-recurrent (looped) models reuse a shared stack of blocks several times to increase effective depth. The paper argues that the common interleaved 1F1B pipeline schedule assumes an acyclic stage graph, so splitting a looped model causes repeated stage visits and out-of-order communication that can stall. The authors present Loop-PP, which redesigns the schedule and communication for this cyclic layout and emits a static per-rank instruction stream that overlaps communication with compute. On 8B to 60B MoE models it reduces peak memory by up to 32.8%, and at fixed compute budget raises the best observed throughput by 12.7%.

Yunjia Xi, Jiarui Qin, Di Yin et al.

Optimal XOR Lemmas for Information Complexity

In briefOptimal XOR lemmas for information complexity

SummaryInformation complexity measures how much the parties to a communication protocol must reveal about their inputs to complete a task. This paper asks whether the information complexity of the XOR of n independent copies of a Boolean function obeys an XOR lemma, and fully resolves this for every error level from 0 to 1/3. For total functions, the result is Theta(n times the information complexity at error epsilon/n), except for degenerate functions of the form a(x) XOR b(y). The paper also characterizes partial functions and proves an optimal distributional XOR lemma and matching direct sum theorems.

WhyThe authors resolve the open XOR lemma for information complexity, giving tight bounds across all error levels with matching direct sum theorems via information-theoretic and geometric methods.

SummaryThis paper studies how to sell supplementary training data to improve a predictive model. In briefCoarse Price Discrimination: Revenue Cost of Limiting Posted Prices

SummaryThis paper studies how much revenue a seller loses when restricted to L posted prices, while heterogeneous buyers with independent values compete for scarce inventory. Under a large-market assumption, buyers are first made arbitrarily replaceable at fixed stock, and then stock is allowed to grow. The Complexity of Full-Information Iterated Protocols

In briefComputational cost of iterated full-information protocols

SummaryThis paper studies iterated full-information protocols in distributed computing, where processes share their entire knowledge each round, usually assumed to carry unbounded information. The authors limit each process to sharing at most b bits with every other process per round and analyze the cost of simulating r iterations. Tightness of the Partition Bound for Multiterminal Secret Key Capacity

In briefWhether a Partition Bound for Multiterminal Secret Key Capacity Is Exact

SummaryThis paper studies secret key agreement among m terminals who communicate over a public channel, with some terminals acting as helpers. It proves that the partition-based Csiszár–Narayan upper bound is tight for every source exactly when the target set size |A| is 2, m-2, m-1, or m, and extends a known six-terminal counterexample to the remaining cases. It also shows that for two target terminals on a connected Markov random field, an optimal bipartition can be chosen with both blocks connected, reducing candidate bipartitions on cycles from exponential to at most quadratic in m.

WhyThe authors give a complete characterization of when the partition bound is tight, extending Chan's counterexample, and report optimal bipartition structure on Markov random fields.
