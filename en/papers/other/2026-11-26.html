Computing Normal-Form Correlated Equilibria in Extensive-Form Games is in PPAD

In briefProof that computing normal-form correlated equilibria in extensive-form games is in PPAD

SummaryThis paper studies how hard it is to find normal-form correlated equilibria (NFCE) in extensive-form games with perfect recall, a major open problem in algorithmic game theory. The authors show the problem lies in the PPAD class for any number of players. They also prove that computing such an NFCE whose support size is quadratic in the game size is PPAD-hard, even for two-player games.

WhyThe authors show that computing normal-form correlated equilibria in extensive-form games lies in PPAD, and prove PPAD-hardness for two-player games with quadratic support.

Vincent Cheval, Florian Horn, Soumyajit Paul et al.

Last-Iterate Equilibrium Learning in Symmetric Congestion Games under Semi-Bandit Feedback

In briefLearning equilibria in congestion games from resource load and cost feedback

SummaryThis paper studies how players in symmetric congestion games with atomic players can learn mixed Nash equilibria, observing both costs and loads of chosen resources. The authors add a quadratic consensus penalty that makes the potential function globally strongly convex, with the symmetric equilibrium as its unique minimizer. They propose a variant of online projected gradient descent and show convergence rates in expectation, with the played profile converging to equilibrium almost surely.

Katerina Michou, Marina Kontalexi, Dimitris Fotakis

On the Distance Properties of Huffman Sequences

In briefA study of distance properties of Huffman sequences

SummaryThis paper studies pairwise distances between sequences that share the same aperiodic autocorrelation function. The authors show that these distances are determined by a ternary signed pattern that encodes relative zero flips between spectral factors. For Huffman sequences, they prove that dihedral symmetries of the zeros preserve pairwise distances and derive lower bounds on the minimum distance. The results provide a starting point for designing codes with fixed autocorrelation, which are relevant to integrated sensing and communication.

Parker Huggins, Alphan Sahin, Elza Erkip

PipeAxes: Decoupling Compute and Communication Granularities for Pipeline-Parallel LLM Serving

In briefPipeAxes: Separating Compute and Communication Granularity in Pipeline-Parallel LLM Serving

SummaryPipeAxes is a serving system for large language models run with pipeline parallelism across GPUs. It splits wide prefill batches into slices of a few layers so decoding can run between them, and compresses activations with a fused Triton Hadamard-INT4 codec sent in independently sized row groups over parallel TCP sockets. A scheduler uses latency slack to choose batch width, slice depth, and row-group size. The paper reports results from 97 replays covering 5,016 requests, including throughput and latency comparisons with baselines and accuracy changes for INT4 transport relative to BF16.

Xiaoqiang Guo, Yifan Lin, Yang Liu et al.

DéjàVu: Plurality Consensus by First Repetition

In briefDéjàVu: Reaching Majority Agreement by Repeating an Opinion

SummaryThis paper analyzes DéjàVu, a simple protocol in which each agent samples one random agent per round and adopts the first opinion it sees twice, then resets its memory. The rule needs no frequency counters or knowledge of population size or number of opinions. The authors prove that, when the initial plurality has a sufficient lead, the protocol reaches consensus on that opinion quickly with high probability, and is asymptotically never slower than h-majority protocols under the same conditions.

WhyThe authors propose a counter-free plurality consensus protocol using first repetition, proving convergence bounds and comparing them to h-majority dynamics.

Niccol\`o D'Archivio, George Giakkoupis, Fr\'ed\'eric Giroire et al.
