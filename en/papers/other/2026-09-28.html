In briefEnvy-free splits of random assignments, with four agents solved

SummaryThe paper studies whether a random assignment of objects to agents can be decomposed into deterministic assignments so that every agent envies any other with probability at most 1/2. It proves this is always possible for four agents using a computer-aided check over all vertices, and shows 1/2 is the best general threshold. A proof of the generalized packing-covering conjecture

In briefA proof of the generalized packing-covering conjecture (a coding-theory conjecture now proven)

SummaryThis paper proves the generalized packing-covering conjecture for linear codes. The conjecture states that the t-th generalized Hamming weight is at most twice the t-th generalized covering radius plus two. The proof is computer-assisted, combining a parity-check reformulation, length bounds on possible counterexamples, and puncturing arguments. All orders t ≥ 32 are settled, and the remaining finitely many cases are excluded by exact computer verification.

WhyProves a long-standing conjecture via parity-check reformulation, length bounds, puncturing, and exact computer verification of remaining cases.

WaveAlign: Cache-Aware Query-Row Scheduling for Sparse Attention in Long-Video Generation

In briefWaveAlign: Cache-Aware Query-Row Scheduling for Faster Sparse Attention in Long-Video Generation

SummaryLong-video generation with diffusion transformers produces very long token sequences, so attention becomes the main inference bottleneck. The paper proposes WaveAlign, a lightweight framework that reorders query rows in dynamic sparse attention, grouping rows by similar key/value access patterns and sorting them within GPU waves to improve L2 cache hit ratio and reduce HBM traffic. Because only query and mask rows are permuted, sparse-attention semantics and existing kernels are unchanged. Reported results across two GPUs, two video DiTs, and four sparse-attention methods include up to 1.25x kernel and 1.17x end-to-end speedup without quality loss.

In briefDivergence-minimizing distribution matching for parallel and memory channels

SummaryThe paper extends distribution matcher theory to distributions with memory and shows that the divergence-minimizing matcher selects the highest-probability target sequences, as in the memoryless case.
