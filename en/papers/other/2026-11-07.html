Performance Analysis of Entanglement Swapping DV-QKD over MIMO FSO Channels

In briefEntanglement-swapping quantum key distribution over MIMO free-space optical links

SummaryThis paper analyzes a quantum communication system that uses polarization-encoded DV-QKD with entanglement swapping over MIMO free-space optical channels, aiming to extend secure range beyond a direct link. The channels are decomposed into parallel eigenchannels via singular value decomposition, and closed-form expressions for QBER and secret key rate are derived. Numerical results show that entanglement swapping keeps QBER low over longer distances than direct transmission, at the cost of lower key rate at short range, with the crossover distance growing as MIMO configurations increase.

Sushil Kumar, Soumya P. Dash, and George C. Alexandropoulos

Performance Benchmarking of Federated Learning Frameworks for Medical Image Classification

In briefBenchmarking Federated Learning Frameworks on Medical Image Classification

SummaryThis paper compares three federated learning frameworks, Flower, NVIDIA FLARE, and FedBioMed, using ResNet-10 on the MedNIST image classification task in a single-host containerized setup. Across 81 executions, the authors measured execution time, CPU, RAM, GPU utilization, network traffic, and final accuracy. Significant differences were found in server memory, GPU utilization, network traffic, and execution time, while no significant accuracy difference appeared within the short training schedules tested.

Vithor Pileco, Luan Mantegazine, Claudio Geyer

On the Convergence of First-Order Methods in Signaling Games

In briefStudy of whether first-order learning methods converge in signaling games

SummaryThis paper studies how learning algorithms behave in Bayesian signaling games, a simple model where a Sender with private information sends a signal and a Receiver updates beliefs and responds. The authors find that standard online learning methods reliably converge to strict Perfect Bayesian Equilibria but not to non-strict ones. They show that finite signaling games have a PE that is variationally stable exactly when it is strict, which yields local convergence guarantees near such equilibria.

WhyThe authors characterize variational stability of PE in signaling games as equivalent to strictness, and report convergence results plus experiments across several first-order algorithms.

Rilind Sahitaj, Artem Tsikiridis, Martin Bichler

OneReel: Efficient Multi-Scene Video Generation with Scene-Structured Attention

In briefOneReel: Efficient Multi-Scene Video Generation via Scene-Structured Attention

SummaryThis paper presents OneReel, a training-free system for generating videos with multiple scenes. It keeps full attention within each scene and limits information exchange between scenes to a bounded shared memory, reducing attention cost from quadratic to linear in the number of scenes. Across four video generation models, it reports 1.7-4.9x end-to-end speedup over dense attention and 34-37 point gains in text-following accuracy while maintaining cross-scene consistency.

Wei Zhang, Yuchen Wang, Tianrui Feng et al.

In briefReliability of Ground-Air Wireless-Powered IoT Networks

Mobility Information Capacity of Multi-Robot Systems: Safety-Aware Maneuver Design

In briefMeasuring how much freedom a multi-robot team can move with and be observed

SummaryThis paper proposes a mobility information capacity framework that measures how much maneuver freedom a multi-robot system can use while its motion remains observable, defined as the mutual information between a maneuver sequence and its noisy trajectory observation. For linear Gaussian models with feedback, it derives finite-horizon and stationary long-horizon capacity limits and converts workspace, separation, and chance constraints into maneuver covariance restrictions. An aerial-robot simulation evaluates spectral convergence, safety-aware allocation, finite-codebook identification, and fleet-size scaling.

Weijie Yuan

Robust Analysis of First-Price Auctions with Large Supply

In briefRobust analysis of multi-unit first-price auctions with large supply, measuring efficiency loss

SummaryThis paper studies how efficient multi-unit first-price auctions are, where bidders have independent private values and want one unit each. It uses a smoothness framework to measure the price of anarchy when supply is large. It shows that worst-case bid distributions cannot occur in equilibrium under a mild density lower bound on values, yielding a new asymptotic lower bound of 1-Θ(ε log(1/ε)) for ε-approximate equilibria.

WhyThe authors show worst-case bid distributions cannot arise in equilibrium under a density lower bound, yielding asymptotic tight bounds beyond the classic 1-1/e.

Jason Hartline, Anant Shah, Matthew vonAllmen

In briefProportional Elections with Feasibility Limits and Ranked Ballots: A New Solid Coalition Rule

SummaryThis paper studies elections where voters submit ranked ballots and feasibility constraints specify which sets of candidates may be selected. Asymptotic Analysis of Data Deduplication Under a Constant Edit Model

In briefAsymptotic Analysis of Data Deduplication Under a Constant Edit Model

SummaryThis paper studies data deduplication algorithms from an information-theoretic perspective, focusing on data streams where repeats are approximate rather than exact. It considers two probabilistic source models in which repeated blocks are modified by a constant number of substitution edits or burst-insertion edits per block. For both models, variable-length deduplication is shown to achieve asymptotically arbitrarily large compression ratios, and multi-chunk deduplication achieves compression within a constant factor of the source entropy under mild conditions.

Sarvin Motamen, Hao Lou, Farzad Farnoud

In briefRunning FP32 and FP64 units concurrently on GPUs, and mixed-precision placement in WENO reconstruction

SummaryThis paper investigates whether idle native FP64 units on NVIDIA GPUs can run alongside FP32 work, and compares precision placements for WENO-Z reconstruction. Fast and Actionable Classification of HPC I/O Time Series

In briefFast, practical classification of HPC I/O time series for scheduling

SummaryThis paper introduces SAGE, a method that splits HPC job I/O traces into short windows, learns a symbol alphabet, and clusters the resulting symbol sequences to characterize temporal I/O behavior. Evaluated on 819,771 real traces from two HPC systems, SAGE-2 lowers median intra-cluster metrics by 9.1% versus K-shape while cutting clustering time from 3.8 hours to 2 minutes. Using cluster centroids for I/O scheduling reduces I/O stretch by up to 24% compared with round-robin placement.

Maxime Gonthier, Luan Teylo, Francesco Antici (RIKEN CCS) et al.

TPIC: Energy-Conserving Space Plasma Simulations on GPU Supercomputers

In briefTPIC: Energy-conserving space plasma simulations on GPU supercomputers

SummaryThis paper presents TPIC, a massively parallel GPU implementation of the Energy Conserving Semi-Implicit Method for kinetic space plasma simulations. The authors extend its exact energy conservation to closed domains with conducting boundaries by deriving and canceling a spurious term in the discrete Poynting flux theorem. Across several electrostatic and electrodynamic test cases, TPIC matches analytical or reference results and conserves total energy to machine precision. On 4,096 AMD GPUs, it advances 1.9 trillion particles with 96.8% weak scaling efficiency.

Luca Pennati, Ivy Peng, Stefano Markidis
