MMS Allocation for Chores with Item Disposals

In briefFair chore sharing with optional task removal to guarantee each participant a maximin share

SummaryThis paper studies how to fairly split heterogeneous chores among participants on web platforms while leaving as few tasks unassigned as possible. The goal is to guarantee each agent her maximin share (MMS) from the original instance. The authors prove that for any instance with at least four agents, at most n-3 chore disposals suffice, improving a prior bound and removing its dependence on the number of chores. For four agents, they show one disposal always suffices, a tight result.

VS-Flash: Harnessing Vector Search with Computational Memory-Semantic Flash

In briefVS-Flash: Faster Vector Search Using Computational Flash Memory

SummaryThis paper addresses vector databases used in retrieval-augmented generation, which outgrow host DRAM and rely on SSD-based serving, causing contention and high tail latency. It proposes VS-Flash, which treats flash as a large memory-semantic space and dynamically chooses between a computational flash device for large-batch queries and the host CPU for small, latency-sensitive queries, with both sharing the same resident data. On three billion-scale datasets, the authors report average bulk-search throughput gains of 36.8X over vanilla Milvus (up to 57.4X) and up to 24.8X lower p99 latency for latency-sensitive requests under mixed workloads.

Strategy recovery for Mean-Payoff Games is as hard as solving them from scratch

In briefProof that recovering optimal strategies in mean-payoff games is as hard as solving them from scratch

SummaryThis paper studies mean-payoff games, deterministic two-player zero-sum turn-based games on finite weighted directed graphs where players aim to optimize the limiting average of edge costs. It gives a simple reduction from solving these games to the strategy recovery problem, showing that recovering optimal strategies given the vertex values is essentially as hard as solving the game without knowing those values. The authors further show that finding one player's optimal strategy stays hard even when the other player's optimal strategy and all values are known.

Optimal single-track Gray codes from Gray-ordered necklaces

In briefOptimal single-track Gray codes built from Gray-ordered necklaces

SummaryA single-track Gray code is a cyclic Gray code in which each column is a cyclic shift of the first. For prime length n, the longest possible period is 2^n−2, and whether this is achieved for every prime has been open since 1996. The authors show that a conjecture by Degni and Drisko (2007) on the reflected Gray code order of necklaces implies optimal codes for every prime. This implication is formalized in Lean 4, and computation gives optimal codes for all odd primes n≤47.
