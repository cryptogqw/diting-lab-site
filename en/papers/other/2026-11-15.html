The ordered sum-rank metric and linearized multiplicity codes: NRT meets sum-rank

In briefOrdered sum-rank metric and linearized multiplicity codes, combining NRT and sum-rank metrics

SummaryThe paper introduces linearized multiplicity codes, generalizing both linearized Reed-Solomon codes and univariate multiplicity codes. It also defines the ordered sum-rank metric, which generalizes the sum-rank metric and the NRT (ordered Hamming) metric. A Singleton bound is established for this metric, and linearized multiplicity codes are shown to attain it, making them maximum ordered sum-rank distance codes.

Umberto Mart\'inez-Pe\~nas

Two-Robot Evacuation from a Unit Disk in the Segment Visibility Model

In briefTwo-Robot Evacuation from a Unit Disk Under Segment Visibility

SummaryThe paper studies two unit-speed robots starting at the center of a unit disk that must both reach an exit at an unknown boundary point. Under segment visibility, a target is discovered once it lies on the segment joining the robots, even if they have not reached it, and robots may leave the disk while searching. The authors give a three-phase tangent-and-arc algorithm with exact worst-case evacuation time 2√3+π/3 ≈ 4.511299. They also prove a matching lower bound for descending horizontal full-slice sweeps, and the optimal time over all segment-visibility algorithms remains open.

Evangelos Kranakis

Sparse Rotatable Arrays (SRAs) for Cell-Free MIMO: Network-Wide Design of Antenna Activation and Panel Orientation

In briefSparse Rotatable Arrays for Cell-Free MIMO: Joint Antenna Activation and Panel Orientation Design

SummaryThis paper studies a cell-free multiuser MIMO system using sparse rotatable arrays, where distributed access points select active antennas and adjust panel orientations under a network-wide activation budget. It formulates a two-timescale stochastic optimization that sets antenna activation and panel orientations on long timescales and precoders on short timescales. The authors develop an alternating algorithm combining sample-average approximation, WMMSE, and greedy search. Numerical results show the design outperforms a fixed-array benchmark with fewer active antennas.

Ailing Zheng, Qingqing Wu, Xiyuan Liu et al.

Error-correcting codes without parity-check bits

In briefError-correcting codes without parity-check bits: boosting symbol dependence to cut channel errors

SummaryThis paper proposes a new approach to error correction in communication channels. The input sequence is transformed before transmission so that dependencies between symbols increase substantially, and this property is then used to correct errors after transmission. A natural application is binary sequences with per-symbol entropy below one, where the sequence length stays constant through encoding and decoding. The authors develop a maximum likelihood decoding algorithm with linear encoding and decoding time and test it experimentally on popular channel models, finding parameter sets for which the method is quite effective.

Boris Ryabko, Ivan Baksheev, Vyacheslav Zhuravlev

Gaussian mass inequalities for lattices

In briefGaussian mass inequalities for lattice theta series

SummaryThe Gaussian mass of a lattice is its theta series evaluated at a real nome, relevant to lattice coding for the wiretap Gaussian channel. The paper proves inequalities comparing the Gaussian mass of lattices to that of the integer lattice, covering lattices built from binary, quaternary and senary self-dual codes and extremal unimodular lattices of both types. It also gives an averaging bound over the genus of Type II lattices and extends the binary Construction A inequality to Siegel theta series of arbitrary genus.

Minjia Shi, Xinguang Dai, Patrick Sol\'e

Exact Redundancy under Average and Almost-Sure Distortion Constraints

In briefExact Redundancy Under Average and Almost-Sure Distortion Limits

SummaryThis paper characterizes the exact redundancy of lossy source coding under average and almost-sure distortion constraints. For general source and output alphabets, it establishes asymptotic redundancy coefficients under suitable regularity conditions. Under average distortion, the coefficient is half the minimum probability that an output reveals source information among rate-distortion optimal channels; under almost-sure distortion, it is either 1/2 or 1 depending on whether the D-tilted information is constant. Both converses follow from a common change-of-measure inequality, and the required conditions are shown to always hold for finite alphabets.

Yijun Fan

IA-MoE: An Intensity-Aware Mixture of Experts for DNN Inference

In briefIA-MoE: An Intensity-Aware Mixture of Experts for Efficient Split Inference of Neural Networks

SummaryThis paper proposes IA-MoE, a Mixture of Experts framework for running deep neural network inference across edge and cloud servers. It models model partitioning as a bilateral matching between layer tasks and virtual machine resources for each request, dynamically splitting DNNs to jointly reduce end-to-end latency and energy use. Evaluated with AlexNet, VGG16, and ResNet50 on two public datasets in an enhanced CloudSimSDN simulator, IA-MoE reportedly outperforms baseline methods on weighted normalized latency and energy metrics.

Meiyan Zeng, Sasu Tarkoma, Xiaoli Liu

A Recursive Construction of Perfect and Odd Perfect Sequences of Unbounded Length over the Unit Quaternions

In briefA recursive method for building perfect and odd perfect sequences of any length over unit quaternions

SummaryThe paper gives a recursive construction, with closed-form index functions, of perfect and odd perfect periodic autocorrelation sequences over the unit quaternions in every length 2^n. The construction uses twisted periodic extensions and shows that a single sequence of length not divisible by 4 cannot carry the needed twist, while complementary pairs called i-pairs can. Explicit index formulas for i-pairs are given, along with the lengths of the odd perfect and perfect sequences derived from them.

Sam Blake

On the boomerang spectrum of the APN power function x^q+2 over F_q²

In briefBoomerang spectrum of a nonlinear power function over a finite field

SummaryThis paper studies the boomerang spectrum of the almost perfect nonlinear power function x^{q+2} over F_{q^2}, where q is an odd prime power with q≡1 (mod 6). Using a connection between boomerang and (-1)-differential entries, the problem is reduced to counting roots of one equation. The authors determine the full contribution from nonzero parameters in F_q and give a quintic over F_q for parameters outside F_q.

Kirpa Garg, Constanza Riera, Pantelimon St\u{a}nic\u{a}

Sustaining In-Network Computation in Satellite Networks: An Architecture for Semantic Continuity

In briefKeeping In-Network Computation Going in Satellite Networks: A Semantic Continuity Architecture

SummaryThe paper argues that computations on satellites often outlast the paths and providers that first served them, and proposes organizing in-network computing around one continuing logical computation. It presents an architecture that separates logical computation, stream context, and physical binding, illustrated with a wildfire assessment example. In a 300-node Linux prototype, stage-wise placement met deadlines for 82.08% of positions across 120 images, compared with 76.06% for single-node placement.

Zhiyuan Ren, Wenchi Cheng
