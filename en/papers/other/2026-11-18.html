Hull variation of algebraic geometry codes under divisor twists

In briefHow the hull dimension of algebraic geometry codes changes under divisor twists

SummaryThe paper studies how the hull (the intersection of a linear code with its dual) changes when a code is rescaled, focusing on algebraic geometry codes over function fields. It gives a formula and an upper bound for the change in hull dimension and builds explicit twists that raise or lower the hull dimension step by step. These are applied to two classical code families. Starting from known LCD codes, the authors reach a range of hull dimensions, and starting from self-dual or maximal-hull codes, they reach zero, producing LCD codes of various dimensions.

Erik Mendoza, Sihem Mesnager, Luciane Quoos

A Runtime System for Mitigating Crossbar Port Contention on SM-Partitioned GPUs

In briefA runtime system that reduces crossbar port contention on SM-partitioned GPUs

SummaryThe paper explains that even with SM partitioning such as CUDA Green Contexts, concurrent workloads can slow down latency-critical ones, partly because all SMs in a GPC share one crossbar port to the L2 cache. The authors present GPCPlace, a runtime that profiles workloads on the target GPU to choose between spreading and packing them across GPCs, setting SM masks by hooking kernel launches in the CUDA driver without application changes. Across 14 GPUs and 112 workload cases, it reports average speedups of up to 1.15x and a maximum of 2.38x for latency-critical workloads over Green Contexts, without throughput loss.

Ziwen Wang, Qi Luo, Kunlin Li et al.

Ordinal Hegselmann-Krause Systems

In briefOrdinal Hegselmann-Krause Systems: Opinion Dynamics Using Median-Style Aggregation

SummaryThis paper studies a variant of the Hegselmann-Krause opinion model in which agents update opinions using median or other ordinal aggregation methods instead of averages. It analyzes convergence under synchronous and asynchronous update schedules. Under a 1-Lipschitz condition, asynchronous dynamics converge in worst-case time O(n^4), with sharper bounds for median and k-large aggregation, while synchronous dynamics converge in linear time. When the Lipschitz condition is violated, synchronous dynamics may fail to converge at all.

Martin Hoefer, Tim Koglin, Jakob Lindner et al.

Dissecting an Unexplored SASS Control-Code Field: Barrier Exemption and Programmable Warp Priority

In briefAnalyzing an Unexplored SASS Control-Code Field: Barrier Exemption and Programmable Warp Priority

SummaryThis paper studies a previously unexplored control-code field in NVIDIA's native GPU instruction set (SASS), which appears frequently in closed-source libraries such as cuBLAS and cuDNN but is never emitted by nvcc for user programs. Using instruction-level microbenchmarks across GPU architectures from Volta to Blackwell, the authors show the field controls two hardware mechanisms: barrier exemption (EXEMPT) and programmable warp priority (START, TILE, END). They also implement a post-compilation binary optimizer that inserts these marks without changing source code or the compiler, achieving speedups of up to 31.9% on representative kernels across six GPUs.

WhyThe authors systematically study an unexplored SASS control-code field, identifying barrier exemption and warp priority mechanisms, and report speedups on six GPUs and in llama.cpp and vLLM.

Rational v. Radical: Trajectory Properties of Iterated Bayesian Updates

In briefRational vs. Radical: How Repeated Bayesian Updates Drift

SummaryThe paper studies what happens when Jeffrey's update is applied repeatedly to fixed evidence, which under suitable conditions yields a maximum-likelihood estimate. Running the iteration in reverse corresponds to repeated prior hacking, and most interior priors are shown to approach certainty in one possibility, a behavior the authors call radicalization. The work also asks whether some evidence can drive every interior prior to the same conclusion.

DOA Estimation in Antenna Arrays: Super-Resolution Methods in a Common Space

In briefUnified framework for super-resolution direction-of-arrival estimation in antenna arrays

SummaryThis paper presents a unified framework for angular super-resolution in antenna arrays, combining linear prediction beyond the physical aperture, entropy variations, and harmonic spatial spectra averaging. It recasts classical methods such as Capon, Pisarenko, Maximum Entropy, and MUSIC into a single structure. It also proposes J-orthogonalization-based algorithms that produce spatial spectrum estimates for multiple super-resolution methods within one computational architecture.

In briefDrift-aware adaptive update strategy for semantic codecs in dynamic communication systems

SummaryThis paper addresses how distribution drift makes offline-trained semantic codec models stale in dynamic semantic communication systems. It quantifies drift intensity in the semantic feature space and introduces Drift-Aware Age of Model (DA-AoM), which jointly captures drift-induced performance degradation, drift-dependent update service delay, and conventional update latency. Expanding and Evaluating the Applicability of Safe Pareto Improvements

In briefExtending and testing when safe Pareto improvements are possible in games

SummaryThis paper studies safe Pareto improvements (SPIs), where players make commitments that create a new game isomorphic to the original after removing strictly dominated actions, yet Pareto better in every entry. The authors build token games, cheap-talk games made meaningful by commitments tied to token outcomes. They analyze SPIs under three added abilities: burning utility, transferring utility between players, and transferring utility with an outside party conditional on the token outcome. The paper also evaluates frameworks by how often SPIs arise in random games, reporting substantially better results than prior work.

In briefComputational hardness of online stochastic matching under random arrivals

SummaryThis paper studies online stochastic matching on bipartite graphs, where offline vertices are known in advance and online requests arrive from known distributions, each being matched immediately and irrevocably or rejected. It gives the first computational hardness results for random-order arrivals. For known i.i.d. arrivals, computing the online optimum is APX-hard, so no polynomial-time approximation scheme exists unless P = NP. blaris: Windowless Random-Access LZSS for Constrained Microcontrollers

In briefblaris: Windowless Random-Access LZSS Decompressor for Tiny Microcontrollers

SummaryThis paper presents blaris, a lightweight LZSS decompressor for constrained microcontrollers that supports random access during execute-in-place operation. It partitions tokens into bidirectionally traversable streams with power-of-two checkpoints, so back-references are resolved within the compressed payload without allocating a RAM sliding window. On RV32IMC it uses 548-784 bytes of Flash and 104-112 bytes of RAM, and it reports much faster small-slice random reads than ratio-matched, block-indexed heatshrink and tamp.
