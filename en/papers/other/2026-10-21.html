Linear Complementary Pairs of MDS Codes from Elliptic Curves

In briefPaired MDS codes built from elliptic curves, with explicit constructions

SummaryThis paper studies linear complementary pairs (LCPs) of maximum distance separable (MDS) codes arising from elliptic curves. It gives geometric conditions for LCPs of elliptic codes and shows they cannot consist entirely of MDS codes when evaluated over all rational points of the curve. The authors instead restrict evaluation divisors to subgroup cosets to build LCPs of MDS elliptic codes, giving infinite families over Mordell and supersingular curves, including large-length constructions over quadratic extensions.

Adler Marques

UlaCache: Unified Logical Addressing for Sparse LLM Serving

In briefUlaCache: A KV cache system using unified logical addressing for sparse LLM inference

SummaryThis paper presents UlaCache, a key-value cache management system for large language model inference that spans GPU memory and optional CPU memory under one unified logical address space. Stable logical references let the engine allocate and select KV entries regardless of where they physically reside, and a parallel bucketed LRU policy manages a GPU pool shared across requests. Under matched CPU and GPU memory budgets with speculative decoding enabled, the authors report peak decoding throughput speedups of 1.16x to 2.51x over the state-of-the-art baseline on H200 and B300 GPUs.

Isaac Zhu, Alan Cao, Hugh Yin et al.

In briefMaximum length limits for cyclic MDS codes

SummaryThis paper studies the maximum length of nontrivial simple-root cyclic MDS codes over a finite field F_q. The authors prove that the length n is at most q+1 under certain gcd conditions, including cases where gcd(n, q²−1) exceeds 2, or the dimension is even and the gcd exceeds 1. The bound also holds for lengths whose prime factors lie in {2, 3, 5, 7}, with further results for some larger primes.

Some Results on the Weight Spectrum of Binary Reed-Muller Codes RM(m-7,m)

In briefWeight Spectrum of Binary Reed-Muller Codes RM(m-7,m): Partial Results

SummaryThis paper studies the weights that occur in high-order binary Reed-Muller codes RM(m-7,m). For every m≥14, it shows that the weight spectrum contains all even weights in [336, 2^m-336], along with a finite set of explicit low weights and their complements. The proof establishes a base inclusion for RM(7,14) and extends it to all m≥14 by a concatenation-based induction. The authors also show that several restricted construction routes cannot produce the four remaining base-code weights 322, 326, 330 and 334.

Computing the Nucleon of Matching Games

In briefExact nucleon computation in matching games: a theoretical game-theory paper on fair payoff allocation

SummaryThis paper studies exact computation of the nucleon, the multiplicative counterpart of the nucleolus, in matching games and two extensions: b-matching games and partitioned matching games. It gives a polynomial-time combinatorial algorithm for ordinary matching games with nonnegative rational weights. It also shows that computing any nucleon allocation is NP-hard for certain restricted b-matching and partitioned matching games. For the unresolved capacity-two case, it proves polynomial-time Turing reducibility to the shortest non-zero cycle problem.

WhyResolves open nucleon problems for matching games with a polynomial algorithm, several NP-hardness results, and a Turing reduction; proofs are rigorous but impact is confined to a niche subfield.

In briefS-PASS: Star-Topology Pinching-Antenna Systems with Pipelined Pre-Positioning

SummaryThis paper proposes S-PASS, a star architecture in which several waveguides share one radio-frequency chain through a switch, along with a time-division protocol called pipelined antenna pre-positioning (PAP). A Matroidal Framework for Random Access in Linear Codes

In briefA Matroidal Framework for Random Access in Linear Codes: a paper on how evenly random sampling recovers each coordinate of linear codes.

SummaryThis paper studies random access in linear codes when codeword symbols are sampled independently and uniformly with replacement. A code is called recovery balanced if every coordinate needs the same expected number of samples to be recovered. The authors express these expectations through the associated matroid and derive a coordinatewise identity linking a code and its dual. This shows a linear code is recovery balanced if and only if its dual is, confirming a conjecture of Gruica et al. The paper also defines recovery balance for arbitrary matroids and shows that asymptotically almost every sparse paving matroid is not recovery balanced.
