Measuring the Value of Communication in Multi-Agent Tracking Under Finite-Rate Belief Sharing

In briefMeasuring How Much Communication Helps Multi-Agent Target Tracking at Limited Data Rates

SummaryThis paper studies how useful communication is for stationary agents cooperatively tracking a moving target when they can only exchange information at limited rates. Each agent uses range and bearing measurements with an extended Kalman filter, quantizes its state estimate, and fuses shared information via covariance intersection. Results show very low rates can hurt performance due to coarse quantization, while moderate rates capture most of the gain, with diminishing returns at higher rates.

TRACEs: Fine-Grained Embedded Provenance for Distributed Scientific Workflows

In briefTRACEs: An Embedded Provenance Architecture for Distributed Scientific Workflows

SummaryLarge-scale scientific workflows run on distributed HPC resources, which makes it hard to explain how data products are generated and why similar runs give different results. TRACEs is a provenance architecture for distributed containerized workflows that embeds data lineage and execution context directly in every generated data artifact, and it stays compatible with existing workflow management systems. Evaluated on a seven-stage E. coli variant-calling workflow across multiple HPC nodes, it achieved 100% provenance completeness under distributed execution. It also traced a 200-fold difference in variant counts to differences in input sequencing data without re-executing the workflow.

In briefLevel crossing rate bounds for products of independent nonstationary fading processes

SummaryThis paper develops a unified framework for the instantaneous total level crossing rate (LCR) of products of independent positive nonstationary fading processes. Universal upper and lower bounds are derived from an exact decomposition of the product derivative and signed crossing imbalance, using only factor-wise crossing statistics. The authors introduce a drifting Gaussian-derivative (DGD) class, giving bounds via an exact Gaussian-mixture representation that capture aggregate drift and derivative fluctuations. Numerical results show that the DGD bounds closely bracket the exact LCR.

In briefFragmented Obedient Equilibria in Routing Games: a new equilibrium concept for competing mediators recommending routes

SummaryThe paper studies routing games where competing mediators recommend routes to fixed nonatomic populations. Each mediator chooses a randomized flow allocation to minimize its population's expected travel cost, subject to users having no conditional expected gain above a tolerance ε from ignoring recommendations. The authors define fragmented obedient equilibria (FOE) and prove existence under a lower-hemicontinuity assumption on obedient policy correspondences. Under affine edge latencies, the problem reduces exactly to first and second flow moments, and for networks with at most four routes each best response is a semidefinite program. A Braess network example shows that relaxing obedience can lower FOE costs, though gaps to the social optimum and optimal obedient pooling can persist.

In briefPhase retrieval of bandlimited signals via Morlet and companion wavelets

SummaryThe paper shows that complex bandlimited signals can be uniquely recovered, up to a global phase factor, from two families of wavelet transform magnitudes.
