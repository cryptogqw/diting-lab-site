Computing Feasible EF1 Allocations under Generalized Assignment Constraints

In briefComputing fair allocations of goods under budget-style size limits

SummaryThis paper studies fairly dividing indivisible goods when each agent's bundle must fit within her budget and goods can have different sizes for different agents, with unallocated goods going to charity. It shows that feasible envy-freeness up to one good (FEF1) allocations can be computed in strongly polynomial time, provided each agent's value can be almost exactly maximized over her feasible bundles. The allocation can also be made approximately envy-free up to any good while keeping nearly half the Nash welfare of a given allocation.

WhyThe authors extend FEF1 computability to agent-specific sizes, giving a strongly polynomial algorithm, a near-tight quadratic repair bound, and NP-hardness results.

Eugene Lim, Nicholas Teh

High-performance metapopulation models for epidemic emergencies: Scalability and performance of implicit and explicit mobility representations on CPUs and GPUs

In briefHigh-performance epidemic models: comparing mobility methods and their speed on CPUs and GPUs

SummaryThis paper compares two ways of modeling population movement in spatial epidemic models: implicit coupling through transition and transmission intensities, and explicit tracking of traveling subpopulations by home and current location. A four-patch experiment shows the two trajectories stay close, but diverge as the mobility cycle length increases. The authors present CPU and GPU parallel implementations and test networks of up to 16,384 patches on 128 CPU cores and NVIDIA A100 and H100 GPUs. In a synthesized central European outbreak, a worldwide pandemic with 12,865 patches and four billion state variables runs in 24 seconds on GPUs.

Henrik Zunker, Carlotta Gerstein, Martin J. K\"uhn

Same Seed, Different Top Prediction: Cross-Platform Reproducibility of AlphaFold 3 Inference on TPUs, GPUs, and CPUs

In briefSame seed, different top prediction: whether AlphaFold 3 gives the same top structure across chips

SummaryThis paper examines whether AlphaFold 3 returns the same top-ranked structure when the same input, weights, seed, and settings run on different hardware. Tests on two TPU generations, three NVIDIA GPUs, and an Intel CPU across 60 protein-ligand complexes found the top sample changed in 18.0% of predictions when switching TPU generations. Structures changed only slightly, with correct-versus-incorrect pose switches in about 3.3% to 3.7% of predictions. The authors recommend reporting the device and all near-tied samples.

Functional Factor Graphs and the Functional Sum-Product Algorithm

In briefFunctional Factor Graphs and Functional Sum-Product Algorithm for Estimating Unknown Functions

SummaryThis paper extends factor graphs and the sum-product algorithm to unknowns that are functions, such as waveforms, time-varying channels, and continuous-aperture array currents. Because functions lack probability density functions, densities are measured relative to each function's prior, so messages become likelihood ratios. For Gaussian models, the functional sum-product algorithm reduces to Gaussian belief propagation with kernels, and its convergence condition does not depend on the grid. Multi-user detection simulations show that scalar belief propagation on grid samples slows under refinement, and that sampling first can fail even for smooth priors.

Kuranage Roche Rayan Ranasinghe, Giuseppe Thadeu Freitas de Abreu

The Weak Negotiation Function

In briefWeak Negotiation Function: A New Tool for Weak Subgame-Perfect Equilibria in Graph Games

SummaryThis paper studies subgame-perfect equilibria (SPEs), a refinement of Nash equilibria that rules out non-credible threats, in graph games. It introduces the weak negotiation function, a variant of the existing negotiation function, and shows that weak SPEs can be characterised by it in the same way SPEs are. Using this characterisation, the authors show that deciding whether a mean-payoff game has a weak SPE with payoffs in a given interval is NP-complete, and give a new proof that the same problem for Büchi games is in P. They also present a much shorter proof of weak SPE existence in mean-payoff games.

Coverage of THz Networks in Presence of Blockages and Scattering

In briefCoverage of Terahertz Networks with Blockages and Scattering

SummaryThis paper develops an analytical framework for cellular terahertz networks that accounts for blockages, scattering elements, directionality, atmospheric absorption, and noise. Scattering elements are modeled as a Poisson point process, and the Laplace transform of their aggregate interference is derived, alongside coverage probability, SINR, rate coverage, and area spectral efficiency. In briefEstimating GPU Roofline arithmetic intensity using off-the-shelf LLMs and compiler artifacts

SummaryThis paper examines whether general-purpose large language models can estimate GPU Roofline arithmetic intensity, the ratio of floating-point operations to device-memory bytes, without running the target workload. In briefOrdering Information in Multispecies Long-Range Swap Processes

SummaryThis paper studies how much the left-to-right species order of interacting particles on the integer lattice reveals about their initial configuration. Under long-range swaps of distinct species, deleting the stronger particles yields coordinates with independent Poisson increments.
