Almost-MMS allocations with small agent redundancy

In briefNear-MMS fair allocations when few extra agents are present

SummaryThe paper studies fair allocation of indivisible goods, asking whether a subset of n agents among more agents can each receive at least a fixed fraction of their n-maximin share (MMS). The authors show that when agent redundancy is positive, this fraction approaches 1 as n grows. This also holds without redundancy when the number of goods is at most (2−c)n. Adapting their techniques, they improve a result of Ezra and Garbuz, showing that without redundancy, n−O(log²n) agents can always receive their full MMS.

WhyThe authors show that with agent redundancy the MMS fraction tends to 1 as n grows, yielding a corollary for m≤(2-c)n and improving Ezra and Garbuz's result.

Uriel Feige, Vadim Grinberg

Plotkin-optimal pure quantum LRCs with large minimum distances from NMDS codes

In briefPlotkin-optimal pure quantum LRCs with large minimum distances from NMDS codes

SummaryThis paper presents a general framework for building optimal pure quantum locally recoverable codes (qLRCs) that reach a Plotkin-like bound, using Hermitian dual-containing near maximum-distance separable (NMDS) codes. It focuses on extended subcodes of generalized Reed-Solomon (ESGRS) codes, determining their localities by analyzing subset sum problems over finite fields. Using the algebraic structure of these codes, the authors construct four families of such qLRCs with large lengths and more flexible minimum distances than existing constructions.

Yang Li, Shitao Li, Gaojun Luo et al.

Computational Aspects of Distortion

In briefComputing Distortion in Social Choice

SummaryThis paper studies the distortion framework in social choice theory, which measures the efficiency of randomized selection of an alternative based on agents' preferences. The authors present a linear-programming algorithm that computes the optimal randomized decision for a given instance, which is simpler and faster than prior methods. They also give a non-convex quadratic programming algorithm to compute the exact distortion of any randomized positional scoring rule.

Soroush Ebadian, Aris Filos-Ratsikas, Mohamad Latifian et al.

Efficient Equilibrium Computation in Colonel Blotto Games

In briefFast Equilibrium Computation for the Colonel Blotto Game

SummaryThe paper studies Colonel Blotto, a resource-allocation game where two players split budgets across battlefields and the higher allocation wins each battlefield's value. It gives a fully polynomial-time approximation scheme that computes an epsilon-Nash equilibrium in time polynomial in the input length and 1/epsilon. For sufficiently balanced budgets and battlefield values, it also computes exact equilibria in linear time.

WhyThe authors report a fully polynomial-time approximation scheme for equilibria in Colonel Blotto games, plus linear-time exact equilibria for balanced cases.

Martin Bichler, Abheek Ghosh

Parallel Sparse Identification of Nonlinear Dynamics in Python: Task- and Data-Parallel SINDy with Numba, OpenMP, Threads and Multiprocessing for the Lorenz and Rössler Systems

In briefParallel Python implementation of sparse nonlinear dynamics identification (SINDy) using Numba, OpenMP, threads and multiprocessing, tested on Lorenz and Rössler systems

SummaryThis paper presents an open-source Python implementation of SINDy, a method that recovers interpretable governing equations from time-series data by sparse regression. The same loop-based kernels are compiled with Numba and run on a shared-memory multicore node through four interchangeable back ends: Numba prange, OpenMP via PyOMP, Python threads, and multiprocessing with shared memory. On the Lorenz and Rössler systems, all back ends recover the exact sparsity pattern, reach 14.8 to 20.3 speed-up on 24 cores, and cut total time to solution by up to 117 times relative to PySINDy.

Pablo Guillen-Rondon

In briefTight Bound for Randomized Truthful Scheduling

SummaryThe paper studies randomized truthful mechanisms for minimizing makespan on unrelated machines. It proves a lower bound of (m+1)/2 on the approximation ratio for truthful-in-expectation mechanisms, determining the optimal ratio as m/2 plus a constant. It also raises the leading constant of the lower bound for universally truthful mechanisms from 3/2−√2 to 1/2, which together with a known upper bound settles that ratio up to lower-order terms.

WhyThe authors prove a (m+1)/2 lower bound for randomized truthful makespan scheduling, improving the leading constant from about 0.0858 to 1/2.

Sharp Bounds for Truthful-in-Expectation Scheduling

In briefTight limits for fair-in-expectation job scheduling on machines

SummaryThe paper studies randomized mechanisms that schedule indivisible jobs on n unrelated machines, where each selfish machine owner privately knows its job processing times. The mechanisms are truthful in expectation and aim to minimize the expected makespan. For n of at least 7, the authors prove the optimal approximation ratio is (n+1)/2, and they give a matching mechanism. For two machines the optimal ratio is about 1.505995705434, and for three machines it is 2.

WhyThe authors report a matching lower and upper bound of (n+1)/2 for n≥7 and exact constants for two and three machines, for truthful-in-expectation scheduling.

Yunpeng Li

Toward Constant-Factor Maximin Share Guarantees for Subadditive Valuations

In briefConstant-factor fair-share guarantees for subadditive valuations

SummaryThe paper studies maximin share (MMS), a fairness measure for dividing indivisible goods among n agents. For general subadditive valuations, it improves the known guarantee from Ω(1/log log n) to Ω(1/log* n). It also introduces q-partitioning valuations, an intermediate class between subadditive and XOS, and proves constant guarantees when q/n has a positive lower bound. For XOS valuations, the universal guarantee rises from about 0.2353 to 0.3125.

WhyThe authors improve the general subadditive MMS guarantee exponentially to Ω(1/log* n) and report constant guarantees for intermediate q-partitioning valuation classes.

Bo Li, Ankang Sun

Rank-metric intersecting codes

In briefRank-metric intersecting codes: extending intersecting codes to the matrix rank metric

SummaryIntersecting codes are codes in which any two nonzero codewords share a common nonzero coordinate. This paper extends the notion to the matrix rank-metric setting, defining the support of a matrix as its row space. Using the slice space of the generator tensor, the authors give a geometric characterization equivalent to the intersecting property, which yields parameter bounds, one of them sharp, along with first constructions. For codes closed under multiplication, i.e. matrix algebras, the intersecting property can be read from the Jacobson radical and the socle, enabling flexible construction of many such codes.

Martino Borello, Martin Scotti, Lune Vila

A Short Proof and Generalization of the Rainbow Cycle Theorem and Improved Bounds for Binary Even-Query LCCs

In briefShort proof and generalization of the rainbow cycle theorem, with improved bounds for binary even-query LCCs

SummaryThe paper studies rainbow cycles, which are cycles in which no color appears more than once. It gives a short proof of an earlier result showing that a properly edge-colored graph with sufficiently large average degree always contains a rainbow cycle, and extends this to weighted graphs with list-colored edges. Using this generalization, the authors prove an upper bound on the dimension of binary linear even-query locally correctable codes, closing the gap with the best known lower bounds for odd-query codes.

Andrew D. Lin
