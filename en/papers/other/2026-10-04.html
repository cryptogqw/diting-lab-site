A FAS Channel Fitting Strategy Using Extreme Value Distributions for Accurate Outage Performance Evaluation

In briefFitting Fluid Antenna Channels with Extreme Value Distributions for Accurate Outage Evaluation

SummaryThis paper proposes a channel fitting strategy for single-antenna fluid antenna systems (FAS) that focuses on accurately evaluating outage probability (OP). It introduces a combined extreme value distribution (CEVD), using a generalized Pareto distribution for the left tail and a generalized extreme value distribution for global behavior. A modified mean-square-error criterion with logarithmic-domain errors and uniform sampling on the log CDF is developed to emphasize tail accuracy. Simulations show improved OP evaluation accuracy in the ultra-low-OP regime.

Improved Bounds on the Ingleton Score of Four Random Variables

In briefTighter Upper Bound on the Ingleton Score for Four Random Variables

SummaryThis paper studies four finite discrete random variables and shows that the negative Ingleton expression divided by joint entropy is at most 19/124 (about 0.15323). This improves the previously reported bound of 3/19 by 11/2356. The proof uses a copy program with four copy steps and six auxiliary variables, verified by exact rational linear-programming certificates. The authors also show that two published inequalities imply the bound 5/32.

Secure Multi-Access Coded Caching: A Lifting Approach

In briefSecure multi-access coded caching via a lifting transformation

SummaryThe paper builds secure cyclic multi-access coded caching schemes by re-encoding the caches of a secure single-access scheme while reusing its multicast message. Each user reads L consecutive shared caches, recovers its requested file, and learns nothing about other files. Deterministic Pascal-window and randomized complement-dual encoders are given, attaining exact minimum per-block storage over a suitable finite field. The resulting memory-rate family has approximation guarantees against converse bounds, and the L=K-1 tradeoff is proved exactly.

Han Fang, Qinyi Lu, Nan Liu et al.

HiNa-MoE: High-Performance, Non-Intrusive MoE Inference on CPUs with Matrix Engines

In briefHiNa-MoE: Fast, Non-Intrusive MoE Inference on CPUs with Matrix Engines

SummaryThis paper presents HiNa-MoE, an operator library that speeds up Mixture-of-Experts model inference on Intel AMX-equipped CPUs. It keeps expert weights in standard layouts, uses a simple page-interleaved policy for NUMA-aware work partitioning, and converts decode-phase matrix-vector operations into small matrix-matrix operations. The authors report speedups of up to 3.37x for FFN kernels and up to 2.09x for end-to-end inference over baselines, and state that it works plug-and-play with existing frameworks.

CommuteProp: Decoupled Training for Communication Bound Split LLM Fine-Tuning

In briefCommuteProp: Decoupled Training for Communication-Bound Split LLM Fine-Tuning

SummaryThis paper addresses inefficiency in privacy-preserving split-learning fine-tuning of large language models, where clients sit idle waiting for server-side gradients. It proposes CommuteProp, an asynchronous algorithm that splits training into two concurrent phases, a cross-block forward-backward pass and an in-block weight update, so computation overlaps with communication. The authors provide asynchronous error and convergence analysis and derive an NS preconditioner to reduce staleness-induced noise. Experiments report substantial throughput gains with accuracy comparable to synchronous methods, and the method can be plugged into existing privacy-enhancement techniques.

CHEN Ding, LUO Haochen, LIU Chen

In briefSecure device-to-device coded caching with limited-radius connections using lifting

SummaryThis paper studies secure coded caching in a device-to-device network where users on a cycle can only communicate with nearby users within a fixed radius. Each user must generate delivery signals from its cache and recover its requested file without learning any other file. The authors construct such schemes using an outer secret-sharing code and a secure local caching scheme, and they prove information-theoretic secrecy along with converse bounds on peak sum load. Within certain memory ranges, the schemes achieve optimal load or a constant-factor approximation.

You Only Convert Twice: Ozaki Scheme II for Chained Tensor Mode Products

In briefYou Only Convert Twice: Ozaki Scheme II for Repeated Tensor Mode Products

SummaryThis paper proposes OzII-RescaleBE, which keeps intermediate tensors in residue form during chained tensor mode products in Ozaki scheme II. Inputs are converted to residues only once at the start and reconstructed only at the end, with an implementation on INT8 Tensor Cores using CuTe DSL. Across three datasets and chain depths 3 to 7, normwise relative errors stay near 1e-16 to 1e-15, but rise to about 1e-13 at depth 8. Throughput and memory use are compared against two GEMMul8 baselines, with gains at smaller mode sizes and a slight deficit at size 256.

Social Optimality Does Not Imply Coalitional Stability in Unweighted Max-k-Cut Games

In briefOptimal colorings in unweighted Max-k-Cut games can still be unstable to group deviations

SummaryThis paper studies Max-k-Cut games on unweighted graphs, where optimal colorings maximize social welfare. The authors disprove a conjecture by showing that for every k≥3 there is a finite, simple, connected graph with an optimal coloring that is not a strong equilibrium. A coalition can profitably deviate to another optimal coloring, benefiting all its members while outside players lose and social welfare is unchanged. The paper also gives an exact identity linking coalition gains, cut changes and internal conflicts, and bounds monochromatic edges remaining after strong deviations from general Nash equilibria.

Scalable Distortion-Aware Clustering for Fronthaul-Limited Cell-Free MIMO Networks

In briefScalable distortion-aware clustering for fronthaul-limited cell-free MIMO networks

SummaryThis paper studies how to group access points in uplink cell-free MIMO networks with fronthaul limits, using maximum ratio combining. The optimization depends only on large-scale fading coefficients, and asymptotic analysis gives deterministic equivalent expressions for average network sum rate that account for quantization distortion. The authors exploit the structure of the resulting quadratic-over-linear objective to develop a polynomial-time scheme that reaches the global optimum. Numerical results show gains over improved existing methods and competitive performance against an exhaustive-search global optimum based on small-scale fading.

Toward a Lean Formalization of Analog Computing with Microwaves

In briefFormalizing Microwave Analog Computing in Lean

SummaryThis paper takes a first step toward formalizing microwave analog computing in Lean, a proof assistant. Microwave signals can perform linear transformations directly as they propagate through a network. The authors formalize components such as hybrid couplers and phase shifters, their series and parallel connections, and the networks they form. They formally prove a necessary and sufficient condition for the transformations these networks can compute, and that the discrete Fourier transform of any power-of-two size is implementable. All proofs are checked by the Lean kernel, and the code is publicly available.

In briefA unified framework linking sensing, computation, and communication in over-the-air federated learning

Approximate Feedback Nash Equilibria in Constrained Differential Games via Model Predictive Control with Upper Bound Guarantees

In briefApproximate feedback Nash equilibria in constrained games via model predictive control with a provable error bound

SummaryThis paper proposes a model predictive control method that approximates feedback Nash equilibrium trajectories in differential games by repeatedly solving finite-horizon open-loop games.
