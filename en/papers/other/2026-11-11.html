Rateless Coding for Efficient Model Broadcast in Federated Learning

In briefRateless coding for efficient model broadcast in federated learning

SummaryThis paper addresses model broadcast in federated learning over wireless channels, where large models and unreliable channels create challenges. The authors propose a joint source-channel coding scheme based on rateless coding to improve downlink broadcast robustness and efficiency. They perform a convergence analysis of federated learning to guide the rateless code and quantization design, and use clients' local models as conditional prior information to aid decoding and reduce transmission cost. Simulations show improved downlink broadcast efficiency with training accuracy comparable for both homogeneous and heterogeneous data distributions.

Zijun Qin, Zuolin Jin, Jingxuan Huang et al.

VR-Net: Subarray-Level Inter-Cell Coordination for Multi-Cell XL-MIMO under Near-Field Spatial Non-Stationarity

In briefVR-Net: Coordinated Beamforming Across Cells for Large Antenna Arrays Near the User

SummaryThis paper proposes VR-Net, a method for multi-cell extremely large-scale MIMO systems where users are only visible to parts of each antenna array due to near-field effects. It uses each user's visibility region to coordinate beamforming at the subarray level, identifying overlapping regions with a conflict graph and restricting beam design and channel feedback to relevant subarray blocks. In simulations based on a 3GPP near-field urban channel model, VR-Net keeps at least 99.6% of the full-aperture sum rate at a 30 dB threshold while uploading 10–39% fewer complex CSI entries.

Muhammad Shoaib Ayub, Rana Muhammad Subhan, Insoo Koo et al.

MetaCom: Meta Codebook Learning for Robust V2X Semantic Communications

In briefMetaCom: Meta-learned codebooks for robust vehicle communication

SummaryThis paper addresses collaborative perception in Vehicle-to-Everything (V2X) networks, where bandwidth is limited and channels are impaired. The authors report better trade-offs between perception precision and communication overhead, with significant gains over state-of-the-art methods at low SNR.

Reduced-Complexity WMMSE Approach for Cell-Free Massive MIMO Systems With Finite-Capacity Fronthaul Links

In briefLow-complexity WMMSE method for cell-free massive MIMO with limited-capacity fronthaul links

SummaryThis paper proposes a reduced-complexity weighted minimum mean squared error (R-WMMSE) framework for cell-free massive MIMO systems whose fronthaul links have finite capacity. Access points are optimized sequentially, with each subproblem solved via Lagrangian duality using closed-form updates, to jointly design beamforming and fronthaul compression. The framework is also extended to hybrid analog-digital beamforming. Numerical results show performance nearly identical to the conventional WMMSE while reducing runtime by over 99%.

Explicit Subspace Evasive Sets via Subspace Designs

In briefExplicit subspace evasive sets built from subspace designs

SummaryThe paper studies subspace evasive sets over finite fields, where a set meets every affine subspace of dimension at most r in at most c points. For constant r and 1/ε, it gives an explicit construction with intersection bound O(min(r·log(r/ε)/ε, r²/ε)), improving on earlier explicit constructions whose c grows exponentially in r. The approach composes subspace design codes with a suitably chosen local subspace evasive set. As a consequence, the authors obtain the first explicit (strongly) capacity-achieving list-recoverable code with nearly optimal list size.

In briefSubGPU Pipelining: Fine-grained GPU-internal parallelism for faster LLM inference

The Price of Anarchy in Markets for Lemons

In briefThe Price of Anarchy in Markets for Lemons: a study of how much trade gain is lost in equilibrium under interdependent valuations.

SummaryThis paper studies the worst-case equilibrium gains from trade in simple bilateral trade mechanisms where buyers and sellers' valuations are interdependent. Proposed prices can reveal the proposer's private information, so equilibria lose gains both from screening inefficiency and adverse selection. The authors identify broad conditions under which sequential equilibria still achieve a constant fraction of first-best gains from trade, highlighting rare pessimistic seller and optimistic buyer signals. The results apply to seller-proposer, buyer-proposer, and random-proposer mechanisms.

Naveen Durvasula, Tim Roughgarden

In briefComBO: A community-based Bayesian optimization method for switching off carriers in HAPS-Hypercell networks

SummaryThis paper studies carrier shutdown in networks assisted by high-altitude platform stations (HAPS) and Hypercells, using network-wide information to optimize which coverage macro cells and capacity cells are switched off. The authors propose ComBO, which builds a graph from user reference signal power reports to find groups of closely linked cells and splits the problem into smaller subproblems solved with trust region Bayesian optimization, followed by a final joint search. In briefArtificial Currency and Separate Selling: A 3-Approximation for Consumer Surplus

SummaryThis paper studies how to allocate indivisible items to buyers whose values are drawn independently, aiming to maximize consumer surplus after subtracting burnt payments. The authors propose artificial-currency mechanisms, giving each buyer a personalized budget of currency that has no value outside the mechanism. Second-Best Trade Is Half Efficient in Multidimensional Markets

In briefSecond-best trade achieves half of first-best efficiency in multidimensional markets

SummaryThe paper proves that at least half of the first-best gains from trade can always be achieved in multidimensional two-sided markets with additive values and costs and arbitrary downward-closed feasibility constraints. The guarantee is delivered by a mechanism that is Bayesian incentive compatible, interim individually rational, and strongly budget balanced. The result extends the known tight one-half bound for single-item bilateral trade, and the proof applies the Cai–Devanur–Weinberg duality framework.

In briefExplicit restricted isometries with sparsity k = m^{2/3−o(1)}
