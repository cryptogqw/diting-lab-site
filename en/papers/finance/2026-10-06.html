Modelling Regime Shifts in Continuous Intraday Electricity Markets with State-dependent Hawkes Processes

In briefModelling Regime Shifts in Electricity Intraday Markets with State-Dependent Hawkes Processes

SummaryThis paper proposes a multivariate state-dependent Hawkes process to model order flow across liquidity regimes in the continuous intraday electricity market. Market states are identified with a Liquidity Stress Index built from bid-ask spread, order book volume, and mid-price volatility. Calibrated on Dutch XBID hourly order data from EPEX SPOT for January-March 2024, the estimates show strongly self-exciting, near-critical order flow, with liquidity stress mainly strengthening same-side transaction self-excitation.

Ayoub Jhabli, Tarek AlSkaif, Kwabena E. Bennin et al.

FinVector-Market-4B: A Controlled Study of LoRA Adaptation for Structured Financial Tasks

In briefFinVector-Market-4B: a controlled test of LoRA tuning for structured finance tasks

SummaryThis paper fine-tunes the Qwen3.5-4B model with rank-16 LoRA on 22,000 examples for structured financial tasks, then compares it with the base model on a 600-example benchmark. Supplying a JSON schema alone raised the base model's JSON validity from 0% to 91.3%. Under matched explicit prompting, several task scores also improved. The authors note that the reported macro-F1 drop reflects a changed label set, and that filing overlap and other issues limit the generalization claims.

A Finite Bid--Ask Spread from Replenishment Displaced from the Quote

In briefFinite bid-ask spread from replenishment placed away from the quote

SummaryThis paper gives an analytic account of a finite bid-ask spread in a two-field reaction-diffusion order book model that tracks bid and ask densities over operational time. A market-making source withholds order placement over a finite interval around the reaction boundary, representing replenishment displaced from the quote. Under a quasistationary, weak-overlap approximation, the quoted spread is the sub-threshold core of the placement-free interval. Market orders remove density at the quote, while replenishment arrives outside it and must diffuse back, producing a delayed and attenuated quote-level response.

Christopher Angstmann, Derick Diana, Tim Gebbie

A Functional Representation of Credit Behavior for Probability of Default Modeling

In briefA functional representation of credit behavior for default probability modeling

SummaryThis paper proposes a framework that models probability of default using functional data analysis, representing credit variables as continuous functions. It shows that commonly used variables such as available funds, utilization rate, and overdraft can be derived from three time-observed quantities: account type, account balance, and credit limit. A relative time framework aligns recurring banking-day cycles across borrowers, and the approach is evaluated with functional logistic regression. The model reportedly matches XGBoost's predictive performance while outperforming standard logistic regression and retaining the interpretability of linear models.

Jonas Brunholm, Bjarne H{\o}jgaard, Thomas D. Nielsen et al.

In briefLearned Monotone Recurrent Features in Regulated Credit Scoring

SummaryThis paper examines whether learned monotone recurrent features add value to regulated credit scoring, testing five production-scale credit datasets. Convex Order Beyond Dimension One: Projection Tests, Counterexamples and Gaussian Mixtures

In briefConvex Order in Higher Dimensions: Projection Tests, Counterexamples, and Gaussian Mixtures

SummaryThis paper studies convex order, a way to compare random vectors with the same mean, in dimensions greater than one. It shows that checking all one-dimensional projections is not sufficient in general and builds radial counterexamples in every dimension at least two. The authors prove that projection tests do suffice for linear transformations of a common radially distributed vector, including centred Gaussian distributions. They also settle when a centred Gaussian is dominated by a mixture of two centred Gaussians, showing this holds exactly when all one-dimensional projections are ordered.

Olivier Gu\'eant

Adversarial Training for Deep Hedging in Nonstationary Markets

In briefAdversarial training for deep hedging when markets shift over time

SummaryThis paper proposes WRAP, a drift-aware adversarial training framework for deep hedging based on a two-budget distributionally robust optimization formulation. It lets an adversary reweight observed trading trajectories under a φ-divergence constraint and perturb their paths under an optimal-transport constraint, to handle mismatch between historical training data and future market conditions. Experiments on stationary and nonstationary Heston dynamics and a generalized affine diffusion (GAD) show that reweighting and transport each help, with joint adversarial training giving the largest gains under nonstationarity.

Philipp J. Schneider, Lukas Looser, Antoine Garin et al.

A posteriori error bounds for the Uncertain Volatility Model

In briefError bounds for option pricing under volatility uncertainty

SummaryThe paper develops a posteriori primal-dual bounds for numerical approximations of European option prices in the Uncertain Volatility Model. A lower bound comes from a feedback control induced by the Hessian of a smooth candidate value function, while an upper bound comes from a dual representation based on a matrix-valued Gamma field. The authors also quantify discretization errors and test candidates from policy-gradient and physics-informed neural networks. The experiments indicate that second-order derivative accuracy is important for tight bounds.
