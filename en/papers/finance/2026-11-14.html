Corporate Stances on Societal Issues in the US

In briefUS Companies Taking Public Stands on Social Issues

SummaryThis paper uses large language models to read 52,000 news articles and press releases about 836 S&P 500 companies from 2008 to 2025, identifying 9,917 corporate stances on nine divisive issues such as climate change, abortion, and racial discrimination. Stance-taking rose fourfold after 2008, peaked in 2021, was mostly liberal, and concentrated among the largest firms. Stances track media coverage and public interest rather than the electoral cycle or lobbying, suggesting they contribute to broad policy debates.

Jacob Conway, Andrew Hall, Magdalena Larreboure et al.

Optimal Capital Injections in a Microlending Group under External Shocks

In briefOptimal capital injections for a microlending group facing external shocks

SummaryThis paper studies how the manager of an emergency fund for a microlending group should inject capital into members hit by external shocks such as droughts or floods. Member wealth evolves between shocks under a linear stochastic differential equation, and the problem is formulated as a Markov decision process embedded at shock times. Under a condition on the discount rate, the authors show that a stationary optimal injection policy exists that does not depend on calendar time.

H\'ector Jasso-Fuentes, Alejandra Quintos, Xinta Yang

Short Windows, Unmodelled Costs, Unknown Cutoffs: A Pre-Registered Evidence Audit of 91 LLM Trading Papers

In briefPre-registered audit of 91 LLM trading papers on test windows, costs and data leakage

SummaryThis paper audits 91 research papers on large-language-model trading agents and alpha mining, using a coding rubric and analysis script pre-registered before coding. Of the 75 papers with a trading test, 51% deduct no trading costs and only 9% model costs from data. Median test windows are 12 months, and about a quarter are six months or shorter. Only five papers combine deducted costs, a leakage control and a reported trial count, and two of five pre-stated hypotheses were supported.

Henry Zhang

The opposing informational effects of AI trading

In briefOpposing information effects of AI trading

SummaryThis paper examines how more capable AI affects the information content of prices. Better algorithms can substitute analysis of public records for private observations, reducing the private information aggregated into prices, while trades can also convey sophisticated public-data analysis to investors who cannot perform it themselves. The authors test these effects with numerical equilibrium calculations, finite-data neural-market experiments, and earnings forecasting and language-model tasks.

Hajime Shimao

Alignment by Socialization: Learning Tradeoffs Under Selection

In briefAlignment by Socialization: Learning Tradeoffs Under Selection

SummaryThis paper studies how AI agents choose between completing tasks and following rules of conduct, and whether experience under evolutionary selection makes those choices match human judgments. In the model, agent copies face an uncertain reward state and only those taking the rewarded action reproduce. A learned policy converges to the tradeoffs its environment rewards, and when humans supply the rewards this is called socialization.

Thomas Brennan, Andrew W. Lo, Chaoyi Zhao

DECODE: Decomposable Environments for COntrolled Sequential-Decision Evaluation

In briefDECODE: A Benchmark for Controlled Testing of Sequential Decision Systems

SummaryDECODE is a benchmark for testing which changes improve sequential decision systems, using controlled experiments on planning horizons and available information. Its consumer-credit collections generator provides five partially observed environments with shared known dynamics. On the deterministic base environment, extending planning from one to six months raised two gradient-boosted solvers' scores by 15 to 17 percentage points of the exact optimum. The authors note that intermediate effects depend on the repayment model and the order of changes, so the gap cannot be uniquely assigned to separate causes.

Diego Silva Prado, Bruno Caldas de Souza, Tiago Amaral Penteado de Jesus et al.
