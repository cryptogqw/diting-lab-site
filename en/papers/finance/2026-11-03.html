Multidimensional Views Behind Target-Price Revisions: An LLM-Based Representation of Analyst Outlooks

In briefMultidimensional Analyst Views Behind Target-Price Changes: LLM-Based Outlook Representation

SummaryThe paper proposes an LLM-based method that extracts analysts' views from research reports as question-based outlook scores on dimensions such as earnings, valuation, and industry conditions, with supporting evidence. In walk-forward evaluation, these scores improve prediction of subsequent target-price revisions beyond current-revision momentum and sector differences. They also outperform financial dictionary and FinBERT sentiment measures.

Kiyoun Kim, Sung-Yeon Nathan Lim

Decomposing Rank Reversals under Temporal Nonstationarity: Alignment, Aggressiveness, and Instability

In briefDecomposing Model Rank Reversals Under Time-Varying Data: Alignment, Aggressiveness, and Instability

SummaryThe paper studies why model performance rankings reverse across different training windows when data is temporally nonstationary. The authors decompose out-of-sample R² into target alignment, miscalibrated aggressiveness, and predictive instability within a model-agnostic framework. They derive closed-form optimal memory equations for ridge regression under local drift and extend the guarantees to complex models via surrogate bounds. Benchmarking 14 linear, tree-based, and neural models on large financial panels, they find rank reversals are driven by predictable surges in miscalibrated aggressiveness.

Deying Song, Xueying Ding

Are LLM Research Agents Reliable Judges of Predictability? Evidence from Planted-Signal Worlds

In briefCan LLM Research Agents Reliably Judge Whether Models Are Predictable? Tests in Simulated Worlds With Planted Signals

SummaryThe authors test whether large language model research agents correctly decide if a forecasting model beats a static benchmark, using simulated worlds where a signal is planted. The agents detected weak signals more often than a pre-specified out-of-sample test, largely because they accepted weaker evidence. They also claimed predictability too often in worlds with no signal. The authors conclude the verdicts are informative but require a neutral brief, a decision rule set outside the agent, and a sealed holdout.

Chang Liu

PanelGP: A Probabilistic Factor Model for Asset-Return Panels

In briefPanelGP: a probabilistic model linking stock traits and economic conditions to returns and risk

SummaryPanelGP is a conditional factor model for large panels of stock returns, where firm characteristics set factor loadings and economic conditions set expected factors, both estimated with Gaussian-process priors. It nests instrumented principal component analysis (IPCA) and jointly forecasts expected returns and their covariance matrix. On S&P 500 constituents from 1995 to 2024, nonlinear loadings rank stocks better than affine loadings and yield higher Sharpe ratios than IPCA, though predicted variances are too compressed.

Andrea Ruglioni, Damir Filipovic (EPFL)

Robust distortion riskmetrics over Bregman--Wasserstein balls and their intersections

In briefWorst-case risk measures over overlapping Bregman–Wasserstein balls

SummaryThe paper studies worst-case values of distortion riskmetrics over Bregman–Wasserstein balls around a reference distribution. Unlike the squared 2-Wasserstein distance, these divergences can penalize upward and downward deviations differently. For strictly convex generators, it gives a one-dimensional dual representation and characterizes when the supremum is attained. The analysis is extended to intersections of such balls and illustrated numerically.

Peng Liu, Steven Vanduffel, Carole Bernard

Thinking Fast, Trading Late: System One Decision Models vs the Market

In briefThinking Fast, Trading Late: System One Decision Models vs the Market

SummaryThis paper examines whether the stock-return predictability of large language models reading company news survives strict tests that rule out look-ahead, memorization and selection. The authors propose OLIVIA, a testing protocol, and apply it to non-generative language models that give probabilities for questions about S&P 500 news, including an open-weights model fine-tuned on 2024–2025 data and three commercial models. Results show the fine-tuned model's signal replicated on validation quarters but weakened on the blinded test period. The commercial models' readings match the overnight move to the open but not the move afterwards, indicating the news is priced before a trader can act on it.

WhyThe authors propose OLIVIA, a pre-registered protocol, and report that LLM news signals decay on a blinded test and are priced before the open.

Amir Dehkordi (Independent Researcher), Alireza Kargarzadeh (TailState Intelligence Ltd), Nariman Khaledian (Independent Researcher) et al.

Symbol-based residual certification of reduced and neural surrogates for tempered-fractional CGMY pricing

In briefCertifying simplified and neural models for option pricing under the tempered-fractional CGMY model

SummaryThe paper checks how accurate simplified and neural approximations are for European put prices under the CGMY option pricing model, using residual error bounds from a spline Galerkin discretisation. The system matrices are built from an explicit periodised Fourier symbol, and a Lévy–Khintchine argument gives a parameter-explicit eigenvalue bound. The authors report that, at full resolution (N=1024), the certified reduced model costs about 3% of a direct solve and settles 99.8% of 10^4 queries without the full space.</summary>

Penalize the Common Trade: A Collision-Aware Layer for Learned Portfolios

In briefPenalize the Common Trade: A Collision-Aware Layer for Learned Portfolios

SummaryThe paper argues that learning systems sharing data, objectives, or representations can make nearly identical trades, creating a "signal collision." A linear-impact game shows allocators take on too much common exposure, and an overlap-dependent toll can recover the social optimum. The authors propose a simple post-model layer that penalizes loading on the recent common return mode, which lowered maximum drawdown in a panel of ten liquid cross-asset ETFs, though the drawdown-improvement confidence interval includes zero.

Eric Benhamou, Jean-Jacques Ohana, Beatrice Guez et al.

Specification LASSO and a Flexible Characteristics-Based Asset Pricing Model

In briefSpecification LASSO and a Flexible Characteristics-Based Asset Pricing Model: a statistical methods paper on variable selection and model specification

SummaryThis paper studies a partially linear semiparametric additive model that allows interactions among variables in high-dimensional settings. The authors propose the Specification LASSO (S-LASSO), a two-step method combining LASSO and Adaptive Group LASSO to perform variable selection and model specification simultaneously. The paper establishes oracle properties, including selection consistency and asymptotic normality, and Monte Carlo simulations show it outperforms existing alternatives. Empirically, the method is applied to a characteristics-based asset pricing model to examine linear and nonlinear effects of asset characteristics on cross-sectional returns and their interactions with firm size.

Chaohua Dong, Shuyi Ge, Shaoran Li et al.

Can We Stop the Backtest Yet? Anytime-Valid Promotion Tests for Adaptive Financial Research Agents

In briefCan We Stop the Backtest? Anytime-Valid Promotion Tests for Financial Research Agents

SummaryThe paper introduces PROMOTE, a protocol that gives each candidate trading rule predictable testing wealth at birth and accumulates a cost-aware e-process from subsequent returns only. If lifetime allocations sum to at most one, a weighted Ville argument bounds the probability of promoting a rule whose bounded net score has always had nonpositive conditional mean. The method allows dependent candidates, adaptive births, and indefinite monitoring. The authors evaluate the implementation in a retrospective replay and a controlled experiment, comparing it with repeated unadjusted tests. The certificate controls historical false promotion under the declared null and does not show that an earlier edge remains profitable at deployment.

Eric Benhamou, Jean-Jacques Ohana, Beatrice Guez et al.

EventGPT: Next-Event Pretraining on Banking Customer Histories

In briefEventGPT: a model pretrained to predict banking customers' next events

SummaryThe paper introduces EventGPT, a hierarchical causal transformer pretrained to predict a customer's next event, which lets it estimate future event probabilities without labels and provide reusable representations for downstream banking tasks. Trained on the public MBD-mini dataset, it scores five next-month first-transaction targets at ROC-AUC 0.79 to 0.92. For product prediction, its frozen embeddings score slightly below engineered features, but combining them with those features improves results. The code and pretrained weights are released.

Denis Burakov
