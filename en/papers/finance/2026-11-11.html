A high-order recombination algorithm with adaptive scale selection for weak approximation of stochastic differential equations

In briefAdaptive-scale high-order recombination for weak approximation of stochastic differential equations

SummaryThe paper proposes a high-order recombination method that keeps weak approximation trees of diffusions small by moment-preserving reduction of measure on each node patch. Its anisotropically weighted Litterer–Lyons (AWLL) rule selects the patch scale adaptively during recombination, instead of relying on a fixed scalar parameter. The scale is also extended to a positive definite matrix with joint error and patch-count bounds, and the elimination stage of batched basis exchange is reduced to linear arithmetic cost. Numerical tests are reported for Heston and shifted SABR systems.

Yuming Ma, Syoiti Ninomiya

Least Expensive Standard Pensions

In briefLeast Expensive Standard Pensions: A Low-Cost Way to Generate Annuity Payments

SummaryThe paper proposes generating annuity payment streams at much lower costs than traditional theories imply. For a given investment universe, it designs a least expensive standard pension that makes accurate monthly payments of savings account units over a set period. The pension invests dynamically in the savings account and the equity market's growth-optimal portfolio to replicate riskless payments at the lowest possible cost. The authors state it is orders of magnitude cheaper than traditional pensions.

Eckhard Platen

Beyond Named Mechanisms: Economic Institutions as a Design Space for Artificial Agents

In briefBeyond Named Mechanisms: Economic Institutions as a Design Space for Artificial Agents

SummaryThe paper argues that multiagent research borrows economic terms such as auctions, markets, and matching from human societies, which may not capture all possible institutions for artificial agents with different constraints. The authors propose a provisional chart that describes institutions along dimensions including positions, rights and duties, allocation, information, time, and external recognition. A simulation shows that one technical act, copying an agent's competence, can be institutionalized as an independent supplier, licensed capacity, or the splitting of a position into successors, producing identical technical traces but different participants, ownership, obligations, and payments.

Nikolos Gurney, Fred Morstatter

When Does a Recommendation Spark Curiosity? Theory, Measurement, and Field Evidence

In briefWhen Recommendations Spark Curiosity: Theory, Measurement, and Field Tests

SummaryThe paper formalizes curiosity, the drive to close the gap between what people know and want to know, into five axioms and builds a computational measure of the curiosity a recommended item evokes. In a survey study, this measure matched consumers' self-reported curiosity and outperformed alternative proxy metrics. A curiosity-aware recommender that incorporates the measure into its utility function outperformed state-of-the-art methods on three real-world datasets and, in a large online experiment at an Asian video-streaming platform, increased click-through rate, video views, and time spent.

WhyThe authors formalize information-gap theory into five axioms, build a curiosity measure, and report offline and large-scale online experiments on a video platform.

Pan Li, Alexander Tuzhilin

The Rebundling of Freelance Work in the AI Age

In briefRebundling of Freelance Work in the AI Era

SummaryThis paper compares activities people perform in ChatGPT with work clients request on Upwork, an online freelance platform, and studies how clients combine tasks into projects. It finds that activities more common in ChatGPT lose marketplace share on Upwork, while less common ones gain share. AI-related work shows more diverse demand, with larger project budgets and more hours, while hourly rates differ little.

Caroline Chin, Shagun Kala, Nadav Kunievsky et al.

Conformal Risk Certificates for Portfolio Optimization Under Uncertainty: A Gated Reinforcement-Learning Approach

In briefConformal Risk Certificates for Portfolio Optimization Under Uncertainty: A Gated Reinforcement-Learning Approach

SummaryThe paper proposes Gated Conformal Semantic Risk Control, which checks reinforcement-learning portfolio allocations against a certified risk budget before execution. A language model converts timestamped disclosures into semantic risk states that define conformally calibrated uncertainty sets, and an asymmetric second-order cone gate intervenes only when a proposed allocation exceeds the budget. In walk-forward tests on nine U.S. Select Sector SPDR ETFs, the gated method removed all observed out-of-sample semantic limit breaches while keeping an 11.7% annual return, compared with 12.7% and a 4.05% violation rate for raw PPO.

Mohammad Zarei, Amirhossein MohammadpourAzari, Moslem Habibi

Point-in-Time Encoders for Earnings Calls

In briefPoint-in-Time Encoders for Earnings Calls: Text Vectors Without Look-Ahead Bias

SummaryThis paper trains text encoders on earnings call transcripts in yearly vintages from 2010 to 2024, where each vintage reads only text dated on or before its cutoff to avoid look-ahead bias in backtests. The models are trained on 412,248 calls and fine-tuned on call question-answer pairs. Vintages trained only on calls retrieve answers better than the general retrievers tested, and their vectors raise the rank correlation between next-day returns and forecasts. Point-in-time vintages matter most for retrieval and offer little gain for return forecasts.

Alessio Brini

Deep Learning for Operator Maps in Optimal Liquidation

In briefDeep learning approximation of operator maps in optimal trade liquidation

SummaryThis paper studies an optimal execution problem where prices follow dynamics affected by a general transient impact kernel. It shows that the optimal trading strategy depends locally Lipschitz continuously on the model parameters governing temporary and transient price impact and the underlying price process, which justifies neural network approximations using these parameters as inputs. The authors propose methods for generating kernel samples, projecting the kernel to a finite-dimensional representation, and reducing dimension based on the relation between inventory and trade signal. They use the trained network to quantify potential financial costs of misspecifying the transient impact kernel.

Ryan Donnelly, Luca Galimberti, Brian Healy et al.
