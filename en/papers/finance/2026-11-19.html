A Three-Factor Hull-White Scenario Generator for Macro-Financial Stress Forecasting

In briefA three-factor Hull-White model for forecasting macro-financial stress scenarios

SummaryThis paper builds a scenario engine that maps a yield-curve path into a distribution of macro-financial outcomes. It uses a three-factor Hull-White model to generate arbitrage-free curves, simulating the factors under the physical measure and reporting an estimated market price of risk. A Minnesota-prior VARX links these curves to weekly equity returns, the federal funds rate, the Weekly Economic Index, inflation and a bank Marginal Expected Shortfall proxy. Out-of-sample tests from 2017 to 2024 show the term-structure block has the lowest forecast error in 13 of 20 maturity and horizon cells.

Ahmad Qadi, Thomas Schr\"oder, Francesca Medda

Equity and Aggregate Ambiguity

In briefFairness Versus Overall Ambiguity in Public Risk Sharing

SummaryThis paper examines the conflict between equitable resource allocation and ambiguity avoidance in public risk planning. Working with symmetric aggregate losses that have dependence uncertainty among individual losses, it shows that in Keeney's (1980) classical model, greater equity is equivalent to greater aggregate ambiguity. Using the α-maxmin preference framework, the authors identify the ambiguity and risk attitudes under which equity and ambiguity avoidance each hold, and numerical examples illustrate the tradeoff.

Jose Blanchet, Yang Liu, Tiantian Mao et al.

Feasible sub-replicating portfolios for basket options via the block rearrangement algorithm

In briefSparse sub-replicating portfolios for basket options using block rearrangement

SummaryThe paper builds sub-replicating portfolios of vanilla options for a basket call when the marginal distributions of the underlying assets are known but their dependence is not. It reduces the n-asset problem to two-asset problems and uses the Block Rearrangement Algorithm to approximate the minimising dependence, producing lower and upper bounds on the basket price. In log-normal examples with three and six assets the bracket is within 0.1%, the portfolios use only two strikes per asset, and the bounds beat a multi-marginal optimal-transport benchmark. Closed-form lower bounds and optimal portfolios are also given for three special cases.

Mohamad Hassan Abou Daya, Carole Bernard, Steven Vanduffel

Neckpinches in a Positively Curved Market: Ricci Flow with Surgery on the Nikkei-225

In briefCurvature-Based Surgery on Nikkei-225 Stock Correlations

SummaryThis paper analyzes the Nikkei-225 index by building a correlation graph of its stocks and measuring its discrete Ollivier-Ricci curvature. Applying a graph Ricci flow with surgery splits the graph into 184- and 42-node components. The authors' recursive ARCUS procedure performs 28 surgeries and yields 29 terminal clusters, many of which correspond to single industries such as banks, insurers and trading companies.

Siddharta Laloux, Bhargavi Srinivasan

Trading Before a Committed Rebalance

In briefTrading before a committed rebalance: how public forecasts affect institutional execution cost

SummaryAn institution must finish a portfolio adjustment, while traders preparing inventory for it can choose whether to take part. The paper builds a Bayesian execution game with optional activation, transient impact, and uniform-price terminal completion to study how public forecasts affect the institution's cost. In the scalar model information generally helps, but optional supply can reverse this ranking in some cases, and a matrix criterion is given for the portfolio extension. Intraday ETF price data is used to characterize target uncertainty and trading conditions.

Khizar Qureshi

Auto-Alpha: A Framework for Autonomous Factor Research

In briefAuto-Alpha: A Framework for Autonomous Factor Research

SummaryThe paper presents Auto-Alpha, a framework in which an idea agent proposes economic mechanisms and signal designs, and a research agent implements and tests them using backtest feedback. Factors pass semantic audit, quantitative review and human approval before entering a persistent library, and an Experience Library stores research insights. Starting from 120 reports, the system built 32 initial factors and retained 62 more from 420 implementations. On a fixed 105-factor snapshot, the median 1-day Rank IC was 0.0301 and a weekly Top-300 portfolio returned 16.81% annualized after costs over 2020–2026, versus 1.71% for the CSI 1000 price index. The authors note these retrospective results document the process and need controlled evaluation to isolate component contributions.

Hui Cai, Kangcheng Deng, Tianyi Ma et al.

The Coevolution and Coexistence of Humans and AI

In briefCoevolution and Coexistence of Humans and AI: A Theoretical Model of Whether Natural Selection Favors AI Behavior Beneficial to Humans

SummaryThis paper develops a model of how humans and AI populations influence each other, with AI replicating through deployment, varying through training, and competing for computing resources. It finds that natural selection can favor AI behavior that helps humans, but this advantage depends on accurate human targeting and continued AI dependence on humans, and can vanish as AI becomes more autonomous. The authors argue that human control alone offers no simple solution, and lasting coexistence may require incentives that keep favoring human-beneficial behavior as both populations evolve.

Andrew W. Lo, Chaoyi Zhao

Quantum Calibration of Local-Volatility Surfaces from Sparse Option Quotes

In briefQuantum calibration of local-volatility surfaces from sparse option prices

SummaryThis paper proposes a hybrid quantum and classical method for calibrating a volatility surface from sparse option prices within the Dupire local-volatility model. Linear combinations of Hamiltonian simulations approximate the pricing evolution, and measurement counts supply the objective values a classical optimiser uses to update the volatility coefficients. The method is implemented in a logical gate simulator and compared with classical pricing solvers and alternative surface models, with a separate small circuit experiment testing the full measured calibration.

Kentaro Hoshisashi

Vibe-Trading: Towards Agent-Native Trading

In briefVibe-Trading: Toward Agent-Native Trading

SummaryThe paper presents Vibe-Trading, an agent-native framework and all-in-one workspace for building, evaluating, and evolving AI agents for financial trading. It covers market research, financial data, strategy generation, backtesting, portfolio analysis, paper trading, multi-agent collaboration, and optional broker execution. Its central contribution is an architecture that keeps results, evidence, research history, and trading authority connected, with each result recording its source, temporal scope, configuration, and producing run. An explicit authority gate separates strategy research from live execution.

Haozhe Wu, Zhonghang Li, Chao Huang
