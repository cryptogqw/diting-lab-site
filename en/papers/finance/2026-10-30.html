A New GAUSS Software Component for Implementing Dynamic Tests of Contagion with an Application

In briefA New GAUSS Software Module for Dynamic Contagion Tests, with an Application

SummaryThis paper presents a new GAUSS software component for dynamic tests of financial contagion, which assess spillover effects between markets during crises or pandemics. Unlike standard tests with fixed break dates, these tests measure the potential contagion effect across the whole time span. Critical values are generated with a leverage bootstrap simulation, and an application using real stock market data demonstrates the software.

Abdulnasser Hatemi-J, Alan Mustafa

QBX: A Compiler for Well-Formed, Auditable Alpha Programs

In briefQBX: A Compiler for Well-Formed, Auditable Alpha Programs

SummaryThe paper introduces QBX, a compiler that defines a well-formed, auditable candidate space for formulaic alpha programs using a versioned block registry and a whole-strategy port-DAG language. It either rejects proposals with diagnostics or emits an intermediate representation with types, time-availability effects, and content identities. On Chinese A-share panels, the share of evaluation-valid proposals rises from 26.8% for free strings and 36.4% for a grammar-only control to 97.7% for typed programs. The authors state these results concern validity, reuse, and search efficiency within the registered vocabulary, not more information from the market data.

Yichong Bai, Kaigui Bian

Expected shortfall estimation and capital coverage under overlapping scenarios

In briefStudy of expected shortfall estimation and capital coverage using overlapping scenarios

SummaryThis paper examines how estimating expected shortfall (ES) from short profit-and-loss samples built from overlapping multi-day returns affects accuracy and capital coverage. It compares six order-statistic-based ES estimators, using a one-year window and 2.5% confidence level as a benchmark, with both simulations and market data. The authors find that overlapping sampling systematically shifts ES estimates downward, weakening capital coverage, with serial dependence and heavy tails amplifying the effect.

Neural Network Methods in the 2BSDE Formalism for Solving the High-Dimensional Black--Scholes Equation with Transaction Costs

In briefNeural network solver for high-dimensional Black-Scholes pricing with transaction costs

SummaryThe paper formulates a 2BSDE system for a (D+1)-dimensional Black-Scholes PDE under the Leland transaction cost model, which accounts for the sign of the curvature. The system takes the curvature's speed (its derivative) as input. A single ResNet neural network learns both the PDE solution and the free degrees of freedom in the curvature matrix. It prices derivatives such as call options and butterfly spreads on geometric baskets, achieving a time-averaged RMSE below 0.1 for D=10 and D=100.

Guhesh Kumaran
