Calibration of Passive Market Impact with a Queue-Sensitive Propagator Model

In briefCalibrating Passive Market Impact Using a Queue-Sensitive Propagator Model

SummaryThis paper proposes a framework for calibrating passive market impact, the price response to liquidity-providing limit orders, separately from the impact of liquidity-consuming market orders. It builds on a model combining Hawkes-driven market-order flow with queue-reactive limit-order and cancellation dynamics. The authors show that prices decompose into a universal propagator term driven by signed order-flow imbalance and a state-dependent correction reflecting how volume at the best quotes affects price sensitivity. Calibrated on high-frequency order-book data, the passive-impact term explains substantial price variation beyond the classical propagator and improves model fit and predicted impact curves.

Yadh Hafsi, Fabrizio Lillo, and Gr\'egoire Szymanski

CADENCE: Distributional Next-Transaction Forecasting for Business Cash-Flow Underwriting

In briefCADENCE: A Model Predicting Business Cash-Flow Timing and Amounts for Credit Underwriting

SummaryThe paper introduces CADENCE, a compact causal Transformer that predicts the amount and a nonparametric timing distribution of each business cash flow's next payment. It uses transaction semantics from an external labeler and a frozen text encoder, along with calendar-aware inputs. Compared with a production FFT baseline, it reports gains in one-day timing accuracy and next-event F1, and improvements in KS, approval rate, and loss rate in an end-to-end credit evaluation.

Alexander Wu, Jingchao Huang

What Language Models Add to Healthcare-Equity Rankings, and What They Could Have Known

In briefWhat language models add to healthcare stock rankings, and what they could have known

SummaryThis study evaluates whether non-market features and language-model representations improve market-based rankings of US healthcare firms from 2020 to 2025. Adding balance-sheet and attention features raises the hybrid model's rank information coefficient at one, three and twelve months, with the twelve-month neutral IC rising from 0.063 to 0.093. Language models contribute most clearly at three months, though the findings are exploratory.

Haotong Fu, Xiyue Zhang, Jack Rose et al.

Contemporaneous Transmission Networks and Systemic Risk in European Banking

In briefSimultaneous return spillover networks and systemic risk among European banks

SummaryThis paper examines how contemporaneous equity-return transmission shapes systemic exposure among 51 listed European banks. Using a recursive non-Gaussian network model on volatility-standardized returns, the authors build two market-capitalization-weighted measures: the Systemic Impact Score, capturing a bank's influence on others, and the Systemic Vulnerability Score, capturing its exposure to shocks from elsewhere. The study also assesses the reproducibility of these roles and the limits of identification.

Minh Duc Duong, Alessio Moneta, Fabrizio Lillo et al.
