Reconstructing risk preferences from data

In briefRebuilding risk preferences from observed data

SummaryThis paper studies how to reconstruct a risk measure from a finite dataset of contingent claims and their evaluations. It gives necessary and sufficient conditions for such a dataset to be compatible with a risk measure, and a reconstruction formula called the universal risk measure. The method is extended to monotone risk measures with sharp Lipschitz constants, and a numerical application to European options is presented.

Anastasis Kratsios, Silvana M. Pesenti, Philipp Zimmermann

Do Econophysics-Inspired Inputs Improve Neural Covariance Forecasting? A Leakage-Free Walk-Forward Study of Intraday DJIA Data

In briefDo physics-inspired inputs improve neural covariance forecasts? A leakage-free walk-forward study of DJIA intraday data

SummaryThe paper tests whether econophysics tools, including Random Matrix Theory, Tsallis tail indices and Hawkes intensities, improve neural network forecasts of one-day-ahead asset covariance. It uses 1-minute data from 29 Dow Jones Industrial Average constituents over 545 trading days, evaluated with a nested walk-forward design of 11 folds and 231 out-of-sample days. Neural models beat static covariance estimators but show no statistically significant gain over HAR-RV or LSTM benchmarks, and the physics-inspired features make no measurable contribution.

Gabriele Ranco

Accountable AI Delegation in Banking: Identity, Commitment Controls, Decision Evidence and Recovery

In briefAccountable AI delegation in banking: identity, controls, and recovery

SummaryThe paper specifies a banking delegation contract that checks current authority, a shared payment cap, and whether eligible instruments can cover losses before an AI-assisted payment is committed. It records the payment, recovery demands, and outcome in one atomic transition. Paired examples show what reviewers need to assess mandate conformance, and tests report results on rule reversal, reused state, pooled recovery capacity, and record retention. Survey responses and an insurance example are also included.

Walter Kurz, Besnik Alidemi, Marco Schmidt

Risk sharing for background risk models with infinite mean

In briefRisk sharing for losses with infinite mean

SummaryThis paper studies how risk sharing affects positively dependent losses with infinite mean, using background risk models. Under both linear and conditional mean risk sharing, it shows that sharing such losses can increase risk in the usual stochastic order. The results cover multivariate models such as Pareto-Clayton and Fréchet-Gumbel, and include stochastic dominance comparisons between diversified portfolios.

Ziting Miao, Yuyu Chen, Han Li

Nonlinear Shrinkage for Higher Moments Estimation in Large Portfolios

In briefNonlinear Shrinkage for Higher Moments in Large Portfolios

SummaryThis paper addresses the noisy estimation of higher-order portfolio moments, such as coskewness and cokurtosis, for large portfolios. It introduces tensor-decomposition-based filters that extend cross-validated covariance cleaning and the Average Oracle method to even-order co-moment tensors. Using historical Russell 1000 constituent returns, the authors report that the filters reduce out-of-sample estimation error for correlation and cokurtosis tensors compared with sample estimators. The improvements also lower errors in portfolio volatility and kurtosis estimates.

Paul Ruelloux, Christian Bongiorno, Damien Challet

The Rise of Agents and the Labor Market

In briefThe Rise of AI Agents and Labor Demand: Economy-Wide Evidence on Job Postings

SummaryThis paper studies how agentic AI affects labor demand, combining about two quadrillion tokens of realized AI usage from OpenRouter with full text of 34.8 million U.S. job postings from Lightcast. Firm exposure is measured by how U.S. public firms' stock returns comove with weekly aggregate agentic AI token consumption. A one-standard-deviation rise in exposure predicts 2.11% and 3.20% lower job posting growth over six and nine months. An event study around the Claude Code 2.0 release shows a 4.73% decline, concentrated in non-senior jobs.

WhyThe authors use realized agentic AI token data and job postings to study labor demand effects economy-wide, finding declines in posting growth concentrated in non-senior roles.

Nicola Borri, Anton Cheremukhin, Yukun Liu et al.

Trust and Lottery-Related Anomalies

In briefTrust and lottery-related anomalies: how social trust shapes the pricing of lottery-like stocks

SummaryThis paper examines whether social trust affects the pricing of lottery-like stocks. Using 8,875 firms across 49 US states from 1996 to 2018, with MAX as a proxy for lottery-like characteristics, it finds the MAX effect concentrated in high-trust regions and weak or absent in low-trust regions. The trust effect is stronger in areas with lower education or income levels.

Reza Bradrania, Ya Gao

Are Calibrated Decision Models Calibrated on Markets? Geolocated News, Markov Switching and a System One Model for Nasdaq-100 Allocation

In briefAre Calibrated Decision Models Still Calibrated in Markets? Geolocated News, Markov Switching and a Nasdaq-100 Allocation Model

SummaryThe study asks each Friday whether the Nasdaq-100 will close lower four weeks later, comparing a two-regime Markov-switching model, the Jev model reading weekly headlines and geolocated news features, and their combination. Raw Jev overestimates declines, averaging 0.48 against an observed frequency of 0.38. Shifting its prior to the historical base rate sharply reduces calibration error.

Jakub Zaj\k{a}c, Micha{\l} Dyrek

Held-to-Maturity Accounting: Hidden Losses and Systemic Fragility

In briefHeld-to-Maturity Accounting: Hidden Losses and Systemic Fragility

SummaryThis paper examines how accounting-induced opacity affects systemic risk in banking systems. The authors build a model in which banks hold correlated interest rate sensitive assets and are linked through an endogenous interbank network. They find that Held-to-Maturity (HTM) accounting amplifies systemic risk by delaying loss recognition, letting losses accumulate and synchronize across institutions. When losses are realized under liquidity stress, correlated exposures and network linkages generate nonlinear increases in defaults.

Dongxu Li, Steve Y. Yang

Path-Independent Deleveraging Rules

In briefRules for path-independent auto-deleveraging

SummaryThe paper characterises path-independent auto-deleveraging (ADL) rules, used by trading platforms to forcibly reduce positions, as an extended log-exponential family. Immunity to account splitting, merging and reopening further restricts this class. Under homogeneity, the rules form a one-parameter family ordered by fairness and platform risk. A counterfactual replay of Hyperliquid's ADL episode on 10 October 2025 finds that intermediate parameter values improve on the observed allocation in burden dispersion and subsequent risk statistics.

Christoph Schlegel, Akaki Mamageishvili

Bond yield dynamics: a multi-country multi-horizon string model

In briefBond yield dynamics: a multi-country model of how government bond yield curves move together

SummaryThis paper extends the forward rate curve models of Baaquie and Bouchaud (2004) and Le Coz and Bouchaud (2024) to government bond markets across several countries. It finds that cross-country correlations of realized yield changes are significantly asymmetric between maturities. The model gives each country its own time scale, and the differences between these scales govern interactions between curves and reproduce that asymmetry. Calibrated to bonds from nine euro-zone countries, it reconstructs the cross-curve correlations with a typical error of 1.7%.

Paul Ruelloux
