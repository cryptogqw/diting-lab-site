Covariate-Indexed Inclusion Probabilities on Unbalanced Panels

In briefEstimating how likely an item is to be in a set, based on its characteristics, in changing and interdependent panel data

SummaryThe paper estimates the probability that an item with given covariates belongs to a set when the cross-section changes over time and items and periods are dependent. It proposes two convex models whose fitted probabilities are nonnegative by construction and valid regardless of dependence between items, with consistency, finite-sample bounds, and limit distributions established for serially dependent panels. On monthly US stock failure data, the rare-event model predicts better than the ordinary logit and the classical failure logit.

Paul Schneider

Beyond Strike Remapping: Conditional-Gamma Pricing of Leveraged ETF Options under Local Volatility

In briefBeyond Strike Remapping: Conditional-Gamma Pricing of Leveraged ETF Options

SummaryThis paper studies how to build the implied volatility surface for options on leveraged ETFs, where path-dependent variance drag makes consistency with the underlying index smile difficult. It compares a closed-form first-order strike remapping, a new conditional-Gamma method that conditions on the terminal underlying return, and a two-dimensional joint-law PDE serving as the benchmark. The conditional-Gamma method solves three one-dimensional forward PDEs under a calibrated local-volatility model. On S&P 500 and Nikkei 225 surfaces it closely tracks the benchmark at every leverage tested and computes a full smile far faster.

Guolin Chen, Jiang Zhou, Richard Ma et al.

Explaining Realized Returns: Roll's R² 40 Years Later

In briefExplaining realized stock returns, revisiting Roll's R² 40 years on

SummaryThis paper revisits Roll's (1988) AFA Presidential Address on R² as a modern empirical study. It finds that conventional macroeconomic, cash-flow, expectations, and flow variables explain less than 10% of monthly U.S. equity return variation out of sample, often with negative R², and lack daily observations. The authors use generative AI to extract reasons stated in Wall Street Journal market summaries, filtering out price-related language. These reasons explain roughly 30-40% of daily and monthly return variation out of sample, spanning themes such as corporate news, macroeconomic conditions, monetary policy, sentiment, and geopolitics.

Bryan Kelly, Yinan Su, Chao Zhang

An axiomatic foundation for optimized certainty equivalents

In briefAn axiomatic foundation for optimized certainty equivalents

SummaryThis paper provides economic axioms that characterize Optimized Certainty Equivalents (OCEs), a major class of risk measures that includes Mean-Variance, Expected Shortfall, and the Entropic Risk Measure. It introduces the Fair-Coin Compensation axiom, which together with Strong Risk Aversion and Normalization characterizes OCEs, and it interprets this axiom through transfers that offset risk from a coin-toss assignment of two losses. Additional axioms such as Monotonicity, Positive Homogeneity with Prudence, and Fair-Coin Independence yield standard OCEs, Expected Shortfall, and the Entropic Risk Measure, respectively. The authors also axiomatize the Mean-Variance criterion using a new Equal-Mean Randomization axiom.

WhyThe authors introduce Fair-Coin Compensation to axiomatically characterize OCEs and special cases including Expected Shortfall and the Entropic Risk Measure.

Christopher Blier-Wong, Jean-Gabriel Lauzier, Zachary Van Oosten

Thinking Harder Doesn't Lead to Better Choices: Reasoning Effort Increases the Confidence but Not the Correctness of LLM Shopping Agents

In briefThinking Longer Raises LLM Shopping Agents' Confidence, Not Their Accuracy

SummaryThis paper tests whether giving large language models more reasoning effort helps them choose better products when acting as autonomous shopping agents. The authors benchmark ten frontier models on three product-choice tasks built from WebShop, Amazon SQID, and Wayfair WANDS. They find that more thinking effort does not meaningfully improve decision quality, but it significantly raises the models' stated confidence. Most models are poorly calibrated at every effort level, and errors are strongly correlated, with several models often picking the same wrong product.

WhyThe authors benchmark ten frontier models on three product-choice benchmarks, finding reasoning effort raises confidence but not correctness, plus poor calibration and shared errors.

Adi Krishnamoorthy, X.Y. Han

Revisiting Equity-Bond Diversification under Tail Risk and Structural Market Change: Evidence from Global ETFs and Model-Implied Option Prices

In briefRevisiting how stock-bond diversification performs under tail risk and market change, using global ETFs and model-implied option prices

SummaryThis study analyzes 30 equity-bond and multi-asset ETFs listed in the US, Canada, and Australia, using daily US-dollar returns from 2020 to 2024. A rolling-window comparison of minimum-variance and CVaR portfolio optimization, under long-only and long-short constraints, is tested against an equally weighted benchmark. Optimized portfolios show lower empirical VaR and CVaR than the benchmark, with long-short strategies generally reducing downside risk more, while the equally weighted benchmark has the highest Sharpe, Sortino, and Rachev ratios.

Farzana Afroz, Abootaleb Shirvani, Nicholas Appiah et al.

Neural Marked Point Processes for Health Insurance Claims

In briefNeural Marked Point Process Models for Health Insurance Claims

SummaryThis paper proposes a neural marked point process that uses a causal Transformer to represent each policyholder's claim history and estimate type-specific conditional intensities, capturing self-excitation and interactions between claim types. The model also includes conditional severity distributions, reporting delays, and stochastic medical-cost inflation, and is calibrated with a likelihood method that accounts for incomplete reporting. On simulated data and MEPS medical-event data, it outperforms two generalized linear model benchmarks in predictive performance, though some discrepancies remain in distribution tails.

Eva Flonner

Strategic Repayment and Latent Fragility in Financial Networks: Why Banks Withhold Payable Liquidity

In briefStrategic Repayment and Hidden Fragility in Financial Networks

SummaryThe paper shows that a financial network in which every bank pays in full can still admit a self-fulfilling settlement freeze under the same balance-sheet fundamentals, so full repayment alone does not prove equilibrium uniqueness or robustness. It derives this from a precautionary liquidity choice in which banks delay feasible payments to insure against an interim funding need, weighing settlement-discipline costs against the value of retained cash. The authors also characterise network amplification and conditions for coexisting equilibria, and separate payment timing from ultimate default.

Hicham El Kaissi
