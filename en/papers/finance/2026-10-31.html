Robust distortion riskmetrics under symmetry, unimodality, and Wasserstein constraints

In briefWorst-case distortion risk measures under symmetry, unimodality and Wasserstein constraints

SummaryThis paper studies the worst-case distortion riskmetric over symmetric distributions with a given mean and variance, lying in a Wasserstein ball around a reference distribution. The authors construct a reference-dependent auxiliary distortion that encodes the Wasserstein constraint while preserving symmetry, yielding sharp upper bounds for bounded-variation distortions and identifying extremal distributions when the bounds are attained. Numerical examples for ES, RVaR and VaR, along with simulations using data-dependent Wasserstein radii, illustrate the effects of shape restrictions.

Liu Peng, Zhang Yiying, Zuo Baishuai

Quantifying Repair Risk in Crypto Option Surfaces

In briefMeasuring repair risk in crypto option price surfaces

SummaryOption desks adjust crypto option quotes into price surfaces that stay within bid-ask ranges and meet no-arbitrage conditions, but the same quotes can admit several such repairs with different Greeks, hedge quantities, and risk measures. The paper defines this variation as repair risk and tests it on Deribit BTC and ETH option chains, comparing L1 and L2 projections with a smoothness-penalized repair. It also finds that a neural-projection repair shows similar dispersion in its downstream outputs. The authors conclude that the repair rule is part of the risk model, not merely a feasibility check.

Tenghan Zhong

Approximate clearing in financial networks with credit default swaps: hardness thresholds, a box algorithm, barriers, and exact solvency at bounded degree

In briefApproximate clearing in financial networks with credit default swaps, covering hardness thresholds and exact solvency

SummaryThe paper studies approximate clearing of financial networks that include credit default swaps. It proves PPAD-hardness below certain thresholds and gives a polynomial-time interval algorithm above them. It also analyzes exact solvency for bounded-degree cases and presents theoretical barriers, leaving some approximation windows open.

Optimal portfolio and consumption under no-shorting constraints: The exploratory formulation and reinforcement learning

In briefOptimal Portfolio and Consumption Without Short Selling: An Exploratory Reinforcement Learning Approach

SummaryThis paper develops a reinforcement-learning framework for continuous-time portfolio and consumption choice that maximizes utility while prohibiting short selling. It uses an exploratory formulation with entropy regularization and enforces no-shorting by imposing a componentwise nonnegative conditional-mean constraint on admissible randomized policies. For CRRA utility, the authors derive the homothetic value function and optimal Gaussian portfolio and Gibbs consumption policies under certain model conditions, and establish a verification theorem.

Xun Li, Xiang Yu, Fengyi Yuan et al.

Designing Behavioral Profiles for Language Model Simulation of Out-of-Distribution Human Behavior

In briefBehavioral profiles for LLM simulation of human behavior in new experiments

SummaryThe paper presents a framework that draws candidate behavioral features from the literature and builds profiles from earlier human experiments, then selects profile contents through forward feature selection. The selected profiles are evaluated unchanged against new participants under new designs, across 616 public-goods games, 144 multi-party bargaining games, and 1,480 generalized 11-20 money-request designs. Single-feature profiles predicted human outcome distributions better than no profile or general-purpose personas, while adding more features gave no clear extra benefit.

Robin Na, Kehang Zhu, Logan Cross et al.

Non-asymptotic analysis of SGLD with discontinuous stochastic gradients: a new route to portfolio CVaR minimization

In briefNon-asymptotic analysis of noisy gradient Langevin sampling for portfolio CVaR minimization with discontinuous gradients

SummaryThis paper analyzes stochastic gradient Langevin dynamics (SGLD) for minimizing Conditional Value-at-Risk (CVaR) in portfolio selection, where stochastic gradients are discontinuous. The authors show that the gradient satisfies linear growth, convexity-at-infinity, and one-sided Lipschitz properties with constants independent of the number of assets. They establish a Wasserstein-2 convergence rate of O(λ^{1/4}) and a finite-time bound on expected excess risk that grows linearly in the number of assets. Numerical experiments on simulated Gaussian portfolios confirm this dimension dependence and compare SGLD with other stochastic optimization methods.

Yan Ding, Sotirios Sabanis, Ying Zhang

Non-Convex Market Makers: Coherence without Convexity, Friction, and Parallel Composition

In briefNon-convex market makers that stay coherent without convexity, friction, or parallel composition

SummaryThe paper challenges the assumption that cost-function market makers must be convex, showing that for path-independent makers the no-arbitrage condition is a chord condition, so convexity is not what makes them coherent. It argues that coherent non-convex makers exist, with rational flow trading only the convex envelope, so non-convexity costs expressiveness rather than soundness. A proportional fee at least equal to the arbitrage depth restores no-arbitrage and corresponds to a bid-ask spread with an exact no-trade interval.

Peter Cotton

Reading the Count: Prediction Markets and Brazil's 2026 Presidential Election

In briefReading the Count: Prediction Markets and Brazil's 2026 Presidential Election

SummaryThe authors rebuild the public count of Brazil's 2026 presidential runoff minute by minute from electoral court section-level records and compare it with every trade on Kalshi and Polymarket. They find that the markets imply a final-margin standard deviation about four times that of a live projection, and that during a two-hour count suspension the markets gave Lula lower probabilities than any later reading from the sections then public.

Raphael Coelho

When is the deepest drawdown also the longest?

In briefDoes the deepest drawdown also last the longest?

SummaryThe paper studies whether the deepest drawdown in a random walk is also the longest one. For random walks with i.i.d. increments of positive finite mean, the two are almost surely distinct under light-tailed losses but coincide with probability tending to one under heavy-tailed losses. The paper gives explicit limit laws and rank results for both cases.

Yinhao Wu
