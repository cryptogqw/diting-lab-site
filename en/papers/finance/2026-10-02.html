FinNextAssist: Towards Professional Financial Deep Research Assistant

In briefFinNextAssist: A Deep Research Assistant for Professional Finance

SummaryThis paper presents FinNextAssist, an end-to-end deep research framework for professional financial analysis. It splits the research process into four stages: task planning, evidence compilation, reasoning, and report assembly, and adds two lightweight sub-agents, TabAgent for cross-market financial table understanding and HeteroAgent for interpreting heterogeneous financial data across modalities. The authors report experiments on three benchmarks showing it outperforms strong proprietary and open-source deep research agents, with ablations confirming each component's contribution.

Xiangyu Li, Fengbin Zhu, Xuan Yao et al.

MintEval: Do LLMs Implement the Trading Strategy You Asked For? A Behavioural-Equivalence Benchmark for Natural-Language-to-Strategy Code

In briefMintEval: Do LLMs Build the Trading Strategy Requested? A Behaviour-Matching Benchmark for Language-to-Strategy Code

SummaryThis paper introduces MintEval, a benchmark that checks whether code generated by large language models from plain-language trading descriptions behaves like the strategy requested. Generated and reference programs are run bar by bar on identical market data and frictions, and compared by their trading actions rather than code similarity or profit. The results show that even a frontier model silently fails on a substantial share of tasks, and that an existing LLM judge accepts all of these failures.

WhyProposes MintEval, a bar-by-bar behavioural-equivalence benchmark for LLM-generated trading strategy code; the authors report 79.2% of correctly read implementations diverge on over 10% of active bars

Shapley-based Structural Analysis of Neural Calibration for Stochastic Volatility Models

In briefExplaining Neural Calibration of Volatility Models Using Shapley Values

SummaryThis paper uses two complementary Shapley-based explainable AI methods, SHAP and νSHAP, to analyze neural network calibration of the Heston and rough Heston stochastic volatility models. It compares several architectures and finds that short maturities and smile wings dominate parameter inference, with a stable attribution pattern across architectures. The authors show that νSHAP explanations can guide a large reduction in input dimensionality for the rough Heston model while keeping calibration accuracy comparable to using the full implied volatility surface.

Sha\"in Afzali, Serena Della Corte, Antonis Papapantoleon

Event History Over Scale: Compact Transformers for Low-Latency Limit Order Book Forecasting

In briefEvent History Over Scale: Compact Transformers for Low-Latency Order Book Forecasting

SummaryThe paper introduces MBOFormer, a 7,203-parameter causal transformer, and MBOFusion, a 14,371-parameter extension with a slow temporal-context branch, for short-horizon price-trend prediction from limit order books. Both models use market-by-order (level-3) histories of order submissions, cancellations, and executions, and are compared with level-2 snapshot-based baselines. The authors report the highest mean macro-F1 in eleven of twelve settings across three markets and four horizons, with sub-millisecond median inference and state dictionaries under 70 kB.

David Schaurecker, Lasse B. Strand, Kevin O'Sullivan et al.

Multi-Agent AI as a Nested Principal-Agent Problem in Private Wealth Management: Mandate Representation and Evidence Control in Switzerland, Germany and Austria

In briefMulti-agent AI as a nested principal-agent problem in private wealth management across Switzerland, Germany and Austria

SummaryThe paper proposes a model-independent framework that combines nested principal-agent delegation, in which a manager hands work to AI acting for the client, with constrained joint maximisation of client and manager outcomes. Legal duties, mandate requirements and evidence sufficiency determine admissibility, with Switzerland, Germany and Austria as the legal context. Simulations using constructed mandates and public-market data show how weights and omitted terms can change selected services. These are illustrative examples, and professional and field studies are needed to assess oversight and client outcomes.

Walter Kurz, Reinhard Magg, Florian Kollberg et al.

Right Order, Wrong Scale: Auditing LLM Judges for Occupational AI Measurement

In briefSame Ranking, Different Scale: Checking LLM Judges for Job-Related AI Measurement

SummaryThis paper introduces O*NET-BENCH, an audit suite built from an existing survey of 45,796 worker ratings, to test 33 LLM judge configurations on whether AI responses meet workplace requirements. Many judges agree with human raters on response rankings, yet their estimated acceptance rates range from 3.0% to 97.9%, compared with 61.1% for occupation-matched workers. The authors conclude that ranking agreement alone is insufficient for occupational measurement and that judges should be validated against the acceptance rates and aggregates they will be used to estimate.

Harry Lyu, Neil Thompson

Expected Utility Regret Rule: Minimax and Bayes Optimal Portfolio Choice

In briefExpected Utility Regret Rule: Minimax and Bayes-Optimal Portfolio Selection

SummaryThis paper studies portfolio choice, aiming to recommend portfolios that maximize an investor's expected utility of wealth. It proposes the Expected Utility Regret (EUR) rule, which jointly selects a portfolio class and estimates its weights. In a regular parametric return model, a single EUR rule attains both minimax and Bayes lower bounds without using the prior distribution. Mean-variance and risk-parity portfolios are shown to be special cases of this framework.

Shared Models, Selective Trading, and Order Flow

In briefShared Models, Selective Trading, and Order Flow: A study of whether diverse language models still differ in the orders they submit when news framing changes in synthetic markets.

SummaryThe paper uses synthetic markets with a fixed mix of three language-model families to examine how news presentation affects the orders they submit. It reports that Qwen's share of submitted orders shifted by 48 percentage points in a financing event with little change in net order counts, and Mistral's share shifted by 40 points in a workforce-reduction event where net counts reversed sign. An analytical decomposition shows selection can improve or worsen price accuracy even when aggregate demand sensitivity is unchanged.

Victoria Ruojie Li, Arka Prava Bandyopadhyay
