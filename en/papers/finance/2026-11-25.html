Graph-Informed Deep Factor Model for Statistical Arbitrage

In briefA graph-informed deep factor model for profiting from temporary price gaps between similar stocks

SummaryThis paper proposes a nonlinear single-factor model, GIDF, for daily statistical arbitrage in equities. It reconstructs a latent network of cross-sectional return dependencies left after removing the dominant eigenmodes of the correlation matrix, then combines it with firm characteristics and macroeconomic variables via a signed-edge graph attention mechanism. In an empirical study of daily U.S. equities from 2015 to 2024, GIDF reports an out-of-sample Sharpe ratio of 1.78 and outperforms classical factor models and state-of-the-art deep learning methods.

Evelyn Zhu, Mucheng Zhu, Brendan Martin et al.

Time-consistency of cash-subadditive risk measures: The infimal cocycle property

In briefTime consistency of risk measures: an infimal cocycle property

SummaryThe paper characterizes time-consistency for convex, cash-subadditive dynamic risk measures through their minimal penalty. The dual variables are conditional sub-probability densities that can factorize across an intermediate date in several ways. The authors introduce an infimal cocycle property, where the penalty is the infimum over all such factorizations, and show it is equivalent to time-consistency with the infimum attained.

Matteo Ferrari, Roger J. A. Laeven, Emanuela Rosazza Gianin et al.

Rank-size analysis of optimal portfolio weights with a reverse-engineering application

In briefRank-size analysis of optimal portfolio weights and reverse-engineering their generating model

SummaryThis paper uses rank-size analysis to study the shape of optimal portfolio weights across four selection models: Global Minimum-Variance, minimum-CVaR, Risk Parity, and Most Diversified Portfolio. Seven rank-size functional forms are fitted to sorted weights over rolling windows and compared using goodness-of-fit measures and Cumulative Second-order ε-Stochastic Dominance, with empirical application to major equity indices. The authors also feed the calibrated parameters into a Random Forest classifier to identify the generating model. The parameters separate minimum-risk from risk-diversification strategies and identify Risk Parity and Most Diversified portfolios, while Global Minimum-Variance and minimum-CVaR are hard to distinguish.

Roy Cerqueti, Francesco Cesarone, Alessio Di Paolo et al.

Will AI Replace Juniors? How Task Complexity Reduces Automation Risk

In briefWill AI Replace Junior Staff? How Task Complexity Lowers Automation Risk

SummaryThis study uses a pre-registered survey experiment with managers from 1,435 German firms in the information economy and manufacturing. Managers were randomly assigned to consider either standardised tasks or complex tasks requiring professional judgement and independent problem-solving. Compared with standardised tasks, complex tasks reduced willingness to use AI instead of hiring entry-level staff by 0.54 points on a five-point scale and raised the importance of human oversight by 0.15 points.

Vincent Rost, Fabian Stephany

Budget, Readout or Reasoning? Where Small Reasoning Models Lose Value in Auction Bidding

In briefBudget, readout or reasoning: where small reasoning models lose value in auction bidding

SummaryThe study gives six open reasoning models of 4B to 14B parameters win-probability curves from eBay closing prices and a simulated advertising market (AuctionNet), then scores their bids on held-out auctions against an exact calculator. The models themselves cause two kinds of losses: they overlook the best option even when their own reasoning ranks it first, and their wrong answers tend toward the highest bid available. Token caps that truncate reasoning and answering before the reasoning block is closed also reduce value, while closing the block first substantially improves results.

Mahbod Khaleti, Seyed Reza Tavakoli, Baran Hoseini et al.

How LLM Agents Reshape Financial Knowledge Graphs: A Survey

In briefHow LLM Agents Are Reshaping Financial Knowledge Graphs: A Survey

SummaryThis is the first survey on how LLM agents change the building, maintenance, and use of financial knowledge graphs. It proposes a taxonomy covering knowledge graph construction, temporal evolution, verification, reconciliation and admission, adaptive acquisition, and graph usage. The survey finds that agents can recover evidence missed in an initial search and revise records as new information arrives. It identifies three open questions: judging whether evidence is sufficient for a financial decision, determining which earlier conclusions must change after a correction, and identifying relationships and rules reusable in new graphs or domains.

Yunbai Zhang, Heze Ma, Nan Jiang et al.

Worth the Wait? How Response Time and Streaming Shape Judgments of Generative AI Answers and Chatbot Engagement

In briefWorth the Wait? Response Time, Streaming and Judgments of AI Chatbot Answers

SummaryThis paper examines how the speed and display style of answers from LLM chatbots affect user judgments. It analyzes blind votes from the French government chatbot arena compar:IA, slower streamed answers caused by provider congestion, and everyday ChatGPT replies in the WildChat dataset. The same text won more votes when streamed as it was written than when shown at once, and slower replies made users less likely to ask follow-up questions.

WhyThe authors separate answer delivery (streaming versus cached replay) from blind votes, reporting three natural-data tests; streamed text won more votes and delays reduced follow-up questions.

Zihan Chen, Di Zhu, Lei Nico Zheng

Delta-Vega Hedging with Deep Reinforcement Learning: From Simulation to Reality

In briefHedging price and volatility risk with deep reinforcement learning, from simulation to market data

SummaryThe paper proposes a deep reinforcement learning method that hedges both price and volatility risk by trading the underlying asset and a longer-dated option under discrete trading and transaction costs. The agent is trained with PPO on synthetic data from a stochastic volatility model, using only market-observable state variables. In simulation, PPO matches the Delta-Vega benchmark at baseline costs and outperforms it as costs rise. On historical S&P 500 option data, the two-instrument strategies roughly halve the profit-and-loss dispersion of Delta hedging, and PPO shows lower profit-and-loss volatility than Delta-Vega.

Michele Colombi, Marco Di Francesco, Pietro Rossi
