Scenario Matching for Credit Dependence Stress Tests: Temporal correlation constraints and empirical risk bounds

In briefScenario matching for credit dependence stress tests with correlation constraints and empirical risk bounds

SummaryThe paper pairs two sets of simulated market and credit paths by minimizing the absolute error in temporal correlation relative to a target, with each market path matched to exactly one credit path so both full-scenario distributions are preserved. A perfect-matching test checks whether a uniform correlation-error tolerance is feasible and finds its smallest value, and further assignments yield exact empirical bounds on mean loss and exceedance probability. A synthetic bond-forward credit stress test illustrates matching precision and tail results. The method does not depend on the scenario generator and can be used to examine dependence assumptions in existing risk engines.

Distributions or Quantiles? A Convex Variational Theory of Worst-Case Risk

In briefDistributions or Quantiles? A Convex Theory of Worst-Case Risk

SummaryThis paper develops a convex variational theory for worst-case risk evaluation using spectral risk measures, unifying and extending existing analyses. It uses a representation principle that picks the quantile or distribution formulation preserving convexity of the ambiguity set, and optimality conditions identify the worst-case law and its risk value. The theory yields closed-form or semi-closed-form solutions for several models, including Wasserstein and Kullback–Leibler ambiguity sets, and supplies projection-based algorithms with fixed-grid convergence. In a high-dimensional portfolio application, an exact projection identity under a Gaussian reference reduces robust portfolio selection to a second-order cone program.

WhyUnifies worst-case spectral risk analysis under a convex variational theory, deriving closed-form solutions for several ambiguity models with strong analytical support and moderate field impact.
