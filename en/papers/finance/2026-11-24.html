Semivariance and Semivolatility in Portfolio Selection: A Unified Framework for Downside-Risk Portfolio Models

In briefDownside-risk portfolio models: semivariance and semivolatility unified

SummaryThis paper studies downside risk in portfolio selection, comparing semivariance and its square root, semivolatility. The authors classify models by the positive homogeneity degree of the risk measure in the weights and give a non-degeneracy condition for long-only downside risk under three thresholds. They run a rolling-window out-of-sample backtest of seventeen long-only strategies on Dow Jones and Nasdaq-100 total-return data, testing differences with block-bootstrap inference.

Francesco Cesarone, Massimiliano Corradini, Nicol\`o Giunta

Dynamic stochastic multi-attribute group decision-making based on fractional-order stochastic dominance criteria

In briefDynamic multi-attribute group decision-making using fractional-order stochastic dominance

SummaryThis paper proposes a dynamic group decision-making model for choosing among alternatives under uncertainty. It uses fractional-order stochastic dominance criteria, which are finer than integer-order ones, to compare alternatives. The model also works for decision-makers whose utility functions are not concave everywhere, and it lets attributes and alternatives be added or removed across decision periods. Numerical examples and comparisons are presented to show its effectiveness and robustness.

Shuqin Wang, Wei Zou, Jiehua Xie

A Kernel Framework for Linear Operator Equations

In briefA kernel-based framework for solving linear operator equations, including option pricing examples

SummaryThe paper proposes a unified method that embeds linear operator equations into a reproducing kernel Hilbert space (RKHS) and solves them in the regularized least-squares sense at finitely many collocation points. The solution is a finite linear combination of operator-induced representers, obtained from a single linear system, and sensitivities follow by direct differentiation. To reduce cost for large collocation sets, the authors introduce a Nyström approximation based on subsampling the representers. Numerical examples cover ordinary, partial, and partial integro-differential equations, including multi-asset and jump-diffusion option pricing.

Damir Filipovic, Valentin Tissot-Daguette

Training decision models for simulating human respondents

In briefTraining decision models to simulate human survey respondents

SummaryResearchers increasingly use large language models as synthetic respondents in market research, conjoint analysis, and experiments, but synthetic choice data from off-the-shelf models biases econometric inference. The paper trains decision models (System One architectures) on historical respondent-level choice microdata by conditional maximum likelihood, before inference and without target-study data. Across four held-out studies from new domains, training lowers treatment-effect error by 22% to 89%, and a small trained model outperforms a much larger off-the-shelf model on three of the four studies.

Davood Wadi, Yu Ma

Know When to Abstain: Conformal Confidence Estimation for Text-to-SQL

In briefKnowing When to Refuse: Conformal Confidence Estimation for Text-to-SQL

SummaryThe paper addresses Text-to-SQL systems that run successfully but answer the wrong question. A generator LLM writes and executes queries, while a separate critic LLM judges them, and a Random Forest fuses these scores with other confidence signals. Two calibration steps, a Learn-then-Test threshold and a conformal p-value, provide finite-sample guarantees on the error rate of accepted queries and on false abstentions. On Spider, the method keeps the error among accepted queries within a set risk budget while accepting about half of queries.

Avinash Kumar Singh, Bhaskarjit Sarmah, Stefano Pasquali

AI-Assisted Programming and Labor Demand

In briefAI Coding Assistants and Tech Hiring: How GitHub Copilot Affects Labor Demand

SummaryThis paper studies how firms adopted GitHub Copilot, an AI coding assistant, across 5,414 multinational firms that account for 38% of the U.S. programming workforce. The authors use firms' predetermined software procurement cycles as a natural experiment to identify causal effects. After one year, adoption reduced quarterly technical hiring by 29%, with no effect on separations or overall technical headcount. The slowdown is concentrated in junior technical roles with the most coding work, and firms shift demand toward more experienced workers and roles combining coding with broader design responsibilities.

WhyThe authors use a natural experiment across 5,414 firms to causally estimate AI coding assistants' effect on technical hiring, with broad labor-market implications.

Lindsey Raymond, Eleanor Wiske Dillon

Critical step size of stochastic gradient descent for a linear neuron: an exact solution and the role of the input spectrum

In briefCritical step size of SGD for a linear neuron, and the role of input spectrum

SummaryThe paper studies online stochastic gradient descent for a single linear neuron in a teacher-student setting with Gaussian inputs of arbitrary covariance. It solves the closed linear recursion for the mean-square errors exactly and proves convergence holds if and only if the step size is below a critical value, given by an explicit scalar equation involving all eigenvalues of the input covariance. The critical step size is governed by the effective rank of the input covariance rather than the number of parameters, and it grows linearly with mini-batch size until saturating beyond the effective rank.

S\'ebastien Valeyre

Cleaning correlation matrices with external information: the Maximum Variance estimator

In briefCleaning correlation matrices with external group information: the MaxVar estimator

SummaryThe paper proposes the Maximum Variance (MaxVar) estimator, which corrects the eigenvectors of an empirical correlation matrix using a grouping of assets such as asset classes or regions. It is a factor model with about n parameters and stays positive definite even when observations are fewer than assets. Applied to 47 futures from 1993 to 2018, the risk parity portfolio built with MaxVar achieves a higher Sharpe ratio than rotationally invariant and empirical estimators, with the advantage growing as the estimation window shortens.

S\'ebastien Valeyre
