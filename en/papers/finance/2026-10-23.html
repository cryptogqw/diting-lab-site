Risk Adequacy and Anti-Discrimination in Insurance Pricing: Classification, Aggregation, and Transfers

In briefBalancing Risk Pricing, Coverage and Anti-Discrimination in Insurance Tariffs

SummaryThe paper proposes a framework for insurance tariff design built on risk classification, aggregation of benchmark risk prices within classes, and balanced transfers. It evaluates tariffs on risk adequacy, portfolio coverage, and anti-discrimination, and frames the trade-offs as a multi-objective problem with a Pareto frontier. The authors prove several mathematical characterizations of these trade-offs and illustrate the results with a numerical motor-insurance study.

Wiebke Hansen, Stefan Weber, Mario W\"uthrich

The Digital-Energy Recomposition of the Kitchin Cycle: Exploratory Evidence from U.S. IT and Irish Data-Centre Series (1995-2025)

In briefWhether digital investment and energy use are reshaping the Kitchin business cycle, tested with U.S. and Irish data

SummaryThis paper uses quarterly U.S. and Irish data from 1995 to 2025 to explore whether IT investment and data-centre electricity use are reshaping the Kitchin cycle around energy and compute assets. It reports a structural break in the U.S. IT-GDP correlation in 2014Q3, and finds that the strong Irish data-centre electricity and GDP correlation largely disappears when GNP replaces GDP. The authors state that all coefficients are exploratory given small samples.

Bartosz Ziollko, Adam Niedzialkowski, Bartosz Szyma

Financial Context-Rich Strategy for Actor-Critic based Agents: A Deep Reinforcement Learning Approach for Stock Trading

In briefContext-rich trading strategy using actor-critic deep reinforcement learning with sentiment for stock trading

SummaryThis paper proposes a deep reinforcement learning approach to stock trading that partially and deeply incorporates sentiment analysis to capture external factors such as social sentiment and political instability. It compares actor-critic agents using only policy optimization (A2C, PPO) with those combining policy optimization and Q-learning (DDPG, TD3, SAC). The authors report that agents combining both principles consistently improve in profit maximization and risk-averse trading when sentiment is deeply infused.

Hrishikesh Singh, Aarti Sharma, Millie Pant

Uncertainty Quantification for Financial Foundation Models: A Survey

In briefSurvey on quantifying uncertainty in financial foundation models

SummaryThis survey examines uncertainty in financial foundation models used for question answering, disclosure analysis, forecasting, and decision-making. It proposes a source-aware framework that separates six uncertainty sources across evidence and context, model and inference, and outcome and action, and a mechanism-based taxonomy for assessing methods across financial tasks. The authors map finance-specific empirical studies onto this taxonomy. They find that most studies address single parts of a workflow, with little work linking evidence and reasoning reliability to downstream prediction and constrained action.

Zijie Zhao, Mingjun Sun, Shenbo Xu

Optimal Size in Market Making under State-Dependent Hawkes Process

In briefOptimal order size for market makers under a state-dependent Hawkes process model

SummaryThe paper models a market maker's executed volume as the product of average order size, an exponential demand function, and a Prony-series multiplier that depends on quote depth, with market-order counts built from a state-dependent Hawkes model. An empirical study on limit-order-book data examines calibrated models and sensitivity to inventory restrictions. In retrospective evaluations conditioned on realised order-flow paths, the selected calibration achieves higher terminal utility than a baseline while respecting the inventory bound, and the authors separate the effects of trading revenue from inventory control.

Jonathan Ch\'avez Casillas, Li Gan

Prospect Theory in Large Language Models: A Meta-Analysis of Loss Aversion, Diminishing Sensitivity, and Probability Weighting

In briefProspect Theory in LLMs: Meta-Analysis of Loss Aversion and Related Biases

SummaryThis paper meta-analyzes 102 fitted estimates from 11 studies covering 32 language models to test whether LLMs show the same prospect-theory properties as people. Loss aversion is modest, with a pooled λ of 1.26, far below the strongest human estimate. Probability weighting shows a human-like inverse S shape, but diminishing sensitivity depends on the measurement method used.

Adi Tarabeih
