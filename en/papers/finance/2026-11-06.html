The End-of-Day Reversal After 2019: A Pre-Registered Test on S&P 500 Stocks Excluding Heavily Traded Names

In briefPost-2019 End-of-Day Reversal: A Pre-Registered Test on S&P 500 Stocks

SummaryThis paper tests whether the end-of-day reversal in U.S. stocks reported by Baltussen, Da and Soebhag (2025) persists after 2019, using S&P 500 members excluding about 80 heavily traded stocks. Under a private pre-registration, the authors first required 2016-2019 data to reproduce the published sign before analyzing later primary-sample data. The reversal is near zero in 2020-2022 and about 1.69 basis points from 2020 onward, and it weakens on the full index. No pre-registered mechanism test passes.

Alex Vidovich, Zivana Zerjal

Joint modelling of financial cost and carbon footprint in motor insurance claims: a BINAR(1) decision framework for circular auto parts adoption

In briefJointly modelling claim costs and carbon footprint in motor insurance: a BINAR(1) framework for reused auto parts

SummaryThis paper proposes a bivariate discrete-time model based on a BINAR(1) structure to link motor insurance claim costs with the carbon footprint of repairs, capturing temporal persistence, cross-dependence and positive or negative correlation. It compares two repair pathways, new parts and circular-economy reused parts (PIEC), and uses scenario analysis varying the acceptance rate of reused parts to quantify effects on expected outlays and CO2-equivalent emissions. Results indicate that higher PIEC acceptance can reduce both expected emissions and average claim cost, with the size of the joint benefit depending on the dependence structure and persistence captured by the BINAR(1) process.

Charles Minier (CNP, LSAF), St\'ephane Loisel (CNAM Paris) et al.

Towards Robust Reinforcement Learning Market Making: Lessons from Loss Function Experimentation

In briefToward more robust reinforcement learning for market making, based on experiments with loss functions

SummaryThis paper examines how different loss function designs affect the risk management behavior of market-making systems trained with reinforcement learning. The authors empirically compare profit-maximizing strategies with risk-averse ones. They find that penalizing inventory and cash management leads to more resilient and adaptable market-making strategies.

Siddharth Nand, Bora Guney

Portfolio-aware scenario transfer of reserve movements: evidence from Lloyd's syndicates

In briefTransferring reserve-movement stress scenarios between insurance portfolios of different sizes

SummaryThe paper uses 632 syndicate-years from 117 Lloyd's syndicates over 2015-2024 to estimate how a claims-development reserve movement observed on one portfolio can be transferred as a stress scenario to a portfolio of a different size. Dispersion of the movement falls with reserve size, and a size-based scale predicts held-out syndicates better than a single market-wide scale. However, the transferred upper tail understates the smallest books.

Colin Priest

Does a Trading Policy Need to Know What It Trades? Microstructure Invariance and Zero-Shot Transfer of Execution Policies

In briefDoes a trading policy need to know what it trades? A study of whether execution policies trained on one set of stocks transfer zero-shot to other assets and markets.

SummaryThe paper tests whether order-execution policies learned on one set of stocks can transfer to unseen assets without retraining. Policies were trained on 954 Nasdaq stocks from 2019 and evaluated on stocks from later years, crypto pairs, and a second exchange. A policy using normalised features such as trade rate, trade size, spread and tick cost performed close to one trained on the evaluated stock. However, the advantage over raw features was small, and no representation extrapolated to unseen spread regimes.

Hieu Tran, Hong Yu
