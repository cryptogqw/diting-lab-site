Hard to Fool: A Bounded LLM Research Loop for Quantitative Finance, and What Two Dozen Campaigns Found

In briefA bounded LLM research loop for quant finance, reporting two dozen campaigns, most failing.

SummaryThe paper presents Autonomous Quant Researcher, an open-source loop in which a language model proposes strategy changes as fixed-vocabulary declarative JSON while trusted code decides acceptance. Evaluation uses immutable chronological data with a single-use holdout, multiple conjunctive gates, duplicate refusal, and a hash-chained ledger of all results. The authors report every campaign to date; most hypotheses fail, and one language-model survivor that passed discovery and validation fell from Sharpe ratios near 3 to 0.26 on its holdout.

Henry Zhang

Constrained defined-contribution pension management: A reparameterized neural network approach

In briefConstrained Defined-Contribution Pension Management: A Neural Network Method with Trading Limits

SummaryThis paper studies how a defined contribution pension member should invest when trading is restricted, using a two-factor financial market model. The participant can invest in cash, two nominal bonds, an inflation-linked bond and a stock index, and can buy term life insurance against pre-retirement death. The authors allow a general closed, convex trading constraint set, covering cases such as no short-selling and no borrowing. They propose a reparameterized neural network method based on Lagrangian duality to compute bounds on the value function, and calibrate it with U.S. data. The method yields a smaller duality gap than benchmark methods, and the constraints substantially change wealth accumulation, portfolio strategies and insurance demand compared with the unconstrained solution.

Yueman Feng, Wenyuan Li

A general technique for explicit quantile representations

In briefA general method for explicit quantile representations

SummaryThe paper proves that exact quantile integrals of strictly positive real-analytic densities admit contours that do not depend on the probability level. It gives explicit constructions covering five broad distribution families. Using forward functions, it also derives a finite-interval representation of Black-Scholes implied volatility.

Wolfgang Schadner

Exponential Seniority Weighting Chain-Ladder Reserving

In briefChain-ladder reserving with exponential seniority weighting

SummaryThe paper proposes a tractable state-space version of the chain-ladder model in which the dynamics act along the accident year axis, making accident years dependent. This formulation introduces exponential seniority weighting when estimating chain-ladder factors and provides an analytical likelihood representation. It allows computation of seniority-weighted chain-ladder reserves and their prediction uncertainty, with the one-year uncertainty available in closed form and total uncertainty obtained via Monte Carlo simulation from the likelihood.

Mario V. W\"uthrich

Decision Models as Scalable Economic Surrogates that Capture Human Variation

In briefDecision Models: Scalable AI Stand-ins for Human Economic Behaviour

SummaryThis paper evaluates Decision Models (DMs), a new class of AI models that quickly return probability distributions over fixed options, as substitutes for humans in economic simulations and surveys. The authors compare 10 DMs with 8 Large Language Models (LLMs) across a household spending survey, classic economic games, and a labour-market field experiment. The top-performing DMs reproduce the variation in human responses more closely than LLMs in all three settings, and they run roughly ten times faster than the fastest LLM and can be far cheaper.

Maxime Saxena, Fabio Caccioli, R. Maria del Rio-Chanona

Mitigating Return Drag and Regime Whipsaws in Sector Rotation: An Asymmetric Downside-Aware Nested Clustered Optimization Framework

In briefReducing Return Drag and Regime Switching in Sector Rotation: An Asymmetric Downside-Risk Nested Clustered Optimization Framework

SummaryThis paper addresses two weaknesses in sector rotation strategies: symmetric variance penalizes upside and downside equally, causing return drag, and Hidden Markov Model regimes switch too often, raising turnover. The authors propose Asym-Sticky-NCO, which combines a Bawa-Lindenberg downside semi-covariance matrix with a Gaussian HMM that has a persistence prior, applied to all 11 S&P 500 GICS sectors. In 65 out-of-sample months from August 2020 to December 2025, the framework reports a 13.41% CAGR and a 14.22% maximum drawdown, with regime switches cut from 16 to 4 and monthly turnover reduced from 60.78% to 30.48%.

Supriadi

Beyond Anonymous Order Flow: Wallet Identity as a Feature for Short-Horizon Return Prediction

In briefWallet Identity as a Predictive Feature in Hyperliquid Order Flow

SummaryThis paper uses Hyperliquid's Level-4 data to reconstruct full-depth order books from consensus-ordered messages and tests whether pseudonymous wallet identity helps predict short-horizon returns. The July 1–27, 2026 dataset covers 17.1 billion messages, 14.3 million aggressive orders from 147,113 wallets, and $84.3 billion in taker notional across ten markets. In three model markets, wallets' notional-weighted ten-second markout rankings persist across adjacent ten-day windows, and on a 100-millisecond grid, eleven features describing previously top-scored wallets raise one-second out-of-sample R2 from 10.88%.

Jingqiu Ding, Daojing Zhai
