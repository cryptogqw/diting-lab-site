Sensitivity analysis in real options studies: systematic review and recommendations

In briefSensitivity Analysis in Real Options Studies: A Review and Recommendations

SummaryThis paper reviews over 100 peer-reviewed real options articles to assess how sensitivity analysis is conducted and how well it is done. It finds a heavy reliance on one-at-a-time methods and very little use of global sensitivity analysis. The authors conclude with concrete recommendations for incorporating global sensitivity analysis into future studies to improve transparency, relevance, and credibility.

Mariia Kozlova, Julian Scott Yeomans

Algorithmic Market Making Can Lead to Rough Volatility Through the Order Book

In briefHow Algorithmic Market Making Can Produce Rough Volatility via the Order Book

SummaryThe paper proposes a mechanism in which market makers adjust their order distances from the mid price using a gradient rule toward unknown target distances, updating only when new information arrives over different lookback horizons. Together this forms stochastic gradient descent with noise, which in the continuum limit gives a conservative stochastic PDE (Dean–Kawasaki) for order book volume. The resulting order flow has power-law decaying memory, leading to rough volatility. Key predictions are checked on simulated agents and on six months of Binance data with about 790 million orders.

Sai Niranjan Ramachandran

When Price Charts Rhyme: Historical Visual Retrieval for Stock Return Prediction

In briefWhen Price Charts Rhyme: Visual Retrieval of Past Charts to Predict Stock Returns

SummaryThe study tests whether stocks with visually similar price charts have similar future returns, using a vision model to retrieve each stock's most similar historical charts from three decades of U.S. price data. A representation pretrained only on natural images already retrieves neighbors whose realized returns predict cross-sectional stock returns. Aligning the representation with subsequent returns more than doubles the strategy's Sharpe ratio.

Hui Chen, Haoyu Liu, Ke Wu et al.

Spectral Pricing of Corridor Products under Layered Local Volatility

In briefPricing corridor products with layered volatility using spectral methods

SummaryThe paper prices corridor products with knock-out barriers under an N-layered local volatility model that is piecewise constant in price and time. A spatial scale transformation and the GIT method reduce the killed Green's function to a symmetric tridiagonal system of size N-1, whose spectrum is obtained via eigen-decomposition and explicit eigenfunctions. Solutions across consecutive time intervals are linked by a matrix re-expansion. Benchmarks show agreement with independent calculations, retained accuracy at volatility interfaces, and computation about sixty times faster than the tested finite-difference scheme.

Andrey Itkin, Dmitry Muravey

Which Past Matters? Training History and Multi-Forecast Horizon in Equity Transformers

In briefWhich Past Data Matters? Training History and Forecast Horizons in Stock Transformers

SummaryThis paper examines how the amount of training data and the way historical data is selected interact with forecast horizon in intraday stock prediction. The authors compare twelve configurations that combine four sampling and validation policies with 6, 12, and 24 months of training data, using a fixed cross-sectional Transformer and monthly walk-forward refits. Six-month recency sampling performs best at 5 to 15 minutes, uniform sampling performs best at 60 to 120 minutes, and increasing training data does not uniformly improve accuracy.

Chariton Chalvatzis

Integrating market sentiments for stock price prediction: A comparative study of Bi-LSTM and multilayer perceptions

In briefUsing market sentiment to predict stock prices: comparing two neural network models

SummaryThis study uses Nvidia as a case to test whether sentiment scores from financial news headlines improve stock price forecasts. Sentiment was measured with FinBERT and TextBlob and fed, with historical prices, into Bi-LSTM and multilayer perceptron models. Performance was compared across the full period and bullish, stagnation, and strong bullish phases. Sentiment features improved accuracy in some contexts, notably stagnation, and Bi-LSTM outperformed MLP in capturing temporal dependencies.

Ahmet Akusta
