Taxonomizing Synthetic Data for Law

In briefA legal taxonomy of synthetic data sorting it into three types by how it is generated

SummaryThis paper examines the legal and policy issues raised by synthetic data, arguing it should not be treated as wholly separate from collected real data. Building on Gal and Lynskey's taxonomy, the authors divide synthetic data into transformed, augmented, and simulated categories based on background knowledge and ground-truth assumptions. They find that no category prevents group-based inference privacy harms, that augmented and simulated data can amplify bias, and that synthetic data rarely helps smaller competitors overcome incumbents' data advantages.

HARE: Fast Read Disturbance Threshold Profiling in Modern DRAM Chips via Reduced Timing Constraints

In briefHARE: Faster Read Disturbance Threshold Profiling in DRAM Chips

SummaryThis paper presents HARE, an online profiling technique for measuring the read disturbance threshold (RDT) of modern DRAM chips, which is the minimum number of row activations that causes the first bitflip. HARE reduces the timing constraints between consecutive DRAM commands during profiling, which shortens profiling time and intensifies read disturbance. Characterization of 180 DDR4 chips reports that HARE measures a row's RDT 31.2% faster than the RowHammer profiler and flips more than 95% of the cells that the RowHammer pattern flips.
