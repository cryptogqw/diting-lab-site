SANAI: Secure ECC Distributed Keyed Parity Embedded in Weights for Protecting Neural Networks against Bit-Flip Attacks

In briefSANAI: Keyed parity hidden in neural network weights to defend against bit-flip attacks

SummaryQuantized neural networks store their weights in dense memory, where bit flips from voltage scaling, aging, radiation, or attacks such as RowHammer can degrade model behavior. The paper proposes SANAI, a sensitivity-guided scheme that selects bit planes and embeds keyed parity within the weights with no extra parity storage. The authors report that across ten vision backbones, three quantization schemes, and ten language models, SANAI stays within about one percentage point of clean accuracy at bit-error rates up to 12%, about twice the limit of prior embedded-ECC approaches.

Accelerating QUIC Encryption in WebAssembly Using WASM-SIMD

In briefSpeeding up QUIC encryption in WebAssembly with SIMD instructions

SummaryThis paper examines the cryptographic cost of running the QUIC transport protocol inside WebAssembly (Wasm). The authors write explicit kernels using Wasm's 128-bit SIMD extension for AES and ChaCha20. On two hosts linked by 10-Gigabit Ethernet, these kernels raise receiver goodput over a non-SIMD baseline by 61.34% for AES-128-GCM and 31.55% for ChaCha20-Poly1305. The authors conclude that cryptographic acceleration is necessary but not sufficient, since memory allocation, runtime overhead and per-datagram UDP I/O still limit pure-Wasm transports.

SummaryCoding-agent services are priced according to the model they claim to use, so verifying those claims matters to users. The paper argues that existing model auditing methods assume a fixed deployment, while providers may legitimately change sampling settings, reasoning mode, or system prompts without changing the model, causing false accusations or missed cheap substitutes. J-Anchor: Defending Against Prefill Attacks via J-Space Selective Refusal Restoration

In briefJ-Anchor: Defending Language Models Against Prefill Attacks by Restoring Refusal in J-Space

SummaryThis paper studies prefill jailbreaks, where an attacker supplies an unsafe assistant prefix that makes a safety-aligned language model continue generating harmful content. The authors observe refusal-state erosion: during prefilling, the refusal signal falls back to its benign baseline while the harmfulness signal stays elevated. They propose J-Anchor, a training-free inference-time defense that uses the pre-attack signals as an anchor to selectively restore refusal in J-space. The method reduces attack success across model families while largely preserving benign behavior and reasoning ability.

Running Yang, Beidi Zhao, Xiaoxiao Li
