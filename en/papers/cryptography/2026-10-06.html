Federated Bayesian Surveillance of Mechanical Thrombectomy Adverse Events: A Population Risk Layer for Surgical Digital Twins

In briefFederated Bayesian Monitoring of Thrombectomy Adverse Events for Surgical Digital Twins

SummaryThe paper proposes population-scale adverse-event surveillance as a separate belief layer within surgical digital twins and tests a federated Bayesian method for learning it under formal privacy guarantees. It uses the complete FDA MAUDE cohort of 8,617 thrombus-retrieval catheter reports, with 6,491 classified into five complication classes and split across eight manufacturer sites. At a matched privacy budget, the method scores better on held-out data than FedAvg with differential privacy, and the non-private federated model beats centralized pooling. Because the data lack procedure denominators, the outputs are relative rate orderings rather than absolute risks.

One-Shot Private Confidence Regions via Resampling

In briefOne-shot differentially private confidence regions via resampling

SummaryThe paper proposes a simple method that adds noise only to the final resampling quantile to build differentially private confidence regions in one shot, instead of privatizing each resample's estimate. Under with-replacement (m-out-of-n) sampling the privacy cost grows only logarithmically with the number of resamples B, and under without-replacement subsampling it does not depend on B. The authors give nonasymptotic Gaussian Differential Privacy and utility guarantees for quantiles and degenerate U-statistics, among other estimators.

SummaryThe paper studies how much shape information is lost when recognizers compare rotation-invariant descriptors.
