I Want to Ride My BIKE: Customizing Bit-Flipping Decoders for Ephemeral Key KEMs

In briefShrinking code-based post-quantum key sizes for one-time keys via customized bit-flipping decoders

SummaryThis paper studies how to reduce public-key and ciphertext sizes in BIKE, a code-based post-quantum key encapsulation mechanism, when each secret key is used only once. The authors present the first optimized implementation of the BF-max decoder and combine it with other decoders to trade off decoding failure rate, execution time, and key and ciphertext sizes. Experiments report 7.8% smaller public keys and ciphertexts than LeanBIKE, with similar execution time and failure rate.

In briefEvaluating whether tiny language models can write post-exploitation malware code

Secure Compilation of Probabilistic Side-Channel Countermeasures

In briefSecurely compiling probabilistic side-channel defenses, with a verified compiler framework and open-source masking library

SummaryThe paper presents a framework, independent of language and leakage model, for provably secure compilation of probabilistic side-channel security notions. Concrete Functions in Private Simultaneous Quantum Messages: Quantum Advantages and Structural Limitations

In briefConcrete Functions in Private Simultaneous Quantum Messages: Quantum Advantages and Limits

SummaryThis paper studies communication complexity of familiar total functions in the private simultaneous quantum messages (PSQM) model. For three-party equality, it gives an entanglement-assisted protocol using about 1.633n qubits asymptotically, while perfectly correct protocols without prior entanglement need at least 3n qubits. For three-bit majority, a shared GHZ state and one qubit per player achieve perfect correctness and privacy, beating the six-bit classical cost. For three-bit AND, no qubit protocol achieves both properties, but qutrits give a one-shot protocol with total communication 3log₂3, and a general construction for k-bit AND is provided.

Harumichi Nishimura
