E-UDE: A Scientific Machine Learning Model for Malware Propagation Dynamics

In briefE-UDE: A machine learning model that predicts how malware spreads across the internet.

SummaryThis paper proposes an Enhanced Universal Differential Equation (E-UDE) framework that incorporates more detailed factors affecting malware propagation, aiming to improve both interpretability and predictive accuracy. On real-world propagation data it achieves an overall RMSE of 783.70 and MAE of 421.65, reducing RMSE by about 66 percent versus the classical ODE model and about 39 percent versus the UDE model. The model stays relatively stable as noise increases and supports scenario analysis under different cybersecurity conditions, and it was evaluated on the Code Red and Witty Worm datasets.

Vineet Kumar, Samiran Ghosh, V Anil Kumar

Poly Versus Polyval

In briefPoly versus Polyval: comparing polynomial-evaluation hash functions

SummaryThis paper revisits Poly, a polynomial-evaluation hash function over binary extension fields GF(2^n) with n=64k and k≥2. Unlike GHASH, it avoids per-block bit reversals and is faster, and it differs from the faster Polyval by using usual rather than Montgomery reduction. The authors give algorithms with correctness proofs and optimised assembly implementations for n=128, 256 and 512. They report that Polyval is faster for n=128, while Poly is faster for n=256 and larger.

Kaushik Nath, Palash Sarkar

Anamorphic Proofs of Knowledge from Prover Randomness

In briefAnamorphic Proofs of Knowledge from Prover Randomness (proofs that also secretly prove a hidden claim to a designated checker)

SummaryThe paper introduces anamorphic proofs of knowledge, in which a normal-looking proof publicly certifies its statement while also convincing a designated checker that the prover knows a witness for a separately registered hidden statement. The construction reuses the prover's commitment to its own randomness, adding a secret mask to the hidden witness. Normal verifiers and external observers cannot distinguish the two modes, and the checker learns neither witness. Instantiations are given for random-base Compressed Sigma protocols and Bulletproofs with range aggregation, and three-move protocols are shown unable to support the primitive under local checking.

Zhi Lu, Songfeng Lu, Jiaheng Zhang

In briefBeyond Sensitive APIs: Finding Access Control Flaws in Android Firmware

SummaryThis paper points out that existing tools flag dangerous behavior only when an app calls a predefined set of sensitive APIs, missing vulnerabilities where a generic API, such as a database read, operates on protected data without adequate access control. The authors present Callix, which judges how sensitive each callsite is by analyzing both the API's function and the data it handles, recovers each security check as an explicit property, and uses a restriction context to decide whether those checks suffice. In briefPractical preimage attacks on reduced 19- and 20-step SHA-256 with standard padding

SummaryThis paper presents preimage attacks on SHA-256 reduced to 19 and 20 steps, respecting the standard IV, Davies-Meyer feed-forward, and full FIPS 180-4 padding for arbitrary 256-bit targets.
