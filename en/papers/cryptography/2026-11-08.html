Revisiting the Security of OleF, t-OleF, and OlÆF

In briefRe-examining the Security of OleF, t-OleF, and OlÆF

SummaryThis paper revisits the security of the online authenticated encryption schemes OleF, t-OleF, and OlÆF and identifies two independent flaws. It shows a mask-compensation relation between continuation and termination processing in OleF and t-OleF, which enables a two-query distinguisher against OleF and a similar attack on t-OleF using concrete AXU hash families. The attacks are extended to OlÆF, breaking its privacy and integrity. The paper also refutes a claimed ideal integrity bound for variable-expansion online authenticated encryption.

WhyThe authors identify a mask-compensation relation in OleF and construct a two-query distinguisher, and report counterexamples refuting an ideal RUP bound.

Tingting Guo, Peng Wang, Shuping Mao et al.

RoCoMark: Robust Code Watermarking Against LLM-Based Functionality-Preserving Rewriting Attacks

In briefRoCoMark: A robust code watermarking method resisting LLM-based functionality-preserving rewriting attacks

SummaryThis paper addresses the problem that watermarks in generated code are often lost when large language models rewrite it while keeping its function. It proposes RoCoMark, a code watermarking framework in which the embedder and an LLM rewriter play a multi-round minimax game, with an adaptive attack-evolution mechanism based on Reflexion and memory pools. On four benchmarks against attacks generated by five LLMs, it reports an average detection AUROC of 74.2%, a Rewrite BitACC of 75.3%, and a functionality pass rate above 99.7%.

Hongjie Zhang, Xingcheng Li, Hanzhou Wu et al.

Attacking Slide Puzzle CAPTCHAs Based on Time and Space Generative Adversarial Networks

In briefBreaking Slide Puzzle CAPTCHAs with GAN-Generated Trajectories

SummaryThis paper systematically analyzes the defense strategies of six popular slide puzzle CAPTCHAs: Capy, Dingxiang, Geetest, Kyger, Shumei and Tencent. For each, the authors build a notch detection dataset and a slide trajectory dataset, use YOLOv11 to locate notches, and train generative adversarial networks to generate attack trajectories by learning human slide characteristics. The method reportedly succeeds over 90% of the time against all six CAPTCHAs in about 5 seconds, and the paper proposes ways to strengthen these CAPTCHAs.

In briefAI agents identify who owns cloud resources

SummarySecurity tools flag issues across cloud environments, but someone must still be found to fix each one, and recorded ownership is often outdated or missing. SkillCODER: Towards Auditing and Attribution of Copyright Infringement in LLM Agent Skills

In briefSkillCODER: Auditing and Tracing Copyright Infringement in LLM Agent Skills

SummaryThis paper presents SkillCODER, a method for auditing copyright and identifying the buyer of LLM agent skills in a black-box, content-only setting. It embeds instructions that activate only under verification queries and encodes buyer-specific fingerprints using controlled-vocabulary error-correcting codes. Across three domains, six models, and three agent frameworks, it reports 99.2% owner verification and 99.3% buyer Top-1 attribution accuracy while preserving task fidelity. The signals remain detectable under paraphrase, compression, and several adaptive attacks.

Enhao Huang, Chunshu Xia, Yiming Li et al.

Threshold Batched Attribute-Based Encryption from Succinct LWE

In briefA lattice-based scheme for threshold batched attribute encryption.

SummaryThis paper presents what the authors believe is the first lattice-based candidate for threshold batched attribute-based encryption (TB-ABE), a scheme where decryption is shared among multiple parties. The construction combines a succinct polynomial-digest mechanism from lattice-based batched identity-based encryption, key-homomorphic evaluation for arithmetic-circuit policies, and linear secret sharing. Security is proven under the ℓ-succinct LWE assumption, assuming the underlying ramp threshold sharing scheme has the required share-uniformity property.

Shayeef Murshid, Ramprasad Sarkar, Mriganka Mandal

Least-Privilege Auditing of Agent Tool Calls with Intent Graphs and Reasoning-Guided Escalation

In briefAuditing Agent Tool Calls with Intent Graphs and Reasoning Escalation

SummaryLLM agents that call tools can be steered by instructions hidden in tool outputs into actions the user never requested. The paper proposes a runtime audit that builds an executable intent graph from the user's objective and tool catalog with one LLM call per task, then checks each tool call with a deterministic, LLM-free loop (median 5.7 microseconds per call). Flagged calls are escalated to a reasoning guardian that sees the objective, plan, and call history but not tool outputs, and can verify facts with a restricted Python-expression tool. The authors note that a current agent showed no baseline attack success, so end-to-end validation remains open.

Doron Shavit

ErosionBench: Probing Alignment Decay in Enterprise Multi-Agent Systems

In briefErosionBench: A Benchmark Testing Alignment Decay in Enterprise Multi-Agent AI Systems

SummaryThis paper introduces ErosionBench, a benchmark for testing how multi-agent language model systems in enterprises can drift toward unsafe actions when benign requests propagate through shared state, long documents, and trusted peer messages. Designing Quantum-Safe Network Architectures via Certified Randomness Distribution

In briefQuantum-safe network design using certified randomness distribution

SummaryThis paper proposes an alternative design for quantum-safe network architectures based on distributing certified randomness generated by quantum random number generators (QRNGs) of different trust levels. The modular approach combines high-trust entropy from upper network layers with local contributions to strengthen the security of locally available random sequences. It uses the physical guarantees of QRNGs and symmetric-key algorithms for node authentication, so it can be deployed immediately. The authors argue it avoids the practical security limits of QKD-based solutions and the large key sizes and latency of asymmetric post-quantum cryptography.

Filip-Ioan Ceară, Diana Maimuţ

Chatter Attack: Resource Consumption Attack for Large Language Models

In briefChatter Attack: A resource-draining attack that makes LLMs produce excessively long outputs

SummaryThe paper introduces Chatter Attack, a type of resource consumption attack on large language models that optimizes adversarial prompts to induce short self-doubt phrases, sustaining long responses without an exploding search space. It maintains this state during generation using inference attention loss and global entropy loss. For black-box settings, the authors propose Bayesian Prompt Optimization (BPO), and experiments report maximum response length increases of up to 31.5 times.

Yibo Miao, Yichuan Cao, Xiao-Shan Gao et al.

In briefMinimum bucket size for reliable Path ORAM, a memory-access-hiding method

SummaryPath ORAM hides memory access patterns by storing data blocks in a binary tree with buckets of capacity Z, and keeping blocks that do not fit in a client-side stash. The paper shows that Z=4 is the smallest bucket capacity giving a uniform exponential bound on stash overflow probability for binary Path ORAM. For Z=3, it constructs instances where a single sequential scan of all blocks produces a stash of size Ω(N) with probability bounded away from zero.']

Do Benign-Trained Hidden-State Probes Detect Tool Errors Under Adversarial Conditions?

In briefCan probes trained on benign data detect tool errors under attack?

SummaryThis paper asks whether hidden-state probes trained only on benign tool-use trajectories can still detect incorrect tool actions under adversarial conditions such as prompt injection, system override, and tool poisoning. The authors test three models on four AgentDojo suites and compare the probes against three scalar uncertainty metrics. They conclude that benign-trained hidden-state probes should not be assumed to reliably detect tool-action errors under adversarial conditions.

In briefBeyond AUROC: Deployment-Aware Evaluation of Jailbreak Detectors

SummaryThis paper studies detectors that use a model's internal signals to flag jailbreak prompts aimed at LLMs. It finds that thresholds calibrated on curated benign prompts often fail to hold their false-positive rate on real-user traffic. Recalibration improves false-positive control but exposes large coverage gaps across attack families. The authors recommend evaluating realized false-positive rates on deployment-like traffic and per-family true-positive rates at low false-positive points, with AUROC as a secondary metric.

How to Revoke Users in Encryption Schemes?

In briefHow to revoke users in encryption schemes

SummaryThis paper proposes a way to revoke users from encryption schemes that keeps keys and access rules unchanged, is anonymous and collusion-resistant, and adds only succinct ciphertext overhead. Revoking a user publishes a single random-looking pseudonym without revealing whose key was revoked, and the revocation authority cannot decrypt. Building on the witness encryption framework of Garg et al. (CRYPTO 2025), the authors design two reusable succinct non-membership gadgets and apply them to add revocation to arbitrary public-key encryption and to several advanced encryption schemes.

Sanjam Garg, Dimitris Kolonelos, Benjamin Ostrovsky et al.

In briefAgents of Chaos: A Knowledge Systematization on Malicious Attacks Against Agentic AI

SummaryThis paper systematically reviews malicious attacks on agentic AI systems, which extend large language models with tools, memory, and code execution. The authors screened 186 sources, retained 98, and identified 284 atomic attacks, 452 safeguards, and 660 attack-safeguard relationships. SIExBench: A Benchmark for Automated Repair of Sensitive Information Exposure

In briefSIExBench: A Benchmark for Automatically Repairing Sensitive Information Exposure Bugs

SummaryThis paper introduces SIExBench, the first benchmark dedicated to automated repair of sensitive information exposure (SIEx) defects. It contains 161 execution-verified instances drawn from 64 Python and Java repositories. Evaluating six repair systems with three LLMs across 18 configurations, the best setup resolves only 39.1% of instances, and 62.1% of failed attempts produce patches that still leave information exposed.

WhyThe authors present the first benchmark for automated repair of sensitive information exposure, evaluating 18 configurations, with the best resolving 39.1% of instances.
