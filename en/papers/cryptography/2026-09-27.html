Climbing the Hill: Prompt Injection Red-Teaming Against Frontier Models with Curriculum Reinforcement Learning

In briefClimbing the Hill: Curriculum RL Red-Teaming for Prompt Injection Against Frontier Models

SummaryThis paper studies prompt injection, a major security risk for LLMs and LLM-based agents, and proposes a curriculum reinforcement learning method for red-teaming. It addresses the cold-start problem, where an attacker model receives no learning signal because every attack fails against frontier models. The attacker is trained against progressively more robust targets, with each stage partially succeeding to provide learning signals. On AgentDyn, the reported attack success rates (ASR@10) are 93.8% against GPT-5.6-Luna and 45.0% against GPT-5.6-Terra, while RL-Hammer and PISmith achieve 0%.

Chenlong Yin, Xiaolong Jin, Wei Zou et al.

The Price of Peeking: Anytime-Valid Leakage Detection on ML-KEM EM Traces

In briefThe Cost of Peeking: Anytime-Valid Leakage Detection on ML-KEM Traces

SummarySide-channel evaluators often check leakage tests during data collection and decide whether to continue or stop based on the results. This paper studies anytime-valid leakage detection using betting-based tests, which keep the false-alarm probability controlled at all times under an explicit conditional symmetry null. On an open ML-KEM electromagnetic dataset, the method needed about 1.68 to 2.38 times as many traces as a fixed-horizon randomization test to detect leakage in synthetic and degraded recordings. On undegraded recordings, the monitored procedure stopped early, with median stopping points of 62 to 316 traces.

Georgios Feretzakis, Alexandros Papaspyridis

Lower Bounds on Random-Oracle-Model Signature Length

In briefLower bounds on signature length for hash-based schemes in the random oracle model

SummaryThe paper asks whether the large signature size of hash-based post-quantum signatures is unavoidable. Within the pure random oracle model, it proves lower bounds for schemes with non-adaptive verifiers that satisfy a property called salted soundness. For one-time signatures, the combined public key and signature length must be Ω(λ²/log λ), and for many-time signatures the signature length alone must meet this bound.

A Complete Classification of Whole-Output Linear Structures in FEILIAN-Type Components

In briefComplete classification of whole-output linear structures in FEILIAN-type components

SummaryThe paper determines all input differences that give constant whole-output XOR derivatives for a four-word ARX structure containing a FEILIAN SubColumn. For any word width and XOR-linear internal maps, the linear-structure space contains a universal two-dimensional MSB family and has dimension at most five, with this bound attained. For the 64-bit FEILIAN component, the complete space equals this universal family. The results concern component structure and constancy at successive boundaries, and do not classify all endpoint structures of the iterated permutation or establish an attack on the hash.

Practical Null-Branch Witness Attacks on In-the-Head Signatures

In briefPractical null-branch witness attacks on AIMer v2.0 in-the-head signatures

SummaryThis paper presents a classical, public-key-only forgery attack on the AIM2-based AIMer v2.0 signature scheme, which was selected in Korea's KpqC competition. For every honestly generated public key, the authors claim signatures on arbitrary messages can be forged without signing queries. The attack exploits zero factors that leave intermediate values unconstrained, completing them into witnesses that satisfy the full relation. The paper also argues that a satisfying witness need not decode to a one-way function preimage, revealing a gap in AIMer's security reduction.

HESP: Separating What to Probe from When to Stop in Local LLM Alert-Triage Agents

In briefHESP: A controller that separates what to probe from when to stop in local LLM alert triage

SummarySecurity operations centers receive more alerts than analysts can handle, and organizations that cannot send telemetry to hosted models must automate triage with small open-weight LLMs on their own hardware. The paper presents HESP, a controller that keeps the investigation procedure outside the model, tracking competing explanations, choosing read-only probes by expected information gain per cost, accepting only evidence-backed verdicts, and ending investigations itself. Across five open-weight models (7B to 72B) and 7,272 audited episodes in a controlled triage environment, HESP substantially raises verified completion, showing that choosing what to probe and deciding when to stop are separable failures.

Mind the Gap: Proving and Improving RPKI

In briefClosing the Gap: Rigorously Proving and Strengthening RPKI Security

SummaryThis paper presents the first rigorous security analysis of the Resource Public Key Infrastructure (RPKI), an IETF standard that protects inter-domain routing against prefix and subprefix hijacks. Using modular security specifications, it analyzes RPKI's interactions with BGP and IP and identifies sufficient conditions under which RPKI provably meets its goals. The authors find that existing deployments do not always satisfy these conditions, for example due to circular dependencies, and propose standard-compliant improvements.

Cryptanalysis of the ICCS NGCC Round-1 Public-Key Candidates

In briefSecurity Analysis of 84 Candidate Public-Key Schemes in a Chinese Crypto Competition

SummaryThis paper reviews the 84 public-key candidates submitted to the ICCS NGCC round-1 public evaluation, focusing on design flaws that local code fixes cannot resolve. It identifies eight such weaknesses, similar to known pitfalls from NIST post-quantum standardization, including sub-ring projections that reduce IND-CPA security, public data that exposes supposedly secret values, static key reuse without a chosen-ciphertext transform, and rejection sampling that leaks an equivalent signing key. The authors state that all findings were obtained with AI assistance and provide a link to their artifacts.
