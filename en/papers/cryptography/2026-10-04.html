Who Is Your Agent Serving? Provider-Side Indirect Prompt Injection in Proactive Agents

In briefWhose Interests Does a Proactive Agent Serve? Provider-Side Indirect Prompt Injection

SummaryThis paper studies how an external provider could exploit proactive personal agents that make recommendations and offer follow-up help to steer users toward the provider's own target. The authors describe three mechanisms, Target Control, Private Binding, and Prospective Support, and test them across three environments and six simulated user models. The full attack raised target authorization in every tested combination, with a macro gain of up to 77.4 percentage points. The work concludes that capabilities built to serve users can be redirected toward objectives outside the user-agent relationship.

Which Image Property Carries the Jailbreak? A Controlled Dissection of Image-to-Text Jailbreaks

In briefWhich image property carries image-to-text jailbreaks? A controlled breakdown of attack factors

SummaryThe paper examines image-side factors in four published image-to-text jailbreak attack families, using a 313-prompt StrongREJECT slice and five multimodal models, plus an appendix evaluation of InternVL3.5-8B. Bare harmful text queries produce little attack success, while attack images substantially increase it. Per-tile entropy and JPEG size do not reliably separate attack tiles from size-matched benign distractors, and removing query-specific relatedness lowers attack success by about 0.12 on Qwen3-VL-8B. The conclusions remain conditional on an automated rubric judge.

Where Does the Audio Jailbreak Live? A Controlled Frequency-Depth Audit of AdvWave-P on Qwen2-Audio

In briefWhere the audio jailbreak lives: a controlled frequency and depth audit of AdvWave-P on Qwen2-Audio

SummaryThis paper audits claims about which frequency bands and decoder layers matter for AdvWave-P, an additive audio jailbreak attack on Qwen2-Audio. The authors mask frequency components of the perturbation in the STFT domain and measure attack success on 520 AdvBench prompts, with 76.7% of adversarial inputs judged as jailbreaks. They find the apparent frequency ranking depends on how bands are partitioned, and a finer-resolution result remains unresolved without a matched control. The authors conclude the results support partition-aware auditing of frequency claims, but the layer association is not a causal localization.

In briefRemoving query-depth losses from O2H theorems via amplitude amplification

SummaryThe one-way to hiding (O2H) theorems are widely used to prove security in the quantum random oracle model, especially for key encapsulation mechanisms.
