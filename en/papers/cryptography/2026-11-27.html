Beyond Stepwise Transformation: MILP Model for Low-Area Implementations of Linear Layers

In briefBeyond Stepwise Transformation: A MILP Model for Smaller Linear Layer Circuits

SummaryThis paper studies low-area implementations of linear layers used in lightweight symmetric-key cryptography. The authors argue that an existing stepwise method can restrict the search space, and they propose a mixed-integer linear programming (MILP) model that starts from an XOR2 circuit and jointly selects two-, three-, and four-input XOR gates. For AES MixColumns in the STM 130 nm library, the model yields 236.99 GE, improving on the previous 243 GE. On more than 5000 32×32 MDS matrices, the authors report average area reductions of up to about 28% over initial XOR2 circuits and about 8% over previous stepwise multi-input implementations.

Pengju Zhao, Qun Liu, Jingwen Chen et al.

Exact Post-Quantum Security Analysis of Ascon Authenticated Encryption under Nonce Misuse

In briefExact post-quantum security analysis of Ascon authenticated encryption under nonce misuse

SummaryThis paper studies the post-quantum security of Ascon AEAD, a lightweight authenticated encryption algorithm standardized by NIST as SP 800-232, when nonces are reused. Using the quantum random permutation model, the authors prove confidentiality and authenticity security up to min{2^(κ/2), 2^(c/3)} queries, improving the key-length dependence of a previous bound from 2^(κ/3). They also construct quantum attacks based on classical nonce-misuse attacks, showing that the bounds are tight in the exponent.

WhyThe authors introduce a new decomposition of Ascon AEAD, improve the key-length dependence of a prior bound, and report matching quantum attacks showing tight bounds.

Lightning-fast Two-Party Secure Sampling Protocol for Arbitrary Public Discrete Distributions

In briefA Fast Two-Party Protocol for Securely Sampling Any Public Discrete Distribution

SummaryThis paper presents a two-party secure computation protocol that samples from arbitrary public discrete distributions without revealing the generated randomness. The target distribution is compiled into a representation built from table-representable mixtures and deterministic transformations, and arithmetic shares of a sample are generated from this approximation. Security is proven against a static semi-honest adversary corrupting one party. The method is evaluated on five families of discrete distributions used in randomized algorithms and differential privacy.

Masanobu Kii

Aggregation techniques for the SPoCK scheme

In briefAggregation methods for SPoCK, a way to prove shared secret knowledge across many parties

SummaryA prior paper formalized Specialized Proof of Confidential Knowledge (SPoCK), which lets two independent parties convince a third party they share a secret without revealing it. This work introduces Multi-SPoCK, which lets two sets of provers show they derived their proofs from the same secret, and Aggregate Multi-SPoCK, which combines proofs over multiple secrets into one. The authors give a security model and pairing-based constructions from BLS multisignatures, with security proofs under suitable computational assumptions.

Extending the Security of Arithmetic Masking to All Finite Cyclic Groups via Fourier Analysis

In briefExtending Arithmetic Masking Security to All Finite Cyclic Groups via Fourier Analysis

SummaryThis paper studies a secret-sharing scheme in which a secret in the integers modulo N is split into k shares, and bounds how far the joint distribution of the shares' Hamming weights is from the distribution obtained when the secret is uniform. The authors prove that for every N≥2 and fixed k≥3, this total variation distance is O(n^(-k/2+5/4)) uniformly over all secrets, where n is the ceiling of log2 N. The proof uses Fourier analysis on the Boolean cube and a decomposition of the range into intervals with power-of-two lengths. The result removes the earlier requirement that the modulus be close to a power of two, while keeping the exponent of the general bound by Biswas et al.

WhyThe authors extend arithmetic masking security bounds from near-power-of-two moduli to all finite cyclic groups via Fourier analysis on the Boolean cube.

Ishan Kumthekar

Near-n-Bit Preimage and Second-Preimage Security of the JH Mode

In briefNear-n-bit preimage and second-preimage security of the JH hash mode

SummaryThis paper revisits the JH hash mode in the ideal-permutation model, where earlier direct bounds on preimage and second-preimage resistance were only at the birthday scale. Using Menger's theorem and a potential function, the authors derive bounds that give negligible advantage for messages up to 2^64 bits within about 2^(n-2√(2n)-O(log n)) queries. For JH-512, the certificates keep preimage success below 1/2 up to 2^431 queries and second-preimage up to 2^416. A Lean 4 formalization of the proofs is included.

WhyIn the ideal-permutation model, the authors use Menger's theorem and a potential to improve JH preimage and second-preimage bounds from birthday scale, with Lean 4 proofs.

On Perfect Additive Randomized Encodings

In briefImpossibility Results for Perfect Additive Randomized Encodings

SummaryThis paper studies Additive Randomized Encodings (AREs), which encode each input locally so that the function output can be decoded from the sum of the encodings while revealing nothing else about the inputs. The authors show that no ARE can achieve both perfect security and perfect correctness for OR over any finite Abelian group, and likewise for XOR over Z_p with prime p>2 under a rationality assumption. They also partially rule out perfectly correct AREs for OR with imperfect security under natural restrictions, and rule out perfectly secure AREs with nontrivial correctness error over histograms, which extends to protocols in the shuffle model.

WhyThe authors prove nonexistence of perfect AREs for OR over finite Abelian groups and XOR over Z_p, with partial negative results for perfectly correct AREs.

Yuval Ishai, Eyal Kushilevitz, Sahar Alon Yerushalmi

Stable Private Intersection with Consistency

In briefStable Private Intersection with Consistency (SPICy): a framework that binds both parties to their committed sets

SummaryThis paper introduces SPICy, a framework for private set intersection that binds both parties to their exact committed input sets across repeated intersections. It prevents a party from omitting elements, which would otherwise cause false negatives and biased outputs. SPICy builds on MiniPSI and introduces a new primitive, the Mutual Oblivious PRF, to achieve complete input consistency with a single round of online communication. The authors report online runtime overhead of 1.7–1.9× and communication overhead of about 4.5× compared to MiniPSI for set sizes from 2⁷ to 2¹⁰.

Manya Garg, Debayan Gupta, Arup Mondal et al.

Parity Leakage and Sparse-Mask Sampling in Distorted-McEliece Pseudorandom Codes

In briefParity leakage and sparse-mask sampling in distorted McEliece pseudorandom codes

SummaryThe paper examines the binary public-key pseudorandom codes proposed by Dyseryn, Francati and Venturi, showing that with the full support their public key can be distinguished from a uniform matrix by computing parities, which refutes their Distorted McEliece Public Key assumption. It characterises which supports leak through vanishing power sums and analyses how sparse distortions act on dual codewords. It also studies sampling methods for the mask, and states that the ciphertext-level assumption is not refuted.

WhyThe authors show the DFV key assumption fails for full support via parity leakage, characterize support restrictions, and analyse mask sampling methods.
