Extending the Schur--Macaulay Framework for UOV Attacks via Truncated Polynomial Algebras

In briefExtending Schur–Macaulay attacks on UOV with truncated polynomial algebras

SummaryThe paper builds on the wedge-attack framework of Ding, Li, and Tao and restates the Schur–Macaulay matrix approach of Merz and Ran in polynomial terms. It uses truncated polynomial algebras over positive-characteristic fields to cover Unbalanced Oil and Vinegar (UOV) over arbitrary finite fields. Divided powers and transfer relations add public relations, yielding quotient-dimension criteria for recovering graded parts of the hidden oil-space ideal. The authors apply this to third-round QR-UOV parameter sets and obtain lower estimates of attack complexity for some of them.

SummaryThis paper studies how large unpruned lattice enumeration trees are on random n-dimensional lattices, and how the tree-size exponent c depends on the chosen basis. In briefEffectiveness of Coarse NetFlow Data in Deanonymizing Tor Onion Services

SummaryThis paper examines whether coarse NetFlow-style traffic counts, such as one-minute samples of one-in-a-thousand packets, can deanonymize Tor onion services. Large-scale experiments on live Tor data show that when an adversary can induce recognizable traffic patterns through controlled downloads, such coarse telemetry suffices to identify a target. The authors also propose a stronger active attack using controlled introduction requests and a passive attack that sends no traffic to the target, instead using Tor's public puzzle difficulty as a side channel. The authors conclude that coarse network data is a practical threat today and call for mitigations and a redesign of globally visible defense signals that may leak service-load information.
