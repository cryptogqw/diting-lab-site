Factual Yet One-Sided: Stance Poisoning and Evidence Balancing in Retrieval-Augmented Generation

In briefFactually Correct but One-Sided: Stance Poisoning in RAG and a Balancing Defense

SummaryThis paper studies a retrieval-augmented generation attack in which injected documents contain correct facts but present a one-sided stance on a controversial topic, shifting the stance of generated answers. At about 18.9% poisoning share, all four generation models from different vendors shifted in the poisoning direction. The authors propose a Stance-Balancing Middleware between retriever and generator, combining stance detection, fixed balancing of supporting, opposing and neutral evidence, and a debiasing prompt, without extra LLM calls.

Puyuan Yang, Shaoxuan Dong, Liujie Wu et al.

Repairing Broken Access Control in Web Applications with LLMs: An Empirical Benchmark Study

In briefUsing LLMs to Fix Broken Access Control in Web Apps: A Benchmark Study

SummaryThe paper introduces BAC-Bench, the first benchmark for repairing broken access control vulnerabilities, containing 46 real-world cases from six web applications in four programming languages. It evaluates eight base LLMs and two commercial LLM agents, finding that single-turn models repair 37.68% of vulnerabilities on average. Adding two minimal scaffolds raises the average to 66.03%, and some LLM-generated patches surpass official ones.

In briefNameTrap: Using Variable Names to Lead AI Coding Agents into Writing Vulnerable Code

SummaryThis paper presents NameTrap, an attack that only renames identifiers in third-party Python files to steer AI coding agents toward specific CWE vulnerabilities such as CWE-78, CWE-79, CWE-327, and CWE-502. HNSecW: A Logarithmic-Time MPC Protocol for Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search

In briefHNSecW: A Logarithmic-Time MPC Protocol for Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search

SummaryThis paper proposes HNSecW, a secure multi-party computation protocol for approximate nearest neighbor search that achieves O(log N) search time for the first time. It redesigns the HNSW algorithm's control flow and data structures for fully data-independent execution, using offline reshuffling and partial database duplication. The protocol also supports multiple sequential queries and batch queries. Experiments report that it preserves plaintext HNSW accuracy and delivers over 20 times efficiency improvement on million-scale datasets.

WhyFirst MPC approximate nearest neighbor protocol reaching logarithmic search time via redesigned HNSW control flow; experiments on million-scale data show over 20x gains.

Revisiting AES Key Wrap: Forgery Bounds, Structural Observations, and Recommendations for the Revision of NIST SP 800-38F

In briefRevisiting AES Key Wrap: Forgery Bounds and Recommendations for NIST SP 800-38F Revision

SummaryThis paper expands earlier technical comments to NIST on SP 800-38F, which is being revised and specifies the AES Key Wrap modes KW and KWP. It argues that the forgery probabilities in the standard ignore adaptive attacks, and gives corrected forgery probabilities for KW, KWP and TKW. The authors also show that checking the expected plaintext length can considerably raise KWP's security level, and conclude that KW lacks beyond-birthday-bound security.

WhyShows SP 800-38F forgery bounds ignore adaptive attacks, corrects KW collision conditions, and derives a distinguisher, with direct impact on the standard's revision.

A Kelsey–Kohno Attack Variant against SHA-2 Message Digests in HSS/LMS and XMSS

In briefA Kelsey–Kohno attack variant on SHA-2 message digests in HSS/LMS and XMSS

SummaryThis paper presents a multi-target second-preimage attack on the message hashing of the stateful hash-based signature schemes HSS/LMS and XMSS, using SHA-256 and truncated SHA-256/192. The attack uses herding to merge chaining states from many signatures into one state, so a single search covers all of them and bypasses the key and leaf-index commitments meant to prevent multi-target attacks. The authors estimate the cost against SHA-256 with 2^64 signatures falls from the nominal 2^256 to about 2^166 for messages up to 4 GiB, and about 2^153 for SHA-256/192; the SHAKE256 parameter sets are unaffected.

WhyAdapts Kelsey-Kohno herding to a multi-target second-preimage attack, quantifying concrete cost reductions for SHA-256 in HSS/LMS and XMSS.

Falko Strenzke

Nearly Optimal Hardness for CVP and NCP with Preprocessing

In briefNear-Optimal Hardness of Closest Vector and Nearest Codeword Problems with Preprocessing

SummaryThis paper studies the approximation hardness of the closest vector problem (CVP) and nearest codeword problem (NCP) in a preprocessing model, where an algorithm receives the lattice or code in advance, produces polynomial-size advice, and then solves any target in polynomial time. Assuming NP is not contained in P/poly, the authors show polynomial-exponent hardness bounds for both problems. They extend the moment method to the preprocessing setting and give a direct reduction from PCPs yielding a factor of M^{Ω(1/(log log M)^2)}.

WhyLifts preprocessing-model hardness for CVP and NCP from subpolynomial to polynomial factors via an extended moment method, a substantial theoretical advance with impact mainly in complexity and crypto

On the Inapplicability of Residue-Confined Error Attacks to CRT-FHE with Non-Degenerate Error Distributions

In briefPaper note: Why residue-confined error attacks do not apply to CRT-FHE with non-degenerate errors

SummaryThis paper responds to a cryptanalysis claiming key and plaintext recovery against a CRT-based fully homomorphic encryption scheme. The authors argue that the attack relies on an artificially constructed error distribution supported in the kernel of the CRT encoding, which makes the effective encryption error zero. They conclude that under the intended centered-binomial error distribution, such errors occur with negligible probability at the reported parameters, so the attack does not compromise the concrete scheme.

CVE-to-ATT&CK Mapping via Evidence-Constrained Ranking

In briefMapping vulnerabilities (CVEs) to attacker techniques (ATT&CK) with evidence-based ranking

SummaryThis paper addresses automatically linking software vulnerabilities (CVEs) to MITRE ATT&CK attack techniques, to help defenders infer adversary behavior. The authors propose TRACE, which first combines structural and semantic evidence from multiple sources to build a bounded candidate set, then reranks candidates using CVE-technique text interactions and frequency-aware scoring. Tested on six public datasets, candidate construction cuts the 201-technique search space by about 60% while keeping at least one correct technique for 95.11%–100% of CVEs.

Chosen Commitments and Certified Decoding Regions in the Adaptive Threshold Schnorr Attack

In briefChosen-Vector Attacks on Threshold Schnorr Signatures: Bounding Forgeries via Decoding Regions

SummaryThe paper analyses the adaptive forgery attack on threshold Schnorr signatures by Crites and Stewart, asking whether a chosen rather than random coefficient vector helps the adversary. The authors prove that an adversary committing to fixed vectors and recovering witnesses only in certified decoding regions succeeds with probability at most QM/p, where p is the group order. For the original parameter pairs the bound is very small at realistic field sizes. The work also classifies the decoder theorems used, while the hardness of the underlying oracle and the adaptive security of threshold Schnorr schemes remain open.

Hüseyin Eren Demirtaş, Oğuz Yayla

SummaryThis paper examines defenses against poisoning attacks on retrieval-augmented generation (RAG).
