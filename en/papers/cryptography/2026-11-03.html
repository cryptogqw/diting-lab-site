Say the Same, Act Differently: Diagnosing and Steering Text-Action Divergence in Reasoning VLAs

In briefSame words, different actions: diagnosing text-action divergence in reasoning robot models

SummaryThis paper studies whether visual perturbations can change the physical actions of reasoning vision-language-action models while leaving their generated rationale word-for-word identical. The authors introduce a text-neutral action-subspace (TNAS) diagnostic and test it on the driving model Alpamayo and the manipulation model InstructVLA. Perturbations along this direction shift actions far more than they change text, and TNAS-guided attacks induce new collisions under unchanged rationales. The authors conclude that an unchanged rationale cannot certify a safe action, so monitors must check the predicted action directly.

WhyThe authors show visual perturbations can change actions while the rationale stays word-for-word identical, introducing TNAS diagnostic; experiments report on driving and manipulation VLAs.

Zihao Feng, Qingzhao Zhang, Chunyu Xia et al.

ScrambleMark: Zero-Query Watermark-Agnostic Black-Box Evasion of Modern Speech Watermarking

In briefScrambleMark: Query-free, watermark-agnostic black-box attack that evades speech watermark detection

SummaryThis paper presents ScrambleMark, a black-box attack that evades speech watermark detection without any queries and without knowing the specific watermarking scheme. Rather than erasing the watermark, it disrupts the structural consistency of the embedded pattern by scrambling harmonic structure, which preserves perceptual quality. The authors report nearly 97% evasion across three commercial platforms and a 4.2/5 human rating, compared with 1.1 to 1.7 for prior attacks.

WhyThe authors propose evading watermarks by scrambling harmonic structure rather than erasing it, reporting about 97% evasion on three commercial platforms.

Binhao Ma, Weikang Ding, Hanqing Guo et al.

Permitted but Unauthorized: Affordance-Induced Delegated-Scope Violations in Multi-Agent LLM Systems

In briefPermitted but Unauthorized: Affordance-Induced Scope Violations in Multi-Agent LLMs

SummaryThis paper studies whether AI agents in multi-agent systems cross their delegated task scope to act on peers' resources under competitive pressure. Tests across eight LLMs in a CI/CD pipeline and a security-operations incident queue found that whether a peer action is listed as an option drives crossings far more than permissions do. Two defenses blocked every crossing, but neither prevented harm done through an authorized action, such as misreporting incident severity.

Shravya Kanchi

On the (Quantum) Communication Complexity of Secure MPC from a Random Oracle

In briefCommunication complexity of secure multiparty computation using a random oracle, including quantum settings

SummaryThis paper studies the communication complexity of secure multiparty computation (MPC) in the random oracle model. It shows that for MPC with preprocessing, a random oracle cannot reduce online communication, which remains bounded below by Ω(|C|·t/(n-t)). For honest majority MPC, it proves an Ω(|C|) communication lower bound and gives a matching construction for degree-2 polynomials, including inner products, when n = O(log λ). The paper also analyzes protocols that use quantum communication.

WhyThe authors extend MPC communication lower bounds to the random oracle model, give matching constructions for degree-2 polynomials, and study quantum communication limits.

Alexander Hoover, Giuseppe Persiano, Kevin Yeo

Gather or Act? Belief-Level Counterfactual Labels for Deadline-Aware Incident Response in Cyber-Physical Systems

In briefGather evidence or act now? Belief-level counterfactual labels for deadline-aware incident response in cyber-physical systems

SummaryThe paper addresses how a defender of an industrial control system should choose between acting immediately on the likeliest fault cause and first gathering evidence to separate look-alike causes before the process nears shutdown. It introduces TE-GvA, a counterfactual decision dataset built on the revised Tennessee Eastman process, with 9,908 decision points from 1,990 simulated incidents. The authors show that belief-level labels, weighted by exact posterior over twin episodes, make gathering the best action far more often in ambiguous cases and yield lower regret than hindsight labels on stress decisions.

Ali Mohammadi Ruzbahani

Towards Effective Fuzzing of EDK2 Components

In briefA new automated fuzzing method for edk2 boot-time components

SummaryThe paper presents Metis, an automated technique for fuzzing edk2, the UEFI-based bootloader, by rehosting its boot-time protocols as user-space applications. It mocks protocol dependencies using a Direct-dependency Component Rehosting strategy and generates fuzzing harnesses through a mix of static analysis and LLM-guided synthesis. Evaluated on 60 drivers covering 162 protocols, Metis reaches 73.1% coverage, about 1.7 times that of FuzzUEr, and discovers 137 previously unknown bugs.

Datta Manikanta Sri Hari Danduri, Connor Everett Glosner, and Aravind Machiry

In briefWhat Malware Does After Infection: An Empirical Study of 37 Families

SummaryThis paper analyzes how 37 malware families behave after infection, using dynamic sandbox runs in CAPEv2. Quantum Private Information Retrieval on Forests

In briefQuantum private information retrieval on forest graphs, a way to retrieve data privately using quantum-entangled servers on tree-structured networks

SummaryThe paper studies graph private information retrieval in the quantum setting, focusing on forest graphs made of disjoint trees. The authors design a scheme that achieves rate 3/N on forest graphs, using a GHZ state shared among servers, where each server sends one qudit per round. For the special case G=3K_2, they give a scheme with rate 4/6 and a matching upper bound, establishing this as the capacity.

Mohamed Nomeir, Sennur Ulukus

MCP Tool Poisoning in Agentic AI Frameworks: A Cross-Framework Measurement Study

In briefMCP Tool Poisoning in Agentic AI Frameworks: A Cross-Framework Measurement Study

SummaryThis paper measures how attacker-controlled tool descriptions, schema fields, and response data in the Model Context Protocol (MCP) can manipulate agentic AI systems. The authors propose a 25-class root-cause taxonomy and build the open-source tool mcpvenom to run a 9,720-cell susceptibility matrix across 12 frameworks, 18 models, and 45 attack scenarios. The overall attack success rate is 22.9%, and 12.1% of vulnerable cells verifiably exfiltrate a secret.

WhyThe authors present a cross-framework measurement of MCP tool poisoning with real execution, a 25-class taxonomy, and a 9,720-cell matrix across 12 frameworks and 18 models.

RuntimeTickets: Source-Grounded Security Obligations for Agent Skills

In briefRuntimeTickets: Source-Based Security Obligations for Agent Skills

SummaryThird-party agent skills extend what autonomous agents can do, but malicious skills can hide data leaks or output manipulation within legitimate workflows. The paper introduces RuntimeTickets, a protocol that enforces and audits security requirements while a skill runs, using a language model to propose task-specific obligations and a deterministic runtime gate to check outbound sends. IAMRAGBENCH: Benchmarking Authorization Expansions in LLM-Generated AWS IAM Policies under RAG Poisoning

In briefIAMRAGBENCH: Testing whether AI-generated AWS IAM policies grant excess access under poisoned retrieval

SummaryThis paper introduces IAMRAGBENCH, a 60-case benchmark that tests whether large language models using retrieval-augmented generation produce AWS IAM policies granting more access than the task allows when attacker-controlled documents are in the context. Across several model endpoints, poisoned context led to many policies that exceed the trusted authorization envelope, and the authors also assess how well AWS CheckNoNewAccess detects these changes. The authors conclude that IAM copilots should be treated as untrusted proposal generators whose outputs need independent authorization checks before deployment.

WhyThe authors present a benchmark quantifying RAG-poisoning-induced IAM authorization expansion and propose pre-deployment independent authorization checks.

O2N2: Efficient Doubly Oblivious Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search

In briefO2N2: A Faster Doubly Oblivious Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search

SummaryThis paper presents O2N2, a doubly oblivious framework for approximate nearest neighbor search that hides query contents from a server running in trusted execution environments. It is built on single-path search over randomly rotated k-d tree forests, with the search structure stored in a pointer-based Circuit ORAM. The authors report a 9.8 to 125.9 times speedup over the fastest doubly oblivious baselines for 10-NN and 100-NN search at specified high-recall targets.

Zhechen Li, Zimai Guo, Mingxun Zhou et al.

End-to-End NTT on FPGA for FHE Acceleration

In briefFPGA-based end-to-end number theoretic transform to speed up fully homomorphic encryption

SummaryFully homomorphic encryption allows computation on encrypted data without decrypting it, but it carries heavy computational and memory costs. This paper designs a fully unrolled streaming NTT/INTT kernel placed across the three dies of an AMD Alveo V80 FPGA. The kernel was integrated into the Lattigo FHE library and evaluated end to end. With 128-bit secure parameters, the authors report 1.8–2.2x speedups for core operations such as key switching and bootstrapping, and 1.8–2.1x for neural network inference.

Mohamed Allam, Thomas Spendlhofer, Hugo Sanz-Gonz\'alez et al.

A Symbolic-Neural Framework for Context-Aware Evidence Extraction from Mobile SQLite Databases

In briefA framework combining symbolic and neural methods to extract forensic evidence from mobile SQLite databases.

SummaryThis paper presents SNEEF, a framework that extracts forensic evidence from mobile SQLite databases by encoding each row as context-value pairs with provenance metadata. A language model recognizes nine forensic entity types, and the PA-EEP protocol links each entity to its source table and row. Evaluated on five corpora from Android and iOS device images, SNEEF reaches an F1 score of 0.967, compared with 0.728 for a context-free configuration.

Jeel Piyushkumar Khatiwala (University of Baltimore), Divyangkumar Patel (Cox Automotive Inc), Dianxiang Xu (University of Missouri-Kansas City) et al.

TrafficInstruct: A Multi-Task Network Traffic Dataset and Benchmark for Instruction Tuning

In briefTrafficInstruct: A Multi-Task Network Traffic Benchmark for Instruction Tuning

SummaryThis paper introduces TrafficInstruct, a network traffic dataset with raw packet captures and 55,000 structured fine-tuning records covering ten analysis tasks, including malware, botnet, Tor, and encrypted VPN detection. Traffic is collected automatically from controlled software runs and attack experiments, and the data is split by complete collection sessions. The authors compare several baselines against independently trained task-specific LoRA adapters on a shared frozen model. Task-specific adaptation improves classification performance.
