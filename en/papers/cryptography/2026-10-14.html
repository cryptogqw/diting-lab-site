P2Patch: Exploit-Guided Vulnerability Repair

In briefP2Patch: Exploit-Guided Vulnerability Repair (testing each patch with new attacks to catch fixes that only block the original exploit)

SummaryThis paper presents P2Patch, a generative adversarial system for automated vulnerability repair. An exploitation agent first builds a reproducing proof of vulnerability (PoV), a patching agent then repairs the program, and the exploitation agent attacks the patched code for a distinct same-vulnerability exploit. Each successful exploit becomes a permanent repair requirement that the final patch must block. Across 101 C/C++ and Java CVE subjects, the loop found a new confirmed exploit in 41% of runs, and held-out fix-closed coverage rose from 78.1% to 84.7% compared with single-pass repair.

Secret Sharing from Non-Extractable Randomness

In briefSecret sharing without extractable randomness

SummaryThis paper studies the randomness needed for two-out-of-two secret sharing. Bosley and Dodis asked in 2007 whether such schemes must also allow deterministic extraction of nearly uniform bits from the randomness source. The authors answer no: for each b≥2, they build a class of flat sources with min-entropy at least b and a perfectly correct, perfectly private scheme that splits a b-bit secret into two (b+1)-bit shares. The idea is extended to other threshold settings.

WhyA parity-based counterexample answers an open question negatively and extends the result to other sharing thresholds.

In briefStudying how hard it is to find permutation automorphisms of linear codes

SummaryThis paper examines the computational difficulty of finding permutation automorphisms of linear codes, covering three problems: finding a non-trivial automorphism, a generating set, and the group order. Bilinear Compressive Security with Finitely Many Samples

In briefBilinear Compressive Security with Finitely Many Samples: How attackers recover keys from limited encryption observations

SummaryThis paper studies key recovery against Bilinear Compressive Security (BCS), a lightweight encryption scheme, when an attacker sees only finitely many encryptions. The authors propose a covariance-based attack that estimates linear measurements from repeated encryptions of the same plaintext and then recovers the secret measurement matrix, with finite-sample guarantees and sample-complexity bounds. Experiments show that dense matrices such as Gaussian ones need a number of measurements growing roughly linearly with signal dimension, while sparse-row matrices show quadratic growth consistent with theory. The results suggest the sparsity structure of the measurement matrix matters for BCS security.

Axel Flinth, Semira Einsele, Matilda Kylberg et al.

Global and Local Entropy Tests for STL Files Under IEEE-754 Validity Constraints

In briefEntropy tests for STL files that account for IEEE-754 format validity rules

SummaryThis paper studies entropy-based randomness tests for ciphertexts of STL files. Most numerical data in STL is stored as single-precision IEEE-754 floats that must be finite, which makes byte probabilities nonuniform and creates dependence between exponent bytes. The authors derive the exact mean and variance of entropy under the valid-STL distribution and give second-order expansions for practical computation at large file sizes. They show the global entropy limit shifts to about 7.99991479609 bits and the local reference values also change, so unadjusted tests may wrongly reject ideal ciphertexts; they recommend format-specific reference values.
