Rerandomizable Garbling for Arithmetic Circuits

In briefRerandomizable garbling for arithmetic circuits

SummaryThis paper studies rerandomizable garbling, which lets a garbled circuit be refreshed without changing its function while preserving privacy if at least one rerandomization step is honest. Prior constructions only handle Boolean circuits and have high communication costs. The authors present the first rerandomizable garbling scheme for arithmetic circuits, requiring 17 group elements per multiplication gate, with addition gates free. The scheme can be instantiated under standard subgroup-indistinguishability assumptions, including DCR.

Anasuya Acharya, Pierre Meyer, Claudio Orlandi et al.

Rounded Gaussians are Discrete Gaussians

In briefRounded Gaussians Are Discrete Gaussians: A Correction for Lattice-Crypto Sampling

SummaryThe paper addresses sampling integers from Gaussian-like distributions, which lattice-based cryptographic schemes often need. Rounded Gaussian samplers are efficient and easy to implement but are hard to analyze securely. The authors show that setting σ = s − 1/(24s) makes the rounded Gaussian close to a discrete Gaussian with parameter s, with Rényi divergence 1 + O(1/s^8). This lets practitioners use the corrected sampler for mild to large parameters while analyzing security as if using an ideal discrete Gaussian, at very little cost.
