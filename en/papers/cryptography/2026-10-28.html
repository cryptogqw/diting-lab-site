Heads of Security: Learned Attention Gates Improve Prompt Injection Robustness

In briefHeads of Security: Learned Attention Gates for Stronger Prompt Injection Defense

SummaryThis paper introduces Heads of Security (HoS), a defense against prompt injection that uses a small set of learned attention gates in each attention head to separate instructions from untrusted data. The gates learn how attention should be divided between trusted and untrusted tokens, so models can process untrusted content without following injected instructions. Tested on Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct and GPT-OSS-20B, HoS shows greater robustness to adaptive attacks and better utility than SecAlign++ and ASIDE, and matches top results on agentic benchmarks.

Igor Shilov, Xiaoxue Yang, Alexander Panfilov et al.

Classical Verification of Quantum Computation Beyond the Polynomial Hierarchy

In briefClassical verification of quantum computation beyond the polynomial hierarchy

SummaryThe paper studies whether a classical polynomial-time verifier can check arbitrary quantum polynomial-time computations without computational assumptions. Relative to a classical oracle, it constructs a language outside the polynomial hierarchy that is exactly decidable in quantum polynomial time, with a public-coin protocol having perfect completeness and soundness error at most 1/4. The message count is shown to be optimal up to a constant factor.

WhyConstructs an oracle-relative language beyond the polynomial hierarchy with a perfectly complete public-coin proof system, using a new switching theorem for correlated restrictions; novel and rigorous

Atul Mantri

Revisiting Privacy Risks in Quantized Deep Neural Networks

In briefPrivacy risks of quantized neural networks: do compressed models leak more training data?

SummaryThis paper presents the first comprehensive empirical study of privacy risks in quantized deep neural networks, evaluating eight membership inference attacks across six quantization methods. Practical quantization levels of 4 to 8 bits have limited effect on membership privacy, leaving quantized models about as vulnerable as full-precision ones. Aggressive 2-bit quantization yields some privacy gains but substantially degrades utility. Existing defenses designed for full-precision models offer limited protection for quantized models while further amplifying utility loss.

Jie Fu, Ren Li, Wendy Hui Wang

When POST Becomes GET: Investigating HTTP Method Interchangeability and Its Security Implications

In briefWhen POST Becomes GET: How Interchangeable HTTP Methods Can Create Security Gaps

SummaryThis paper studies HTTP method interchangeability, where a POST request to an endpoint is rewritten as a GET request with its body parameters moved into the query string, yielding an equivalent response. The authors present MIDE, an automated pipeline that crawls web applications, converts captured POST requests, and compares responses. In an unauthenticated experiment on the Tranco top 10k, they found high-confidence interchangeable requests on 407 sites. The study also reports CSRF token-replay cases and conditions related to Web Cache Deception.

Matteo Golinelli, Seif Errahmane Hannachi, Bruno Crispo

Penny for Your Prompts: A Measurement Study of Contextual Leakage in AI Chat Platforms

In brief"Penny for Your Prompts": A Study of Chat Data Leaks on AI Platforms

SummaryThis paper measures how 42 AI chatbot web interfaces, including general-purpose and health, legal, and education platforms, share data with third parties. The researchers instrumented the browser's JavaScript engine to trace each data flow back to the user action that triggered it. They found that 21 platforms leak conversation titles and nine leak verbatim user prompts to third-party trackers, and many also send users' emails.

Paschalis Bekos, Billy Tsouvalas, Nick Nikiforakis et al.

SecDMP: Provably Secure Data Memory-Dependent Prefetching (Extended Version)

In briefSecDMP: A Provably Secure Data Prefetcher Defense (Extended Version)

SummaryData memory-dependent prefetchers (DMPs) are a CPU optimization in recent mainstream processors that can improve performance but may leak secrets through side channels. The paper classifies DMP leakage, categorizes existing and new attacks, and validates the analysis by implementing open-source DMPs on a RISC-V processor. It proposes SecDMP, a hardware defense in which secrets are marked in memory so DMPs never encode them in the microarchitecture. The authors formally prove SecDMP adds no leakage beyond a baseline without DMPs.

WhyClassifies DMP leakage and proposes SecDMP, a formally proven hardware defense blocking all leakage classes while preserving public-data performance.

Jens Sprengers, Marton Bognar, Lesly-Ann Daniel et al.

The Envelope Layer: An Underestimated Surface of LLM Agent Defense

In briefThe Envelope Layer: An Underestimated Defense Surface for LLM Agents

SummaryThe paper builds on prior work that treats the envelope layer as the channel through which malicious content enters an agent. Using the A2A-TIBA attack as a test vehicle, it runs a two-by-two factorial experiment and a memory experiment to examine how label strength, envelope authenticity, scaffold differences, and existing memories affect defense. Strong labels reduce semantic breach rates when envelopes are authentic, but weak labels are not always better than no label, and strong labels lose effect when envelopes can be forged. Memory has a larger effect than label manipulation. The authors refine the envelope layer into an admission channel and a context assembly surface, arguing its security boundary depends on label claims, frontend permissions, and historical memory together.

Yuelin Han
