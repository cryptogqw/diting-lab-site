Connection Escrow: Holding AI-Agent Pod Egress in the Kernel Until an Isolated Verifier Rules

In briefConnection Escrow: Holding AI-Agent Pod Network Connections in the Kernel Until an Independent Verifier Decides

SummaryThe paper proposes connection escrow, which uses an eBPF program on a pod's cgroup to hold each outbound TCP connection inside the kernel's connect() path before any packet is sent. A node interceptor parks the agent's first bytes and releases the connection only on a signed verdict from a separate verifier that names that specific socket, while expired or late approvals are refused. The authors report a 36-check adversarial test suite on real cgroups and a Kubernetes evaluation, with a median added delay of 4.5 ms per released connection.

In briefMal-AAEGAN: A GAN that generates adversarial malware using a latent space and two critics

SummaryThis paper proposes Mal-AAEGAN, a method for generating adversarial malware that can evade machine-learning detectors. Reliable Estimation of Differential Privacy under Selection Bias

In briefReliable Privacy Audit Estimates Under Selection Bias

SummaryThis paper examines statistical privacy audits that check whether differentially private mechanisms meet their theoretical guarantees. The authors found that many prior audit implementations, which use membership inference attacks, introduce a selection bias that makes estimated privacy leakage lower bounds invalid and overestimated. They present several statistical correction methods and compare their advantages. Re-analyzing prior audit results, they show that uncorrected audits violate theoretical upper bounds up to four times more often than their claimed confidence level implies, while corrected bounds stay within the nominal violation rate.

From if to Oops: Turning a conditional into a key-recovery oracle

In briefFrom if to Oops: Turning a conditional into a key-recovery oracle

SummaryThis paper examines deterministic versions of CSIDH, including Houben's deterministic CSIDH and OSIDH-LD, which remove randomness from the group action. The authors show that determinism places every secret-dependent step at a fixed public point in each execution, letting an attacker repeatedly fault the same step. By skipping one instruction, the attacker forces the mask that selects the next isogeny computation to zero, and whether the output changes reveals if a secret exponent exceeds a threshold. A binary search over these thresholds recovers the full key with one fault per execution. The attack was validated in software fault simulation and on a ChipWhisperer-Lite, and the authors argue that fault resistance requires giving up determinism in favor of randomized countermeasures.

WhyIdentifies a new fault attack surface in deterministic CSIDH, turning a conditional into a key-recovery oracle, validated by simulation and hardware.
