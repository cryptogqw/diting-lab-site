Do Agents Still Follow the Rules? Benchmarking Rule Compliance under Agent Context Compaction

In briefDo Agents Still Follow the Rules? Testing Rule-Following After Context Compression

SummaryLLM agents compress long histories to fit their context windows, but the compressed context must still keep the rules that govern later actions. The authors introduce ARCBench, a configurable benchmark built on the open-source pi agent harness that measures rule compliance from actions taken during execution. They find that compression rarely deletes the rule, and most post-compaction violations occur while the rule is still in the compressed history. Retaining a rule does not guarantee compliance: compacted runs that keep the rule violate more than twice as often as runs with the full history.

Yiwei Zha, Qingyu Meng, Herbert Bos et al.

Toward Deployable Hardware Roots of Trust for Edge AI: Software PUFs and Their Trade-offs

In briefSoftware PUFs as hardware roots of trust for existing edge AI devices

SummaryEdge AI devices with physical access risk exposing secret keys stored in non-volatile memory. Software physical unclonable functions (PUFs) derive unique responses from analog device characteristics through software-only interfaces, avoiding post-fabrication hardware changes. This paper surveys 37 software PUF works and compares them against on-device model protection requirements such as boot-time versus runtime key derivation and response latency. It analyzes trade-offs, outlines four key challenges for edge AI deployment, and highlights promising research directions.

Yilin Li, Farhad Merchant

An Information Hierarchy of Privacy Filters and Odometers

In briefA study of how much information privacy filters and odometers need to work well.

SummaryThis paper studies how the information used by differential privacy filters and odometers limits their performance. It defines three information levels (spec, mechanism, and realisation) and classifies filters and odometers by the least informative level sufficient to determine their behaviour. Systematic Derivation of Leakage in Massey Secret Sharing Schemes

In briefSystematically deriving information leakage in Massey secret sharing schemes.

SummaryThis paper studies leakage attacks on secret sharing schemes, where partial information about each share is used to recover the secret. Building on the Guruswami-Wootters construction, the authors derive new explicit linear exact repair schemes and analyze their algebraic properties. They also introduce a Gröbner-basis-based computational method for finding leakage functions across arbitrary linear codes. Applied to Reed-Solomon, generalized Reed-Muller, and Tamo-Barg codes, the method identifies several repair schemes not previously described in the literature.

Nadja Aoutouf (X, SURYCAT), Daniel Augot (X et al.

Cyber-JEV: Matched Contrastive Fine-Tuning of Language Models for Fast Source-Code Vulnerability Triage

In briefCyber-JEV: Contrastive fine-tuning with fix commits for fast vulnerability triage in source code

SummaryThe paper presents Cyber-JEV, a method that fine-tunes language models to give each source-code function a vulnerability score in a single pass, so security teams can rank functions for review. It builds comparison groups from fix commits, pairing each vulnerable function with its patched version and unchanged functions from the same file, and trains one model on classification and ranking. On a 7,270-function test benchmark, an ensemble of five fine-tuned Qwen3.5-4B models reaches an AUROC of 0.814, versus 0.743 for Jev 1.13.

Leakage-Aware Encrypted Semantic Communication with Key-Conditioned Signal Perturbation

In briefEncrypted semantic communication with key-controlled signal perturbation and leakage-aware training

SummaryThis paper improves encrypted semantic communication by adding a key-conditioned signal-domain perturbation layer, called LAPI, and an InfoNCE-guided training rule that adds an adaptive leakage penalty to the fixed adversarial objective. Experiments on the Europarl corpus over AWGN and Rayleigh fading channels show that InfoNCE-guided training mainly suppresses the eavesdropper, lowering its BLEU to about 0.07 at high SNR. Adding LAPI further reduces the InfoNCE-estimated leakage by 5-9%, and the two mechanisms can be applied jointly.

In briefMovable-Antenna-Assisted Secret Key Generation over Temporally Correlated Channels

Accelerating QUIC Encryption in WebAssembly Using WASM-SIMD

In briefSpeeding up QUIC encryption in WebAssembly with SIMD instructions

SummaryThis paper examines the cryptographic cost of running the QUIC transport protocol inside WebAssembly (Wasm). The authors write explicit kernels using Wasm's 128-bit SIMD extension for AES and ChaCha20. On two hosts linked by 10-Gigabit Ethernet, these kernels raise receiver goodput over a non-SIMD baseline by 61.34% for AES-128-GCM and 31.55% for ChaCha20-Poly1305. SummaryThis paper examines how LLM agents that rely on external tools may keep trusting those tools after updates without re-verifying their safety, creating a supply-chain vulnerability.
