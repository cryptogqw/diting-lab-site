A note on zero-knowledge ring switching

In briefA note on making ring switching zero knowledge: an extension of Diamond and Posen's technique

SummaryThis paper makes the ring-switching technique of Diamond and Posen zero knowledge, resolving a problem left open in the literature. Over F_{2^n}, the technique uses n-1 auxiliary masks and n-1 quadratic constraints. These constraints are checked in zero knowledge by the underlying commitment scheme.

Correlation Power Analysis on SHA-2-Based SLH-DSA

In briefPower-analysis attack study on SHA-2-based SLH-DSA signatures

SummaryThis paper applies non-profiled correlation power analysis to the address-based pseudorandom function of SLH-DSA across all six SHA-2 parameter sets, targeting SHA-256 compression rounds and message-schedule expansion. Experiments on an STM32F303 ARM Cortex-M4 used 30 secret seeds with 5,000 power traces per seed. Reported rates are conditional stage-wise results with preceding secret values supplied correctly, not end-to-end seed recovery rates. The authors conclude that side-channel protection must cover both compression rounds and message-schedule expansion.

Jinwook Chang, Minjeong Lee, JIN YONG MIN et al.

Information Loss Is All You Need: TC⁰ Pseudorandomness from Worst-Case Lattices

In briefInformation Loss Is All You Need: Post-quantum pseudorandomness in TC⁰ from worst-case lattice hardness

SummaryThe paper introduces the learning with algebraic rounding (LAR) assumption, which replaces LWE's random noise with an explicit deterministic integer rounding map. Its pseudorandomness is based on quantum worst-case lattice problems such as GapSVP and SIVP. The authors use this to construct the first post-quantum weak pseudorandom function with joint key-and-input evaluation in TC⁰. They also show hardness of learning two-hidden-layer ReLU networks from noiseless uniform random examples.

Practical Optimal Bucket Sets with Power-of-Two Radices for LFG-Based MSM Algorithms

In briefEfficient bucket sets for power-of-two radices in LFG-based MSM algorithms

SummaryThis paper addresses multi-scalar multiplication (MSM), a key computation in pairing-based SNARK proof generation. For the Luo–Fu–Gong (LFG) framework, it presents two bucket set constructions for power-of-two radices q=2^c, using multiplier sets {1,2,3} and {1,3}. TFNP in the Random Oracle Model

In briefHardness of TFNP Classes in the Random Oracle Model

SummaryThis paper examines which major TFNP classes are hard in the random oracle model. It shows that PPAD is hard on average in this setting, which together with known PLS hardness yields average-case hardness for EoPL. It also shows that UEoPL can be solved with polynomially many queries by an unbounded solver, and begins studying separations between TFNP subclasses.

Information Loss Is All You Need: TC⁰ Pseudorandomness from Worst-Case Lattices

In briefInformation Loss Is All You Need: Post-Quantum TC⁰ Pseudorandomness from Worst-Case Lattices

SummaryThe paper introduces learning with algebraic rounding (LAR), which applies deterministic integer rounding to blocks of secret-dependent linear measurements and encodes the result in a lattice quotient. For a fixed family with polynomially bounded modulus and dimensions, the authors show these deterministic labels on uniform, secret-independent public matrices are computationally indistinguishable from uniform labels given any polynomial number of classical random examples. Security rests on quantum worst-case hardness of GapSVP and SIVP over general lattices at approximation factor Õ(q). The construction yields a post-quantum weak pseudorandom function with joint key-and-input evaluation in TC⁰, and the paper also proves hardness of learning polynomial-size two-hidden-layer ReLU networks from noiseless uniform random examples.

WhyThe authors propose learning with algebraic rounding, a deterministic-rounding construction giving a TC0 weak PRF from worst-case lattice hardness, plus ReLU network learning hardness.

Chen Qian

Differential Cryptanalysis of SPEEDY: Revised Security Evaluation and Improved Key Recovery Attack

In briefDifferential Cryptanalysis of SPEEDY: Revised Security Evaluation and Improved Key Recovery

SummaryThis paper re-evaluates the resistance of the low-latency block cipher SPEEDY against differential cryptanalysis. The authors propose a search method based on search patterns that imposes no structural restrictions, using computational pruning and a conservative evaluation over the global space. For 4 rounds, the minimum number of active S-boxes is narrowed to [33,34], and the highest-probability differential characteristics known for 2 to 5 rounds are presented. The authors also optimize a prior key recovery attack, yielding the best known attack on SPEEDY-7-192.

Xinwei Yu, Wentao Zhang, Zairui Zhang et al.

The Price of Agreement in Secure Multiparty Computation

In briefThe Cost of Agreement in Secure Multiparty Computation

SummaryThis paper studies secure multiparty computation (MPC) when a dishonest majority of parties may be corrupted, where output delivery cannot be guaranteed and protocols settle for security with abort. It focuses on unanimous abort, where either all honest parties receive output or all abort. The authors prove that for t ≤ n−2 corruptions, unanimous-abort broadcast and hence UA-MPC require at least t/(2(n−t)) rounds in expectation, matching the asymptotic bound for broadcast without abort. Consequently, expected-constant-round UA-MPC is possible if and only if a constant fraction of parties is honest.

Ran Cohen, Pouyan Forghani, Juan Garay et al.

The Power of Interaction in Locally Edge-Private Subgraph Counting

In briefInteractive Privacy Boosts Accuracy in Edge-Private Subgraph Counting

SummaryThis paper studies counting subgraphs of a fixed pattern in any undirected graph under interactive local edge differential privacy. The authors propose a two-round algorithm, Linear Noise Cancellation, with error O(n^{k-3/2}/ε^ℓ) on every graph, showing that one extra interaction round can polynomially improve worst-case accuracy over noninteractive methods. They also give a Predict and Correct framework for graphs with bounded maximum degree and prove matching lower bounds in n and d.

WhyThe authors propose a two-round interactive method improving triangle counting error by √n, with matching lower bounds showing tight bounds.

Xian Chen, Pan Peng

Phantom Timing Leaks on Cortex-M4: A Pre-registered Evaluation of ML-KEM Decapsulation

In briefApparent Timing Leaks on Cortex-M4: ML-KEM Decapsulation Evaluation

SummaryThis pre-registered study uses dudect-style fixed-versus-random timing tests to evaluate nine PQM4 ML-KEM decapsulation implementations on an STM32F411 (Cortex-M4). Invalid ciphertexts showed no detected timing difference, while fresh valid ciphertexts flagged all nine implementations with differences of 4 to 15 cycles. Matched-control and A/A tests suggest that these few-cycle differences can arise from the measurement harness alone, and the authors also found a functional bug in the ML-KEM-1024 m4fstack implementation.

Amadou Tidiane Anne
