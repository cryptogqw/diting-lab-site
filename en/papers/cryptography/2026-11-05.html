Breaking mathsfTraintoDefend via Adaptive Neuron Clustering in Cryptanalytic Model Extraction

In briefBreaking TraintoDefend, a defense against model extraction, using adaptive neuron clustering

SummaryThis paper examines cryptanalytic model extraction, a threat that recovers neural network parameters. It shows that the defense TraintoDefend, which makes neurons learn similar weights, can be circumvented by an adaptive clustering method called SimBreak. Across six protected fully connected networks trained on MNIST, SimBreak raises target layer signature recovery from 0% to 100%. The authors argue that weight similarity alone is not a sufficient basis for protection and that adaptive evaluation is needed.

Haojie Wu, Haolin Liu, Tianshuo Cong et al.

Exact ℓ_p-CVP via Euclidean SVP in Polynomial Space

In briefExact ℓp closest-vector problem solved via exact Euclidean shortest-vector problem in polynomial space

SummaryThe paper gives a randomized, rank-preserving reduction from exact ℓp closest-vector problem (ℓp-CVP) to exact Euclidean shortest-vector problem (SVP) for every integer p≥2. For full-rank lattices, the reduction makes (Cp)^N oracle calls while using polynomial working space. Instantiated with known SVP algorithms, it yields randomized polynomial-space CVP algorithms running in time N^{N/(2e)+o(N)} and N^{N/(4e)+o(N)}.

WhyThe authors give a randomized polynomial-space reduction from exact ℓp-CVP to Euclidean SVP, improving reported CVP time bounds.

Jiseung Kim, Minkyu Kim, Hyung Tae Lee

Quadratic Extractors

In briefQuadratic Extractors: an explicit randomness extractor for quadratic sources

SummaryThis paper studies quadratic sources, random variables formed by applying n quadratic polynomials to a hidden uniform string. The previous best explicit extractor for this model required min-entropy of roughly n minus the square root of log n. The authors give an explicit construction that works for min-entropy polylog(n). For 2-local sources, where each bit depends on two hidden uniform bits, the result also goes below the √n barrier and surpasses sumset extractors.

WhyThe authors report an explicit extractor for quadratic sources with polylog(n) min-entropy, beyond the sqrt(n) barrier and sumset extraction.

Gautam Chandrasekaran, Jesse Goodman, David Zuckerman

SPILLWAY: Separating Target Admission from Sanitization to Defend Tool-Using Agents against Prompt Injection

In briefSPILLWAY: Separating Target Admission from Sanitization to Defend Tool-Using Agents Against Prompt Injection

SummaryThe paper presents SPILLWAY, a defense against prompt injection for AI agents that act through tools such as email, files, and payments. It checks whether the target of an action comes from a typed field in a read-only tool's return, and a fixed rule-based sanitizer removes instruction-like text before the model reads it. The method needs no per-task plan and adds almost no model calls. The authors report that across three backbones and multiple task suites, standard templated injections could not redirect actions to an attacker's target while utility scores stayed high.

WhyThe authors propose a deterministic defense reading action targets from typed tool returns, reporting results across three backbones and seven task suites.

Separating Pseudorandom Codes from Quantum Random Oracles

In briefSeparating Pseudorandom Codes from Quantum Random Oracles

SummaryThis paper studies pseudorandom codes, which must be both robust to noise and computationally indistinguishable from random strings. It proves that symmetric-key pseudorandom codes robust to Bernoulli noise have no black-box construction from quantum random oracles, and so none from quantum-secure one-way functions. The authors then introduce quantum pseudorandom codes and present several positive constructions under various assumptions.

WhyThe authors extend the black-box barrier for pseudorandom codes to quantum random oracles and propose quantum pseudorandom codes with several constructions.

In briefVectorized (2,2)-isogenies in the theta model using AVX-512 for faster SQIsign

SummaryThis paper presents a high-performance AVX-512 implementation of two-dimensional isogenies that exploits data-level parallelism. Beyond Proof of Possession: A Relational Theory of Authority

In briefBeyond Proof of Possession: A Relational Theory of Authority

SummaryThe paper argues that proving an executor holds a credential does not by itself determine its relational profile or the causal history of an execution. It shifts authorization's semantic domain to execution occurrences within causal histories, adds relational attenuation alongside authority attenuation, and proves that authorization decisions depend on the relational profile. It concludes that successor-side proof of possession is one possible evidence mechanism rather than a necessary premise.

STIR/SHAKEN: A Cocktail of Cryptographic Clumsiness

In briefSTIR/SHAKEN: A Cocktail of Cryptographic Clumsiness

SummaryThis paper examines whether STIR/SHAKEN, the caller-identity authentication protocols the U.S. mandated in 2019 to fight call spoofing, achieves its security goals and what it costs the phone system. Analyzing a year of production certificates and test calls, the authors report widespread certificate noncompliance, a flaw letting spoofed caller IDs display as verified, and a way for providers to block later verification by revoking certificates. They also describe new privacy harms, including publicly verifiable proof of calls and leakage of metadata through outsourced signing, and propose fixes such as certificate transparency and blind signing for asymmetric message franking.

WhyThe authors analyze STIR/SHAKEN's security and privacy costs, report production-traffic measurements and test calls, and design a blind signing protocol for asymmetric message franking.

Josh Brown, Matthew Hardeman, Paul Grubbs

Sumcheck bit packing

In briefSumcheck bit packing: packing many bits into one field element while keeping them verifiable

SummaryThis paper studies how circuit-based zero-knowledge proofs can store m bits in one element of GF(2^m) while still verifying linear claims about each bit. The authors present a simpler reduction that uses only the GF(2)-linearity of the field trace and achieves soundness error 2^(-m) for arbitrary bit-linear claims. Using a characterization of trace-zero field elements, the reduction is made zero-knowledge over any hiding linear commitment scheme, and the protocol requires only linear constraints.

Keyber: Recovering the Signal by Amplifying the Noise in ML-KEM

In briefKeyber: A fault attack on ML-KEM key generation that amplifies noise to recover the secret key

SummaryThis paper presents Keyber, the first statistical ineffective fault attack (SIFA) on ML-KEM key generation. A single fault adds a known value to the public key's error vector while leaving the public matrix seed intact, so later decapsulations carry a known induced error. The attacker keeps only ciphertexts whose error peaks at exactly one coefficient, obtaining one linear inequality on the secret key per query. The authors implemented the attack on ML-KEM-512 on an ARM Cortex-M4 device and recovered the full secret key end-to-end, showing that key generation must be hardened as well as decapsulation.

Cryptanalysis of Pairing-Based Additive Masking with Prime-Field Secrets

In briefCryptanalysis of a pairing-based masking scheme that reveals prime-field secrets
