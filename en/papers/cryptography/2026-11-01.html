Step-Zero Attack: Agent Safety Before Observability

In briefStep-Zero Attack: Agent Safety Before Observability

SummaryThis paper argues that existing attacks and defenses for coding agents target the stage after content enters the model, while agents often must first process unknown files, network responses, or images to judge their safety, and that processing can itself cause harm. The authors call this initial processing "Step Zero" and test seven harnesses and six models. Most combinations exceeded a disk budget before the model saw any content, and the paper proposes a gate at the processing-primitive level that keeps extraction within budget.

WhyThe authors introduce Step-Zero attacks, where harm occurs before the model reads content, testing seven harnesses and six models with a byte-counting gate defense.

CertClaim: Towards Certified Model-Claim Verification for Coding-Agent Services

In briefCertClaim: A certified method for verifying model claims in coding-agent services

SummaryCoding-agent services are priced according to the model they claim to use, so verifying those claims matters to users. The paper argues that existing model auditing methods assume a fixed deployment, while providers may legitimately change sampling settings, reasoning mode, or system prompts without changing the model, causing false accusations or missed cheap substitutes. The authors propose CertClaim, which treats all authorized deployments as part of the claim and checks whether some authorized deployment explains the service's evidence. The paper provides theoretical analysis of its false-accusation rate and detection condition, and experiments show it is valid across settings and resistant to potential adaptive attacks.

Jiacheng Lu, Qixuan Yang, Tao Song et al.

Reddit Community Signals as a Supplementary Domain-Reputation Feature in Spam Classification: A Conservative Controlled Evaluation

In briefReddit community signals as a supplementary domain-reputation feature for spam classification, evaluated conservatively

SummaryThis study tests whether signals derived from Reddit discussions can serve as an extra domain-reputation feature for email spam classification. Using a 5,582-email corpus preprocessed from SpamAssassin, it compares a baseline, a version with urgency and action scores, and a version adding Reddit-based features. The Random Forest model with Reddit-enhanced features reaches 97.94% accuracy, a 0.55-point gain over the baseline that is not statistically significant in the primary split. The authors frame the results conservatively and call for further validation before deployment.

Fatima Salcedo, Jide Edu

In briefJ-Anchor: Defending Language Models Against Prefill Attacks by Restoring Refusal in J-Space

SummaryThis paper studies prefill jailbreaks, where an attacker supplies an unsafe assistant prefix that makes a safety-aligned language model continue generating harmful content. The authors observe refusal-state erosion: during prefilling, the refusal signal falls back to its benign baseline while the harmfulness signal stays elevated. They propose J-Anchor, a training-free inference-time defense that uses the pre-attack signals as an anchor to selectively restore refusal in J-space. The method reduces attack success across model families while largely preserving benign behavior and reasoning ability.

A Large-Scale Evaluation of Cookie Notice Privacy and Accessibility for Blind and Low Vision Users

In briefLarge-scale check of cookie notice privacy and accessibility for blind and low vision users

SummaryThis paper presents the first large-scale evaluation of cookie notices across 2,061 domains, focusing on blind and low vision users. Using a crawler, a custom screen reader add-on, and accessibility tools, it measures UK GDPR/PECR compliance, tracker activity before and after consent choices, and screen reader accessibility. It finds that 51% of sites show a cookie notice, and at least 50% of those violate UK GDPR/PECR requirements. Accepting a notice produces a mean five-fold increase in tracker activity. SHield: Watermarking 4D Gaussian Splatting via Spherical Harmonic Coefficient Perturbation

In briefSHield: Watermarking 4D Gaussian Splatting via Spherical Harmonic Coefficient Perturbation

SummaryThis paper presents SHield, a watermarking method that protects 4D Gaussian Splatting (4DGS) model files from unauthorized copying. It embeds a zero-mean payload across the spherical harmonic coefficients of a key-selected subset of Gaussians, with a brief anchored optimization to preserve visual fidelity. Verification needs no neural decoder and recovers the payload in closed form. Across 30 scenes from D-NeRF, HyperNeRF, and DyNeRF, payload recovery reaches a Pearson correlation of at least 0.991 with a mean rendering penalty of at most 0.13 dB, and the image payload survives INT8 quantization and 30% opacity pruning.

Kunal Bhosikar, Prajas Wadekar, Venkata Sai Pranav Bachina et al.

In briefDefending Post-Quantum Cryptography Against Side-Channel Attacks Using 3D Shielding

SummaryThis paper notes that post-quantum cryptography (PQC) algorithms, though resistant to quantum attacks in theory, remain vulnerable to side-channel attacks in practical implementations. The authors propose a defense based on 3D integration, which uses stacked hardware for physical shielding and introduces decoy keys to conceal cryptographic activity. Hidden Curves and Short Duals: Cubic Jets, Dual Leaks, and the Limits of Masked-Code Encryption

In briefHidden Curves and Short Duals: Limits of Masked-Code Encryption

SummaryThis paper analyzes two masking schemes, Wang's RLCE and the (U|U+V) construction of UVW-KEM, which were proposed as compact alternatives to Goppa codes. The authors show that for Reed-Solomon and algebraic-geometry codes the mask hides a curve, which can be detected through cubic jet counts, and they explain a public-key key recovery attack on UVW-KEM that is not specific to UVW. They also show that both masks preserve the dual of the hidden code, leaking low-weight dual words. The authors conclude that no compact masked trapdoor code is currently known that avoids these structural weaknesses.

WhyThe authors propose hidden-pairing codes, give a closed-form cubic jet dependency bound, and report that both masks leak the hidden code's dual across parameters.
