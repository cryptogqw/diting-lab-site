Extraction meets Distillation - cryptanalytic techniques to recover parameters from a Deep Neural Network

In briefCombining weight extraction with label-based learning to reconstruct neural network parameters

SummaryThis paper studies reconstructing fully connected neural networks by combining cryptanalytic weight-direction extraction with learning from predicted labels, adapting an extraction method from ReLU to Leaky ReLU. On the largest synthetic architecture, Leaky ReLU recovers 51 to 57 neurons in the deepest hidden layer while ReLU recovers none, though this advantage does not hold uniformly across architectures and layers. The authors also note that high prediction agreement does not identify the network's parameters.

Biprarshi Biswas, Arya Anshuman Ojha, Yubaraj Das et al.

In briefProbeID: Checking That an LLM Agent's MCP Tool Is the Real One When Registries Change

The Exact Bit Security of Hash-Based Signatures

In briefExact Bit Security of Hash-Based Signatures

SummaryThis paper analyzes the security of hash-based signatures in Lean4, showing how different ways of counting an adversary's work produce different bit-security figures. Using two XMSS instances with 128-bit digests as examples, it finds about 127 bits of strong unforgeability under most counting modes, but only 122 bits under the most conservative mode that counts direct random oracle queries. The bounds are proven in the random oracle model without computational hardness assumptions, with matching attacks provided.

Mikhail Kudinov, Benedikt Wagner, Emile Wambsgans

Provable Security of SHA-256-Based LMS Revisited in the Ideal-Cipher Model

In briefRevisiting provable security of SHA-256-based LMS in the ideal-cipher model

SummaryThe hash-based LMS signature scheme is standardized by IETF, NIST and ISO. The paper argues that earlier proofs ignored the real structure of SHA-256, a chopped Merkle-Damgård construction, and shows forgery attacks that make those random-oracle bounds inapplicable to the concrete schemes. The authors give security proofs in the ideal-cipher model for additive Davies-Meyer abstractions of LMS_SHA256_M32 and LMS_SHA256_M24. They conclude that both schemes offer a moderate, though not full, level of security.
