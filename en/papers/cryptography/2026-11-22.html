Adaptation or Poisoning? Security Risks of Conversational Test-Time Learning in Large Language Models

In briefAdaptation or Poisoning? Security Risks of Conversational Test-Time Learning in LLMs

SummaryThis paper examines security risks of test-time learning in large language models, where a lightweight adapter is updated on user inputs during deployment. The authors introduce P-TAP, a query-only multi-turn attack that gradually poisons the session adapter through ordinary interactions so that a final harmful request is answered after the model is jailbroken. Across four open-weight instruction-tuned models, enabling updates raises attack success rates on average, and adapting on unrelated or same-topic benign text removes most of that increase.

WhyThe authors propose P-TAP, a query-only multi-turn attack poisoning session adapters via test-time updates; the paper reports attack success gains across four open-weight instruction-tuned models.

Honghao Feng, Shanhan Qu, Kai Tong et al.

In briefThreshold hardened key derivation for BIP32-style wallets using the Hydra PRF

SummaryThis paper proposes a threshold version of BIP32 hardened key derivation that replaces HMAC-SHA512 with the arithmetization-oriented PRF Hydra, computed within a multi-party computation. The derived keys differ from BIP32's, so the scheme is a sibling of BIP32 rather than wire-compatible with it. The authors give security proofs and compare a five-party implementation against HMAC-SHA512 in rounds and running time.

Finding Optimal Small-Root Bounds for the Bivariate Coppersmith Method over the Integers

In briefOptimal Small-Root Bounds for Bivariate Coppersmith Method over Integers

SummaryThis paper settles the long-open question of the optimal small-root bounds achievable by Coppersmith's method for bivariate polynomials over the integers. Using a newly introduced notion of quasi-Newton polygons, it determines the optimal bounds for Coron's lattice construction and shows they reduce to solving a univariate equation defined by the Newton polygon's vertices. The authors apply these results to cryptanalysis, evaluating bounds from over twenty cryptographic settings and proving that most are optimal.

WhyThe authors introduce quasi-Newton polygons, prove optimality of small-root bounds for Coron's lattice construction, and report that most bounds across twenty-plus cryptographic settings are optimal.

Zhaopeng Ding, Yanshuo Zhang, Zhaopeng Dai et al.

RSWP: Robust SO(3)-Equivariant Watermarking for Panoramic Images

In briefRSWP: SO(3)-equivariant watermarking that stays robust for panoramic images

SummaryThis paper addresses robust watermarking for panoramic images, where spherical content and flat representations do not match and existing methods break under 3D viewing rotations. It proposes RSWP, which uses icosahedral group theory to split sphere rotations into local planar rotations and facet permutations, embedding watermarks into discrete facets. An equivariant feature extractor and a permutation-invariant sorting module decode the watermark, allowing blind retrieval from partial 2D renderings. The authors report state-of-the-art robustness to continuous SO(3) rotations and viewport transformations with high visual fidelity.

Pengzhen Chen, Yanwei Liu, Xiaoyan Gu et al.

In briefNRSnooper: A passive sniffer that decodes live two-way traffic on commercial 5G networks

SummaryThis paper presents NRSnooper, a passive sniffer that decodes downlink control and bidirectional data traffic in commercial 5G NR networks in real time, including traffic from already-connected devices and after encrypted configuration updates. In briefPhysical-layer authentication for near-field signals with noisy enrollment

SummaryThis paper studies physical-layer authentication in the near field, where the receiver estimates a spatial reference from noisy enrollment pilots.
