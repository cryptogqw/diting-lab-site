SCR: Detecting Backdoor-Poisoned Samples through Clean-Reference Residuals

In briefSCR: Detecting Poisoned Training Samples via Clean-Reference Residuals

SummaryThis paper proposes Semantic Counterfactual Residualization (SCR), a method that uses a small set of clean reference samples to reconstruct each sample's features and separate benign variation from poisoning signals. It detects backdoor-poisoned samples by finding coherent deviations from these clean baselines, without prior knowledge of the attack. Tested on twelve dirty-label and clean-label attacks across CIFAR-10, CIFAR-100, and Tiny ImageNet, SCR reports attack-averaged true positive rates above 99.5% and false positive rates below 2%, outperforming eight baseline detectors in macro-TPR.

In briefRECLAIM: Robot-Vision Adversarial Patch Defense via Embodied Attention Feedback

SummaryThis paper introduces RECLAIM, a training-free defense that protects vision-language-action robot policies from adversarial patches at inference time. It uses the frozen model's attention responses to local visual edits as feedback to decide where and how to purify the image, combined with robot geometry, and rechecks these choices as the scene changes during manipulation. Evaluated on patches from four attack methods across several manipulation task suites, RECLAIM succeeds in at least one rollout on 95% of tasks and raises LIBERO-Object success from 0% to 70.0%. Its mean decision time is 548.9 ms per control step.

Jiayou Zhu, Peng Guo, Litian Zhang et al.

Who Pays for Byzantine Robustness? Validity, Data-Share Dictatorships, and Fairness When Only a Few Hospitals Collaborate

In briefWho Pays for Byzantine Robustness? Validity, Data-Share Dictatorships, and Fairness in Small Hospital Federated Learning

SummaryThis paper empirically audits eight standard aggregation rules for federated learning, including FedAvg, coordinate-wise median, Krum, and GeoMed, on three medical datasets from the FLamby benchmark. It compares unweighted and sample-size-weighted versions across collaborations of 4 to 6 hospitals under attacks by one or two malicious clients. Exact-twin leave-out controls separate losses from lost honest data from damage caused by attacks, and the study finds that skewed data shares can make weighted rules collapse or become dictated by one hospital.

Adnan Malkani
