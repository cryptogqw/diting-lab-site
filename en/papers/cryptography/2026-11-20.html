SoK: Assessing the Evolution of LLM Capability in Software Vulnerability Research

In briefA review of how LLM capability in software vulnerability research has evolved

SummaryThis paper proposes a framework that rates LLM-based agents' ability to discover, validate and patch vulnerabilities on an autonomy scale modeled on driving automation levels, and systematizes the published evidence. It analyzes a decade of public vulnerability records, advisories and exploitation data, finding that disclosed vulnerabilities more than doubled in 2026 with a rising share rated critical or high. The time between report and fix, and between disclosure and attack, has not noticeably changed so far. The authors also grade proposed mitigations with an explicit rubric.

Amine Lbath, Manan Suri, Aurelien Delaitre et al.

Classical Oracle Separation of QMA from the QCMA Hierarchy and Quantum Cryptography Even When P = QCMA

In briefClassical oracle separation of QMA from the QCMA hierarchy and quantum cryptography, even when P = QCMA

SummaryThe paper introduces a new problem called Codeword-Hash pairs in Noise (CHN) and proves, in the query complexity model, that it lies in QMA but is hard for the QCMA hierarchy (QCMAH). Using this problem, it constructs a classical oracle under which QMA is strictly more powerful than QCMAH. The paper also shows that, relative to an oracle where P = QCMA, various quantum cryptographic primitives, such as pseudorandom states with classical keys, can still exist.

WhyThe authors introduce CHN, prove a classical-oracle separation of QMA from the QCMA hierarchy, and answer an open question of Kretschmer, Qian, and Tal.

Alper Cakan

Text-Aware, Vision-Blind: How Multimodal Image Editing Systems Handle Unsafe Inputs

In briefText-Aware, Vision-Blind: How Multimodal Image Editing Systems Handle Unsafe Inputs

SummaryThis paper introduces EditSafetyBench, a benchmark of 5,940 image-instruction pairs covering 11 risk categories and three edit intents, for measuring the safety awareness of image editing systems. Four systems, Gemini, GPT, Qwen API, and Qwen Local, were evaluated on it. The authors find that these systems refuse far more often when the text prompt is unsafe than when the uploaded image is unsafe with a neutral instruction. They conclude that safety guardrails should reason jointly over both visual and textual inputs.

Ziqing Yang, Yixin Wu, Michael Backes et al.

GEOHunter: Mapping a Generative Engine Optimization Network in the Wild

In briefGEOHunter: Tracing a Paid Promotion Network That Influences AI Search Answers

SummaryThis paper examines a network that sells visibility in AI-generated answers by publishing paid promotional articles on three major Chinese user-generated-content platforms. The authors identified 1,985 associated accounts and 466,357 inferred promotional placements spanning multiple months and product categories. Across nine generative engines from the US and China, 4.5% to 20% of retrievable cited pages were attributable to the network, and its clients were named in 52% of answers on eight engines. The authors conclude that content-level defenses are insufficient, and that account-level and platform-level signals are needed.

WhyThe authors measure a paid GEO content network across three platforms, identifying 1,985 accounts and 466,357 placements and assessing impact on nine generative engines.

Sean Tang, Peiran Wang, Ying Li et al.

Replication of Agentic Security Events: Audit and Investigation (ROGUE-AI)

In briefReplicating AI Agent Security Incidents for Audit (ROGUE-AI)

SummaryThe paper presents ROGUE-AI, a multi-agent framework that rebuilds reported incidents involving LLM-based agents offline, paired with a control lacking only the blamed cause. Across 108 attempted replications, about 89% reproduced the reported harm. Most replications had no attacker, and agents missed ordinary hazards while doing their assigned tasks, causing more harm than prompt-injection replications.

WhyThe authors rebuild 108 real incidents with matched controls lacking the blamed cause, reporting 89% harm reproduction and 18% to 33% error rates across five models.

Mengxiao Wang, Mahbub Alam, Kalyan Nakka et al.

Backtracking Agent Applications Security Incidents

In briefTracing security incidents in AI agent apps with Origin, a tool that finds entry points

SummaryThis paper addresses how organizations investigate security incidents in agent applications by tracing back to the compromise entry point (CEP). Because trajectory logs describe agent functionality while CEP candidates are code-level function calls, analysts must manually map between the two. The authors present Origin, which uses a cross-layer dependency graph, a query language, and a region inference algorithm to link these semantic layers. In case studies on five popular agent applications across eight attack incidents, an analyst using Origin correctly identified all CEPs.

Yicheng Cai, Taegyu Kim, Peng Liu

Visual Attribution Limits and Graded Provenance Auditing in Commercial Video-Generation APIs

In briefVisual attribution limits and graded provenance auditing for commercial video-generation APIs

SummaryThe paper examines whether a delivered video file can establish which model or tier produced it, when intermediaries might substitute cheaper models or cached clips. It combines passive attribution probes with cryptographic provenance checks such as C2PA across 19 commercial endpoints. The authors conclude that only authenticated provenance supports accusations, while passive detection accuracy drops sharply after re-encoding.

Haocheng Mei, Yuchuan Tian, Yingte Shu et al.

When Suspicion Adds Up: A Stochastic Framework for Optimizing Honeypot-Based Insider-Threat Detection

In briefOptimizing honeypot-based detection of insider threats through accumulating evidence

SummaryThis paper proposes a decision framework for detecting insider threats, where trusted users misuse their access to organizational systems. It models evidence accumulated from honeypot interactions as a continuous-time Markov chain and jointly selects the number of honeypots and the monitoring horizon to minimize expected losses from malicious activity and false detections. For thresholds of two and three interactions, the authors derive closed-form true- and false-detection probabilities and expected detection times, and present numerical results.

Saharnaz Mehrani, Lujia Zhan, Mohsen Emadikhiav et al.

DailySafe: Benchmarking Dynamic Safety Awareness in Everyday Multi-Turn Multimodal Interactions

In briefDailySafe: A benchmark for testing whether AI models stay safe during everyday multi-turn, multi-image conversations

SummaryThis paper introduces DailySafe, a benchmark for evaluating how well multimodal large language models maintain dynamic safety awareness in everyday interactions. It contains 195 naturalistic multi-turn, multi-image conversations spanning 10 daily domains and 42 task categories. Safety-critical evidence is revealed gradually across dialogue turns and visual observations, and the benchmark checks whether models recognize evolving risks while still supporting users' legitimate goals.

Xuankun Rong, Xinhao Deng, Wenzhang Jiang et al.
