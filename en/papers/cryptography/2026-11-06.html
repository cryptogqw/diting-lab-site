SoK: Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) Integration in Software Development: Approaches, Gaps, and Future Directions

In briefSurvey of Post-Quantum Cryptography Integration in Software Development

SummaryThis paper systematises existing approaches to integrating post-quantum cryptography (PQC) into software development, covering implementation, migration, and developer education. The authors analyse these approaches against NIST PQC standards and migration guidance, along with lifecycle coverage and testing evidence. They find that prior work builds useful technical and organisational foundations but offers disconnected support across the software lifecycle, with evaluations concentrated in controlled or early-stage settings. The paper identifies cross-cutting gaps and calls for lifecycle-oriented, empirically validated approaches.

Shakya R.D.N., Craig Costello, Nalin A.G. Arachchilage

FedReason: Large Language Models as Weight-Space Meta-Aggregators for Heterogeneous Federated Learning

In briefFedReason: Using Large Language Models to Choose How Federated Learning Combines Client Models

SummaryThis paper introduces FedReason, in which a large language model inspects client model weights every ten rounds, runs code in a restricted server sandbox, and writes a short Python aggregation program the server applies. It falls back to FedAvg when the output is invalid, and full weights never leave the server. In 168 runs on CIFAR-10 and CIFAR-100, it beat FedAvg by 1.26 points on average under label skew and excluded all sign-flipping malicious clients in the first round.

Ali Mohammadi Ruzbahani, Abbas Yazdinejad, Hadis Karimipour

SensorGuard-RV: Sensor-to-Register Data Protection for Resource-Constrained RISC-V Devices

In briefSensorGuard-RV: A hardware-first way to protect sensor data on small RISC-V devices

SummaryThis paper addresses the risk that sensor readings in embedded systems can be intercepted, overwritten, fabricated, or replayed as they travel through hardware and firmware. It proposes SensorGuard-RV, which hands the sensor to a fixed-function controller that performs authenticated encryption under a key software cannot access, and adds a RISC-V instruction that writes a sample to a register only after verifying its tag and rejecting replays. Implemented on the NEORV32 core with an ADXL362 accelerometer on an Artix-7 FPGA, a protected sample reaches a register in 51.9 microseconds and the protected loop runs 5.3% faster than a software-driven SPI baseline. The added logic and estimated power increase, with the Ascon engine accounting for most of the added logic.

Hamid Noori, Carlton Shepherd

LABBA: LLM-Assisted Behavioral Biometric Authentication

In briefLABBA: LLM-Assisted Behavioral Biometric Authentication

SummaryThis paper proposes a context-aware authentication framework that adds an LLM-based reasoning module to a phone-blowing behavioral biometric technique. Blow-acoustic signals were collected from 50 participants, with spectral and temporal features converted into similarity representations and structured into prompts. The framework reported 99.46% accuracy in rule-based mode and 99.38% in score-based mode, outperforming its non-LLM counterpart.

Howard Halim, Eyasu Getahun Chekole, Jianying Zhou

Decision Models as Retrieval Gates Against Injection and Pollution

In briefUsing Small Decision Models as Retrieval Gates Against Injection and Pollution

SummaryThe paper tests whether open-weight decision models can screen retrieved web passages for hidden injected instructions and synthetic misleading pages. On a 1,000-question benchmark of forum-style pages, attack success fell from 27.9% with no defence to 5.7% with the gate added, while clean-query responsiveness stayed nearly unchanged. In a pollution test, filtering the candidate pool before selecting the top ten kept fake pages out of answers (0.5% exposure) and held accuracy at 83%.

Mahdi Ghadamyari, Reza Habibi

Escalate or Close: How Fast-Tier Models Handle Held-Out Web Attack Families in Two-Tier Triage

In briefEscalate or Close: How Fast-Tier Models Handle Unseen Web Attacks in Two-Tier Triage

SummaryThis paper examines two-tier triage systems, where a fast model decides most web requests and passes the rest to a slow tier. The authors hold out seven attack families from training and splice their payloads into real browsing traffic, comparing zero-shot decision models, supervised detectors, a local language model, the OWASP Core Rule Set and an anomaly baseline. Detectors trained on known families often give held-out families a confident benign verdict, while two hosted decision models escalate at least 97.6% of every family within the same benign budget.

Maciej Kisielewicz, Micha{\l} K\k{e}dziora, Ngoc Thanh Nguyen

Zirah: A Post-Quantum True Random Number Generator Hardware Signer With an Avalanche Noise

In briefZirah: A Hardware Signer with Avalanche-Noise Random Numbers for Post-Quantum Signatures

SummaryZirah is a low-cost hardware signer built on an STM32F407 microcontroller, intended to show where a post-quantum signing key is exposed on inexpensive hardware and what protects it. It draws random bits from avalanche breakdown in a reverse-biased 2N3904 transistor and computes ML-DSA-65 signatures with arithmetic masking, adding ECDSA P-256 from an ATECC608 secure element. The paper reports NIST SP 800-90B test results, signing times, and a key-storage weakness where an unprotected key could be copied from flash.

Muhammad Shaheer Bin Junaid

arXiv cs.IT · cs.CR · cs.LGNov 6, 2026 #

AI-Native Task-Oriented Semantic Communications over a Relay: Benefits and Vulnerabilities

In briefAI-native task-oriented semantic communication via a relay: gains and security risks

SummaryThis paper studies semantic communication in which a relay node helps deliver task-relevant information from a source to a destination, with meaning defined by classification labels. The authors jointly design the source encoder, relay transformation, and destination decoders to optimize both semantic inference accuracy and perceptual reconstruction quality for image data. Relay-assisted schemes outperform direct transmission, with larger gains at low signal-to-noise ratio. The paper also identifies a security vulnerability introduced by the relay: a Byzantine attack in which the relay injects learned perturbations into its transmitted signal, significantly degrading the destination's semantic inference accuracy.

Yalin E. Sagduyu, Tugba Erpek, Aylin Yener
